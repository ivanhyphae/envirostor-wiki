# Timeline: Origin and Siting of the Modesto Soil Stockpiles

## Scope note

This document currently answers one specific question: **were the three soil stockpiles built where they sit today, or moved there later, and were they pre-sited for the future SR-99/132 interchange?** It is intentionally narrow. The plan is to broaden this into a **general chronology of the entire site** (investigation → risk assessment → remedy selection → construction → institutional controls — see [contaminants-primer.md](contaminants-primer.md) for a first pass at that fuller timeline) once the origin question is settled and reviewed. Treat this as a seed document, not a finished chronology.

## Summary

The stockpiles were **not relocated from elsewhere** — they were built in place, on Caltrans right-of-way, as a byproduct of SR-99 construction starting in 1961, and the source documents explicitly say the excavated material was placed "for the future SR99/132 Interchange." However, the three stockpiles were **not all built at the same time**: Stockpiles 2 and 3 already existed at their current footprint by June 1963; Stockpile 1's footprint was still vacant, rural/agricultural land at that date, and wasn't filled until sometime between 1963 and 1967.

No document in the reviewed corpus contains a map or figure literally labeled "future stockpile 1." The phrase found in the record is "future SR99/132 Interchange," describing the highway project the fill material was intended for.

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1930s–1970s | FMC Corporation and predecessors process barite/celestite ore at the adjacent facility; liquid tailings discharged to unlined evaporation ponds.[^ssi-hist] |
| 1961 | State purchases a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwest portion of the FMC facility, including part of the tailings ponds, for SR-99 construction. Pond tailings and native soil are excavated and placed in lifts "to form bridge abutments and embankment fills for the future SR99/132 Interchange," creating Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3 within Caltrans right-of-way.[^ssi-hist] |
| Jun 1963 | Aerial photograph (Figure 3) shows SR-99 mainline and Kansas Avenue interchange grading underway, and **Stockpiles 2 and 3 already placed** at their current locations. **Stockpile 1's footprint is still rural residential/agricultural land** — not yet filled.[^ssi-1963] |
| 1963–1967 | Stockpile 1 is placed in its current footprint sometime in this window (no aerial photo or design drawing from this specific interval was found in the reviewed corpus).[^ssi-gap] |
| 1967 | Aerial photograph shows SR-99 fully completed through the interchange, and **"Stockpiles 1, 2 and 3 existed essentially as they do today."**[^fs-1967] |
| 2003 | Shaw Environmental & Infrastructure conducts an Initial Site Assessment — the first documented environmental investigation of the stockpiles.[^fs-hist] |

## Key sourced quotes

**On the 1961 origin and SR-99/132 connection**, from the *Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) Report*, Rev. 03/2013 — cross-checked against the raw PDF (`raw/S9525-06-44 Modesto Stockpiles SSI Report Rev.0313.pdf`), text matches exactly:

> "From the 1950s to the 1970s, a liquid residue generated by FMC at this facility was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwest portion of the FMC facility, including a portion of the ponds, was purchased by the State for the construction of SR99 through Modesto. Pond tailings and native soil were removed from this parcel and placed in lifts to form bridge abutments and embankment fills for the future SR99/132 Interchange south of FMC."

**On the June 1963 aerial photo**, same report, §2.2 ("Additional Site History Research") — cross-checked against raw PDF:

> "We obtained a June 1963 aerial photograph (Figure 3) to aid in evaluating the soil stockpile and adjacent property conditions during construction of the excavated depressed section of SR99 north and south of Kansas Avenue. The 1963 photograph shows grading/construction of the SR99 mainline including the southwestern portion of the FMC property, interchange ramps at Kansas Avenue, and placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3... Adjacent property conditions included rural residential and agricultural property west of Emerald Avenue at the current location of Stockpile 1."

**DTSC flagging the documentation gap for Stockpile 1**, same report:

> "As noted in Figure 3, placement of soil in the vicinity of Stockpile 1 had not been implemented at the time of this photograph (1963) and residential/commercial development is absent along the footprint of the existing Stockpile 1. If possible, please include additional aerial photographs showing the placement of soil material in the ROW of Stockpile 1 and/or design drawings for this purpose."

**On the 1967 photo resolving Stockpile 1's timing**, from the *Final Feasibility Study Report* (June 2014) — cross-checked against the raw PDF (`raw/S9800-01-17 Modesto Soil Stockpiles Final FS Report.0614.pdf`); the same language recurs verbatim in the 2018 RAP and the SR 132 EIR/EA appendices:

> "The 1967 photograph shows that SR-99 north and south of the Kansas Avenue interchange had been completed and Stockpiles 1, 2 and 3 existed essentially as they do today."

## Caveats

- All technical reports (SSI 2013, HHRA Update 2013, FS 2014, RAP 2018, DEIR/FEIR 2018) repeat this same history in near-identical language, indicating it was researched once and copied forward rather than independently re-verified in each report. It is internally consistent but not independently corroborated across sources.
- No contemporaneous 1960s-era planning document or construction drawing was found in this corpus — all historical evidence here is retrospective analysis performed in 2012–2013 (SSI) and 2014 (FS) using aerial photos obtained after the fact.
- The specific label "future stockpile 1" was not found anywhere in the reviewed documents. If that label exists (e.g., as a handwritten annotation on an original 1960s planning map), it is not part of this document set.
