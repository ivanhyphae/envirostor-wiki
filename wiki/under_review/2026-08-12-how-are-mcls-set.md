Based on the wiki context provided, MCLs (Maximum Contaminant Levels) are set as regulatory drinking water standards. The context notes:

- **California MCLs** are used to evaluate groundwater quality, and are described as "drinking water standards" [[wiki/concepts/california-mcl.md]].
- **Primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (primary MCLs)** are "regulatory standards for drinking water quality" used as benchmarks for surface water safety [[wiki/concepts/primary-maximum-contaminant-levels.md]].
- **California Primary MCLs** are "regulatory standard[s] used to assess groundwater quality," applied specifically to general mineral constituents [[wiki/concepts/california-primary-mcl.md]].

The provided excerpts do not describe the legal or administrative process by which these levels are established (e.g., which agency sets them, or the scientific/risk-based methodology). The text only references their **role** as regulatory benchmarks.
