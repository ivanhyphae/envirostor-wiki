- **Aerially Deposited Lead (ADL) at the project site** originates from historical leaded gasoline emissions, accumulating in shallow surface soils along transportation corridors. It is characterized as low-level, laterally extensive contamination, distinct from point-source industrial releases. Specific ADL concentration values are not documented in the available wiki sources [[aerially-deposited-lead.md]].

- **Comparison to other lead sources at the site**:
  - **ADL** is generally low-level and widespread across the SR 132 corridor.
  - **Other lead sources** (e.g., from Caltrans maintenance operations such as lead-based paint and roadway debris) are more concentrated in the soil stockpiles. For example, stockpile S-3 had a maximum lead concentration of 760 mg/kg, with a 95% UCL of 410 mg/kg, exceeding the DTSC residential screening level of 80 mg/kg [[lead.md]]. Post-remedial verification of that stockpile showed residual lead at 45 mg/kg, below the action level [[lead.md]].
  - **Borrow area clean fill** (used as cover) contained lead at 45 mg/kg, consistent with background and well below the 80 mg/kg threshold [[clean-fill-suitable-as-cover.md]].
  - **Groundwater lead** near the stockpiles (well MW-5) was detected at 37 µg/L, exceeding the MCL of 15 µg/L, indicating leaching from concentrated lead sources [[lead.md]].

- **Key distinction**: ADL represents the ambient, historically deposited lead in topsoil, while other lead sources at the site are derived from maintenance activities and exhibit higher, more localized concentrations in stockpiles and groundwater.
