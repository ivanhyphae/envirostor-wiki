The Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR), prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans and dated December 23, 2021, along with supporting analytical reports and technical memoranda from Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) and Geocon, details the remediation of barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially deposited lead (ADL) impacted soil in Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3 at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project. This interim report fulfills a requirement for Interim Phase I of the SR 132 West Project, with a Final RACR anticipated in 2028.

The project addresses barium and lead contamination that, while not posing an unacceptable risk to offsite receptors or groundwater, exceeded remedial design implementation plan (RDIP) verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg for barium and 80 mg/kg for lead in certain areas. Groundwater monitoring consistently showed contaminant levels below California Maximum Contaminant Levels, and storm water and air monitoring during excavation confirmed no offsite migration.

The chosen remedy is "Containment," achieved by consolidating the BCS stockpiles as highway embankment fill within their existing footprint and capping them with Caltrans-maintained structural pavement or DTSC-approved clean fill. This minimizes offsite disposal. Specific activities included the removal and containment of barium-contaminated material from Stockpile 3 and portions of Stockpile 2, as well as soil from the slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2 for Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) wall construction. ADL-contaminated soil exceeding hazardous waste criteria was placed within the BCS Stockpile 1 containment zone, at least five feet above groundwater and covered.

Laboratory analytical reports from March 2020 show initial barium concentrations in Stockpile 3 reached up to 7,000 mg/kg. However, subsequent re-excavation successfully reduced final barium concentrations in Stockpiles 2 and 3 to below 1,000 mg/kg, and lead concentrations to below 80 mg/kg. Similarly, Stockpile 2 remediation involved removing BCS from the west and east ends and MSE wall footing excavation, with initial barium levels up to 4,100 mg/kg being reduced to well below the 1,000 mg/kg threshold after additional excavation. Stockpile 1 soil characterization for the MSE wall footing revealed barium concentrations generally exceeding site-specific background but remaining below removal thresholds, making the material suitable for re-use within the project's containment zones or as embankment fill.

Variances to the RDIP, issued in April and September 2020, approved the use of clean cover fill materials, including a minimum of 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase, for interim capping of Stockpiles 1 and 2 until permanent pavement or hardscape is placed, ensuring effective long-term containment. Five-year reviews will be conducted to assess the long-term effectiveness of these measures. The analytical data uniformly demonstrate the successful remediation and characterization of barium and lead in the project's stockpiles, with all final excavation samples meeting established removal thresholds and characterized materials deemed suitable for on-site re-use within containment zones, embankment fill, or as capping material, pending final regulatory approvals for inclusion in the RACR. Quality control data indicated some matrix interference for barium recoveries but overall validated by laboratory controls.
