## Document identity
- **Document type and purpose**: Final Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement between the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and Caltrans, covering the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Phase 1 Interim SR 132 West Project. The purpose is to establish enforceable requirements for maintaining the installed remedy (consolidation and clean fill capping of barium-containing soil used as highway embankment). The cover letter transmits the final agreement and announces the forthcoming Land Use Covenant.
- **Author or preparer**: Cover letter signed by Mr. Steven Becker, P.G., Branch Chief, Santa Susana Field Laboratory Branch, DTSC Site Mitigation and Restoration Program. O&M Plan prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. (John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG). Legal description prepared by NorthStar Engineering Group, Inc. (Rien Groenewoud, P.L.S. 6946).
- **Date or period covered**: Cover letter dated June 20, 2023. O&M Plan dated December 1, 2022. Agreement effective date upon signature by DTSC's authorized representative after Caltrans signed (Caltrans signed May 31, 2023). Five-year review cycle begins from January 18, 2023 (approval date of Remedial Action Completion Report).

## Key claims
- DTSC asserts that cleanup of the Site has been achieved and the property remains safe for the community, referencing the need for a Land Use Covenant (cover letter).
- The O&M Plan asserts that the installed clean cap/containment system over Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zones is effective in preventing direct exposure to contaminated soil (Section 4.3).
- The remedy (consolidation and clean fill capping as highway embankment fill for SR 132 West Expressway) is stated to be DTSC-approved and installed at the Site (Section 2.0 of Agreement).
- BCS material contains elevated barium concentrations (up to 72,000 mg/kg) exceeding regulatory screening levels, and to a lesser extent elevated lead concentrations; aerially deposited lead (ADL) impacted soil with lead exceeding California hazardous waste thresholds was also placed within Stockpile 1 (Section 1.3 of O&M Plan).

## Regulatory decisions
- **Decision type**: Final Operation and Maintenance Agreement (enforceable under Health and Safety Code Sections 25355.5(a)(1)(C) and/or 25187) approving a DTSC-approved remedy of consolidation and clean fill capping of BCS as highway embankment fill.
- **Agency**: California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), with Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) as co-regulator.
- **Date**: Cover letter June 20, 2023 (agreement transmitted); O&M Plan approved December 1, 2022; Remedial Action Completion Report approved January 18, 2023 (start of five-year review cycle).
- **Conditions imposed**: Caltrans must: implement the DTSC-approved O&M Plan including inspections, monitoring, reporting, and record keeping (Section 4.0); leave installed remedy in place until DTSC authorizes modification or discontinuation (Section 3.0); perform five-year reviews (Section 7.0); provide advance notice (minimum 10 working days) of any BCS disturbance activities (Section 2.1 of O&M Plan); submit annual reports by January 31 for prior calendar year (Section 4.3 of O&M Plan); record Land Use Covenant (LUC) on remediated area (cover letter); provide site access to DTSC (Section 12.0).

## Analytical results
- **Contaminants**: Barium (primary contaminant of concern), lead (secondary, including aerially deposited lead).
- **Media**: Soil (Barium-Containing Soil stockpiles).
- **Concentration ranges**: Barium up to 72,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) in BCS material, exceeding regulatory screening levels (Section 1.3 of O&M Plan). Lead concentrations in ADL-impacted soil exceeded California hazardous waste thresholds.
- **Applicable thresholds**: California hazardous waste thresholds for lead; California Human Health Screening Levels for barium; Title 22 metals analysis; Waste Extraction Test (WET) and Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP) for hazardous waste determination; DTSC's 2001 Information Advisory Clean Imported Fill Material for clean fill evaluation (Section 2.5).
- **Exceedances noted**: Barium concentrations exceed regulatory screening levels; lead in ADL soil exceeds California hazardous waste thresholds.

## Remedial actions
- **Activity**: Consolidation, clean fill capping, and use of BCS as highway embankment fill for SR 132 West Expressway during Interim Phase 1 construction.
- **Quantities**: Total estimated volume of BCS stockpile material originally ~160,000 cubic yards (average maximum thickness ~20 feet). Stockpile 3 entirely removed from east side of SR 99 and placed within Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. Stockpile-East area: 5.83 acres; Stockpile-West area: 3.45 acres (Exhibit B/C).
- **Phase status**: Completed remedial actions between 2020 and 2022 (partial removal of Stockpile 3 in 2012 for offramp widening; consolidation and containment of Stockpiles 1–3 during Interim Phase 1 construction). SR 132 opened to traffic November 3, 2022. Ultimate Phase 2 (widening to four lanes) is planned but not yet constructed.
- **Verification outcomes**: As-built plans (Figures 3-1, 3-2), typical cross sections (Figures 4-1 through 4-4), profiles (Figures 5-1, 5-2), legal description, boundary survey, and photographs of completed highway improvements are included in O&M Plan. Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) dated 2022 summarizes completed remedial actions.

## Key terms and conditions
- **Inspection frequency**: Minimum annual inspections during third quarter (July–September) prior to rainy season; supplemental inspections after significant events (fires, ≥1 inch rainfall in 24 hours, seismic activity, traffic accidents, homeless encampment cleanups) (Section 3.1 of O&M Plan).
- **Maintenance deadline**: Required maintenance completed within 30 days of site inspection unless extension approved by DTSC (Section 3.2 of O&M Plan).
- **Reporting deadlines**: Annual O&M Inspection Summary Report submitted to DTSC by January 31 for prior calendar year (Section 4.3). BCS disturbance documentation within 30 days of completion (Section 4.2).
- **Five-year review**: Every five years from January 18, 2023 (RACR approval date) (Section 7.0 of Agreement; Section 4.4 of O&M Plan).
- **Notification requirements**: Minimum 10 working days advance notice to DTSC for any planned BCS disturbance; no BCS disturbance without prior written DTSC authorization (Section 2.1 of O&M Plan). Seven days advance notice for all field activities (Section 15.0 of Agreement). Immediate notification of conditions posing immediate threat to public health/safety/environment (Section 11.1).
- **Modification/discontinuation**: Requires written request to DTSC at least 60 days prior, with justification meeting one of four specified criteria (Section 5.0 of Agreement).
- **Cover fill requirements**: Minimum 12-inch-thick DTSC-approved clean fill material, or alternative 4-inch-thick aggregate base beneath pavement or hardscape, over BCS (Section 2.5; cross-section legends). Clean fill sources evaluated per DTSC's 2001 Clean Imported Fill Material advisory.
- **Temporary stockpiling**: Excavated BCS stored <30 days must be placed on and covered with minimum 6-millimeter-thick plastic sheeting with erosion control; stockpile cover secured with ropes and weights (Section 2.4 of O&M Plan; California Health and Safety Code 25123.3(b)(4)(A)).
- **Offsite disposal**: Requires characterization sampling per landfill requirements, analysis for Title 22 metals (EPA 6010B, 7471A for mercury), and potentially WET/TCLP for hazardous waste determination (Section 2.5).
- **Financial assurance**: Not required because Caltrans is a state government entity (Section 9.0 of Agreement).
- **Cost recovery**: Caltrans liable for all DTSC costs incurred in implementing Agreement, invoiced quarterly (Section 10.0).
- **Record retention**: Minimum six years after Agreement termination (Section 14.0).
- **Land Use Covenant**: Next project task is recording LUC on remediated area to restrict use (cover letter).
