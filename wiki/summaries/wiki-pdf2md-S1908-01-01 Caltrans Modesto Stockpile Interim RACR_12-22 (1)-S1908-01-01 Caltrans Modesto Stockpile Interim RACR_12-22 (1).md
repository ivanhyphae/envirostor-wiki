The Caltrans Modesto Stockpile project (S1908-01-01), detailed in this Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans, DTSC, and CVRWQCB, addresses barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially deposited lead (ADL) contamination from former chemical facilities along the SR 132 West Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County. Remediation efforts focused on three contaminated soil stockpiles (Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3), where barium concentrations initially reached up to 7,000 mg/kg, exceeding the 1,000 mg/kg verification threshold.

Remediation involved re-excavation at the west and east ends of Stockpile 2 and at Stockpile 3. Post-remediation verification samples confirmed that all barium levels, ranging from 46 to 970 mg/kg, were below the threshold, and lead levels were below 80 mg/kg. Clean fill for capping the consolidated BCS stockpiles (Stockpiles 1 and 2) was sourced from historically agricultural land, with analysis confirming barium levels between 60-110 mg/kg and lead within background, making it suitable for clean cover. Additional characterization during Mechanical Stabilized Earth (MSE) wall footing excavation on Stockpile 2's southerly slope revealed barium concentrations between 53 and 930 mg/kg, with some exceeding site-specific background but all lead levels acceptable. Soil from the upper three feet of this excavation requires placement within the designated Stockpiles 1 and 2 fill containment zones.

Throughout the project, extensive air monitoring during activities like "Stockpile 2 Mowing and Grading" and "Stockpile 3 BCS Transport to Stockpile 1" consistently showed total dust, barium, and lead levels well below regulatory limits, indicating effective dust control and safe remedial operations. The project will conclude with a final RACR after the Ultimate Phase 2 construction, anticipated in 2028. An Operations and Maintenance (O&M) Plan prepared in December 2022 outlines long-term management strategies for the consolidated BCS stockpiles, now capped with clean fill and incorporated into the highway embankment. This plan details procedures for soil disturbance notification, health and safety, dust control, material handling, stormwater management, inspections, and reporting, ensuring the ongoing integrity of the containment zones as outlined in foundational documents like the 2018 Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP).
