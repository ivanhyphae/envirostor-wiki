Here is the structured summary of the provided document, extracted and organized for the case-file wiki.

## Document identity
- **Document type and purpose:** Stockpile 3 Excavation Monitoring Plan (the Plan). Prepared to support planned highway safety improvement construction excavations adjacent to the SR99 Kansas Avenue Off-ramp in Modesto, CA. It summarizes site investigation results, human health risk, and describes procedures for air monitoring and verification of excavations within Stockpile 3 during construction.
- **Author or preparer:** Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Prepared by John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG, Project Manager; and Douglas S. Krause, CIH, Certified Industrial Hygienist).
- **Date or period covered:** June 2012 (Plan date). The document covers site investigation data from April 16, 2012, and references prior investigations from 2003–2009.

## Key claims
- The highest barium level detected in Stockpile 3 (44,300 mg/kg in sample B17-4.5) does not exceed the commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) for barium of 63,000 mg/kg (Shaw 2004 PSI data).
- With the exception of arsenic, none of the metals reported in the 2012 Geocon investigation were at or above respective commercial/industrial CHHSLs. Arsenic was within the range of site-specific background levels.
- None of the soil samples contained total metal concentrations at or higher than the California hazardous waste Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC).
- Calculated 95% UCLs for barium and lead are significantly less than ten times the respective Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) values, indicating that excavated soil materials would not require management or disposal as a hazardous waste.
- Based on the 2007 Shaw Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA), the cumulative excess lifetime cancer risks for future construction workers and associated offsite residents (9.2E-7 and 6E-10, respectively) are below the generally recognized 1E-06 cancer risk criterion. Noncancer hazard quotients did not exceed the threshold of 1.0.
- The DTSC stated in a December 17, 2009 letter that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents.

## Regulatory decisions
- **Decision type:** DTSC Response to Final PEA (Letter dated December 17, 2009).
- **Agency:** California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).
- **Conditions imposed:** The DTSC directed Caltrans to continue managing the stockpiles until the SR99/Highway 132 Interchange is constructed and to maintain the existing groundwater monitoring system.
- **Regulatory Oversight:** The Plan notes that the excavated Stockpile 3 soil materials should be suitable for offsite disposal as non-hazardous soil following disclosure and review of site characterization data, in consultation with DTSC and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) representatives.

## Analytical results
- **Contaminants:** Barium, lead, arsenic, other Title 22 metals (including aluminum, iron, manganese, strontium, mercury), nitrate, sulfate, sulfide.
- **Media:** Soil (stockpile fill and native soil), groundwater (monitored but not tested in this plan).
- **Concentration ranges (Barium):** 34 to 1,600 mg/kg (Geocon 2012 data); 31 to 44,300 mg/kg (Shaw 2004 PSI data). The highest detected barium (1,600 mg/kg in sample DP1-14.5) was at or higher than ten times the STLC for barium (1,000 mg/kg).
- **Concentration ranges (Lead):** Max reported 190 mg/kg (sample HA9-0). Five samples contained total lead at or higher than ten times the STLC for lead (50 mg/kg).
- **Concentration ranges (Arsenic):** Less than 1.0 mg/kg to 3.2 mg/kg (within site-specific background).
- **Applicable thresholds referenced:**
    - Commercial/Industrial CHHSL for barium: 63,000 mg/kg.
    - Residential CHHSL for barium: 5,200 mg/kg.
    - California TTLC (hazardous waste threshold).
    - California STLC (soluble threshold); ten times STLC = 1,000 mg/kg for barium, 50 mg/kg for lead.
    - Cal/OSHA PEL-TWA for barium: 10 mg/m³ (T8 CCR §5155).
    - Cal/OSHA PEL-TWA for inorganic lead: 0.05 mg/m³ (T8 CCR §1532.1).
    - ARB-OEHHA 30-day average ambient lead standard: 1.5 µg/m³ (0.0015 mg/m³).
- **Exceedances noted:** One sample (DP1-14.5) exceeded ten times the STLC for barium. Five samples exceeded ten times the STLC for lead. No exceedances of CHHSLs or TTLCs were noted.

## Remedial actions
- **Activity:** Excavation of approximately 2,800 cubic yards of Stockpile 3 soil embankment for off-ramp widening and retaining wall construction.
- **Phase status:** Planned (pre-construction). The Plan describes monitoring procedures to be performed during construction.
- **Verification outcomes:** Excavation will be overseen by a Geocon California Professional Geologist (PG) or field geologist/engineer to confirm when excavation limits within Stockpile 3 fill materials have been completed (i.e., native soil conditions reached).
- **Disposal:** Excavated fill materials will be loaded into trucks for disposal as non-hazardous waste to the Fink Road Sanitary Landfill in Crows Landing, Stanislaus County, California.

## Responsible parties
- **California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) – District 6:** Site owner/operator, responsible for managing the stockpiles and maintaining the groundwater monitoring system. Key contact: Richard Stewart, PG.
- **Geocon Consultants, Inc.:** Consultant preparing the Plan, performing site investigation, excavation monitoring, and air monitoring. Key contact: John Juhrend, PE, CEG.
- **Teichert Construction:** Highway contractor responsible for excavation and grading work. Key contact: Raul Ortiz.
- **California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC):** Co-regulator.
- **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB):** Co-regulator.
- **Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC):** Former operator of the chemical processing facility where the stockpile materials originated.

## Key terms and conditions
- A scoping meeting will be held prior to construction with Caltrans, Geocon, and Teichert Construction representatives to discuss excavation activities, dust mitigation, health and safety, and project scheduling.
- **Air Monitoring:** Ambient perimeter air monitoring will be conducted during excavation and loading activities using MIE pDR-1200 real-time particulate counters with PM-10 inlets. A downwind "Total Dust Trigger" concentration of 4 mg/m³ above upwind background dust levels will be used for real-time monitoring.
- **Reporting:** A draft Excavation Monitoring Report (Completion Report) will be submitted to Caltrans approximately 30 days following completion of the monitoring activities.
- **Follow-on actions:** The Plan is valid only as of its date (June 2012) and would require an update to reflect additional site activities.
