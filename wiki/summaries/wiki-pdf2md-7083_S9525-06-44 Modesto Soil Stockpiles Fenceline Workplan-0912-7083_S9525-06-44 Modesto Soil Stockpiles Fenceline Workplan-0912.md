The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remediation Project (S9525-06-44) addresses approximately 121,000 cubic yards of fill material at the intersection of State Route 99 and Kansas Avenue in Stanislaus County, some of which contains elevated levels of heavy metals (barium and cadmium) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. in September 2012, this workplan outlines a supplemental site investigation and comprehensive health and safety protocols for the project, which involves stakeholders including Caltrans, DTSC, RWQCB, Shaw Environmental, Inc., and Advanced Technologies Laboratories (ATL).

The investigation, prompted by DTSC and RWQCB, aims to further characterize contaminants in the stockpiles and native soil beneath the stockpiles to re-evaluate human health risks to adjacent residents and vertical contaminant migration. This data is critical for incorporating the stockpiled material into roadway fill embankments for the SR132 Project in 2015. The investigation will involve extensive soil sampling: twenty-nine 5-foot deep fenceline borings, twenty-two 3-foot deep perimeter borings, and five 20-foot deep borings within Stockpiles 2 and 3. Samples will be analyzed for Title 22 metals, strontium, mercury, barium, and cadmium, building upon previous analyses from 2004 that assessed metals against California and federal hazardous waste thresholds (TTLC, TCLP, WET) and EPA Region 9 Preliminary Remediation Goals (PRGs). The ultimate goal is to generate analytical data for a DTSC and RWQCB-approved Remedial Action Plan (RAP).

The workplan prioritizes personnel and site safety during all field activities, specifically addressing the contaminated soil stockpiles and associated groundwater monitoring. It details administrative requirements, including defined roles for the Project Manager (John Juhrend), Site Safety Officer (Bob Kimball), and Consulting Certified Industrial Hygienist (Doug Krause), along with mandatory training and daily "Tailgate" safety meetings. A thorough hazard analysis identifies potential risks such as mechanical, electrical, noise, and biological hazards. Chemical hazards are a key focus, specifically T22 (CAM17) metals (arsenic, cadmium, inorganic lead) and barium sulfate, for which specific exposure limits are listed.

Proposed safety measures include stringent personal hygiene protocols, a mandatory "buddy system," and work zone controls. Underground Service Alert (USA) and private utility locators will be contacted prior to excavation. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) requirements specify Level D protection as standard, with the option to upgrade to Level C (including full or half-mask air-purifying respirators with P100 filter cartridges for dry, dusty materials potentially containing lead), and detailed equipment decontamination procedures. The plan also outlines emergency response procedures, including actions for catastrophic events and contact information for services. Though a "working document" subject to modification, it requires approval by a Certified Industrial Hygienist and expires six months from approval unless updated. Deliverables include the Health and Safety Plan, training rosters, air monitoring records, site personnel logs, and medical approvals, all of which will be reviewed by project personnel, Caltrans field inspectors, and subcontractors.
