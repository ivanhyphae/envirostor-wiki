Geocon Consultants, Inc. conducted a series of groundwater monitoring events for the Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles site (Project No. S9525-06-44A), with a primary focus on the January 2013 sampling period (January 22-31, 2013). Groundwater samples were collected from ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) and an equipment blank, with historical data from June 2006 also considered. Groundwater depth ranged from 31.04 to 40.02 feet, with a southeast flow direction and an average gradient of 0.001.

Analysis of groundwater samples, performed by Advanced Technology Laboratories, included dissolved metals, anions, alkalinity, total dissolved solids (TDS), and total sulfide. Most dissolved metals and general minerals were below water quality thresholds, but exceedances were consistently noted in 2012 and January 2013. Nitrates (as nitrogen) exceeded the 10 mg/L CDPH Primary MCL in wells MW-1, MW-5, and MW-6 (ranging from 6.9-30 mg/L). TDS surpassed the 500 mg/L CDPH Secondary MCL in MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 (ranging from 253-730 mg/L), and manganese in MW-6 also exceeded its threshold. Other analytes like barium, strontium, arsenic, and chromium were detected, generally at levels consistent with historical data and below their respective MCLs if applicable; lead was consistently undetected. Ionic content showed slight variation since March 2012, primarily being calcium- or sodium-dominant. PAH analysis was discontinued due to consistent non-detection.

Quality control data highlighted several issues, including relative percent difference (RPD) values outside acceptance criteria for Thallium and Zinc, matrix spike (MS) and matrix spike duplicate (MSD) recoveries outside limits for Chloride, Nitrate as N, Sulfate, Calcium, Magnesium, Sodium, and Total Sulfide (often due to matrix interference), and holding time exceedances. Despite these quality control flags, Geocon followed DTSC-approved protocols, including field filtering of dissolved metal samples, and prepared the report for the California Department of Transportation – District 6 Hazardous Waste Branch.
