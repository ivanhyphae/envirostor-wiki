This document is the **Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP)** for the **Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles** (S1200-01-01), dated March 14, 2018, and prepared by **Geocon Consultants, Inc.** It addresses three large soil stockpiles (totaling ~160,000 cubic yards) within the State Route 132 right-of-way in Stanislaus County. The soil was imported in the 1960s from a former **FMC chemical processing facility**, resulting in **barium** as the primary contaminant of concern. Human Health Risk Assessments (HHRA, 2007–2013), concurred with by the lead oversight agency **Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)**, concluded that under current management (fencing, vegetative cover, access control), the stockpiles pose **no unacceptable risk** to offsite residents, trespassers, or future construction workers. However, the soil is classified as hazardous waste based on barium concentrations.

The RAP fulfills an Interagency Agreement between **Caltrans** (owner/operator), **DTSC** (lead oversight), and the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)** (co-regulator). After evaluating four alternatives—No Action (rejected), Institutional Controls alone (acceptable as interim only), full Removal (costing ~$21.5 million but rejected due to lack of funding and high environmental impacts), and Containment—the selected remedy is **Alternative 4 – Containment**. This alternative encapsulates the contaminated soil as highway embankment fill during the planned **SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project**, eliminating direct exposure pathways while being significantly more cost-effective than removal. If the SR-132 project is not constructed, a stand-alone 1-foot vegetated clean soil cap would be implemented.

Key obligations include recording a **Land Use Covenant** to prohibit sensitive uses, preserving the vegetative cover, continuing annual groundwater monitoring from 10 wells, and conducting stormwater monitoring. DTSC may conduct five-year reviews until project completion (Ultimate Build-Out ~2028), at which point site closure is anticipated. Caltrans is responsible for implementation and funding.
