## Document identity
- **Deliverable type**: Errata to a Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) – parent document: *Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1* (January 30, 2019)
- **Version / status**: Post Construction Contract Award; final (not a draft); administrative update to the approved RDIP
- **Preparer / author organization**: Caltrans (State of California); the original RDIP was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., but the errata removes Geocon as consultant; Caltrans is now responsible for all work
- **Date**: Not explicitly stated in the extract – appears to be mid‑2019 (after the April 3, 2019 DTSC approval of the parent RDIP)
- **Project number or task order**: Not provided

## Purpose and regulatory context
- **Milestone / requirement**: Updates the approved RDIP to reflect changes in roles and responsibilities following award of the construction contract for Interim Phase 1 of the SR‑132 West Project. Specifically, it strikes Geocon as the consultant and clarifies that Caltrans is the responsible party for implementation.
- **Oversight framework**: Submitted to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) with concurrence from the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). The parent RDIP implements the DTSC-approved Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles.
- **For an errata**: Parent document: *Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1* (Geocon, January 30, 2019), conditionally approved by DTSC on August 20, 2018 and October 12, 2018, and finally approved April 3, 2019. Corrections: updates Sections 2.0 (Project Organization) and 2.1 (Project Team Responsibilities) to name the construction contractor (Bay Cities Paving and Grading, Inc.) and to identify Caltrans as the entity responsible for supervision, verification sampling, air monitoring, and reporting – replacing Geocon.

## Site and contamination summary
- **Contaminants of concern**: Barium (primary); lead (secondary). Both are present at elevated concentrations in the soil stockpiles.
- **Affected media**: Soil (stockpiles) – also mentions groundwater and stormwater monitoring (ongoing).
- **Sources and areas of concern**: Three soil stockpiles (Stockpile 1, Stockpile 2, Stockpile 3) within the SR‑132 right‑of‑way in Stanislaus County, CA. The remedy addresses barium‑contaminated fill originally placed by Caltrans.
- **Other constituents**: The Health and Safety Plan (Appendix F) notes that most COPCs are below residential screening levels; barium and lead require special precautions.

## Findings and risk characterization
- **Key analytical findings**: Not a new risk assessment. The errata retains technical specifications from the RDIP: verification soil samples are compared to thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg for barium and 80 mg/kg for lead (EPA Method 6010B). Excavation is considered complete when results (total or 95% UCL) are below those thresholds.
- **Human health / ecological risk**: No HHRA or SSI was included in this errata. The parent RDIP relied on earlier risk evaluations; the errata notes that barium and lead are present at concentrations that are “much less than commercial/industrial or construction worker screening levels” for most COPCs, but elevated barium and lead require dust control and air monitoring.
- **Conclusions about site conditions (attributed to Caltrans)**: Barium‑contaminated soil remains in Stockpiles 1 and 2 after consolidation; complete removal occurs at the ends of Stockpile 2 and at Stockpile 3. The containment alternative is being implemented as designed.

## Selected or recommended remedy
- **Remedial alternatives evaluated**: Not discussed – the errata does not re‑evaluate alternatives; it implements the “containment alternative” already approved in the RAP.
- **Selected / recommended approach**: Containment via on‑site consolidation, capping, and engineering controls. Specific elements:
  - Excavation of barium‑containing soil from the ends of Stockpile 2 and entire Stockpile 3 (areas to be removed);
  - Consolidation of remaining barium‑containing soil within Stockpiles 1 and 2 into designated containment zones;
  - Cap over containment zones: minimum 12‑inch‑thick clean imported fill (or 4‑inch AB material where pavement/hardscape is placed);
  - Land use covenant (LUC) / deed restriction for the containment zones only (not for removed areas);
  - Clean fill source (vacant/agricultural land along SR‑132 ROW) to be sampled per DTSC’s Clean Imported Fill Material Advisory;
  - Verification soil sampling (1 per 5,000 ft² removal areas, 1 per 100 ft of activity area) for barium and lead (EPA 6010B);
  - Air monitoring (fugitive dust) and stormwater BMPs per a SWPPP;
  - Groundwater monitoring continues on an annual basis (per Shaw, 2006 protocol).
- **Performance/cleanup standards**: Barium <1,000 mg/kg; lead <80 mg/kg for verification samples. No visible dust; dust control via water spray; stop‑work authority during high winds or uncontrolled runoff.

## Conclusions and path forward
- **Closure status**: Not closure – the errata acknowledges ongoing construction under Interim Phase 1.
- **Conditions / monitoring obligations**:
  - Caltrans to prepare a **Remedial Action Completion Report**.
  - Groundwater monitoring continues annually.
  - Stormwater monitoring (observations and sampling) during construction.
  - Caltrans to record a LUC with Stanislaus County for the barium‑containing soil containment zones (Stockpiles 1 and 2).
  - Monthly status reports to DTSC and CVRWQCB.
- **Land use restrictions**: LUC is required only for the containment zones (not for areas where soil was completely removed).
- **Required follow‑on deliverables**: Remedial Action Completion Report; final survey data (latitude, longitude, elevations) for LUC; clean fill source pre‑approval from DTSC.
