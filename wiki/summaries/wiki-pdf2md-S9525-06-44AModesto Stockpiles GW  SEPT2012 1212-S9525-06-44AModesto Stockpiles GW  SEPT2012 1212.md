Geocon Consultants, Inc. compiled a Groundwater Monitoring Report (Project No. S9525-06-44A, Task Order No. 44, EA 10-403500, Contract No. 06A1580) summarizing groundwater sampling activities conducted on September 19 and 20, 2012, with additional data from March, May, July, June, and October 2006 monitoring rounds. Groundwater samples were collected from ten wells (MW-1 through MW-10) and analyzed for Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs), dissolved Title 22 Metals, and General Minerals. PAHs were consistently not detected above practical quantitation limits (PQLs) in any samples.

A more detailed analysis by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) for project S9525-06-44 in September 2012 focused on wells MW-5, MW-6, MW-7, MW-8, MW-9, MW-10, and an equipment blank, confirming that SVOCs (including various PAHs) and dissolved Mercury were consistently "Not Detected" (ND). However, numerous dissolved metals and general minerals were detected. Specific wells exhibited elevated concentrations; for instance, MW-5 had high levels of Chloride (25 mg/L), Nitrate as N (22 mg/L), Sulfate (26 mg/L), Total Alkalinity (390 mg/L), and Total Dissolved Solids (590 mg/L), along with various dissolved metals. MW-10 showed notable levels of Chloride (33 mg/L), Nitrate as N (14 mg/L), Sulfate (99 mg/L), and specific dissolved metals like Manganese (16 µg/L) and Selenium (4.4 µg/L).

The report also included tables of groundwater monitoring data from an expanded set of 18 wells, including MW-1 through MW-17, a Caltrans Well, and an Agriform Well. This data provided concentration ranges for various analytes, including Electrical Conductivity (220-4100 umhos/cm), pH (6.88-8.20 pH units), Temperature (18.0-22.0 Degrees C), Turbidity (<0.02-31 NTU), Dissolved Oxygen (0.1-5.6 mg/L), and Bromide (<0.50-13 mg/L). Chloride levels ranged from 8.8-700 mg/L, Sulfate from 1.1-660 mg/L, Total Alkalinity from 76-590 mg/L CaCO3, and Total Dissolved Solids from 230-2200 mg/L. Dissolved metals, such as Barium (<0.0050-0.086 mg/L), Calcium (10-140 mg/L), Chromium (<0.0010-0.0019 mg/L), Iron (<0.0050-0.29 mg/L), Manganese (<0.0010-0.015 mg/L), Nickel (<0.0010-0.0044 mg/L), Potassium (1.1-23 mg/L), Sodium (14-360 mg/L), and Vanadium (<0.0010-0.0069 mg/L) were also measured.

Notably, several analytes in the September 2012 sampling event, and previously in May 2012, exceeded applicable Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) or Secondary MCLs set by the California Environmental Protection Agency (May 2009). Nitrate (as N) exceeded the 10 mg/L MCL in MW-1 (12 mg/L) and MW-5 (27 mg/L). Boron met the 2,000 µg/L MCL in MW-6 (May 2012). Iron exceeded its 300 µg/L Secondary MCL in MW-1 (1,900 µg/L) and MW-5 (430 µg/L), and Manganese exceeded its 50 µg/L Secondary MCL in MW-1 (540 µg/L). Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) also exceeded the 500 mg/L Secondary MCL at multiple locations in the May 2012 sampling, with MW-6 having the highest concentration at 2,500 mg/L. Field parameters for the September 2012 sampling indicated varying turbidity levels, with some wells showing high initial turbidity, and Dissolved Oxygen (DO) levels ranging from 5.23 to 7.34 mg/L. QA/QC notes highlighted instances of matrix interference and the need for sample dilutions, which could affect the accuracy of some results.
