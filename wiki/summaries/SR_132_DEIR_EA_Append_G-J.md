### Summary of `SR_132_DEIR_EA_Append_G-J.md`

This document compiles Appendices G–J of the Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (DEIR/EA) for the SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Stanislaus County, California. The core environmental issue addressed is the remediation of barium-contaminated soil stockpiles at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. The appendices include a Final Feasibility Study (FS, June 2014) and a Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP, October 2014), with investigations spanning from 2003 through 2014 and cost estimates in 2013 dollars. The document also records regulatory concurrences from multiple agencies.

**Key Human Health and Environmental Findings**

Two Human Health Risk Assessments (2007 baseline, 2013 update) concluded that barium concentrations in the stockpiles, under continued management (fencing, vegetative cover, groundwater monitoring), do not pose unacceptable cancer risks or non-cancer hazards to current offsite residents, trespassers, future construction workers, or hypothetical shallow groundwater users (cancer risk <1E-6, HI <1). DTSC formally concurred with the 2013 update (April 2013), conditioned on proper stockpile management. However, groundwater monitoring results for constituents of potential concern (COPCs) were deemed inconclusive regarding definitive migration from the stockpiles, and CVRWQCB expressed ongoing concern about potential groundwater quality degradation. Soil barium concentrations showed variability between 2006 and 2012 sampling events (e.g., surface soil maximums of 4,300 mg/kg in Stockpile 2 vs. 1,100 mg/kg in 2006) but none exceeded residential California Human Health Screening Levels. Groundwater metals showed minor increases but remained below applicable thresholds.

**Remedial Alternatives Evaluated**

The Feasibility Study evaluated four alternatives:

1. **No Action (Alternative 1):** Least protective and least acceptable to regulators and community.
2. **Institutional Controls (Alternative 2):** Ongoing fencing, vegetative cover, and monitoring. Accepted by DTSC and CVRWQCB only as a short-term measure while the SR-132 project proceeds; community prefers a more proactive remedy.
3. **Removal and Offsite Disposal (Alternative 3):** Excavation and transport to Class I/II landfills (~175 truckloads/day for 60 days), generating 529,200 lb CO₂. Cost ~$21.5 million with no identified funding source; least implementable.
4. **Containment (Alternative 4):** Uses stockpile soil as fill within the SR-132 project, with two phases: interim progress (by 2018) covering southern portions of Stockpiles 1 & 2 under a two-lane roadway, with northern portions capped with clean soil; ultimate build-out (by 2028) complete containment behind retaining walls beneath pavement, with median covered by pavement or synthetic liner plus clean soil. This alternative is cost-effective, protective, and readily implementable. The FS recommends Alternative 4, though no formal remedy decision document is cited in these appendices.

**Regulatory Decisions and Responsible Parties**

- **DTSC** (2009 memo, 2013 memo/concurrence): Considers stockpiles under current management to pose no unacceptable hazard; accepts institutional controls as interim but anticipates long-term acceptance of containment.
- **CVRWQCB:** Supports stockpile maintenance to protect groundwater; accepts Alternative 2 only short-term.
- **SJVAPCD:** Removal alternative would require a dust control plan and compliance with Indirect Source Review Rule 9510.
- **Caltrans:** Owner/operator responsible for property maintenance and implementing the selected remedy.
- **DTSC and CVRWQCB:** Co-regulatory agencies overseeing remedy selection.

**Additional Environmental Clearances and Concurrences**

- **SHPO (2015):** Concurred that two properties—3530 Maze Boulevard and 416/418 I Street (Dania Hall)—are eligible for listing on the National Register of Historic Places at the local level.
- **EPA and FHWA (2016):** Concurred that the project is not a Project of Air Quality Concern and will not cause new violations of federal PM2.5 and PM10 standards.
- **USACE (2015):** Approved a jurisdictional determination finding approximately 0.09 acre of waters of the U.S. subject to Clean Water Act Section 404, and two isolated wetlands (0.17 and 0.48 acres) not subject to that section.
- **USFWS (2016):** Provided a species list identifying eight threatened or endangered species potentially present, with no critical habitat within the project footprint.

**Project Schedule and Horizon**

The project anticipates ultimate build-out by 2028, with a 30-year operations and maintenance horizon used for cost evaluation of remedial alternatives.

In summary, this document provides the regulatory framework, risk assessments, remedial alternative evaluations, and environmental clearances necessary to support the remediation of barium-contaminated soil stockpiles as part of the larger SR-132 project. It concludes that current management is protective while evaluating more active remedial options, with the Feasibility Study recommending containment within the project footprint.
