Geocon Consultants, Inc. conducted extensive groundwater monitoring at the Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles in November and December 2012 (Project No. S9525-06-44A, Task Order No. 44). Samples were collected from up to ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) and analyzed for a wide range of contaminants, including Title 22 dissolved metals, general minerals, Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs), Anions, Alkalinity, Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), and Total Sulfide.

Groundwater flow was consistently southeastern, with depths to water measurements varying from 29.30 to 41.18 feet below the top of the casing. Some wells, notably MW-5 and MW-6, experienced low recovery during purging. Key exceedances of California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) were observed, with nitrates (as nitrogen) exceeding the primary MCL of 10 mg/L in MW-1, MW-5, and MW-6 (up to 24 mg/L in MW-1). TDS also exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/L in MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 (up to 730 mg/L).

While PAHs were not detected above Practical Quantitation Limits (PQLs) in any sample, leading to the discontinuation of their analysis, dissolved metals showed varying concentrations. For example, arsenic ranged from ND to 11.5972 µg/L, barium from ND to 410 µg/L, and lead from ND to 160 µg/L. Several quality control issues were noted for dissolved metals analysis, including matrix spike failures and high relative percent differences (RPDs) in duplicates for various metals, indicating potential matrix interference, though analytical batches were generally validated by laboratory control samples. Purged groundwater is slated for disposal at the Inviro-tec Disposal facility.
