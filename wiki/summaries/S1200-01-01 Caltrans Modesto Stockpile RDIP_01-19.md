Based on the two summaries you provided, here is a combined coherent summary of the source document.

The document is a **Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) – Interim Phase 1** for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, prepared by **Geocon Consultants, Inc.** in January 2019 under DTSC and CVRWQCB oversight. A key component of the document is a **Health and Safety Plan (HASP)** with an original reference date of September 2012. The RDIP implements the approved **Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP)** for the SR-132/SR-99 interchange site, focusing on **Interim Phase 1** of the SR-132 West Project (construction scheduled 2019–2020).

The primary contaminants of concern are **barium** (from historic FMC operations) and **lead** (including **aerially deposited lead (ADL)** ), along with other metals like **inorganic arsenic, cadmium, and chromium VI** in Stockpile 3. The selected remedy is a **containment strategy** consolidating contaminated soil (approx. 160,000 yd³) for use as highway embankment fill, capped with pavement or a 12-inch soil cover. Post-excavation verification thresholds are **1,000 mg/kg for barium** and **80 mg/kg for lead**, with a **Land Use Covenant (LUC)** required for consolidation zones.

The HASP establishes worker safety protocols, noting a validated **Negative Exposure Assessment** for lead (airborne exposures found well below the 30 µg/m³ Action Level and 50 µg/m³ PEL). Baseline protection is **Level D**, with **Level C** (APR with P100 filters) for dry/dusty materials. Medical surveillance is mandated per OSHA/Cal-OSHA standards for cadmium (PEL 5 µg/m³) and lead (BLLs below 40 µg/dL, 30 µg/dL for workers planning children). The document also outlines post-construction obligations, including annual inspections, five-year reviews, groundwater monitoring, and a final **Remedial Action Completion Report (RACR)** .
