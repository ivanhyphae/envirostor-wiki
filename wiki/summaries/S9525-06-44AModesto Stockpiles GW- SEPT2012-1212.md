### Document Summary

This document is a Groundwater Monitoring Report for the September 2012 sampling event at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA), prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of Caltrans District 6. The report, dated December 19, 2012, details the results of a biennial monitoring event conducted on September 19-20, 2012, in accordance with the 2006 *Final Work Plan, Groundwater Assessment* (Shaw, 2006). Geocon’s Josh Ewert performed the field sampling, with oversight from project manager Rebecca Silva.

The document consists of a Chain of Custody (COC) record and a report appendix. The COC, prepared by sampler Ewert, documents the collection and transport of groundwater samples on September 20-21, 2012, verified as meeting all condition criteria (chilled, proper headspace, intact/sealed containers, matching counts, preservation). The report appendix provides Stiff diagrams and sample details, identifying two distinct groundwater types across monitoring wells MW-1 through MW-10: "Calcium Dominant" (MW-1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10) and "Sodium Dominant" (MW-3, 6, 8), with bicarbonate as the dominant anion in all wells. The report specifies that all samples must be field-filtered for dissolved CAM 17 metals plus strontium, and that anion/cation balance calculations are required.

Groundwater samples were collected from ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) and analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories for a comprehensive suite of analytes including Title 22 metals (dissolved and total), dissolved mercury, general minerals (alkalinity, TDS, anions, cations), and semivolatile organic compounds (PAHs via EPA 8270/SIM). The analytical results were compared to California Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) and Secondary MCLs. Data from earlier sampling events (pre-March 12, 2012, and from the 2007 *Site Investigation Report* by Shaw Environmental) are also reproduced in the report’s tables.

The primary chemicals of concern (COCs) identified by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)—barium, lead, and strontium—were detected at concentrations similar to historical levels and well below their respective thresholds. Lead was not detected at or above the reporting limit. However, consistent exceedances of the primary MCL for nitrate (10 mg/L) were noted in five wells (MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10), with concentrations ranging up to 22 mg/L in MW-5. Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) exceeded the secondary MCL (500 mg/L) in three wells (MW-5, MW-6, MW-10), with a maximum of 630 mg/L in MW-10. Dissolved manganese also exceeded the secondary MCL at wells MW-3 (490 µg/L) and MW-9 (430 µg/L). The report notes that the upgradient well MW-1 exhibited elevated nitrate (12 mg/L), suggesting a potential natural background level. No PAHs were detected above practical quantitation limits. Laboratory quality control data was acceptable, though one mercury duplicate had an RPD outside criteria and a potential matrix interference was noted for one spike. Sampler observations for MW-3 noted high turbidity (601 ntu) and an insufficient volume of water for a full sample. No regulatory decisions or remedial actions are identified in this document.
