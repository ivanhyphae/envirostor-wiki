A groundwater monitoring event was conducted in April 2015 at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (GEOCON PROJECT NO. S9800-01-17, Caltrans Task Order No. 17), with a report prepared for the California Department of Transportation – District 6 Hazardous Waste Branch. This monitoring followed DTSC-approved protocols based on a 2006 work plan.

Out of ten monitoring wells (MW-1 to MW-10), only four (MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7) contained sufficient groundwater for sampling, a recurring issue since September 2014. Historically, groundwater was found around 30-35 feet below ground surface (bgs), generally flowing southeast. Samples were analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) for Title 22 dissolved metals and general minerals, with polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) analysis discontinued since November 2012 due to consistent non-detection. Field QA/QC indicated normal conditions, though one sample was received unsealed and unpreserved.

Key findings included:
*   **Nitrate (as nitrogen):** Exceeded the California primary MCL of 10 mg/L in MW-1 (17 mg/L) and met it in MW-2 (10 mg/L). Historically, MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 have consistently exceeded this.
*   **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS):** Exceeded the CDPH Secondary MCL of 500 mg/L in MW-1 (660 mg/L) and met it in MW-4 (500 mg/L). Historically, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 also consistently exceeded this threshold.
*   **Dissolved Metals:** Barium, strontium, chromium, vanadium, arsenic, manganese, molybdenum, and nickel concentrations were below their thresholds and similar to historical levels. Other dissolved metals were non-detect or below MCLs. Manganese was sporadically above secondary MCLs in past events.
*   **General Minerals:** Samples consistently showed "calcium-dominant" ionic content with only "very slight changes."

While no dissolved metal concentrations exceeded water quality thresholds in April 2015, nitrate and TDS continue to exceed primary and secondary MCLs, respectively, in some wells. Annual groundwater monitoring will continue, with the next event in April 2016, and semi-annual depth-to-water measurements in October 2015. The stockpiles originated from 1961 highway construction on land previously owned by the Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC), which used an evaporation pond for waste discharge from the 1950s to 1970s. Quality assurance issues included matrix spike recoveries outside limits for some analytes due to potential matrix interference. Geocon Consultants, Inc. requested an Anion/Cation Balance calculation and EDF preparation for GeoTracker from ATL.
