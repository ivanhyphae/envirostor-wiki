Appendix H of the Final Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) details a Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, which originated from soil imported from a former FMC facility (1929-1985) that discharged liquid waste into unlined ponds. Regulatory bodies, including the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), provided input that shaped the RAP, concluding that the stockpiles, *if properly managed*, do not pose an unacceptable risk to human health, based on Human Health Risk Assessments (HHRAs) from 2007 and 2013. These assessments identified elevated barium (primarily in low-solubility barite form) and some cadmium but found no significant migration or impact on groundwater.

The primary Remedial Action Objective (RAO) is to protect human health and prevent future groundwater impact. Four remediation alternatives were evaluated: No Action, Institutional Controls, Removal, and Containment. The "No Action" alternative was deemed unacceptable due to increased public risk perception despite its low cost. "Removal" of the stockpiles was rejected due to its high cost (estimated $21.5 million), short-term risks (dust, diesel emissions), and insufficient additional protection compared to other options. "Institutional Controls", involving ongoing monitoring, fencing, and restricted access, was short-term acceptable but did not provide a permanent solution. The DTSC rejected "Treatment" and "Reuse/Recycle/Reclaim" options due to implementability challenges.

The preferred alternative for the stockpiles is "Containment," which involves integrating the contaminated soil into the SR-132/SR-99 interchange project as embankment fill, securing it beneath roadways, retaining walls, bridge abutments, or a clean, vegetated cap. This option is rated "good" for overall protection, technical feasibility, and cost-effectiveness, with estimated costs ranging from $495,000 for Institutional Controls to $21.5 million for Removal. The Containment strategy aims to eliminate exposure pathways and reduce contaminant mobility by leveraging the SR-132 project infrastructure. Caltrans has assumed full responsibility for remediation costs.

The broader document, including various appendices, the "Statement of Reasons," "Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility (NBAR)," and "Agency Coordination," covers the period from June 1, 2004, to February 15, 2018, outlining environmental assessments, public engagement, and remedial strategies for the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. Numerous agencies and individuals, including Caltrans, OHP, StanCOG, EPA Region 9, FHWA, and FWS, were involved in the environmental assessments and interagency consultations. The project received "No Effect" determinations regarding endangered species and was deemed not a Project of Air Quality Concern.

Caltrans selected Alternative 2 for the SR 132 West project, which focuses on improvements to east-west travel, balancing environmental impacts, feasibility, right-of-way acquisition, cost, and project purpose, while alleviating future traffic congestion. Public comments highlighted significant concerns, particularly regarding potential health effects from the toxic stockpiles, traffic projections, and the rejection of alternative designs. Residents expressed grave health concerns, citing multiple illnesses, and distrusted the containment plan, fearing long-term health effects and reactivation of dormant chemicals. Property owners and businesses raised issues about acquisitions, compensation, and project impacts during construction. Critics also questioned the project's necessity, cost, and perceived casual attitude toward environmental impact, advocating for full removal of the stockpiles. Despite these public objections, Caltrans, with DTSC concurrence, maintains that the chosen approaches for both the SR 132 West project and the stockpile remediation, particularly the Containment alternative, effectively protect public health and the environment.
