The "Final Caltrans Modesto Stockpile RAP," prepared by GEOCON Consultants, Inc. in March 2018, addresses the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3), which contain approximately 160,000 cubic yards of barium-contaminated soil, along with minor levels of PAHs and previously identified cadmium (later deemed not a COPC). These stockpiles originated from fill material near a former chemical processing facility in the early 1960s. Although earlier Human Health Risk Assessments (2007 and 2013) concluded that the stockpiles do not pose unacceptable risks to offsite residents, trespassers, or construction workers under current and construction-phase land uses, and groundwater consistently meets drinking water standards, a remedial action was still deemed necessary under the oversight of the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

After evaluating four alternatives—"No Action," "Institutional Controls," "Removal," and "Containment"—the RAP selects **Containment/Encapsulation** as the preferred remedy. This alternative leverages the planned construction of the SR-132/SR-99 interchange. The contaminated soil will be incorporated into the highway embankment fill, effectively isolating and encapsulating the material under pavement, retaining walls, bridge abutments, or a clean, vegetated soil cap. This approach is considered protective of human health and the environment, technically feasible, and cost-effective due to shared project resources.

Specifically, Stockpiles 1 and 2 will be covered by SR-132 roadways and structures, with an interim phase involving grading and capping with a clean soil layer by 2018, and full containment by 2028. Stockpile 3 will be consolidated into the eastern abutment of the SR-132 bridge over SR-99 and the Stockpile 2 consolidation zone for full containment during the interim project phase. The implementation of a Land Use Covenant (LUC) will restrict incompatible land uses, and continuous monitoring (stockpiles, groundwater, stormwater) will occur until project completion. A Health and Safety Plan (HSP) will be in place during construction to manage potential hazards like barium exposure. This selected remedy is designed to eliminate exposure pathways, reduce contaminant mobility, and achieve regulatory and community acceptance as a long-term solution integrated with existing infrastructure projects.
