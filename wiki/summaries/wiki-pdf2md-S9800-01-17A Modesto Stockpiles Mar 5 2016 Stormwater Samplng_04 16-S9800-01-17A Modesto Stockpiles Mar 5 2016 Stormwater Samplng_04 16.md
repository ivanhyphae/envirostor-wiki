This consolidated report from Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans details surface water sampling at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles on March 5, 2016, with subsequent analysis and reporting in March and April 2016. The primary objectives were to assess contaminant levels and the effectiveness of stormwater management, under the oversight of DTSC and CVRWQCB.

Analytical results for the March 5, 2016, sampling showed that most general minerals and Title 22 metals were below Primary or Secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs). The only exceedance was manganese in sample PL2 (60 µg/l), which slightly surpassed its secondary MCL of 50 µg/l but remained within historical ranges. Across all samples (BG1, PL1, PL2, PL3, PL4, PL5), various parameters were analyzed:
*   **Residue:** Total Dissolved Solids ranged from 61-100 mg/L, Total Suspended Solids from 9.0-67 mg/L.
*   **Sulfide:** Detected between 0.013-0.024 mg/L in some samples.
*   **Dissolved Metals:** Notable detections included Antimony (0.66-2.4 ug/L), Barium (24-210 ug/L), Calcium (8100-20000 ug/L), Chromium (0.71-1.3 ug/L), Copper (8.6-13 ug/L), Magnesium (660-2600 ug/L), Manganese (up to 60 ug/L), Molybdenum (0.88-4.3 ug/L), Nickel (1.9-3.7 ug/L), Potassium (2100-8300 ug/L), Sodium (1900-2300 ug/L), Strontium (94-300 ug/L), Vanadium (3.0-7.0 ug/L), and Zinc (16-22 ug/L). Arsenic was detected in PL2 (1.1 ug/L). Many other metals, including mercury, were consistently non-detect. A historical high barium concentration (2,000 µg/L) from 2006 was deemed an isolated incident.
*   **Anions:** Chloride (3.2-3.6 mg/L), Nitrate (0.62-0.63 mg/L), and Sulfate (5.9-6.9 mg/L) were detected.
*   **Alkalinity:** Total alkalinity (as CaCO3) ranged from 25-38 mg/L.

Remedial actions at the site include ongoing surface water sampling, maintaining vegetative/fiber cover and straw wattles on Stockpile 2, and an interceptor berm to prevent offsite stormwater migration. Observations confirmed that runoff was contained within the Caltrans right-of-way, and a gate valve near sample point PL5, which could release water to the Tuolumne River, remained closed.
Advanced Technology Laboratories, Inc. (ATL) performed laboratory analyses with generally good QA/QC, though some samples required dilution and exhibited matrix spike recovery issues (e.g., Calcium in MSDs). An instruction to analyze out-of-hold-time nitrates was noted. Caltrans plans further sampling during future qualifying rain events. The reports provide detailed PQLs but do not explicitly state regulatory thresholds for all analytes.
