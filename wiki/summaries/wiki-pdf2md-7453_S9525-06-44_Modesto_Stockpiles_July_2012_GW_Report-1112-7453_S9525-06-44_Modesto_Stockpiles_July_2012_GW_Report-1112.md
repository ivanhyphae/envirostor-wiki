Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared the July 2012 Groundwater Monitoring Report (Project No. S9525-06-44) for the California Department of Transportation, under DTSC oversight, to detail groundwater monitoring activities at the Modesto Stockpiles site. From July 16 to 27, 2012, ten monitoring wells (MW-1 to MW-10) and one surface water location were sampled.

Groundwater depth ranged from 31.34 to 39.74 feet, with a consistent southeasterly flow at an average gradient of 0.0006. Analytical results, assessed against California Environmental Protection Agency MCLs (Maximum Contaminant Levels), revealed two main exceedances: Nitrate (as N) at 12 mg/L in MW-1 (exceeding the 10 mg/L MCL) and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) at 540 mg/L in MW-1 and up to 710 mg/L in MW-10 (exceeding the 500 mg/L secondary MCL). Other dissolved metals like Antimony, Arsenic, Barium, Chromium, and Strontium were detected, but generally remained below or within MCLs, except for high Strontium concentrations for which no MCL was available. Importantly, Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs) were below detection limits in most groundwater samples, though some Semivolatile Organic Compounds (SVOCs) like Acenaphthene, Phenanthrene, and Pyrene were detected at low levels in specific wells (e.g., MW-4, MW-7, MW-8).

Despite these findings, the report highlighted significant quality control (QC) issues across various analytical methods. Numerous matrix spike (MS) and matrix spike duplicate (MSD) recoveries for anions (Chloride, Nitrate, Sulfate), alkalinity (Carbonate), and metals (Calcium, Magnesium, Potassium, Sodium, Sulfide) frequently fell outside acceptable ranges (e.g., 70-130% or 80-120%), with some recoveries as high as 698% for Calcium. Relative Percent Differences (RPDs) for duplicate samples of metals like Arsenic, Barium, Calcium, and Magnesium also exceeded the 20% acceptance limit. These pervasive QC deviations, often attributed to matrix interference, introduce uncertainty regarding the overall reliability of the analytical data, warranting careful consideration in future assessments.
