This comprehensive Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (FEIR/EA) by Caltrans addresses the SR 132 freeway/expressway project, detailing environmental impacts, proposed mitigations, and extensive relocation assistance for displaced entities.

The environmental assessment (2009-2014) identified **aesthetics** (visual degradation, light/glare) and **biological resources** (adverse effects on special status species) as areas requiring significant mitigation. **Agriculture and forest resources, air quality, geology and soils, and cultural resources** were generally deemed to have less than significant impacts, or, in the case of cultural resources, manageable with mitigation. Caltrans deferred significance determination for **greenhouse gas emissions**, citing the speculative nature for individual projects. Impacts on specific parks (Cesar E. Chavez, Maddux Youth Center, Mellis Park) were deemed negligible, thus not triggering federal protection acts.

A significant portion of the document, particularly Appendices C and D, outlines Caltrans' detailed **relocation assistance program**, adhering to the Uniform Relocation Assistance and Real Property Acquisition Policies Act of 1970 and civil rights legislation. This program aims to ensure fair and equitable treatment for all displaced persons—individuals, families, businesses, farms, and non-profits—preventing disproportionate harm from public projects. Key provisions include:
*   At least 90 days' notice before displacement.
*   Access to "decent, safe, and sanitary" (DS&S) replacement housing, which Caltrans inspects and certifies as comparable, affordable, and non-discriminatory.
*   Comprehensive financial aid:
    *   **Moving Costs:** Reimbursement for actual costs or fixed schedules.
    *   **Replacement Housing Payments (RHP):** For owner-occupants, this covers price differential, mortgage differential, and incidental expenses. For tenants, it includes rent differential or a down payment option.
    *   **Fixed In Lieu Payments:** For businesses, ranging from $1,000 to $40,000 based on average annual net earnings.
    *   **Loss of Goodwill:** Potential payments for businesses under California law.
*   Extensive relocation advisory services, including finding suitable replacement properties and assisting with claims.
*   "Last Resort Housing" options, allowing Caltrans to construct, rehabilitate, or exceed payment limits if adequate housing is unavailable.
*   Assurances that relocation benefits will not affect eligibility for other aid programs (Social Security, Welfare, Income Tax).
*   The right for displaced individuals to appeal Caltrans' decisions, with information provided in Spanish and a commitment to non-discrimination based on protected characteristics.

Finally, Appendices E, F, and G address the **Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles** associated with the project. Investigations identified Barium as the primary Contaminant of Potential Concern (COPC), along with other substances like Strontium and PAHs. Human Health Risk Assessments concluded that current and proposed land uses, including for workers and residents, do not pose unacceptable health risks. The recommended remedial action is **Containment**, utilizing the stockpiled soil within the SR-132 Project's construction (e.g., retaining walls, bridge abutments), with interim measures (drainage, vegetated caps) by 2018 and full containment by 2028. Stockpile 3 will mainly be placed in the SR-132 bridge's eastern abutment over SR-99. This plan also includes strict environmental compliance, monitoring, and a land use covenant to restrict future uses and ensure long-term oversight, all under the regulatory guidance of Caltrans with DTSC and RWQCB involvement.
