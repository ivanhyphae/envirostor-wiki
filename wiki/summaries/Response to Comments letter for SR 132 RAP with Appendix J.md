### Combined Summary of "Response to Comments letter for SR 132 RAP with Appendix J"

This document is a formal "Response to Comments" letter, dated March 13, 2018, from the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) to community members. It transmits and compiles responses to 62 public comments received on the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, included as **Appendix J** of the Final Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (Final EIR/EA).

The public comment period (January 18 to March 17, 2017) is now closed, and the document's responses clarify and finalize project decisions. Key reaffirmed determinations include:

- **Preferred Project Alternative:** **Alternative 2** is reaffirmed, balancing environmental impacts, feasibility, and cost.
- **Selected Remedy:** **DTSC confirms concurrence with Alternative 4 (Containment)** for the soil stockpiles. The contaminated material will be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway, rather than removed. DTSC affirms the stockpiles pose **no unacceptable risk** to human health under current management (fencing, cover, monitoring), as contaminants are solids that do not evaporate.
- **Regulatory Actions:** To ensure long-term protection, Caltrans must enter into an **Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement** with DTSC, prepare an **O&M Plan**, conduct annual inspections, continue groundwater monitoring, and submit to **5-year remedy evaluations**.

**Key Responses and Clarifications:**
- **Remedy Selection:** Containment (Alternative 4) was selected over removal (Alternative 3) due to cost-effectiveness, long-term protection, and regulatory compliance. Traffic vibrations will not cause contaminant migration.
- **Safety and Design:** The document addresses Dakota Avenue widening, noise abatement (only Noise Barrier D is feasible; others were not), safety, traffic congestion, and farmland impacts.
- **Air Quality:** Project-level air quality conformity was received from the USEPA and FHWA.
- **Relocations:** Right-of-way acquisition will begin in 2018, handled by the **City of Modesto**, with offers under the Uniform Relocation Act. Property impact maps in **Appendix F** were revised to reduce full acquisitions.
- **Regulatory Context:** DTSC identified analogous California sites (Richmond Parkway, Union Pacific Curtis Park Site) where roads were built over hazardous substance sites.
- **Coordination:** Caltrans commits to coordinating with the Modesto Irrigation District (MID) on irrigation and electrical facility plans.

**Next Actions:**
- **Construction Phasing:** Phase 1 begins in 2018 (traffic by 2020); Phase 2 begins in 2026 (completion in 2028). A Traffic Management Plan will be developed.
- **Safety Measures:** All stockpile areas must be wetted and air-monitored during construction.
- **Project Communications:** A commenter was added to the mailing list, and a county health department was identified to assess potential health concerns.

The lead agency is **Caltrans**, with **DTSC** and the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB)** as co-regulators. The comment period is closed, and no further resubmittals are required by this letter.
