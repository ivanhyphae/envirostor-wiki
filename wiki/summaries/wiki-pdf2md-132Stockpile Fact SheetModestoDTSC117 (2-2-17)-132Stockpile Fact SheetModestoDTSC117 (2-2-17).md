## Document identity
- **Document type and purpose**: Community Update, Fact Sheet. Informs the public about the proposed remedial action for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site and invites public comment on the Draft Remedial Action Plan (RAP).
- **Author or preparer**: Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)
- **Date or period covered**: February 2, 2017

## Key claims
- **Site contamination history**: Asserted that soil contamination in the stockpiles resulted from excavation through an industrial waste pond during the construction of State Route 99 in Modesto in the 1960s. (DTSC)
- **Primary contaminants**: Asserted that the primary contaminants in the stockpile soil are barium, strontium, and lead. (Caltrans investigations)
- **Groundwater flow**: Asserted that groundwater beneath the base of the stockpiles flows consistently toward the southeast. (Caltrans investigations)
- **Groundwater and surface water quality**: Asserted that "Contaminants of Concern for groundwater and surface water are below Water Quality Objectives." (Caltrans investigations)
- **Human health risk (current)**: Asserted that, as currently managed, soil and groundwater at the site do not pose an unacceptable human risk for off-site residents, trespassers, or Caltrans workers. This is contingent on maintaining the perimeter fence, limiting access to Caltrans workers, maintaining vegetative cover, prohibiting soil placement/removal, and maintaining groundwater monitoring. (2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA), confirmed by 2012 additional soil sampling)
- **Long-term human health protection**: Asserted that containment of the stockpiles within the State Route 132 West project will achieve the overall goal of long-term protection of human health. (DTSC, Caltrans, RWQCB)
- **Environmental impact**: Asserted that the containment of the stockpile site along with mitigation would not have a significant environmental impact, as determined by an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA). (Caltrans, as lead agency under CEQA)

## Regulatory decisions
- **Decision type**: Approval for public notice and comment for the Draft Remedial Action Plan (RAP).
- **Agency**: Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB)
- **Date**: Prior to February 2, 2017 (implied by public notice issuance)
- **Conditions imposed**: Public review and comment period for the draft final RAP.

- **Decision type**: Concurrence with recommended remedial alternative.
- **Agency**: DTSC and RWQCB
- **Date**: Prior to February 2, 2017
- **Conditions imposed**: Recommendation for Alternative 4 (containment by construction of State Route 132 West project) from Caltrans is concurred with.

## Analytical results
- **Contaminants (soil)**: Barium, strontium, lead.
- **Media (soil)**: Stockpile soil.
- **Contaminants (groundwater)**: Undisclosed specific contaminants, but referred to as "groundwater contaminants that are monitored by FMC" and "Contaminants of Concern".
- **Media (groundwater)**: Groundwater beneath stockpiles.
- **Depth (groundwater)**: 30 to 44 feet below ground surface.
- **Monitoring frequency (groundwater)**: Originally collected in 2006, bi-monthly or quarterly beginning in 2012, currently annually.
- **Groundwater quality**: "Contaminants of Concern for groundwater [...] are below Water Quality Objectives."
- **Contaminants (surface water runoff)**: Metals (including primary contaminants of concern), general mineral parameters.
- **Media (surface water runoff)**: Samples from annual wintertime storm events.
- **Monitoring frequency (surface water runoff)**: Nine times since 2013 (annual wintertime storm events).
- **Surface water quality**: "Contaminants of Concern for [...] surface water are below Water Quality Objectives."

## Remedial actions
- **Proposed Remedial Action (Selected Remedy)**: Containment of stockpiles within a structural section of the proposed State Route 132 West Expressway/Freeway project (State Route 132 West).
- **Components of proposed remedy**:
    - Stockpiles to be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement of the State Route 132 project between Needham Street and Carpenter Avenue.
    - Unpaved portions to have a clean soil cover in Phase 1 (if State Route 132 West project were not constructed, containment would consist of a clean soil cap over stockpiles).
    - Establishment and maintenance of a deed restriction and land use covenant.
    - Site inspections.
    - Water quality monitoring.
- **Phase status**:
    - **Initial construction phase (Phase 1)**: Building retaining walls and bridge abutments, paving the southern half of the site, covering the northern half with a graded engineered soil layer.
        - **Timeline**: Begin in 2018, end in 2020.
    - **Ultimate build-out phase (Phase 2)**: Begin in 2028.
- **Remedial alternatives considered**:
    - **Alternative 1**: No Action (stockpiles remain in place without fences, monitoring, or maintenance – baseline).
    - **Alternative 2**: Institutional Controls (fences, signage, public notices, deed restriction, land use covenant, site inspections, air and water quality monitoring).
    - **Alternative 3**: Removal (excavating and transporting 160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil to an off-site disposal facility).
    - **Alternative 4**: Containment (as described in proposed remedy).

- **Responsible Reviewing Agencies (CEQA)**: DTSC, RWQCB

## Key terms and conditions
- **Public comment period**: DTSC invites review and comment on the draft final RAP. DTSC and RWQCB will consider all comments and prepare a Responsiveness Summary.
- **RAP approval process**: DTSC may approve the draft final RAP after public review and comment, and following completion/certification of the EIR/EA. Modifications to the RAP may be necessary based on public comments.
- **Remedial Design Implementation Plan**: If the draft final RAP is approved, DTSC and RWQCB will oversee the development of this plan to establish details on how to contain the stockpiles as part of the State Route 132 project.
- **Current management conditions (for no unacceptable human risk)**:
    1. Maintaining the perimeter fence.
    2. Limiting access to Caltrans workers.
    3. Maintaining a vegetative cover.
    4. Prohibiting placement or removal of soil from the site.
    5. Maintaining groundwater monitoring.
- **Future management conditions (under Alternative 4)**:
    - Establishment and maintenance of a deed restriction and land use covenant.
    - Site inspections.
    - Water quality monitoring.
    - Maintenance of the containment structures (retaining walls, bridge abutments, roadway pavement, clean soil cover).
