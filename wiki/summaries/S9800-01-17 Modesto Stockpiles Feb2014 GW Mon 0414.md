### Combined Summary: February 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Round, Modesto Soil Stockpiles (S9800-01-17)

This report, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans (Project No. S9800-01-17), documents the February 2014 quarterly groundwater monitoring event at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in Stanislaus County, California. Sampling was conducted on February 25-26, 2014, at all ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10).

**Laboratory Analysis and Quality Control**

Samples were analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) for a suite of constituents including dissolved Title 22 metals (by EPA 6020), mercury (EPA 7470A), general minerals, and nitrates. All samples were reported as non-detect for total sulfide. Key quality control issues were noted for sulfide analysis in one batch (B4B0489), with a low matrix spike recovery (11.2%) and high relative percent difference (27.4%), leading to data qualifiers for possible matrix interference.

**Key Findings and Regulatory Exceedances**

- **Nitrate:** Concentrations at wells MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 exceeded the California Primary MCL of 10 mg/L. This is a consistent finding from prior monitoring rounds; well MW-1 may reflect upgradient background conditions.
- **Dissolved Metals:** Barium and strontium, the primary constituents of concern (COCs), were detected in all wells but remained significantly below water quality thresholds. Lead was not detected. However, manganese exceeded its secondary MCL at well MW-10.
- **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS):** Concentrations at wells MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 exceeded the Secondary MCL of 500 mg/L, another historically consistent result.

**Water Chemistry Characterization**

Stiff diagrams were used to characterize water types. Wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10 were calcium-dominant, while MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8 were sodium-dominant.

**Conclusions and Recommendations**

The report concludes that, aside from manganese at MW-10, no dissolved metals exceeded regulatory thresholds. The primary ongoing issues are consistently elevated nitrate and TDS levels in specific wells. The report recommends continuing quarterly monitoring, with the next event scheduled for May 2014.
