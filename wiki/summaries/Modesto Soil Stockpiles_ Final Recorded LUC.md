### Document identity
- **Document type and purpose:** This is a final, recorded Land Use Covenant (LUC) and an incorporated Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement. The LUC imposes environmental restrictions on remediated property to protect human health. The O&M Agreement defines the ongoing maintenance obligations for the installed remedy.
- **Author or preparer:** The Covenantor is the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans). The Department is the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). The O&M Plan was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans.
- **Date or period covered:** The LUC was signed by Caltrans on December 14, 2023, and by DTSC on January 18, 2024. It was recorded in Stanislaus County on April 18, 2024. The O&M Agreement was signed on June 20, 2023. The document covers the post-remedial, ongoing operational phase of the project.

### Key claims
- **Assertion of residual contamination:** Barium-containing soil (BCS) with concentrations as high as **72,000 mg/kg** remains at the Property, which is "above levels acceptable for unrestricted land use" (Section 1.2).
- **Assertion of remedy adequacy:** DTSC concluded that the Property, as remediated and when used in compliance with the LUC, "does not present an unacceptable risk to present and future human health or safety or the environment" (Section 1.3).

### Regulatory decisions
- **Decision type:** Recorded Land Use Covenant (LUC) and an enforceable Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement.
- **Agency:** California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) named as co-regulator in the O&M Plan.
- **Date:** The RACR (Remedial Action Completion Report) was approved on January 18, 2023. The O&M Agreement was signed on June 20, 2023. The LUC was executed on January 18, 2024.
- **Conditions imposed:** The LUC mandates perpetual restrictions on land use (no residential, school, hospital, or daycare), requires a pre-approved Soil Management Plan for any soil disturbance, prohibits groundwater extraction without an approved plan, and obligates annual compliance inspections and reporting to DTSC. The O&M Agreement mandates implementation of the O&M Plan, five-year remedy reviews, and cost recovery.

### Analytical results
- **Contaminants of concern:** Barium (primary), lead (secondary, from aerially deposited lead [ADL]).
- **Media:** Soil.
- **Maximum concentration:** Barium up to **72,000 mg/kg**.
- **Thresholds:** Barium levels are above regulatory screening levels for unrestricted land use. Lead concentrations in ADL-impacted soil from the SR 99 northbound shoulder exceeded California hazardous waste thresholds (specific value not given, but requiring management as hazardous waste).

### Remedial actions
- **Activity:** Consolidation and capping of barium-containing soil (BCS). This involved moving material from three historic stockpiles (1, 2, and 3) into two designated "Containment Zones" (Stockpile 1 and 2).
- **Method:** The BCS was used as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway. It was then capped with clean fill material (minimum 12 inches) and a structural pavement section.
- **Quantities:** Total BCS volume was approximately **160,000 cubic yards**. The combined area of the two containment zones is approximately **9.3 acres** (Stockpile 1: 3.45 acres; Stockpile 2: 5.83 acres).
- **Phase status:** This is an "Interim Phase 1" remedy. "Ultimate Phase 2" will involve widening the highway to four lanes. The remedy was constructed between 2020 and 2022, and SR 132 opened to traffic on November 3, 2022. Stockpile 3 was entirely removed and placed within the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. The remedy is considered complete (as per the approved RACR), and the project is now in the O&M phase.

### Responsible parties
- **Owner/Operator:** California Department of Transportation (Caltrans). As the Covenantor and Proponent, Caltrans is liable for all present and future obligations including compliance with the LUC, implementation of the O&M Plan, and cost recovery to DTSC.
- **Regulator:** Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), the lead oversight agency. The Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) is a co-regulator.
- **Consultant:** Geocon Consultants, Inc., the preparer of the O&M Plan. Rien Groenewoud, P.L.S. (Northstar Engineering Group) prepared the legal descriptions and plats.

### Key terms and conditions
- **Annual Inspection:** The Owner must conduct an annual inspection of the Property and submit a compliance report to DTSC by January 18th of each year. Violations must be reported and addressed within 10 calendar days.
- **Perpetual Term:** The LUC is perpetual unless terminated by DTSC in accordance with Health and Safety Code section 25224.
- **Five-Year Review:** The O&M Agreement requires a review and reevaluation of the remedy every five years to ensure it is protecting human health and the environment. The first review is due five years from January 18, 2023 (the RACR approval date).
- **Soil Management Protocol:** Any soil disturbance requires a pre-approved Soil Management Plan. Excavated BCS must be stockpiled on 6-mil plastic sheeting and covered. Offsite disposal requires characterization for landfill acceptance (Title 22 metals and WET/TCLP).
- **Dust Mitigation:** Water spray and covering of stockpiles are required to control fugitive dust during any BCS handling.
- **Cost Recovery:** The Owner agrees to pay all of DTSC's costs for administering, implementing, and enforcing the LUC and the O&M Agreement.
