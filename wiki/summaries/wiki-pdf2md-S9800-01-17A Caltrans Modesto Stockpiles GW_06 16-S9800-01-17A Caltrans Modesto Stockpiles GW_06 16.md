GEOCON Consultants, Inc. conducted groundwater monitoring at the Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles site (Project S9800-01-17A, Task Order No. 17) for the California Department of Transportation — District 6 Hazardous Waste Branch. This report summarizes sampling activities, primarily focusing on events in October 2015 and May 2016, while also referencing historical data from 2006 to 2014.

During the May 2016 sampling event, groundwater samples were collected from wells MW-1, MW-7, and MW-11, with many other wells being dry or having insufficient water. Depth to groundwater consistently ranged from approximately 37 to 44 feet below the Top of Casing, with a historical flow direction generally towards the southeast. Analytical parameters included Title 22 dissolved metals, specific general minerals, and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), with analyses performed by Advanced Technology Laboratories.

Results indicated that most dissolved metal concentrations in May 2016, such as Barium, Strontium, Arsenic, and Chromium, were below California Primary and Secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs). Nitrate as nitrogen was also below the primary MCL of 10 mg/L in all sampled wells. However, TDS in MW-7 (510 mg/L) and MW-11 (510 mg/L) exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/L. Historical data (2012-2014) showed that nitrates and TDS in other wells (MW-5, MW-6, MW-10) had also consistently exceeded their respective MCLs, and manganese sporadically exceeded the secondary MCL. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) analysis was discontinued due to a lack of detections, a decision approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).

Quality control data from the May 2016 analyses, including blanks, laboratory control samples, duplicates, and matrix spikes, generally indicated acceptable results for various analytes, despite some noted QA/QC deviations for dilution factors. Key stakeholders in this project include Caltrans, Geocon, DTSC, and the California Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). GEOCON recommends continued annual groundwater monitoring, with the next event scheduled for April 2017, and semi-annual depth-to-water measurements.
