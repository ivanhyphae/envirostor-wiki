## Decision or request
Caltrans responds to DTSC’s March 1, 2012 letter and commits to the following actions:

1. **Limit access to Caltrans authorized personnel:**  
   - Chain-link fence with locking gates already exists around stockpiles #1 and #2.  
   - For stockpile #3, a fence is under construction on the west side (previously unfenced due to SR 99 access constraints) and will be completed early next week; “No Trespassing” signs will be posted.  
   - Perimeter fencing will be inspected every two weeks; a contractor will be notified to make repairs with before/after photographs.  
   - A column in the monthly Site Report will document all fence inspections and repairs.  
   - Trash and debris inside each stockpile area will be inspected and removed quarterly, also documented in the monthly Site Report.

2. **Maintain the current grade and vegetative cover:**  
   - Existing grade and drainage will be maintained as they are; no activities that would alter them are occurring.  
   - For bare soil areas on stockpile #2 (southern slope—past fire; top deck—gravel/asphalt), Caltrans will consult with its Maintenance and Landscaping divisions to identify options to reduce/minimize bare soil exposure.  
   - A report describing those options will be presented to DTSC by **April 20, 2012**.  
   - Quarterly inspection and status of actions taken to reduce soil exposure will be noted in the monthly Site Report.

3. **Maintain the groundwater monitoring system:**  
   - All eight monitoring wells were sounded on March 8, 2012; well vaults and locking caps inspected; all determined fully functional.  
   - Quarterly groundwater monitoring resumed on March 12, 2012, following the DTSC-approved Groundwater Assessment Work Plan (January 26, 2006); analytical parameters include strontium and general minerals.  
   - A groundwater monitoring report for the March sampling event will be prepared and submitted to DTSC and the Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB).

Additionally, Caltrans states it is in the process of verifying oversight agreements with both DTSC and RWQCB, under which Caltrans will reimburse both agencies for all costs associated with review of existing documents, collection and review of additional data for site characterization, updating the risk assessment, preparing the Feasibility Study/Remedial Action Plan, public participation, etc. Study results will be coordinated with StanCOG.
