## Document identity
- Document type and purpose: Task Order for Interagency Agreement 43A0260; outlines DTSC's oversight and review services for Caltrans' remediation of the Modesto Soil Stockpiles and Caltrans' obligations for payment and remedial actions.
- Author or preparer: Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and California Department of Transportation (Caltrans)
- Date or period covered: June 22, 2012, through May 31, 2013

## Key claims
- The Site consists of three stockpiles containing over 120,000 yd³ of contaminated soil. (Caltrans/DTSC Agreement, I. Task Order Purpose)
- The stockpiled soil was generated during sub-grade construction of State Route 99. (Caltrans/DTSC Agreement, I. Task Order Purpose)
- Excavation near Kansas Avenue removed tailings from a former Food and Machinery Corporation (FMC) barite and celestite processing disposal pond. (Caltrans/DTSC Agreement, I. Task Order Purpose)
- The Site is impacted by hazardous substances (metals), including barium, lead, and strontium. (Caltrans/DTSC Agreement, I. Task Order Purpose; Informational Terms and Definitions, 5. Substances Found at the Site)
- DTSC determined, via a 2006 Interagency Agreement task order, the need to address the use of soil stockpile material in the State Route 132 West Expressway project and/or its final disposition prior to design and construction, due to human health and water quality concerns. (DTSC letter dated 12/7/2012 to Caltrans, cited in Caltrans/DTSC Agreement, I. Task Order Purpose)
- The Site is owned by Caltrans. (Informational Terms and Definitions, 4. Ownership)

## Regulatory decisions
- Decision Type: Agreement (Task Order 6) under Interagency Agreement 43A0260
- Agency: California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) and Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)
- Date: Execution Date: June 22, 2012
- Conditions imposed:
    - Caltrans to reimburse DTSC for oversight and consultative services related to the Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation.
    - Caltrans to update the Human Health Risk Assessment and collect additional technical data for Site remediation decisions.
    - DTSC to provide professional staff services for Site characterization oversight, data review, report review, and remedy evaluation/decision document approval.
    - Caltrans to submit a Sampling and Analysis Workplan, a Site Characterization Report, an updated Risk Evaluation, a Feasibility Study, and a Remedial Action Plan (RAP).
    - Caltrans to address California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) obligations through the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Environmental Impact/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) Report, incorporating the RAP by reference.
    - Caltrans to conduct public participation activities, in cooperation with DTSC, including community profiles, fact sheets, public notices, and public meetings.
    - All sampling and analysis by Caltrans to be performed in accordance with a DTSC-approved Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC) Plan.
    - Caltrans to submit quarterly groundwater monitoring reports.
    - Caltrans to notify DTSC immediately of any immediate threat to public health, safety, or the environment.
    - Caltrans to make all data and information concerning contamination available to DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board upon request.
    - Caltrans to retain all task order-related data and documents for a minimum of six years after termination.
    - Caltrans to inform DTSC at least seven days in advance of all field activities.
    - Caltrans to designate a Project Manager and ensure work is performed under the direction of a California-licensed professional engineer or geologist with hazardous substance site cleanup expertise.
    - All work is subject to DTSC review and approval.
    - Caltrans to pay costs incurred by DTSC, including an advance payment of $25,000 within 10 days of the effective date.
    - Termination for convenience clause with 30-day advance written notice, with Caltrans responsible for DTSC costs through the termination date.

## Analytical results
- Contaminants: Barium (BaSO₄), Strontium (SrSO₄), Lead. These are categorized as hazardous substances (metals).
- Media: Soil, surface water (prospective), groundwater (prospective).
- Concentration ranges: Not specified in this document.
- Applicable thresholds: Not specified in this document.
- Exceedances noted: Stockpiles containing "contaminated soil" and "hazardous substances" indicate exceedances of acceptable levels.

## Remedial actions
- Activity:
    - *Oversight and Consultative Services (DTSC)*: Review of investigative data, reports, remedy evaluation/decision documents (Human Health Risk Assessment, Feasibility Study, Remedial Action Plan), and other regulatory instruments. On-site evaluation of field activities.
    - *Site Characterization (Caltrans)*: Submittal of existing data, and, if needed, additional characterization including a Sampling and Analysis Workplan (describing proposed activities for soil, surface water, and/or groundwater), health and safety plan, quality assurance/quality control plan, sampling plan, implementation schedule. Implementation of approved workplan. Submittal of a Site Characterization Report.
    - *Risk Evaluation (Caltrans)*: Updating of existing risk evaluation consistent with U.S. EPA Risk Assessment Guidance for Superfund and DTSC Supplemental Guidance for Human Health Multimedia Risk Assessments of Hazardous Waste Sites and Permitted Facilities. Identification of chemicals of concern, potential routes of exposure, potential risks to human and ecological receptors, and site cleanup goals.
    - *Feasibility Study (Caltrans)*: Evaluation of appropriate remediation and response alternatives, including a "no action" alternative. Identification of cleanup goals based on current and projected future land uses, evaluation of feasible alternatives (effectiveness, implementability, cost), and recommendation of a preferred alternative.
    - *Remedial Action Plan (Caltrans)*: Preparation of a RAP according to Health and Safety Code section 25356.1, summarizing site characterization, risk evaluation, and other technical studies. Detailing steps to remedy soil, surface water, and groundwater contamination and providing an implementation schedule.
    - *CEQA Compliance (Caltrans & DTSC)*: Caltrans to address CEQA via the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Environmental Impact/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) Report, incorporating the RAP. DTSC to review and comment on hazardous substances information in the Draft EIR/EA.
    - *Public Participation (Caltrans & DTSC)*: Caltrans to conduct activities (community profile, fact sheets, public notices, public meetings) with DTSC review and approval, ensuring public involvement in DTSC's decision-making. DTSC to participate in public outreach.
    - *Quality Assurance/Quality Control (Caltrans)*: All sampling and analysis to adhere to a DTSC-approved QA/QC Plan covering sample collection, preservation, transport, instrument calibration, data processing, chain of custody, and qualified personnel.
    - *Groundwater Monitoring (Caltrans)*: Preparation of quarterly groundwater monitoring reports, discussing depth to groundwater, well purging/sampling, chain of custody, laboratory analysis (analytes, methods, results), QA/QC, and comparison of results.
- Quantities: Three stockpiles, in excess of 120,000 yd³ of contaminated soil.
- Phase status: Initial phases of planning, characterization, risk assessment, and remedy selection are required, leading to RAP approval and implementation.
- Verification outcomes: Not yet applicable; document outlines actions to be taken.

## Responsible parties
- State of California, Department of Transportation (Caltrans): Owner/operator of the Site, responsible for costs incurred by DTSC for oversight, conducting remedial investigations, preparing decision documents, implementing remedial actions, public participation, and all associated work.
- Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC): Lead regulatory agency, responsible for oversight and consultative services, review and approval of all technical documents, formal decision-making, and participating in public outreach.
- Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB): Co-regulator, reviewing existing Site information and receiving copies of all data and information.
