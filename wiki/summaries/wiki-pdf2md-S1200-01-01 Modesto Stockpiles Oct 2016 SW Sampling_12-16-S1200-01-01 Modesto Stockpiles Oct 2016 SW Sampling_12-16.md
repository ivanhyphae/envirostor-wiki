Geocon Consultants, Inc. conducted surface water sampling on October 28, 2016, at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles in Stanislaus County, California, under Project No. S1200-01-01. This report, along with others from October and November 2016, details analyses of various surface water and stormwater samples collected during a rain event where visible puddles and runoff were present.

Sampling locations included PL1 through PL5, and background locations BG1 and BG2, with some sites historically noted as sometimes dry. The samples were analyzed for a broad range of parameters including dissolved metals (e.g., Antimony, Arsenic, Barium, Cadmium, Chromium, Copper, Lead, Manganese, Nickel, Thallium, Zinc), general minerals (such as chloride, nitrate, sulfate, and alkalinity components), and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) and Total Suspended Solids (TSS) using methods like ICP-MS EPA 6020 and Cold Vapor AA EPA 7470A.

With the exception of manganese at PL3, which equaled the secondary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL), all measured dissolved metal and general mineral concentrations in the October 2016 surface water samples were below their respective primary or secondary MCLs. This indicates generally low concentrations of tested parameters relative to regulatory thresholds. Historically, a barium concentration of 2,000 µg/L was observed at location SW03 in March 2006, contrasting with the 6.5J µg/L to 210 µg/L range found in current and recent sampling.

Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC) information was consistently present across the reports, noting data qualifiers such as "J" (estimated value), "<" (below reporting limits), "R" (RPD outside acceptance criteria), "M1" (matrix spike recovery outside limit), and "D1" (sample diluted). Specific QA/QC issues included some samples requiring dilution due to laboratory issues, an elevated Relative Percent Difference (RPD) for a TDS duplicate, and instances where zinc's % recovery and RPD were outside acceptable limits in a dissolved metals batch. Despite these, matrix spikes and Laboratory Control Standard (LCS) recoveries were generally acceptable.

Overall, the surface water management efforts at the site, which include vegetative/fiber cover, straw wattles, and an interceptor berm, were deemed effective as no offsite migration of stormwater runoff was observed, indicating stormwater was retained within the site boundaries. Caltrans plans to continue surface water sampling during future qualifying rain events.
