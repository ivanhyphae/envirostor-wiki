### Summary of the Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Update for Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (Rev. 0313)

This document is the Revised Final **Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Update** for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (Project Number S9525-06-44, Task Order 23), prepared by **Geocon Consultants, Inc.** and dated March 1, 2013. It was prepared under the oversight of the **California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)** and co-regulation by the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB)** , fulfilling an Interagency Agreement with **Caltrans**. The document serves as an update to a May 14, 2007 HHRA, incorporating new soil and groundwater data collected in 2012 to validate the earlier conclusions.

The site consists of three soil stockpiles (Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3) totaling approximately 12.5 acres, located within the Caltrans right-of-way near the Kansas Avenue interchange of SR 99 and SR 132 in Modesto, Stanislaus County. The stockpiles, totaling about 160,000 cubic yards, contain fill material generated during 1960s highway construction. This material originated from the excavation of former **FMC Corporation** evaporation ponds and is primarily contaminated with **barium** (up to 4,300 mg/kg in surface soil) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), along with other metals. The affected media include surface soil (0-1 ft bgs), subsurface soil (0-20 ft bgs), and shallow groundwater (~35 ft bgs) in eight monitoring wells.

Key findings from the updated risk characterization, which uses U.S. EPA and California DTSC/OEHHA guidance, confirm **no unacceptable human health risks** for all evaluated scenarios. The maximum detected concentrations from 2012 were less than an order of magnitude different from the values evaluated in 2007. Specifically, the risks for current trespassers, future construction workers, and off-site residents were all found to be below acceptable thresholds. Cumulative cancer risk and noncancer hazard indices (HI) are well below their respective targets (1E-06 for cancer risk, 1.0 for HI). Lead exposure is also below the 10 µg/dL blood lead level of concern. Notably, arsenic was excluded as a contaminant of concern for Stockpile #2 because its concentration (0.7–4.9 mg/kg) was determined to be equivalent to background levels. The **Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO)** of DTSC formally concurred with these conclusions in a February 14, 2013 memorandum.

Because the current management is deemed protective, no specific remedial alternative or remedy is selected in this document. The report acknowledges that its conservative assumptions (e.g., using maximum detected concentrations, applying chronic toxicity values to short-term exposures) likely overestimate true risk, meaning actual risks could be considerably lower. Instead, DTSC has directed Caltrans to continue managing the site with existing controls, which include: limiting access to authorized personnel, maintaining perimeter fencing and vegetation, preventing excavation or off-site soil removal, and continuing the ongoing **bimonthly groundwater monitoring program**. DTSC also required specific revisions to the HHRA report, including clarification on cadmium concentrations and a plan to address stockpile material found outside the fenceline at the western boundary of Stockpile 2 along Emerald Avenue. The path forward requires Caltrans to maintain these conditions and consult with DTSC and the RWQCB regarding the ultimate disposition of the stockpile material.
