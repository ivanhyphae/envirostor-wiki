## Document identity
- **Document type and purpose:** Site Investigation Workplan (SIWP) for the Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project. The purpose is to describe the scope of services for a site investigation to characterize soil in Stockpile 3 within the project boundaries to support health and safety planning, soil management evaluation, and preparation of an Interim Remedial Action Workplan (IRAW).
- **Author or preparer:** Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Project Manager).
- **Date or period covered:** April 13, 2012 (document date). The proposed field activities were scheduled for April 25–27, 2012, with a draft IRAW due by June 6, 2012.

## Key claims
- Barium is the primary contaminant of potential concern (COPC) in Stockpile 3, with concentrations ranging from 31 to 44,300 mg/kg, but the highest detected level (in sample B17-4.5) does not exceed the commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) for barium of 63,000 mg/kg (Shaw, 2004 PSI data).
- Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were not detected in samples from borings B16 and B17 within the project boundaries (Shaw, 2004 data).
- Barium concentrations in Stockpile 3 samples from 2006 borings (S24, S25, N12) were generally within the range of site-specific background levels (31 to 150 mg/kg) (Shaw, 2006 data).
- Nitrate, sulfate, and sulfide concentrations in the 2006 stockpile samples were within the range of site-specific background levels (Shaw, 2006 data).
- The 2009 DTSC response to the Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) found that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents.

## Regulatory decisions
- **Decision type:** Letter of Determination
- **Agency:** California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)
- **Date:** December 17, 2009
- **Conditions imposed:** The DTSC directed Caltrans to continue managing the stockpiles until the SR99/Highway 132 Interchange is constructed and to maintain the existing groundwater monitoring system.

## Analytical results
- **Media:** Soil (stockpile and native)
- **Contaminants:** Barium, PAHs, nitrate, sulfate, sulfide, pH, title 22 metals, strontium
- **Concentration ranges (Barium; Stockpile 3):**
    - 31 to 44,300 mg/kg (2004, borings B16, B17)
    - 64 to 230 mg/kg (2006, boring S24)
    - 38 to 93 mg/kg (2006, boring S25)
    - 35 to 178 mg/kg (2006, boring N12)
- **Applicable thresholds:** Commercial/Industrial CHHSL for barium = 63,000 mg/kg. Background barium range for Stockpile 3 = 31 to 150 mg/kg.
- **Exceedances noted:** None identified for barium against the CHHSL. Barium concentrations in 2006 samples were within background.

## Remedial actions
- **Activity:** Planned excavation of Stockpile 3 for retaining wall construction.
- **Quantities:** Approximately 3,539 cubic yards of soil embankment to be excavated.
- **Phase status:** Pre-remedial; this workplan is for investigation to support an IRAW. The physical work (excavation) is proposed but not yet authorized.
- **Verification outcomes:** None; the document is a workplan, not a completion report.

## Responsible parties
- **California Department of Transportation (Caltrans):** Owner/operator of the site; contract holder for the investigation; responsible for future management of stockpiles and compliance with DTSC conditions.
- **Geocon Consultants, Inc.:** Consultant preparing and executing the workplan.
- **Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC Corporation):** Historical source of the waste (liquid residue from its facility) that was deposited in the stockpiles.
- **Shaw Environmental, Inc.**: Conducted the 2004 PSI and 2006 supplemental site investigation.

## Key terms and conditions
- The IRAW must be prepared and submitted to DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) for review and approval before project approval.
- Caltrans must maintain the existing groundwater monitoring system.
- Caltrans must continue to manage the stockpiles until the SR99/Highway 132 Interchange is constructed.
- Caltrans will provide advance notification of field work to DTSC and CVRWQCB.
- All soil borings will be backfilled with bentonite slurry (direct-push) or soil cuttings (hand-auger).
- The investigation will include traffic control measures (shoulder closure, advance warning signs, traffic cones).
- Soil samples will be analyzed under expedited 24-hour turnaround time.
