## Document identity
- Document type and purpose: Cover letter transmitting revised documents (Administrative Record, Statement of Reasons, and Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility) for inclusion in the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP).
- Author or preparer: Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist, Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program, Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).
- Date or period covered: May 18, 2015

## Key claims
- Per DTSC, the revised Administrative Record, Statement of Reasons, and Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility have been prepared based on communication with Mr. Richard Stewart, P.G. on April 23, 2015. These documents are to be included as appendices in the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132 (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, following the addition of the referenced appendices, the Draft Final RAP will be the document referenced in the Caltrans Draft Environment Impact Report (EIR) and will be made available for public review and comment concurrently with the Draft EIR (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, the Draft Final RAP complies with California Health and Safety Code (HSC) section 25356.1, including the requirement for a statement of reasons addressing the basis for selected remedial actions and consistency with federal regulations (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, based on human health risk assessments and existing management practices (fences, limited access, vegetative cover, groundwater monitoring), the Site does not present an unacceptable risk to current residents, trespassers, and Caltrans workers and contractors (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, there is no reported municipal or domestic use of shallow groundwater within one mile of the soil stockpiles according to the City of Modesto and a Department of Water Resources survey (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, groundwater under the stockpiles does not contain Contaminants of Potential Concern (COPCs) that exceed primary maximum contaminant levels for drinking water (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, the incorporation of stockpile soil into the construction of State Route 132 at the Site is consistent with the Final Feasibility Study (FS) and Draft Final RAP and is protective of human health and the environment, including groundwater (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, the recommended remedial alternative (containment) is protective of groundwater and surface water quality (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, COPCs in the stockpiles and groundwater have been extensively characterized, with groundwater COPCs, including barium, being below regulatory primary maximum contaminant threshold values for drinking water (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, the cost implementation for the recommended remedial alternative (containment), including purchase of clean replacement soil, is approximately 20 times less than the cost to excavate and transport excavated soil stockpile material for offsite disposal (DTSC, May 18, 2015).
- Per DTSC, all potential remedial action impacts will be mitigated under the recommended remedial alternative (DTSC, May 18, 2015).

## Regulatory decisions
- Decision type: Submittal of `Revised Administrative Record, Statement of Reasons, and Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility` for inclusion in the `Draft Final Remedial Action Plan`.
- Agency: Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)
- Date: May 18, 2015
- Conditions imposed: The Draft Final RAP, including these appendices, will be referenced in the Caltrans Draft Environment Impact Report (EIR) and made available for public review and comment concurrently with the Draft EIR.

## Analytical results
- Contaminants: Barium, strontium, lead (primary COPCs); antimony, arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, chromium, cobalt, copper, mercury, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, silver, thallium, vanadium, zinc, polycyclic aromatic compounds, nitrate, sulfate, sulfide (additional COPCs).
- Media: Stockpile soil, underlying groundwater.
- Exceedances noted: Groundwater COPCs, including barium, are currently below regulatory primary maximum contaminant threshold values for drinking water.

## Remedial actions
- Activity: Containment of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil.
- Locations: Beneath roadway pavement, behind retaining walls, and behind bridge abutments for the State Route 132 Freeway/Expressway Project.
- Design elements: A clean vegetated soil cap engineered to minimize water infiltration and convey surface water away from stockpile areas.
- Phase status: Recommended as Alternative #4 in the Final Feasibility Study and Draft Final RAP. Implementation contingent on DTSC and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board review and concurrence of the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP) and Operation and Maintenance Plan (OMP).
- Verification outcomes: Groundwater monitoring and surface water monitoring will be required as part of the Operation and Maintenance Plan to evaluate the effectiveness of covered surfaces in preventing infiltration and mobilization of COPCs.

## Responsible parties
- Caltrans: Owner/operator of the soil stockpiles, assumes full responsibility for remediation and operation and maintenance costs.
- DTSC (Department of Toxic Substances Control): Prepared the revised documents and acts as the lead regulatory agency.
- Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB): Co-regulator, will review and concur with the RDIP and OMP.

## Key terms and conditions
- Required follow-on actions: Inclusion of revised documents as appendices in the Draft Final RAP. Concurrent public review and comment period for the Draft Final RAP with the Caltrans Draft EIR.
- Monitoring obligations: Groundwater monitoring and surface water monitoring are required as part of the Operation and Maintenance Plan. Monitoring frequencies and reporting requirements to be established in the RDIP.
- Restrictions: A land use covenant will be recorded to preclude the use of the property for residences, schools, daycare centers, and hospitals.
- Agreements: Operation and Maintenance Agreement, including an Operation and Maintenance Plan, will require maintenance, annual inspections, and reporting for all surfaces overlying the stockpiles.
