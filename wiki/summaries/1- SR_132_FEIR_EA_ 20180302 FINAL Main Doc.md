### Combined Summary of "SR_132_FEIR_EA_20180302 FINAL Main Doc"

**Document Identity:** This document is the **Final Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (FEIR/EA)** for the **State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway project**, prepared by **Caltrans District 10** and finalized on **March 2, 2018**. It evaluates the environmental impacts of a ~4-mile highway realignment in Stanislaus County, including a Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for managing contaminated soil stockpiles.

**Key Claims and Regulatory Decisions:** The document recommends **Alternative 2** for the highway, finding it provides the best balance of minimizing environmental impacts and meeting project needs. A **Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI)** was issued under NEPA. For the contaminated stockpiles, **Alternative 4 (Containment)** is recommended, which places the contaminated soil beneath new highway pavement. The **California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)** will make the final determination on this RAP. A **Statement of Overriding Considerations** was adopted for unavoidable significant noise and visual impacts. Key regulatory concurrences include the U.S. EPA confirming the project is not an air quality concern (2016) and the State Historic Preservation Officer concurring with Section 106 findings (2012, 2015).

**Soil Stockpile Contamination and Remediation:** Three Caltrans-owned soil stockpiles (totaling ~160,000 cubic yards) are contaminated primarily with **barium** (up to 196,000 mg/kg) and lead, a legacy from 1960s excavation of a former **FMC Corporation** property. The recommended **Containment** remedy will permanently encapsulate the soil, preventing direct exposure and contaminant migration. Groundwater and stormwater monitoring data are below applicable thresholds. A **land use covenant** restricting the site to transportation uses is required.

**Natural Environment and Biological Resources:** The study area consists of degraded habitat. The project would permanently remove up to 70 acres of marginal foraging habitat for **Swainson's hawk**, and up to 20.8 acres of habitat for **burrowing owls**. Mitigation requires preconstruction protocol surveys, with buffers and work restrictions determined in consultation with the **California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW)**. No Clean Water Act Section 404 permit is needed. While 0.65 acres of seasonal wetlands will be impacted, they are not "waters of the U.S." but may be "waters of the state" under **Central Valley RWQCB** jurisdiction.

**Physical and Socioeconomic Impacts:**
- **Traffic/Noise:** Build alternatives will significantly improve traffic flow (Level of Service). However, noise impacts are significant: in 2048, Alternative 1 would impact 260 receivers, and Alternative 2 would impact 276 receivers. One barrier, **Noise Barrier D**, is recommended (reducing noise by 5–17 dBA), but its construction depends on a survey of benefited property owners.
- **Visual/Aesthetics:** The proposed flyover structure at the SR 132/SR 99 interchange will cause a **significant and unavoidable visual impact**, particularly degrading the **Elm Tract neighborhood** viewshed.
- **Land Use/Agriculture:** The project will convert 38.92 acres of prime farmland, 6.7 acres of Williamson Act contract lands, and increase impervious surfaces by 55.8 to 57.5 acres.
- **Socioeconomic/Environmental Justice:** The project will cause business and residential relocations. Mitigation measures will ensure relocation and construction impacts are not disproportionate. However, visual impacts will remain disproportionate for minority and low-income populations, primarily in the **Elm Tract neighborhood**.
- **GHG and Air Quality:** Operational CO2 emissions will increase relative to the 2010 baseline but decrease compared to the future No-Build Alternative, aligning with state climate goals.

**Responsible Parties and Conditions:** **Caltrans** is the lead agency, with the **Federal Highway Administration** providing federal oversight. Mitigation and permitting involve multiple co-regulators, including the **Central Valley RWQCB** (water quality), **CDFW** (species), **DTSC** (contaminated soil), **StanCOG** (transportation planning), and the **City of Modesto** (tree ordinance). Key conditions include ongoing groundwater and stormwater monitoring, mitigation measures for noise, visual impacts, and tree replacement, as well as strict health and safety plans for construction. The document underwent a public circulation period (Jan 18 – Mar 17, 2017) and a public hearing on February 22, 2017, resulting in 305 public comments.
