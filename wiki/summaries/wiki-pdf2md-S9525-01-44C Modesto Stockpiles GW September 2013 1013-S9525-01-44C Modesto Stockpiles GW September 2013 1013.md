Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared three groundwater monitoring reports for the Modesto Stockpiles site (Project S9525-01-44C) for the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) with oversight from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). The reports cover sampling events between September 4-12, 2013, with analysis and reporting through September 13, 2013.

Groundwater was sampled from ten to seventeen wells (MW-1 to MW-17), with depths ranging from 30.22 to 42.05 feet below the top of the casing. The groundwater flow direction was consistently towards the southeast. A damaged well box at MW-6 was replaced, and new locks were installed on all wells. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) analysis was discontinued in November 2012 with DTSC's approval.

Samples were analyzed for dissolved metals (including Title 22 metals), general minerals, nitrates, total dissolved solids (TDS), and sulfide. Concentrations were compared against California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Primary and Secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) and EPA Health Advisories. Key exceedances included:
*   **Nitrates (as nitrogen)**: Exceeded the 10 mg/L MCL in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 (up to 36 mg/L), a consistent trend since March 2012.
*   **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)**: Exceeded the 500 mg/L secondary MCL in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 (up to 985 mg/L), also a consistent issue.
*   **Arsenic**: Exceeded the 10 µg/L MCL in MW-1 and MW-4 (up to 140 µg/L).
*   **Manganese**: Sporadically exceeded secondary MCLs.
Other dissolved metals and general minerals were generally below thresholds, though specific elevated concentrations were noted for Molybdenum, Barium, and Strontium in certain wells.

The reports detailed extensive Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC) data, including Laboratory Control Samples (LCS), Duplicates, Matrix Spikes (MS), and Matrix Spike Duplicates (MSDs). While most analytes were within acceptable recovery and RPD limits, several specific deviations were noted. Matrix interference ("M1" qualifier) and recoveries outside acceptance limits were observed for various parameters, including Chloride, Carbonate Alkalinity (low recovery of 19.6%), dissolved Mercury, Calcium, Magnesium, Sodium, Barium (up to 223%), Chromium (up to 181%), Vanadium (up to 355%), and Strontium (up to 154%). Despite these issues, the analytical batches were generally validated by their respective LCS, and samples were received in good condition.
