### Document Identity

- **Document type and purpose:** Appendices to a Final Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) for the **State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project**, which includes a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) and Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP). The appendices cover CEQA compliance, Section 4(f)/Section 6(f) resource evaluations, Title VI policy, relocation assistance (for residences, businesses, farms, and nonprofits), and the remediation of barium-contaminated soil stockpiles.
- **Author or preparer:** California Department of Transportation (Caltrans), with contributions from various consultants (Geocon Consultants, Inc., Shaw Environmental, Inc., Kleinfelder).
- **Date or period covered:** Document finalized March 2, 2018; references data and correspondence from 1930s-1970s (site history) through 2014. Relocation assistance brochures are effective October 1, 2014.

### Key Claims

- **No Section 4(f) Use:** The project will not result in a permanent, temporary, or constructive use of seven evaluated Section 4(f) resources (parks, historic properties, trailway), due to distance, non-public ownership, or no acquisition within the historic boundary.
- **No Section 6(f) Conversion:** Property from the Virginia Corridor Trailway and Mellis Park (Land and Water Conservation Fund Act resources) will not be converted to non-recreational use.
- **Soil Stockpiles Pose No Unacceptable Risk (as managed):** The DTSC concurred (Dec 17, 2009) that the soil stockpiles pose no risk to human health, provided Caltrans maintains access restrictions, fencing, vegetative cover, and groundwater monitoring.
- **Groundwater Meets Standards:** Groundwater from monitoring wells meets all primary and secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for drinking water. Stormwater runoff is not a chronic exposure pathway.
- **Relocation Policy:** Displaced persons shall not suffer unnecessarily as a result of the public project, and relocation benefits will not adversely affect Social Security, welfare eligibility, or income taxes.

### Regulatory Decisions

- **SHPO Concurrence (May 16, 2012):** The State Historic Preservation Officer concurred that two properties (3530 Maze Boulevard and 416/418 I Street) are eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.
- **DTSC Concurrence:** Multiple DTSC letters (2007, 2009, 2012) provided determinations on site risk and management conditions.
- **Interagency Agreement (June 22, 2012):** Caltrans, DTSC, and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) entered an agreement for site characterization, risk evaluation, and remediation.
- **Remedial Action Plan (Preferred Alternative):** The Feasibility Study evaluated five alternatives. The preferred remedy — **Alternative 4 (Containment)** — involves encapsulating the stockpiles beneath the SR-132 roadway as embankment material, behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, or under a synthetic liner/clean soil cap in medians. Interim progress (by 2018) includes grading and capping northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 with 6–12 inches of vegetated clean soil. Ultimate build-out (by 2028) will complete encapsulation; Stockpile 3 will be consolidated into a fill zone. **Alternative 3 (removal/offsite disposal)** was rejected due to cost (~$21.5 million), logistics (~175 truckloads/day for 30 days), and lack of funding. **Alternative 2 (institutional controls)** maintains existing fencing, mowing, and quarterly groundwater monitoring (~$50,000/year). A RAP was to be published for public comment.
- **Appeal Rights:** Displaced persons have the right to appeal any Caltrans decision regarding eligibility or payment amounts, with judicial review available after agency determination.

### Analytical Results

- **Section 4(f) Determination:** Seven resources evaluated within 0.5 miles of the project; no permanent or temporary use found for historic properties (3530 Maze Boulevard, 416/418 I Street), parks (Charles M. Sharp, J.M. Pike, Cesar E. Chavez, Mellis), or the Virginia Corridor Trailway.
- **Soil Barium:** Elevated barium concentrations were found in stockpiles (maximum 4,300 mg/kg in 2012 testing; previous samples exceeded commercial/industrial screening levels up to 130,000 mg/kg). Barium is the primary contaminant of potential concern (COPC).
- **Soil Cadmium:** Exceeded commercial/industrial screening levels in some stockpiles per 2004 data but was not detected in elevated areas during a 2012 follow-up investigation, deemed false positives.
- **Stormwater Barium:** One sample (SW03 from Stockpile 3) showed elevated barium (2,000 µg/L, exceeding the 1,000 µg/L MCL), but this was isolated and confined to the Caltrans right-of-way.
- **Groundwater:** Groundwater flow is toward the east-southeast at a gradient of ~0.0005, with a depth of approximately 31.7–40.1 ft below top of casing (2013 data). All water quality samples met MCLs. Fenceline and perimeter borings (2012) showed no lateral/vertical migration of metals.
- **Stockpile 3 Excavation:** ~2,800 yd³ of soil removed in 2012 was designated non-hazardous; air monitoring during removal showed lead and barium levels "well below levels of concern."

### Remedial Actions

- **Relocation Assistance Program (Administrative Remedy):** Caltrans provides advisory services and payments to displaced persons, including moving cost reimbursement, replacement housing payments (price/rent differential, down payment assistance), and reestablishment expenses (up to $25,000 for businesses). Claims must be filed within 18 months of vacating.
- **Stockpile Remediation (Planned):** The Feasibility Study (FS) evaluated alternatives for containing the barium-contaminated soil stockpiles (Stockpile #1: ~34,000 yd³; #2: ~102,000 yd³; #3: ~24,000 yd³). The planned remedy is to use the soil as embankment material for the SR-132 roadway construction. A Remedial Action Plan (RAP) will be published for public comment.
- **Stockpile 3 Removal Action (Completed):** In September 2012, soil from Stockpile 3 was excavated and transported to a Class II landfill under non-hazardous waste manifests, with active dust suppression and air monitoring.
- **Debris Removal:** Existing debris (asphalt, metal, brick) within stockpiles must be removed during highway construction excavations.
- **Construction Conditions:** Final unpaved slopes must be 2:1 or flatter with approved erosion protection. For remedial work execution, specific mitigation measures are required: **truck tire trackout** is controlled by placement of rock for rough tire cleaning per trip; **truck decontamination** requires daily washout and operation/maintenance of the decontamination station.

### Responsible Parties

- **California Department of Transportation (Caltrans):** Owner/operator of the project and soil stockpiles. Responsible for providing relocation advisory services, computing payments (including "Last Resort Housing" options), ensuring non-discrimination, and managing the site per DTSC conditions.
- **Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC):** Oversight agency for stockpile remediation, co-regulator under the Interagency Agreement (IA).
- **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB):** Co-regulator for stockpile remediation under the 2012 IA.
- **State Historic Preservation Officer (SHPO):** Concurring authority on historic property eligibility determinations.
- **Stanislaus Council of Governments (StanCOG):** Collaborating agency for the SR-132 project.
- **City of Modesto:** Owner/maintainer of all evaluated Section 4(f) parks and the Virginia Corridor Trailway.
- **Consultants:** Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Feasibility Study, Stormwater Monitoring); Shaw Environmental, Inc. (Historical PSI, SI, HHRA); Kleinfelder (Geotechnical Investigation).

### Key Terms and Conditions

- **Relocation Eligibility:** 90+ days of occupancy before initiation of negotiations is required for full replacement housing payment (RHP) benefits. Displacees must be offered comparable, decent, safe, and sanitary (DS&S) replacement dwellings.
- **Relocation Payment Caps & Deadlines:** Moving costs based on actuals or a fixed schedule; reestablishment expenses capped at $25,000. Claims must be filed within 18 months of vacating or payment of acquisition cost.
- **Last Resort Housing:** Caltrans may pay above statutory limits ($7,200 down payment; $31,000 RHP total) to make housing affordable if adequate housing is unavailable locally.
- **Soil Geotechnical Adequacy:** Stockpile soil is "geotechnically adequate" to support planned highway improvements, including up to 20 ft of additional fill and retaining walls.
- **Project Timeline:** The SR-132 project includes planned interim progress by 2018 and ultimate build-out by 2028.
- **Non-Discrimination:** All services are administered without regard to race, color, national origin, sex, or religion under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968.
