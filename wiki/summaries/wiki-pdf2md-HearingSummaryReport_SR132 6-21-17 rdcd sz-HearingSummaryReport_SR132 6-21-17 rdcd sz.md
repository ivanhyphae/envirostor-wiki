The State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, led by Caltrans in cooperation with the Stanislaus Council of Governments (StanCOG), aims to construct a four-lane freeway/expressway in Modesto, Stanislaus County, to improve traffic flow and relieve congestion. A key aspect of this project involves addressing approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil stockpiles contaminated with barium, lead, and strontium, located south of Kansas Avenue. These stockpiles, originating from soil acquired from the Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) property in the 1960s, are the subject of a Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP).

A public hearing was held on February 22, 2017, with a public comment period from January 18 to March 17, 2017, for the Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and the Draft Final RAP. Caltrans, in collaboration with the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), proposes to contain these contaminated stockpiles by encapsulating them within the new freeway construction—specifically, behind retaining walls, within bridge abutments, beneath highway pavement, and covered with engineered clean soil in unpaved areas. This containment strategy is viewed by Caltrans and regulatory agencies as protective of human health, feasible, cost-effective, and designed to minimize contaminant migration, with preliminary human health risk assessments indicating no unacceptable risk under current management. Construction for Phase 1 is projected to begin in 2018.

Despite the agencies' assurances, extensive public concerns were raised during the hearing and comment period. Many residents, citing a long history of contamination and potential health issues (including cancers), strongly advocated for the complete removal of the contaminated soil rather than mere encapsulation, fearing dormant toxins could become airborne during construction or leach into drainage systems. Doubts were also expressed regarding the efficacy of soil sampling tests. Beyond environmental and health impacts, significant opposition to "build" alternatives emerged, with some favoring a "No-Build" option due to concerns about farmland loss, project costs, and community disruption. Business owners feared negative economic impacts from road closures and altered traffic patterns, while property owners expressed frustration over unclear communication regarding property acquisition. Caltrans and DTSC have been designated as points of contact for submitting concerns, and all collected public comments will be reviewed and responded to in the Final EIR/EA and Final RAP before a final decision on the project and RAP is made.
