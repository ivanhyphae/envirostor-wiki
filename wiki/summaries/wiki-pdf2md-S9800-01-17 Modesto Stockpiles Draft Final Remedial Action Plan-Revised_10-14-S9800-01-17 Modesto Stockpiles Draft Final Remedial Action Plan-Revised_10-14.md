The Modesto Soil Stockpiles Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) outlines a "Containment" strategy for approximately 160,000 cubic yards of barium-contaminated soil from the SR-99/SR-132 interchange construction. Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and with oversight from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), the plan addresses human health risk assessments concluding that, with ongoing site management, the stockpiles do not pose an unacceptable risk to Caltrans workers, trespassers, or nearby residents.

The recommended remedial action, known as Alternative 4, integrates the soil stockpiles into the new SR-132/SR-99 interchange as fill. This involves encapsulating the soil behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement to prevent exposure, stormwater infiltration, and erosion. An interim phase (by 2018) will see a two-lane roadway built over parts of Stockpiles 1 and 2, with Stockpile 3 fully contained within the eastern abutment of the SR-132 bridge. The final build-out (by 2029) will complete the integration of all stockpiles into the interchange foundation. This approach is deemed protective of human health and the environment, technically feasible, and cost-effective at an estimated \$1,570,000, significantly less than removal and offsite disposal.

Regulatory compliance is critical, with remediation activities adhering to California's Porter-Cologne Water Quality Control Act, requiring stormwater and groundwater monitoring. DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board will review and concur with the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP) and Operation and Maintenance Plan (OMP) prior to implementation. Caltrans, as the owner/operator, is responsible for managing the site, preparing the RDIP and OMP, and leading the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) process. A Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded to restrict the site to transportation uses, prohibiting residential, school, daycare, and hospital facilities due to contaminant concentrations exceeding residential screening levels. DTSC endorsed the inclusion of an Administrative Record and a "Statement of Reasons" within the RAP. Additionally, a "Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility" (NBAR) was included, though it notes that Caltrans has assumed full responsibility for remediation and ongoing costs given the repeal of arbitration provisions supporting NBARs.
