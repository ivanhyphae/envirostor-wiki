Stantec Consulting Services Inc. conducted a surface water sampling event on March 10, 2023, for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles in Stanislaus County, under Contract No. 06A2542, Task Order No. 97. The event involved sampling at four revised locations (BG1, BG3, PL6, PL7) due to ongoing construction, replacing previous sites, and included an inspection of the SR 132 road embankment.

Field observations noted minimal rain, ponding at BG1 and BG3, active flow at PL6, and trash obstructing PL7. Site conditions varied, with straw waddles and netting present, minimal vegetation on Stockpile 1 East's northern slope, and general trash. Field measurements for temperature, pH, conductivity, turbidity, ORP, and dissolved oxygen were recorded.

Laboratory analysis by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) and CALTEST ANALYTICAL LABORATORY, on samples received on March 14, 2023, covered dissolved metals (including arsenic, barium, cadmium, chromium, copper, lead, molybdenum, selenium, vanadium, zinc, strontium, and mercury), total suspended solids, nitrate as nitrogen, and dissolved sulfide. While several analytes were detected, none of the metal or general mineral results exceeded Primary or Secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for drinking water, although the Method Detection Limit (MDL) for antimony (8.8 µg/L) was above its Primary MCL (6.0 µg/L).

Several quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) issues were identified, leading to data qualification. Nitrate (as N) samples for all locations exceeded holding times, resulting in a potential low bias qualification (J-). Dissolved mercury results were estimated ("J" qualified) below the Practical Quantitation Limit (PQL) but above the MDL. Additionally, various metals, including barium, beryllium, cadmium, chromium, copper, molybdenum, nickel, vanadium, and zinc, were detected in method blanks, leading to further qualifications (U, UJ, J+). Despite these qualifications, Stantec concluded that the data were usable for their intended purpose, achieving a 90% data completeness goal, with no surface water discharge or runoff observed beyond Best Management Practices (BMPs). The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) approved the sampling protocols and received the report, respectively.
