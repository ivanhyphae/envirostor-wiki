### Combined Summary: Draft Final Remedial Action Plan, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles

**Document Identity**
This document is the Revised Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP), dated October 2014, for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles in Stanislaus County, California. Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans, the RAP evaluates and selects a remedy for approximately 160,000 cubic yards of contaminated fill material stockpiled along the proposed SR-132 corridor.

**Key Findings & Risk Assessment**
Extensive investigations (2003-2013) identified **barium** (in a low-solubility barite form) as the primary contaminant of concern, along with elevated **strontium** and **lead**. Human Health Risk Assessments (2007, 2013) concluded that the stockpiles pose no unacceptable risk to current trespassers, offsite residents, or future construction workers, provided that existing institutional controls (fencing, vegetative cover, access restrictions) are maintained. Groundwater monitoring confirmed that contaminants remain below primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for drinking water.

**Regulatory Oversight & Selected Remedy**
The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) is the lead oversight agency, with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) as co-regulator. The selected remedy is **Alternative 4 – Containment**. This remedy involves encapsulating all stockpile material beneath the planned SR-132 roadway pavement, retaining walls, bridge abutments, and an engineered clean soil cap (6–12 inches over northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2). The DTSC and CVRWQCB have approved this remedy, which is approximately 20 times less expensive than the removal alternative (~$21.5 million).

**Responsible Parties**
- **Caltrans:** Site owner, project proponent, and responsible party for implementing the remedy and all associated costs.
- **DTSC & CVRWQCB:** Lead and co-regulatory oversight agencies, respectively.
- **Geocon Consultants, Inc.:** Author of the RAP.

**Key Terms & Conditions**
- **Institutional Controls:** A Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded, restricting the property to transportation use and prohibiting residential, school, daycare, or hospital uses.
- **Implementation:** The soil will be incorporated into SR-132 construction within the Caltrans right-of-way; no off-site transportation is planned. An interim cap will be placed on northern stockpile areas prior to ultimate build-out (by 2028).
- **Monitoring & Reporting:** Quarterly groundwater monitoring, annual engineering cover inspections, and an Operation and Maintenance Plan (OMP) are required. A Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) will detail construction requirements, including a Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) and Health and Safety Plan (HSP).
- **Public Participation:** A 30-day public comment period was anticipated for summer 2014.
- **CEQA Compliance:** The RAP supplements the Draft Environmental Impact Report (EIR) for the SR-132 Project.
