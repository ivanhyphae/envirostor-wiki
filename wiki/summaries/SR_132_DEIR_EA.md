**Summary of the Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (DEIR/EA) for the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project**

This document is a Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (DEIR/EA) for the State Route 132 (SR 132) West Freeway/Expressway project in Stanislaus County, prepared by Caltrans (District 10) as the lead agency under both CEQA and NEPA. The project addresses two primary issues: improving a congested, two-lane highway and remediating three large, contaminated soil stockpiles from the 1960s (contaminated with barium, strontium, and lead).

**Proposed Action and Regulatory Oversight**
A phased approach is proposed: Phase 1 (est. 2018-2020) for a two-lane expressway, and Phase 2 (est. 2026-2028) for a four-lane freeway. Two operationally identical build alternatives are analyzed, differing only in SR 99 ramp configurations. The **Containment Alternative** was selected as the recommended remedial action for the soil stockpiles, permanently capping the contaminated soil within new highway structures under a Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP). The **California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)** and **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)** are the lead oversight and approval agencies for the RAP. The project received concurrence from U.S. EPA Region 9 and FHWA that it is not a project of air quality concern.

**Analytical Results Across Environmental Factors**
- **Traffic & Transportation:** Build alternatives would significantly improve traffic flow, e.g., the SR 132/Carpenter Road intersection improving from LOS F to LOS C by 2028.
- **Air Quality & Noise:** The project is not considered a project of air quality concern. CO concentrations would remain well below standards, but construction NOx emissions would exceed local thresholds. Noise analysis identified 260-276 impacted receivers under build alternatives (vs. 162 under No-Build). Proposed noise barriers would mitigate impacts.
- **Environmental Justice & Community:** The study area includes disproportionately high minority and low-income populations. The No-Build Alternative would have a disproportionate adverse impact by failing to improve circulation, while build alternatives would cause disproportionately high and adverse noise, visual, and relocation impacts, mitigated by compensation and aesthetic treatments.
- **Land Use & Farmlands:** Both build alternatives would convert 64.8 acres of prime/unique farmland and impact 3.51 acres under Williamson Act contracts, deemed minimal at a countywide scale (0.016% decrease).
- **Biological Resources:** Special-status species with potential to occur include burrowing owl and Swainson's hawk, though the project affects only marginal habitat. Biological surveys documented 0.65 acres of seasonal wetlands. Up to 591 trees may be impacted.
- **Hazardous Materials:** Stormwater and groundwater monitoring (2015-2016) found no exceedances of Maximum Contaminant Levels for dissolved metals in groundwater, though barium was elevated in one stormwater sample. The containment remedy is designed to protect water with ongoing monitoring. A land use covenant will restrict uses on areas of barium-contaminated fill.

**Responsible Parties and Key Conditions**
- **Caltrans** is the lead agency and owner/operator of the stockpiles.
- **DTSC and CVRWQCB** must approve the Final RAP before or concurrent with project approval.
- **Construction Conditions:** Caltrans must implement mitigation measures including preconstruction surveys and buffers for burrowing owl and Swainson's hawk (under CDFW oversight), use of certified weed-free erosion control materials, and compliance with SJVAPCD dust control and NOx reduction rules.
- **Alternative 2** is identified as the environmentally superior build alternative under NEPA, although the No-Build Alternative is superior under CEQA.
