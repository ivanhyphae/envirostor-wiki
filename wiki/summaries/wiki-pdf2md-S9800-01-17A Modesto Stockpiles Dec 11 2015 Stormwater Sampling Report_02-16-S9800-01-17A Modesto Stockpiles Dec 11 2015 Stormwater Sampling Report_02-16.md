On December 11, 2015, Geocon conducted stormwater sampling at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles in Stanislaus County, under Contract No. 06A1895, Task Order No. 17, with DTSC approval. This sampling was a continuation of Caltrans' ongoing management, prompted by a 2006 Shaw report noting an elevated barium concentration (2,000 µg/l) in one sample. Geocon collected surface water samples from puddles and drainage basin outfalls (PL1, PL2, PL3, PL4); other locations were dry. Samples were analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories, Inc. (ATL) for dissolved metals (including antimony, arsenic, barium, chromium, copper, lead, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, strontium, vanadium, and zinc) and general minerals (including chloride, nitrate, sulfate, sulfide, and alkalinity), with field parameters also measured.

Analytical results for dissolved metals varied, with non-detects for beryllium, cadmium, mercury, selenium, silver, and thallium. Barium ranged from 10 to 190 µg/L, and manganese was consistently detected (10 to 290 µg/L), exceeding secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) in samples PL2, PL3, and PL4 due to taste, odor, or welfare considerations, though no primary MCL exceedances were observed. Arsenic was found in PL3 and lead in PL4. General mineral concentrations, including total suspended solids (12 to 670 mg/L) and total dissolved solids (<10 to 240 mg/L), were within historical ranges. The report confirmed consistency with previous data for both metal and mineral concentrations, and no concentrations exceeded drinking water MCLs or Secondary MCLs. Caltrans continues to manage surface water runoff from Stockpile 2 using vegetative covers, straw wattles, and a berm. Geocon's report, dated January 29, 2016, concluded that sampling confirmed data consistency and noted Caltrans' plan for additional surface water sampling during the 2016 rainy season.
