This technical memorandum, prepared by Geocon Project Manager John E. Juhrend for WSP/City of Modesto, details the verification testing for Barium-Containing Soil (BCS) removal at former Stockpile 3 within the State Route 132 right-of-way, aiming to confirm its suitability as clean embankment fill. Approximately 38,440 cubic yards of BCS material were excavated and relocated. Initial soil sampling between February 28 and March 12, 2020, revealed elevated barium concentrations (up to 7,000 mg/kg) in three areas, leading to further excavation and resampling. Resampling successfully reduced barium levels to 160-220 mg/kg, and all final excavation soil samples (ranging from 46 to 970 mg/kg barium and 1.6 to 40 mg/kg lead) met the specified verification thresholds.

Analytical results from Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) for various soil samples collected on March 6 and March 11, 2020, consistently showed barium concentrations meeting verification thresholds, though one sample had a barium concentration of 970 mg/kg. Lead concentrations remained low across all samples. Quality control (QC) data for barium and lead, analyzed using EPA Test Method 6010B, generally showed acceptable recoveries in Laboratory Control Samples (LCS). However, some Matrix Spike (MS) and Matrix Spike Duplicate (MSD) recoveries for barium were outside acceptable ranges, attributed to probable matrix interference, while lead recoveries were within limits. The document includes definitions of analytical terms and details on laboratory procedures, reporting standards, and certifications. A chain of custody record outlines sample handling for various analyses, including Volatiles, GRO/DRO, Organochlorine Pesticides, PCBs, Semi-volatiles, and Title 22 Metals. Despite some matrix interference flags in the QC data, the overall analytical batch was validated by the LCS, confirming the reliability and accuracy of the methods used for characterization of soils from SR132 Stockpile 3.
