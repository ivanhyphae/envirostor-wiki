This memo from Geocon's Project Manager, John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG, to Rick Demi, PE, Construction Manager at WSP/City of Modesto, dated February 27, 2020, details soil characterization testing of a proposed clean cap borrow area. The purpose is to determine if the area, located adjacent to Carpenter Road within the State Route 132 right-of-way, is suitable for generating clean cover fill to cap existing Stockpiles 1 and 2, which contain barium-contaminated soil (BCS). This testing was conducted following DTSC's 2001 Information Advisory Clean Imported Fill Soil guidelines.

Twelve direct-push borings (B1-B12) were completed to a maximum depth of 5 feet. All composite samples were analyzed for Title 22 metals (EPA Methods 6010B and 7471A for mercury), with the 0-2 feet composites also analyzed for organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) (EPA Method 8081A). The analysis of the composite soil samples consistently showed that most Title 22 metals were either not detected (ND) or present at very low concentrations. For instance, Mercury was consistently ND at a PQL of 0.10 mg/kg across all tested samples. Organochlorine pesticides were also consistently ND across all analyzed samples, with varying PQLs. Specific metal concentrations ranged as follows: Arsenic 1.0-1.5 mg/kg (background 1.2 mg/kg), Barium 60-110 mg/kg (background 72.8 mg/kg), Beryllium 1.3-1.6 mg/kg (background 0.2 mg/kg), Chromium 7.5-11 mg/kg (background 7.1 mg/kg), Cobalt 3.2-4.9 mg/kg (background 4.4 mg/kg), and Copper 5.3-9.9 mg/kg (background 7.5 mg/kg). Other metals like Antimony, Cadmium, Molybdenum, Selenium, and Thallium were generally ND. While a "2005 Residential Screening value" is mentioned, its direct applicability to the Borrow Area in this context is noted as "inapplicable" or "information only."

Geocon Consultants, Inc. issued a technical memo on February 26, 2020, to John Juhrend detailing analytical results and quality control data for the Composite B10,B11,B12 0-2 and 2-5 samples from the SR132 Clean Borrow Area (Project Number S9108-01-01). Quality control (QC) data for Mercury (EPA 7471A), Title 22 Metals (EPA 6010B), and Organochlorine Pesticides (EPA 8081A) were included. For Mercury, a post-spike recovery of 122% exceeded the 85-115% limit, indicating a matrix effect. For Title 22 Metals, blank samples showed no detections, LCS recoveries were within 80-120%, and RPDs for duplicates were within 20%. Organochlorine pesticide QC also showed acceptable performance, with LCS recoveries typically within limits and blank samples being ND for all analytes.

A series of Chain of Custody (COC) records from February 2020 document the transfer of soil samples collected by Geocon Consultants, Inc. to ADVANCED A TECHNOLOGY LABORATORIES (ATL) for various analyses. A technical memo dated February 26, 2020, addresses Quality Control (QC) for Organochlorine Pesticides by EPA Method 8081A (Batch B0B0435 - GCSEMI_PCB/PEST_S) performed by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for John Juhrend. While many analytes (e.g., Endosulfan I, Endrin, gamma-BHC, Heptachlor) showed %Recovery within specified limits, Methoxychlor in both MS and MSD had %Rec values (45.2% and 42.7% respectively, and 43.0% and 42.5% for MSD duplicates) that were outside the acceptance limit. Despite the Methoxychlor anomaly, the analytical batch was validated by a laboratory control sample, and all reported RPD values were within the 20% limit.
