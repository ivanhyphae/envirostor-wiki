This Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Update, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. (and PES Environmental, Inc. for the second summary) for Caltrans in March 2013, addresses the potential health risks associated with three large soil stockpiles (approx. 160,000 cubic yards) located along State Route 99 in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. These stockpiles, originating from excavations in the early 1960s from former industrial evaporation ponds, contain soil potentially contaminated with various metals. The update, identified as Rev.0313 for Project S9525-06-44, incorporates recent 2012 soil and groundwater data and responds to a February 2013 DTSC review, aiming to support a Remedial Action Plan for managing these stockpiles.

The primary contaminant of concern (COC) is barium, accompanied by other metals (e.g., lead, chromium, copper, zinc, arsenic) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in soil. While elevated concentrations are noted deeper than one foot, shallow groundwater generally meets drinking water standards. The HHRA evaluated potential risks to various receptors, including current off-site residents/trespassers, future construction workers, future off-site residents during construction, and hypothetical future shallow groundwater users. Exposure pathways considered include incidental ingestion, dermal contact, and inhalation of soil particulates.

A key finding, reaffirmed by the DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO), is that the original 2007 HHRA remains valid. The 2012 data did not significantly alter the risk estimates; under current management, cumulative cancer risks are generally below one-in-a-million and Hazard Indices (HI) below one, indicating insignificant risks and hazards. However, the assessment notes that if the stockpiles undergo substantial grading, reworking, or removal, further evaluation of deeper soil concentrations will be required due to potential new exposure pathways for nearby residents.

Specific findings for different exposure scenarios include:
*   **Current Off-site Resident/Trespasser:** Cancer risks ranged from 8x10^-8 to 4x10^-7 and HIs from 0.009 to 0.4 across the stockpiles.
*   **Future Construction Worker (0-20 ft bgs soil):** Cumulative excess lifetime cancer risk is 9.2x10^-7 (below the 1x10^-6 target) and cumulative noncancer HI is 0.4 (below the target of 1). Lead in soil (54 mg/kg UCL) was not considered an unacceptable hazard. Air modeling for future construction estimated particulate emissions for dump trucks at 2.58E+06 grams with an average rate of 1.23E-05 g/m²-s, and 2.53E-07 g/m²-s for excavators.
*   **Future Off-site Resident (from construction dust):** Excess lifetime cancer risk is 6x10^-10 and noncancer HI is 0.017, both well below regulatory thresholds.
*   **Hypothetical Future Groundwater User:** Cumulative noncancer hazard estimate is 0.9 (below 1), with lead concentrations (3.4 µg/L) below federal action levels.

The assessment acknowledges conservative assumptions, such as using maximum detected concentrations as Exposure Point Concentrations (except for lead), which likely result in plausible upper-bound risk estimates, suggesting true risks are potentially lower. Lead risk assessments, using EPA and DTSC guidelines, established varying blood lead levels and Preliminary Remediation Goals (PRGs) for children with pica behavior versus adults. While the DTSC concluded the stockpiles pose no current cancer or non-cancer risk, including from transient off-site migration like wind-blown dust, any future significant disturbance necessitates further evaluation.
