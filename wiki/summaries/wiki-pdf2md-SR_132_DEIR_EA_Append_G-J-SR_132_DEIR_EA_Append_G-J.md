This document, derived from Appendix G-J of the SR 132 DEIR/EA (June 2014) and the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (October 2014) prepared by GEOCON CONSULTANTS, INC., details the Final Feasibility Study and Remedial Action Plan for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remediation Project. The project aims to address elevated barium concentrations in stockpiled soil, though investigations from 2003-2013 consistently show that groundwater is not impacted and the stockpiles, *as currently managed*, do not pose an unacceptable risk to human health, a finding concurred upon by DTSC.

The study rigorously evaluates four remedial alternatives:
1.  **No Action**: Least protective and unacceptable to regulators.
2.  **Institutional Controls**: Involves ongoing management, fencing, and monitoring. Considered short-term protective but doesn't reduce contaminant toxicity or mobility.
3.  **Removal**: Complete excavation, offsite disposal, and replacement with clean fill, with costs estimated from $2.6 million for specific quantities to $21.5 million for full removal. This option, though highly protective, is prohibitively expensive, creates substantial greenhouse gas emissions (529,200 lbs CO2), and poses significant logistic challenges and short-term impacts.
4.  **Containment**: Capping the stockpiles, either beneath planned SR-132 project infrastructure (roadways, retaining walls, bridge abutments) or with a vegetated clean soil layer. This approach, estimated at $1.57 million, eliminates exposure pathways, reduces stormwater infiltration, and is highly implementable and acceptable to regulators and the community.

The recommended **Alternative 4 (Containment)** involves integrating the approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpiled soil into the SR-132 Project construction by containing it within project components and capping other areas with clean soil. This aligns with regulatory policies, is significantly more cost-effective (approximately 20 times less than full removal), and provides long-term protection of human health and the environment by eliminating exposure routes and minimizing contaminant mobility. The plan requires DTSC approval, ongoing oversight, and includes an interim phase for partial containment by 2018, with ultimate build-out completion projected by 2029. Supporting environmental studies also addressed endangered species, air quality, waters, farmland, and historic properties, further informing the selection of this recommended alternative.
