A groundwater monitoring report by Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Project S9800-01-17, S9525-01-44C) detailed findings from the Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles site in December 2013, with monitoring conducted between December 11-18, 2013. Groundwater flow was consistently southeast at a gradient of 0.001, with depths ranging from 33.46 to 42.46 feet below the top of the casing. Samples were collected from ten wells (MW-1 through MW-10) following strict QA/QC protocols, including filtration for dissolved metals, chilling, and chain-of-custody procedures. Purged water was disposed of properly.

Analysis revealed consistent exceedances of California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for nitrates (as nitrogen) in wells MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10, with concentrations up to 25 mg/L. Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) also frequently exceeded the 500 mg/L MCL in MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10, reaching concentrations as high as 981 mg/L. Manganese sporadically exceeded its Secondary MCL, with a notable detection of 140 µg/L in MW-6.

Other detected analytes included a range of dissolved metals and general minerals, with most below thresholds, though lead was detected at 1.3 µg/L in MW-6. Chloride concentrations ranged from ND to 53.8290 mg/L, Sulfate from ND to 70 mg/L, and various alkalinity parameters were noted. Historical data indicates that nitrates and TDS are consistent exceedance issues, while PAHs have been consistently non-detected since November 2012.

Quality Control (QC) data indicated some deviations, particularly with matrix spike recoveries for Chloride, Nitrate as N, Total Sulfide, and certain dissolved metals (e.g., Barium, Calcium, Magnesium, Nickel) due to matrix interference. However, overall analytical batch validity was maintained through acceptable laboratory control sample recoveries and duplicate RPDs. The California Environmental Protection Agency, Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) oversees this ongoing monitoring initiative.
