The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP), developed by Geocon Consultants, Inc. in January 2019, outlines the interim phase 1 remediation of barium and aerially deposited lead (ADL)-contaminated soils within the SR-132 West Project area near Modesto, California. This plan, approved by the DTSC, utilizes "Alternative 4 - Containment," consolidating contaminated soils—defined by lead concentrations exceeding 80 mg/kg total or 5 mg/L WET-soluble lead, and barium concentrations above a 1,000 mg/kg post-excavation confirmation level—into highway embankment fill. These contained soils will be capped with structural pavement, clean fill, or vegetated soil, ensuring placement at least five feet above the highest groundwater elevation.

This remedial approach aligns with a June 2016 Agreement and a DTSC Variance (V09HQSCD006) that permit Caltrans to reuse ADL-contaminated soil as fill material in projects under specific conditions, deeming such management protective of human health and the environment based on prior risk assessments. The Modesto RDIP specifically details the consolidation and capping of barium- and ADL-contaminated soils from three stockpiles, originating from a former barium processing operation, into designated zones within Stockpiles 1 and 2.

A comprehensive Health and Safety Plan (HASP) by Geocon (April 2018) is a critical component of the RDIP, addressing potential hazards during remediation activities. It mandates Level D personal protective equipment (PPE) for most tasks, with specifics like disposable gloves for soil sampling and emphasizes the use of air-purifying respirators with P100 filters when handling dry, dusty lead-containing materials. The HASP outlines rigorous safety protocols, including air monitoring, personal hygiene, buddy systems, and compliance with Cal/OSHA regulations. Historical air monitoring data from 2012 indicated effective dust control, keeping airborne lead and barium levels below risk thresholds. While acknowledging other contaminants like arsenic, molybdenum, and nickel, the plan also sets a specific Cal/OSHA PEL-TWA of 10 mg/m³ for barium sulfate inhalation, with a 4 mg/m³ trigger for total dust monitoring.

Post-construction, the plan requires verification sampling, monthly progress reports, annual groundwater and periodic stormwater monitoring, and annual containment inspections. A Remedial Action Completion Report (RACR) will be submitted, and a Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded with Stanislaus County to restrict future land use. Caltrans is the responsible party for implementation, with regulatory oversight from DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). An additional RDIP is planned for Ultimate Phase 2 in 2028.
