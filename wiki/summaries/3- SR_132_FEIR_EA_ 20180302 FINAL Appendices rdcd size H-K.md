## Document Summary: SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project – Final EIR/EA Appendices (H-K)

**Document Identity:** This document comprises Appendices H-K of the Final Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (FEIR/EA) and Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the **SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project** in Stanislaus County. Prepared by **Caltrans District 6** and Geocon Consultants, Inc., the document spans public circulation periods in 2017, with supporting data and correspondence from 2010-2018. It serves as a **Response to Comments** appendix addressing public comments from March 2017 and contains the Draft Final RAP.

**Key Claims:** The central claim is that the **Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles** (approximately 160,000 cubic yards of barium-contaminated soil) do not pose an unacceptable cancer risk or noncancer hazard to current offsite residents, trespassers, or Caltrans workers, so long as the stockpiles remain properly managed (fenced, vegetated, access-limited). The **Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)** concurred with this assessment in April 2013 and again during the review process. Analysis of over 440 soil samples detected contaminants like arsenic, barium, and nickel, but maximum surface concentrations were found to be within local background ranges or below U.S. EPA safe levels. Critically, no significant migration of contaminants from the stockpiles into groundwater has been detected, and groundwater meets all drinking water standards.

**Regulatory Decisions:** The primary regulatory decision is the selection of **Alternative 4 – Containment** as the recommended remedial action over a full removal alternative. This involves using the stockpile soil as embankment fill within the SR-132/SR-99 interchange, capped with roadway pavement, retaining walls, bridge abutments, and clean soil cover over temporary areas. DTSC requires an **Operation and Maintenance Agreement** and an **Operation and Maintenance Plan** mandating annual inspections, continued groundwater monitoring, **5-year remedy evaluations** by DTSC, a land use covenant, and dedicated financial assurance. **Alternative 2** was identified as the preferred project alternative for its balance of environmental feasibility and cost. FHWA confirmed the project conforms with the State Implementation Plan (June 5, 2017), and SHPO provided concurrence on historic property determinations (March 16, 2015).

**Analytical Results:** The primary contaminant of concern is **barium** (as barite), with maximum detected concentrations in Stockpile 2 (64,000 mg/kg) and Stockpile 3 (72,000 mg/kg) below commercial/industrial screening levels. Groundwater monitoring from eight wells (2006-present) shows no exceedances of primary drinking water maximum contaminant levels (MCLs) for any constituents, and no carcinogenic chemicals were detected in groundwater. Stormwater sampling occasionally exceeded MCLs for barium (2,000 µg/L at Stockpile 3) but was generally within background levels. Other detected contaminants (lead, arsenic, PAHs, strontium) were found at levels below applicable screening thresholds.

**Remedial Actions:** Under the recommended **Containment remedy (Alternative 4)** , approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil will be consolidated and used as embankment fill within the SR-132/SR-99 interchange. The containment will be behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement, with temporary 6-12 inch clean soil caps over northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 during the interim phase (2018). Current site management includes perimeter fencing, vegetative cover (mowed annually), limited access, surface water/groundwater monitoring, and erosion control measures. A previous removal action (2012) excavated and disposed of ~2,800 cubic yards of Stockpile 3 soil offsite to a Class II landfill during SR-99 off-ramp rehabilitation.

**Regulatory Framework and Conditions:** The document establishes clear conditions for the project. Construction will require dust suppression and air monitoring, with perimeter air quality monitoring and a permit application to the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District (Rule 9510). The project will be built in two phases, with Phase 1 anticipated from **2018–2020** and Phase 2 from **2026–2028**. Key conditions include compliance with the Uniform Relocation Assistance Act for property acquisitions, coordination with the Modesto Irrigation District during final design, implementation of noise abatement (Noise Barrier D) for Area 4, and compliance with the California Manual of Uniform Control Devices for destination signage. Direct property impacts include revisions to parcel maps and noise analyses, such as the **Quality Inn** at 500 Kansas Avenue, which now requires only a partial acquisition of approximately 2,460 square feet, with a noise analysis showing a future increase of only 2 dBA (to 67 dBA), which is not considered noticeable.

**Responsible Parties:** Key parties include **Caltrans** (lead agency, owner/operator of stockpiles, assumes full responsibility for remediation and operation/maintenance costs), **DTSC** (regulatory oversight, prepared NBAR, oversight of remedial action plan), **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB)** (co-regulator for groundwater/surface water), **StanCOG** (co-lead agency, MPO), **FHWA** (project-level conformity determination), **USFWS** (Endangered Species Act consultation), and the **City of Modesto** (responsible for right-of-way acquisition and relocation assistance).
