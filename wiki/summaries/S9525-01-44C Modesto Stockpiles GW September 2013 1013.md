This document is a **groundwater monitoring report** prepared by **Geocon Consultants, Inc.** for the **Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles** site in Stanislaus County, California. It covers the **September 2013 sampling event**, part of a broader monitoring sequence from March 2012 to June 2013.

**Scope and Fieldwork:** All ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) were successfully sampled on **September 4–5, 2013**, though fieldwork was not without complications. Wells MW-3 and MW-4 temporarily went dry during purging, and several wells exhibited issues with silt, turbidity, and slow recharge. A minor field issue was also noted: the well box for MW-6 was found damaged (apparently from a lawnmower) and was replaced on September 23, 2013, though the casing was undamaged. Depth-to-groundwater measurements showed levels ranging from approximately 33.74 to 42.05 feet below the top of casing, with groundwater flow generally toward the southeast at a hydraulic gradient of about 0.0007. Stiff diagrams revealed two distinct groundwater types: calcium-dominant water at wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10, and sodium-dominant water at MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8.

**Analytical Results:** Samples were analyzed for dissolved metals (including primary COCs barium, lead, and strontium), general minerals, and nitrates, with reference to multiple regulatory thresholds (California EPA MCLs, Secondary MCLs, CA Dept. Public Health levels). Key findings include:

- **Barium, Lead, and Strontium (Primary COCs):** There were **no exceedances** of applicable thresholds. Barium (42–230 µg/L) and strontium (230–860 µg/L) were detected in all wells but remained well below their respective limits (1,000 µg/L MCL and 4,000 µg/L EPA Health Advisory). Lead was not detected above the reporting limit in any well.
- **Other Dissolved Metals (e.g., As, Cr, V):** Detected in all wells but remained below their respective MCLs or action levels.
- **Nitrate (as N) and Total Dissolved Solids (TDS):** **Consistent exceedances** were observed in the same five downgradient wells: MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10. The upgradient well (MW-1) also showed elevated levels (12 mg/L nitrate, 590 mg/L TDS), suggesting a possible influence from natural background conditions. The highest concentrations were in MW-5 (36 mg/L nitrate, 750 mg/L TDS).
- **Trends:** Concentrations for the primary COCs (barium, lead, strontium) remained stable and similar to historical levels. Analysis for PAHs was discontinued after November 2012 with DTSC concurrence due to a lack of detections.

**QA/QC and Oversight:** Analytical work was performed by **Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL)** , and the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)** also submitted split samples to Excelchem. QA/QC sample results (MS/MSD) for several analytes fell outside acceptance limits, but these were validated by the laboratory control sample and deemed of sufficient quality for interpretation. Data qualifiers included "U" (non-detect from equipment blank contamination) and "(2)" (laboratory error). Sampling was conducted under DTSC-approved protocols, and DTSC representatives observed the March 2012 event, collecting split samples that showed no notable differences between laboratories.

**Conclusions and Responsible Parties:** The report concludes that **no dissolved metals exceeded their water quality thresholds** for this event. However, nitrate and TDS continued to exceed standards, partly reflecting possible background conditions. The next monitoring event was scheduled for December 2013. The primary responsible parties were **Caltrans** (owner/operator), **Geocon Consultants, Inc.** (preparer/consultant), **DTSC** (lead regulatory oversight), and the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)** (co-regulator).
