This document compiles the "Modesto Stockpiles GW June 2013" Groundwater Monitoring Report (Geocon Project No. S9525-01-44B, Task Order No. 44) by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for Caltrans, detailing groundwater monitoring conducted on June 5th and 6th, 2013. The fieldwork occurred from June 5-12, 2013, with the report finalized between June 7 and June 13, 2013. The monitoring, approved by the DTSC and based on a 2006 work plan, involved sampling ten wells (MW-1 through MW-10) at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in Stanislaus County. Groundwater, found at approximately 30 feet below ground surface, consistently flowed east-southeast.

The groundwater samples underwent extensive analysis for parameters including Title 22 dissolved metals (e.g., Antimony, Arsenic, Barium, Chromium, Molybdenum, Nickel, Selenium, Vanadium), anions (Chloride, Nitrate as N, Sulfate), speciated alkalinity, Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), total Sulfide, and dissolved Mercury. While Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs) were initially analyzed, their analysis was discontinued after November 2012 due to non-detection. During the June 2013 sampling event, issues were encountered with wells MW-5 and MW-6 going dry, necessitating re-development before successful sample collection.

The results showed some analytes exceeding regulatory thresholds. Nitrates (as nitrogen) exceeded the 10 mg/L primary MCL in MW-2, MW-6, and MW-10 (up to 20 mg/L). TDS surpassed the 500 mg/L secondary MCL in MW-1, MW-2, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 (up to 620 mg/L). Dissolved metals, including barium and strontium, were generally detected but remained below their respective MCLs and similar to historical levels, with manganese showing sporadic, non-consistent elevations in past rounds.

Significant quality control issues were noted, particularly with Matrix Spike and Matrix Spike Duplicate recoveries for Alkalinity and Carbonate falling well below the 80-120% acceptable range. Several dissolved metals (Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Vanadium, Barium) also had recoveries outside the 70-130% limit, indicating potential analytical problems or matrix interferences. However, a separate Quality Control data report from Advanced Technology Laboratories for dissolved Mercury analysis (EPA 7470) showed acceptable recovery and RPD values for Matrix Spike, Matrix Spike Duplicate, and Post Spike samples. The overarching document, while providing detailed monitoring and QC data, lacked overall conclusions or recommendations.
