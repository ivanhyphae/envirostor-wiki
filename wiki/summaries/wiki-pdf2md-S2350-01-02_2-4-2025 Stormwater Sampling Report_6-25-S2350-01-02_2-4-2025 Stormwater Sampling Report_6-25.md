Geocon's Stormwater Sampling Report for Caltrans, dated February 12, 2025, details the findings from stormwater sampling at the Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles in Modesto, Stanislaus County, with a primary focus on the February 4, 2025, event and overall data spanning from December 19, 2023, to February 4, 2025. The sampling, conducted by ASSET Laboratories and received on February 5, 2025, followed protocols approved by the DTSC and CVRWQCB as outlined in the *Storm Water Sampling and Analysis Plan (SAP)*. Samples were collected from SW-East, SW-West, and Background (BG-West) locations.

The analysis, using methods like EPA 6010B, EPA 7470A, SM2540 C, and EPA 300.0, primarily focused on inorganic constituents. Key findings indicate that most contaminant levels in surface water samples were below applicable California Primary and Secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs). Specifically, Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) and sulfate concentrations (ranging from 63-210 mg/L and <2.0-22 mg/L, respectively) were well below their Secondary MCLs. Dissolved metals such as barium, copper, and zinc were also detected below their respective MCLs. While molybdenum, vanadium, and strontium were detected, no MCLs have been established for them. Notably, thallium had exceeded its Primary MCL in previous samples from December 2023. During the February 4, 2025, event, no discharge or runoff was observed from the Caltrans right-of-way.

While samples arrived in good condition with proper chain of custody and within acceptable holding times, some Quality Control (QC) issues were noted. The Relative Percent Difference (RPD) for molybdenum and silver in duplicate samples exceeded the 20% acceptance limit, leading to "R" qualifiers for these analytes. However, Laboratory Control Samples (LCS) validated the overall accuracy of the analytical batches. Ongoing stormwater sampling is required before stockpile encapsulation, and Caltrans plans to continue sampling during qualifying rain events in 2025.
