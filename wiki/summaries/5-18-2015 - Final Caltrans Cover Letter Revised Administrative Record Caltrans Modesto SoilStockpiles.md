## Document identity
- **Document type and purpose:** Revised cover letter transmitting three appendices (Administrative Record, Statement of Reasons, and Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility) for inclusion in the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site.
- **Author or preparer:** Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist, Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program, Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).
- **Date:** May 18, 2015. Period covered by referenced documents spans approximately 2004–2015.

## Key claims
- **DTSC (Adams):** The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, as currently managed with fencing, vegetative cover, limited access, and groundwater monitoring, do not present an unacceptable risk to human health or groundwater. — *Statement of Reasons, NCP Factor 1.*
- **DTSC (Adams):** Groundwater under the stockpiles contains no contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) exceeding primary maximum contaminant levels (MCLs) for drinking water; no municipal or domestic groundwater use occurs within one mile. — *Statement of Reasons, NCP Factor 1.*
- **DTSC (Adams):** The recommended remedial alternative (containment beneath SR 132 roadway infrastructure) is cost-effective — approximately 20 times less than full excavation and offsite disposal. — *Statement of Reasons, NCP Factor 5.*

## Regulatory decisions
- **Decision type:** Statement of Reasons (Appendix C) and Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility (NBAR, Appendix D) prepared under California Health and Safety Code (HSC) § 25356.1(d) and (e).
- **Agency:** DTSC.
- **Date:** May 18, 2015.
- **Recommended remedy:** Alternative #4 from the Final Feasibility Study — containment of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil beneath roadway pavement, retaining walls, and bridge abutments on the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project.
- **Conditions imposed:** Prior to implementation, DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) must review and concur with the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP) and Operation and Maintenance Plan (OMP). A land use covenant will preclude residential, school, daycare, and hospital uses.
- **Allocation:** DTSC determined that preparing a percentage-based NBAR would not expedite settlement; Caltrans assumes full responsibility for remediation and operation/maintenance costs. (NBAR, Appendix D; arbitration provisions of HSC Chapter 6.8 repealed by SB 1018, effective June 27, 2012.)

## Analytical results
- **Contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in stockpile soil:** Primary: barium, strontium, lead. Additional metals tested: antimony, arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, chromium, cobalt, copper, mercury, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, silver, thallium, vanadium, zinc. Also tested: nitrate, sulfate, sulfide, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs).
- **Groundwater:** Tested for same COPCs as soil. Barium concentrations below regulatory primary MCLs for drinking water.
- **Risk assessment findings:** Human Health Risk Assessment (Shaw, June 2007) and Human Health Risk Assessment Update (Geocon, March 2013) concluded that stockpiles do not pose unacceptable risk to current or future receptors (offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, hypothetical shallow groundwater users). PAHs did not qualify for risk assessment due to limited detection.
- **Exceedances noted:** None reported for human health risk thresholds or primary MCLs.

## Remedial actions
- **Planned action (Alternative #4, recommended):** Containment of ~160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil via incorporation into SR 132 construction — beneath paved roadways, behind retaining walls, behind bridge abutments, or under a clean vegetated soil cap designed to minimize infiltration and convey surface water away.
- **Phase status:** Pre-implementation; Draft Final RAP referenced in Caltrans Draft Environmental Impact Report (EIR). RDIP and OMP to be submitted by Caltrans for DTSC/RWQCB concurrence.
- **Verification outcomes:** Groundwater and surface water monitoring required to evaluate effectiveness of covered surfaces. Frequencies and reporting established in RDIP.

## Responsible parties
- **California Department of Transportation (Caltrans):** Assumes full responsibility for remediation, operation and maintenance costs, preparation of RDIP and OMP, and land use covenant recording. — *NBAR, Appendix D.*
- **DTSC:** Lead regulatory oversight, prepared Statement of Reasons, NBAR, and reviewed Draft Final RAP. — *Cover letter, Statement of Reasons.*
- **Central Valley RWQCB:** Co-regulator; will review and concur with RDIP and OMP. — *Statement of Reasons, NCP Factor 6.*
- **Geocon Consultants, Inc.:** Prepared Draft Final RAP (October 27, 2014) and numerous groundwater monitoring reports, Supplemental Site Investigation, and Human Health Risk Assessment Update. — *Administrative Record, Appendix B.*
- **Shaw Environmental, Inc.:** Prepared initial site investigations, risk assessments, and work plans (2004–2009). — *Administrative Record, Appendix B.*
- **Kleinfelder:** Prepared Final Geotechnical Design Report (September 6, 2012). — *Administrative Record, Appendix B.*

## Key terms and conditions
- **Follow-on actions:** RDIP and OMP must be prepared by Caltrans and concurred upon by DTSC and RWQCB prior to remedy implementation.
- **Monitoring obligations:** Groundwater and surface water monitoring, maintenance, annual inspections, and reporting required per OMP.
- **Restrictions:** Land use covenant to be recorded to prohibit residential, school, daycare, and hospital uses on the property.
- **Deadlines:** No specific schedule provided in this document.
- **Regulatory basis:** HSC § 25356.1; NCP Factors (6); California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) — Draft EIR references the Draft Final RAP.
