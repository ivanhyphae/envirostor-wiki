## Summary of "Response to Comments letter for SR 132 RAP with Appendix J"

This document is a formal response-to-comments letter issued jointly by the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) and the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on March 13, 2018. It transmits DTSC's and Caltrans' responses to public comments received during a joint comment period (January 18 – March 17, 2017) on the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles and the accompanying Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) for the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Stanislaus County.

The core decision affirmed in the document is the selection of **Alternative 4: Containment** as the recommended remedy for the contaminated soil stockpiles. This remedy will encase the stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath pavement, with unpaved areas receiving clean fill or asphalt cover. The document rejects other alternatives, including the No-Build (deemed environmentally inferior) and Alternative 3 (Removal, found less cost-effective with greater short-term impacts), as well as Alternative 5 (Maze Boulevard improvements, dismissed for not meeting project purpose).

Key conditions and next actions outlined include:
- Caltrans must enter into an Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement with DTSC and prepare an O&M Plan for DTSC approval.
- Annual inspections of pavement and containment features, along with continued groundwater monitoring, are required.
- DTSC will conduct a 5-year remedy evaluation.

The letter contains eleven "master responses" addressing common comment categories, and all individual comments and responses are provided in Appendix J (enclosed on CD). Specific responses address a wide range of issues, including:
- **Modesto Irrigation District (MID)** comments: The project team commits to coordinating with MID during the design phase and submitting irrigation improvement plans for review prior to construction.
- **Property impacts**: Revised Parcel Impact Maps (Appendix F) show many properties as partial acquisitions rather than full takes; no relocations are required at this time.
- **Alternatives**: Alternative 2 is restated as the preferred balance of factors; Alternative 5 (Maze Boulevard) is dismissed; No-Build is evaluated but found inferior.
- **Human health and environment**: Groundwater monitoring shows no exceedances of drinking water standards; stormwater runoff meets water quality thresholds; the stockpiles as currently managed do not pose unacceptable risk.
- **Noise, homelessness, farmland, and public outreach**: Noise analysis is updated; existing security measures (fencing, mowing) are described; Williamson Act contracts are not expected to be cancelled; bilingual notices and mailings are documented.

The document also responds to comments submitted via comment cards and at a public hearing (February 22, 2017), addressing concerns about business losses, property takings, highway access, and suggestions to use stockpile soil for levee raising. Commenters requesting to be added to the mailing list are accommodated.

Responsible parties include Caltrans (owner/operator, implementation), DTSC (regulatory oversight, 5-year reviews), and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (co-regulator for groundwater/surface water). The document does not require further resubmittals from commenters; it finalizes the responses and transmits the complete record.
