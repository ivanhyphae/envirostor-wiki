Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared this Revised Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) report in March 2013 (initially December 2012) for Caltrans, addressing potential environmental data gaps for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles project (Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order No. 44). The report, submitted to the DTSC and CVRWQCB for regulatory approval of the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, specifically incorporates DTSC comments from February 2013.

The investigation focuses on three soil stockpiles (totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards) located south of the State Route 99 and Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, within the planned SR 132 project right-of-way. These stockpiles, formed in the early 1960s from fill material originating from former evaporation ponds that processed ores, were known to contain elevated levels of various contaminants. The SSI involved advancing 68 borings (Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium) to characterize soil and re-evaluate contaminant exposure and migration. Soil samples were analyzed for Title 22 Metals (including barium, cadmium, and lead), petroleum hydrocarbons (GRO, DRO, ORO), and PAHs.

Key findings indicate widespread and significant metal contamination. **Barium** was consistently detected at very high concentrations, ranging from 42,000 µg/kg to 4,300 mg/kg, and in core borings reaching an extraordinary 130,000 mg/kg (130,000,000 ug/kg), with multiple samples exceeding the residential California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) of 5,200 mg/kg. The highest barium levels were found along the north side of Stockpile 2, though Fenceline and Perimeter borings generally showed concentrations below residential CHHSLs, decreasing with depth.

While previous investigations identified barium as a primary contaminant of potential concern, this SSI specifically re-evaluated other impacts. **Cadmium** was generally not detected above laboratory reporting limits in Fenceline or Cadmium borings, leading to the conclusion that it is not considered a COPC for this site, despite one anomalous high detection and some quality control issues noting recovery excursions. **Lead** was present in most samples, with detections up to 34,000 µg/kg (FB39-4.0) and a high of 16,000 mg/kg in some soil samples. **Arsenic** was identified in many samples, varying from 270 µg/kg to 2,100 µg/kg. Other metals such as **Zinc** (up to 190,000 µg/kg and 75,000 mg/kg), **Copper** (up to 41,000 ug/kg and 9,000 mg/kg), **Vanadium** (up to 20,000 mg/kg and 19,000 ug/kg), **Chromium, Cobalt, Molybdenum, and Nickel** were also frequently detected at significant concentrations. Petroleum Hydrocarbons (DRO up to 120 mg/kg, ORO up to 82 mg/kg) and PAHs (e.g., 2-Methylnaphthalene up to 45 mg/kg, Phenanthrene up to 32 mg/kg) were found as well.

The report concludes that there has been no lateral or vertical migration of metals exceeding residential human health screening levels along the stockpile perimeters or adjacent property fencelines, and the barium-impacted materials are contained within Caltrans' right-of-way. The analytical data is deemed sufficient for developing a Remedial Action Plan (RAP), which Caltrans and Geocon are currently preparing to evaluate final remedies such as "no action," removal and disposal, or integration with the SR 132 project construction. This SSI therefore provides crucial data to inform the RAP and secure regulatory approval for the SR 132 Project.
