Reading this chart: each line tracks the maximum barium concentration found in that stockpile at each investigation. The scale is logarithmic (each gridline is 10× the one below) because concentrations span from the hundreds to the hundreds of thousands of mg/kg. A single 2012 boring at the edge of Stockpile 2/3 recorded an extreme outlier of 130,000 mg/kg — not plotted as a stockpile trend line since it reflects one sample, not the stockpile average, but shown as a reference point.

The remedy was containment, not removal. The 2014 Feasibility Study concluded that reaching a numeric soil cleanup target was unnecessary to protect health under managed conditions, so the approved remedy (2018) was performance-based: consolidate and cap the barium-containing soil as highway embankment fill. The April 2024 recorded Land Use Covenant confirms barium as high as 72,000 mg/kg remains in place under the cap — permanently restricted from unrestricted (e.g. residential) land use.
