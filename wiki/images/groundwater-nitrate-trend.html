MW-5 (downgradient, nearest stockpiles) MW-1 (upgradient / background) MCL — 10 mg/L as N

Both wells exceed the MCL throughout 2012–2013 — including MW-1, which sits upgradient of the stockpiles. Site reports interpret this as evidence that nitrate reflects regional agricultural background levels common across California's Central Valley, not stockpile-derived contamination. Wells MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10 also recurrently exceeded the nitrate MCL in 2012–2019 monitoring, though source reports summarized those as threshold flags rather than itemized per-date values.

Health context: nitrate itself is low-toxicity, but in the body it can convert to nitrite, which impairs the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity — causing methemoglobinemia (“blue baby syndrome”), a potentially fatal condition mainly affecting infants under 6 months. The 10 mg/L MCL is set specifically to protect that group. This groundwater is not used as a drinking water source at the site.
