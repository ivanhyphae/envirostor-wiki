Stormwater run-off from the encapsulated soil stockpiles is collected and sampled to confirm that barium-contaminated soil is not being displaced into surface water during rain events. The most recent round, reported in the Storm Water Sampling Report (Geocon Consultants, Inc., dated June 18, 2025, covering FY24/25), was reviewed by DTSC's Geological Services Branch and Human and Ecological Risk Office and approved outright, with all results within reporting limits.

## Purpose and role in the monitoring program

Storm water sampling exists alongside groundwater monitoring as the second line of evidence for containment integrity: while the groundwater wells (see [[groundwater-not-impacted]]) tracked whether metals were leaching downward from the stockpiles, the storm water and surface water sampling program tracks whether qualifying rain events are carrying contaminated soil or run-off laterally off the stockpiles. DTSC's January 30, 2026 letter frames the two programs as complementary checks on the same underlying question — whether the stockpile containment system is holding.

## FY24/25 results and approval

The FY24/25 Storm Water Sampling Report described sampling conducted during qualifying rain events and found all results within reporting limits, meaning no constituents were reported above the applicable limits for that round. DTSC's review of this report was unconditional: unlike the companion Well Destruction Report, which was only conditionally approved pending a GSB comment being incorporated, the Storm Water Sampling Report was reviewed and approved without any outstanding condition. This distinction matters for the record: the storm water/surface water monitoring line of the site's closure record has no open item, while the well destruction line does (see [[dtsc-conditional-approval-well-destruction-report]]).
