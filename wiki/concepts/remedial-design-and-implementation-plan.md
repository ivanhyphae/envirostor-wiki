The Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP) outlines the strategy for addressing soil contamination at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, specifically related to the SR 132 West Expressway project in Stanislaus County, California. The plan details the scope of work, methodologies, and regulatory compliance measures for the remediation activities.

## Site Record

The RDIP addresses soil stockpiles generated during the SR 132 West Expressway widening project. These stockpiles were found to contain constituents exceeding regulatory thresholds. Specifically, the RDIP addresses soil that contained barium at concentrations up to 1,100 mg/kg and chromium at concentrations up to 150 mg/kg. These concentrations were identified in samples collected from the stockpiles. The remediation strategy involved the excavation and off-site disposal of contaminated soil. A total of 10,000 cubic yards of soil were excavated and transported to the Kettleman Hills Landfill for disposal. This action was undertaken to comply with cleanup goals and regulatory requirements.

## Regulatory Status

The remediation activities were conducted under the oversight of the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] and the DTSC. The cleanup goals were established based on applicable state and federal regulations, including California-CHHSL and California-Primary MCL and California-Secondary MCL where relevant for soil and potential groundwater impacts. The disposal of the excavated soil at Kettleman Hills Landfill was in accordance with regulatory permits and requirements.
