# Excavation Monitoring Plan (Stockpile 3, Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project)

The Stockpile 3 Excavation Monitoring Plan, submitted by Geocon Consultants, Inc. to Caltrans District 6 on June 13, 2012, sets out how excavation of barium-affected fill within [[stockpile-3]] would be overseen during construction of the SR 99 Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp safety and widening project. It was prepared under Caltrans Contract No. 06A1634 / EA No. 10-0A671, addressed to Caltrans project manager Richard Stewart, PG, and grounded in supplemental soil borings Geocon performed within the off-ramp excavation footprint on April 16, 2012, together with the 2009 Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) prepared by Shaw for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. Its purpose was narrow and construction-driven: to allow removal of roughly 2,800 cubic yards of Stockpile 3 fill for the widening and retaining-wall work while managing worker and dust exposure to barium and other chemicals of potential concern, and to document when excavation had reached clean native soil beneath the stockpile.

## Basis and scope

Stockpile 3 is a crescent-shaped, roughly 2.5-acre fill mass on the east side of SR 99 south of Kansas Avenue, one of three stockpiles (Stockpiles 1–3, approximately 121,000 cubic yards total) placed within Caltrans right-of-way in the early 1960s using material excavated from evaporation ponds on former Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) property. The planned off-ramp shoulder widening required a retaining wall against part of the Stockpile 3 embankment and laying back its slope, driving an estimated 2,800 cubic yards of stockpile fill excavation; excavation of underlying native soils was also required but, per the Plan, did not need special handling, excavation controls, or air monitoring because contamination was associated with the fill, not the native ground beneath it. The Plan's monitoring and verification procedures therefore applied only to the fill removal, not the full excavation scope.

## Excavation and verification procedures

Under the Plan, Teichert was to excavate the northern portion of Stockpile 3 during daytime off-ramp closures over an estimated five working days, loading fill for disposal as non-hazardous waste at the Fink Road Sanitary Landfill in Crows Landing, Stanislaus County. Oversight was assigned to a Geocon California Professional Geologist (PG), or a field geologist/engineer working under a PG's direct supervision, whose determination governed when the planned excavation limits within the Stockpile 3 fill had been reached. That individual was responsible for daily field logs recording onsite personnel, work performed, air monitoring locations, visible dust observations, descriptions of active excavation, quantities of material removed, and confirmation that excavation had reached native soil — the last item functioning as the Plan's verification endpoint for closing out the fill removal. Logs were to be supplemented with photographs, and the field representative was authorized to collect soil samples with accompanying chain-of-custody documentation. Air monitoring station locations and the proposed excavation area were mapped against the site's existing features — monitoring wells MW6, MW7, and MW8, perimeter fencing, and a Modesto Irrigation District canal (Lateral No. 4) crossing the southern portion of the stockpile — in the Plan's Figure 3 site plan.

## Significance and limitations

The Plan functioned as the operational bridge between the PEA's risk characterization of barium and other COPCs in the stockpiles and the physical act of cutting into Stockpile 3 for a state highway project: it converted a health-risk finding into field procedure (excavation sequencing, air monitoring, PG-verified stopping point, non-hazardous disposal routing) without itself constituting a remedial decision. Geocon's stated limitations clarify its evidentiary weight — the Plan was prepared solely for Caltrans as site owner, does not constitute construction plans or specifications, and its conclusions are predicated on the limited April 2012 boring data and are valid only as of the document's date, requiring update if site conditions or activities changed. The Plan does not itself report excavation results, confirmation sampling data, or final verification that native soil was reached; those outcomes, if recorded, lie outside this document.
