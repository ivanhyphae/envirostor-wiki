# Ramp Clarification Letter

## Overview

The Ramp Clarification Letter is a correspondence issued by the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) on **August 24, 2012**, addressed to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB). The letter provides further clarification regarding the disposition of excavated material from ramp safety improvements at the State Route 99 / Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp in Modesto, California.

The letter's primary significance is its formal determination that completion and implementation of an interim-remedial-action-workplan-unnecessary|Interim Removal Action Workplan was **not needed** for the ramp project, based on additional investigation results and the proposed material management approach.

## Site record

The letter addresses an estimated total excavation of **6,000 cubic yards** of material for the widened roadbed, drainage, and retaining wall construction. Of this total, approximately **2,800 cubic yards** would come from the northwestern end of stockpile-3-excavation|Stockpile No. 3.

Caltrans stated that some excavated native material would be used as structural backfill during ramp construction, while the remainder — including all material excavated from Stockpile No. 3 — would be transported offsite.

The determination relied on site investigation data presented in the Geocon document titled *"Transmittal of Site Investigation Data, Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project SR 99 - Kansas Avenue Northbound Off-Ramp, Modesto California"* dated **April 24, 2012**. Key findings cited from that investigation:

- **No soil samples** in the Stockpile No. 3 excavation area contained total metal concentrations at or above the California hazardous waste **Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC)**.
- With the exception of arsenic, no metals were reported at or above commercial/industrial screening levels.
- **Arsenic** concentrations ranged from **less than 1.0 mg/kg to 3.2 mg/kg**, within the range of site-specific background levels.
- The 95% Upper Confidence Limit (UCL) concentrations for **barium**, **strontium**, and **lead** in the Stockpile No. 3 excavation area were below both residential and commercial/industrial screening levels.
- One soil sample had a barium concentration higher than **10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC)**, and five samples had lead concentrations at or above 10 times the STLC. However, the calculated 95% UCL for both metals was well below 10 times their respective STLC values.
- These elevated samples were further analyzed by **Advanced Technology Laboratories** using the **Waste Extraction Test (WET)**. The WET results fell below the STLC for all samples, confirming the material is **not classified as hazardous waste**.

The letter also stated that native soil material proposed for excavation outside the Stockpile No. 3 footprint was suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as non-hazardous soil to an accepting facility.

The letter was signed by **Sam Haack**, identified in the source as part of Caltrans District 10. A copy was noted for distribution to **Geocon Consultants, Inc.** (Rancho Cordova).

## Regulatory status

The letter responded to regulatory agencies exercising joint oversight:

- **DTSC**: Mr. Steven Becker, P.G., Chief of the Site Evaluation and Remediation Unit, Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program; with attention to Mr. Randy Adams, CEG.
- **Central Valley RWQCB**: Mr. Steven W. Meeks, P.E., Senior Water Resources Control Engineer.

The operative regulatory determination was that soil from Stockpile No. 3 did not meet the TTLC or STLC criteria for classification as California hazardous waste, and that the proposed handling (structural backfill reuse for native material outside Stockpile No. 3; offsite transport for Stockpile No. 3 material) was acceptable such that an Interim Removal Action Workplan was unnecessary for the ramp project.

## Open items

No unresolved issues or pending deliverables are documented in the letter itself. The letter serves as a closure determination for the Workplan requirement as applied to the ramp excavation scope.
