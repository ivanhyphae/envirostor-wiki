The containment of stockpiles is the selected cleanup alternative for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] associated with the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. This approach involves physically enclosing the soil masses to prevent exposure and minimize water infiltration.

## Site record

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] consist of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, primarily [[barium]], strontium, and [[lead]]. These stockpiles are located within the state right-of-way south of [[kansas-avenue]], specifically between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99.

The containment strategy involves constructing [[retaining-walls]], [[bridge-abutments]], and roadway structures to encase the stockpiles. Portions of the stockpiles that will remain unpaved will be covered with clean fill. This containment is designed to provide long-term protection by preventing direct contact with the soil and reducing the amount of surface water that can infiltrate into the groundwater beneath the stockpiles.

## Regulatory status

The [[remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on February 21, 2018. The selected containment remedy is considered protective of human health and the environment. Long-term protection will be ensured through annual inspections of the containment features (pavement and other structures) and ongoing groundwater monitoring. Land use controls will also be implemented.

Caltrans, as the lead agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) for the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. DTSC, as a responsible reviewing agency under CEQA, completed an independent analysis and determined that the containment remedy, with project-specific mitigation measures, will not have a significant adverse environmental effect. DTSC issued a Statement of Findings and a Notice of Determination (NOD) in compliance with CEQA.

## Open items

Annual inspections of the containment remedy and groundwater monitoring are required to ensure the long-term integrity of the containment.
