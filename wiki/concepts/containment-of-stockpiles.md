## Overview
The containment of stockpiles is the selected cleanup alternative for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] associated with the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. This approach involves physically enclosing the soil masses to prevent exposure and minimize water infiltration. The [[remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for these stockpiles was approved by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) on February 21, 2018.

## Site record
The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] consist of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, primarily barium, strontium, and lead. These stockpiles are located within the state right-of-way south of [[kansas-avenue]], specifically between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99.

The containment strategy involves constructing [[retaining-walls]], [[bridge-abutments]], and roadway structures to encase the stockpiles. Portions of the stockpiles that will remain unpaved will be covered with clean fill. This containment is designed to provide long-term protection by preventing direct contact with the soil and reducing the amount of surface water that can infiltrate into the groundwater beneath the stockpiles.

The [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] for the interim phase 1 of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project is titled "REMEDIAL DESIGN IMPLEMENTATION PLAN – INTERIM PHASE 1" and pertains to the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Stanislaus County, California. This plan details the containment design, including the incorporation of stockpiles as highway embankment fill, and covering with structural pavement sections and imported clean fill. Interim Phase 1 construction, planned for completion in 2020, includes construction of two lanes over Stockpiles 1 and 2, with the northern slopes of these stockpiles to be contour graded and capped with a minimum 12-inch-thick vegetated, clean soil cap. Ultimate Phase 2 construction, planned for completion by 2028, will involve the construction of retaining walls along the northern right-of-way of Stockpiles 1 and 2, completing their encapsulation. Stockpile 3 is planned to be entirely removed and used as fill over Stockpile 1 and within adjacent vacant State ROW.

Stockpile 1 is located south of Kansas Avenue and west of Emerald Avenue, with an estimated volume of 34,000 cubic yards. Stockpile 2 is located south of Kansas Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, with an estimated volume of 102,000 cubic yards. Stockpile 3 is located south of Kansas Avenue and east of SR 99, with an estimated volume of 24,000 cubic yards.

The containment remedy is designed to incorporate the stockpiles as highway embankment fill. For Stockpile 2, excavation is planned for the Emerald Avenue bridge abutment (2,200 yd³) and the SR 99 bridge abutment (1,650 yd³). Stockpile 3 will be completely removed (24,000 yd³). The total estimated barium-containing soil removal volume is 27,850 yd³. Designated fill consolidation areas at Stockpiles 1 and 2 have estimated capacities of 15,000 yd³ and 27,000 yd³, respectively, with an additional 12,500 yd³ capacity in the State ROW between Stockpile 1 and Emerald Avenue.

Verification soil sampling for removed stockpile material will compare analytical data to thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg for barium and 80 mg/kg for lead.

## Regulatory status
The [[remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on February 21, 2018. The selected remedy, Alternative 4, Containment, is considered protective of human health and the environment.

Caltrans, as the lead agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) for the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. DTSC, as a responsible reviewing agency under CEQA, completed an independent analysis and determined that the containment remedy, with project-specific mitigation measures, will not have a significant adverse environmental effect. DTSC prepared a Statement of Findings and a Notice of Determination (NOD) in compliance with CEQA.

The S1200-01-01 Caltrans Modesto Stockpile RDIP_01.19 document, dated January 30, 2019, details the Remedial Design Implementation Plan for Interim Phase 1. DTSC provided conditional approval of the Draft RDIP on August 10, 2018, and a further conditional approval on October 12, 2018, pending review of the 100% design plans.

## Open items
Long-term protection will be ensured through annual inspections of the containment features (pavement and other structures) and ongoing groundwater monitoring. Land use controls will also be implemented. A recorded Land Use Covenant (LUC) for the contained soil stockpiles will be required by DTSC to restrict future land use. Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the containment measures.
