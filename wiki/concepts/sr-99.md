# SR 99

## Overview
State Route 99 (SR 99) is a major highway corridor that defines the western boundary of the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] site in Stanislaus County, California. The soil stockpiles originated from excavations conducted during the construction of a segment of SR 99 north of the [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] in the early 1960s. The highway's alignment and associated ramps serve as key geographical references for site features, including stockpile locations and monitoring well installations.

## Site record
SR 99 runs diagonally through the project area near the intersection of Needham Street and Elm Avenue in Modesto. The "Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project" vicinity map (June 2012) by Geocon Consultants identifies three numbered stockpile areas along the SR 99 corridor. Stockpile #3 is situated adjacent to the SR 99 / Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp.

Geocon's improvement plan for the Kansas Avenue NB off-ramp (Figure 2, June 2012) shows the "Barium Pile" and the "Approximate Extent of Proposed Stockpile 3 Construction Excavation" in relation to SR 99's alignment. Monitoring wells MW-9 and MW-10 were installed south of Kansas Avenue, west and east of SR 99, respectively, as documented in the "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012." Wells MW-6, MW-7, and MW-8 are also located within the Stockpile 3 area adjacent to SR 99, as indicated on the site plan (Figure 3, June 2012).

The soil stockpiles along SR 99 comprise excess native soils and pond tailings from the original highway construction. The primary chemicals of concern identified at the SR 99 stockpile site are [[barium]], lead, and strontium, which are associated with historical operations at a former evaporation pond on an adjacent parcel formerly owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC).

## Regulatory status
On November 29, 2012, the dtsc|Department of Toxic Substances Control, in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region, issued a letter concurring with the May, June, and July 2012 groundwater monitoring reports for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 99 and 132). The reports were prepared by Geocon Consultants. DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports for the SR 99 stockpile site be submitted as groundwater data becomes available.

The concurrence letter was distributed to Caltrans District 10, the Stanislaus County Health Agency, and the Regional Water Quality Control Board.

## Open items
As per DTSC's directive dated November 29, 2012, future groundwater monitoring reports for the SR 99 Modesto Soil Stockpiles site are to be submitted as data becomes available. The project record does not specify whether subsequent reporting cycles have been completed.
