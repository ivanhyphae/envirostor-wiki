# SR 99

## Overview
State Route 99 is the highway corridor that borders and defines the Modesto Soil Stockpiles project site in Stanislaus County, California. The stockpiles were generated during construction of a segment of SR 99 north of [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] in the early 1960s, when Caltrans excavated through a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel purchased from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC). An evaporation pond formerly operated by FMC was located in the southernmost corner of that parcel. The highway right-of-way and its associated ramps — including the Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp along SR 99 — form the geographical framework within which soil stockpiles, monitoring wells, and excavation boundaries are referenced in project documents.

## Site record
The project site lies along SR 99 near its intersection with Needham Street and Elm Avenue in Modesto. The June 2012 "Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project" vicinity map prepared by geocon-consultants depicts Highway 99 running diagonally through the project area, with three numbered stockpile areas indicated along the highway corridor. Stockpile #3 is specifically located adjacent to the SR 99 / Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp.

Geocon's improvement plan (Figure 2, June 2012) for the Kansas Avenue NB off-ramp details construction zones along Route 99, showing the "Barium Pile" and the "Approximate Extent of Proposed Stockpile 3 Construction Excavation" relative to highway alignment reference lines. Monitoring wells associated with the SR 99 corridor include MW-9, installed south of Kansas Avenue and west of SR 99, and MW-10, installed south of Kansas Avenue and east of SR 99, as documented in the "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012." Monitoring wells MW-6, MW-7, and MW-8 are also positioned within the Stockpile 3 area adjacent to SR 99, as shown on the site plan (Figure 3, June 2012).

The soil stockpiles along SR 99 consist of excess native soils and pond tailings from the original highway construction. The primary chemicals of concern at the SR 99 stockpile site are [[barium]], lead, and strontium — constituents linked to FMC's historical barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ore processing operations at the adjacent former evaporation pond property.

## Regulatory status
The dtsc|Department of Toxic Substances Control, in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region, reviewed three groundwater monitoring reports for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 99 and 132) in November 2012. In a letter dated November 29, 2012, from Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist, DTSC concurred with the May, June, and July 2012 groundwater monitoring reports prepared by Geocon Consultants. DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports for the SR 99 stockpile site be completed as groundwater data becomes available.

Copies of DTSC's concurrence letter were distributed to Caltrans District 10 (Ms. Sam Haack, P.E., Project Manager), the Stanislaus County Health Agency (Ms. Nicole Damin, Senior Hazardous Materials Specialist), and the Regional Water Quality Control Board (Mr. Steven Meeks, P.E.).

## Open items
Future groundwater monitoring reports for the SR 99 Modesto Soil Stockpiles site are to be submitted as data becomes available, per DTSC's November 29, 2012 directive. The project record reviewed does not document whether subsequent reporting cycles have been completed.
