The March 2012 groundwater monitoring round was a sampling event conducted as part of the remediation efforts for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site. This event, along with others on October 5, 2006, and June 14, 2006, provided data for the "Groundwater Monitoring Report, California Department of Transportation Modesto Soil Stockpiles - State Route (SR) 99 and 132, Modesto, California," prepared by [[geocon-consultants]] and dated May 2012. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), reviewed this report. The RWQCB collected "split" water samples during the March 12, 2012 sampling event. The primary [[chemicals-of-concern]] at this site are [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]].

## Site record

The March 12, 2012 sampling event was one of three sampling events reported on in the May 2012 Geocon Consultants report, which also included data from October 5, 2006, and June 14, 2006. The monitoring wells were installed in 2006 to assess groundwater quality near three soil stockpiles. These stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings from the construction of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. The construction involved excavating a segment that traversed a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC), which processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores.

## Regulatory status

The DTSC reviewed the "Groundwater Monitoring Report, California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) Modesto Soil Stockpiles - State Route (SR) 99 and 132, Modesto, California," prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated May 2012. DTSC concurred with this report. DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports include a discussion comparing groundwater monitoring results and concentration trends from previous sampling events as more data becomes available. DTSC also requested that graphs and maps be prepared depicting concentration trends of primary COCs over time, and that all graphics, including existing "stiff" diagrams, include a legend defining symbols and colors. DTSC stipulated that groundwater monitoring should continue on a quarterly schedule.

## Open items

DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports include a discussion comparing groundwater monitoring results/concentration trends from previous sampling events as more data becomes available. Graphs and maps should be prepared depicting concentration trends of primary COC over time. All graphics, including existing "stiff" diagrams, should include a legend defining symbols/colors used to depict information. Groundwater monitoring should continue on a quarterly schedule.
