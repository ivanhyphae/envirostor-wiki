## Overview

**Barium Barite Form** refers to the chemical speciation of barium in the soil stockpiles at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR-132, Stanislaus County, CA). The site is located south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto. Soil in the stockpiles originated from a former FMC facility (operated from 1929 to approximately 1985) that processed barite (barium sulfate, BaSO₄) and celestite (strontium sulfate) for industrial uses. The stockpiles were created in the early 1960s when soil from one of seven unlined evaporation ponds at the FMC facility was excavated and placed within the current Caltrans right-of-way during construction of SR-99. The barium in the stockpiles is predominantly in the form of barite (barium sulfate), a mineral that is relatively insoluble and less mobile than other barium compounds. This speciation is critical because regulatory thresholds for soluble barium—such as the Title 22 California Code of Regulations Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) of 100 mg/L—were designed for non-barite barium compounds. As noted in the project record, the Title 22 criteria "are not strictly applicable to the Site" because of the predominance of barite.

## Site record

### Source material characterization
- The FMC facility processed barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) from the 1930s to the 1970s for use in greases, lubricating oil, and pigment blanks. Sodium sulfide was a by-product.
- Solid and liquid wastes ("tailings") from the facility were discharged to seven unlined evaporation ponds between the early 1950s and late 1970s.
- During SR-99 construction (early 1960s), soil from one of these ponds was excavated and placed into three stockpiles (Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3) within the Caltrans right-of-way. Aerial photos from 1963 and 1967 confirm placement and existence of the stockpiles.

### Barium concentrations
- Total barium in the stockpiles is associated with barite ore residues.
- Thirty-three stockpile soil samples were analyzed for soluble barium using the Waste Extraction Test (WET) and deionized water extraction (DI-WET).
- WET soluble barium concentrations ranged from **39 to 2,300 mg/L**, with **28 of 33 samples** exceeding the Title 22 STLC for barium of **100 mg/L**.
- DI-WET soluble barium concentrations ranged from **1.8 to 220 mg/L**, with **9 of 33 samples** exceeding the STLC.
- The project record states that the barium compounds at the Site are **primarily barite (barium sulfate)**, and that the Title 22 evaluation criteria for soluble barium are therefore not strictly applicable.
- Total lead exceeding 50 mg/kg (threshold requiring WET testing) was found in only two stockpile samples (150 and 1,500 mg/kg), with WET soluble lead at 2.9 and 5.7 mg/L—both below the lead STLC of 5.0 mg/L.

### Migration assessment
- Surface soil samples at the fence line and edges of stockpiles showed barium concentrations not significantly different from interior stockpile surface concentrations; no unacceptable human health risk from wind-blown dust or surface water runoff was identified.
- Groundwater sampling detected no cancer-causing chemicals attributable to the stockpiles. Low arsenic in groundwater is believed to be naturally occurring. No significant off-site migration of barium or other contaminants via leaching was documented.

### Risk screening comparison
- Maximum surface soil concentrations of **barium** were compared against U.S. EPA residential Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) and found to be below those safe levels, assuming more than 25 years of daily exposure via incidental soil ingestion, dust inhalation, and dermal contact. Incidental soil ingestion is the dominant exposure pathway.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standard**: Title 22 CCR STLC for barium is **100 mg/L**, but this standard applies to **non-barite barium compounds**. Because the barium is in barite form (barium sulfate), the STLC is considered "not strictly applicable" per the project record.
- **Oversight agencies**: California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) are the regulatory oversight agencies for the Remedial Action Plan (RAP), which supplements the draft Environmental Impact Report (EIR) for the SR-132 Project.
- **Current compliance**: The Remedial Action Plan (Draft Final, dated 2018) recommends and implements remedial actions for the stockpiles. Institutional controls (deed restrictions, access restrictions, signage, monitoring) are identified as potentially applicable in combination with other response actions. The RAP is under review and is to be finalized as part of the EIR process. Compliance status is pending finalization of the RAP and its implementation.

## Open items

- The RAP is in draft final form (2018) and is a supplement to the draft EIR for the SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. Final approval by DTSC and CVRWQCB is pending.
- Specific remedial actions beyond institutional controls (e.g., excavation, containment, or removal) are evaluated in the RAP but not finalized in the provided source excerpts.
- The applicability of Title 22 STLC for barium to the barite form remains a contested or unresolved technical interpretation—the DTSC acknowledgement in PHT-5 indicates the project record recognizes the distinction but does not document a formal regulatory determination or variance.
- No verification sampling or long-term monitoring data for barium mobility in barite form are presented in the provided excerpts. Future monitoring requirements are not specified.
