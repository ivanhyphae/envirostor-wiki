The Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) was a critical component of the remediation process for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, located along SR 132 in Stanislaus County, California. Its primary purpose was to gather additional data to refine the Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) and inform the selection of appropriate remediation strategies.

The SSI, conducted by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and documented in a report revised March 1, 2013, involved targeted soil sampling to address data gaps identified by regulatory agencies. This included advancing "Fenceline Borings" at stockpile perimeters to assess potential offsite migration, "Perimeter Borings" to define lateral limits, and "Cadmium Borings" in areas where previous investigations indicated elevated cadmium concentrations. Soil samples were analyzed for Title 22 metals, strontium, barium, and cadmium.

Key findings from the SSI and related investigations include:

*   **Barium:** Barium was consistently detected in soil samples, with concentrations ranging significantly. For instance, the Geocon 2012 SSI reported barium concentrations from 58 to 130,000 mg/kg in Cadmium Borings. The Shaw 2004 PSI identified maximum barium concentrations of 1,730 mg/kg in Stockpile 1, 60,700 mg/kg in Stockpile 2, and 44,900 mg/kg in Stockpile 3.
*   **Cadmium:** While the Shaw 2004 PSI reported elevated cadmium concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) in Stockpiles 2 and 3, subsequent investigations, including the Geocon 2012 SSI, did not detect cadmium above the laboratory reporting limit of 1.0 mg/kg. This led to the conclusion that the earlier cadmium data was likely unreliable, and cadmium was not considered a contaminant of potential concern (COPC).
*   **Other Contaminants:** Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were generally not detected at concerning levels. Nitrate was detected but not at concentrations of concern. One soil sample from a Cadmium Boring showed diesel-range organics at 120 mg/kg, slightly exceeding the San Francisco Bay Area Regional Water Quality Control Board's (SFBRWQCB) residential/industrial Environmental Screening Level (ESL) of 83 mg/kg.
*   **Migration:** The results from the Fenceline and Perimeter Borings did not indicate lateral or vertical migration of soil containing metals (notably barium) at concentrations exceeding State and Federal residential human health screening levels along the stockpile perimeters and adjacent property fencelines.
*   **HHRA Update:** The data from the SSI was incorporated into an update of the 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA). This update, also concurred with by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on April 4, 2013, concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or noncancer hazard as long as they remain in place and are properly managed. However, the DTSC noted that if grading or removal of stockpiles were to occur, elevated concentrations at depth would need re-evaluation.

The SSI report was a key deliverable that supported the development of the Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP), which ultimately recommended containment of the stockpiles as highway embankment fill for the SR-132 Project.
