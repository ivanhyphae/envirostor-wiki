## Overview

The Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) was the primary field investigation phase for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, conducted to characterize the nature and extent of contamination in the soil stockpiles located along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. The SSI was designed to support a Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) and determine whether excavated materials from Caltrans construction projects posed unacceptable risks. The final SSI report, titled “Final Supplemental Site Investigation and Human Health Risk Assessment,” was completed on April 4, 2013, and submitted to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) as part of the regulatory review process.

## Site Record

The SSI was initiated in response to findings from earlier investigations that identified elevated concentrations of metals and other contaminants in the stockpiles. The April 4, 2013 Final SSI and HHRA report documented sampling of multiple stockpile locations along SR 132. Key findings from the SSI include:

- **Sampling locations and media**: Soil samples were collected from stockpile surfaces and at depth (typically 0–6 inches and 6–12 inches), with a focus on fine-grained fractions (silt and clay) that had higher contaminant concentrations.
- **Contaminants of concern**: Metals were the primary focus, including arsenic, lead, chromium, nickel, copper, zinc, and barium. For example, barium was detected at concentrations up to 760 mg/kg in stockpile soil. 
- **Spatial distribution**: Higher concentrations were found in older stockpiles (e.g., Stockpile B) that had been exposed for longer periods, suggesting weathering and concentration of metals in finer soil fractions.
- **Temporal trends**: The SSI reported that some metal concentrations in stockpile soils exceeded site-specific background levels, but the HHRA determined that risks were acceptable under the proposed reuse scenario (commercial/industrial land use).
- **Verification results**: The SSI included quality assurance/quality control samples (duplicates, equipment blanks, trip blanks) that confirmed no cross-contamination during sampling.

## Regulatory Status

The SSI was conducted under oversight by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), which is the lead regulatory agency for this site. The applicable screening levels were based on:

- **California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs)** for metals in soil for industrial/commercial land use: e.g., CHHSL for arsenic = 0.07 mg/kg (carcinogenic endpoint), lead = 320 mg/kg (non-carcinogenic), barium = 820 mg/kg (industrial).
- **Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB)** applicable water quality objectives for groundwater, though no groundwater was sampled directly in the SSI. The site was not yet in compliance with all DTSC requirements because the 2013 document remained under review; however, no formal violation notices were recorded in the source.

The report stated that “no unacceptable risks to human health were identified” under the industrial reuse scenario for the contaminants detected, based on the SSI data (April 4, 2013 Final SSI and HHRA, Section 7.0).

## Open Items

- DTSC had not issued a final approval letter for the SSI report as of April 2013; further review or requests for additional data were possible.
- Groundwater monitoring was not conducted during the SSI, leaving potential off-site migration pathways uncharacterized.
- No confirmation samples were taken after stockpile removal, as removal had not yet occurred at the time of the SSI report.
