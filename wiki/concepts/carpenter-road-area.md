The Carpenter Road Area is a segment of the [[sr-132]] alignment in Stanislaus County, California, identified as a proposed source of clean fill soil. This soil was intended for use as cover material for [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) stockpiles associated with the SR-132 project. The area consists of native soil that has been within the [[caltrans]] right-of-way since the 1960s and was previously agricultural land. A [[sampling-plan]] was developed by [[geocon-consultants]] to characterize the upper five feet of soil for its suitability as clean fill.

## Site record

The contractor for the SR-132 project proposed excavating the upper five feet of soil along the SR-132 alignment, extending eastward from Carpenter Road. This excavated material was designated for use as clean fill cover for the BCS stockpiles, aligning with the [[clean-fill]] capping and clean fill suitable as cover remedial strategy.

[[geocon-consultants]] reviewed the Blackburn 2014 Initial Site Assessment (ISA) and the Rincon 2018 Phase II Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) for potential contaminant sources in the vicinity of the Carpenter Road Area. The review did not identify any high-risk documented contamination releases in the immediate area of the proposed clean fill source. Testing of residential backyards by Rincon, adjacent to and west of Carpenter Road, indicated the presence of pesticides and metals at concentrations below applicable regulatory screening levels.

Geotechnical test borings conducted at the Carpenter Road overcrossing in 2017 documented that the material in this location consists of native soil. Logs from these borings were appended to the sampling plan submittal to support the lithologic description.

A [[sampling-plan]], prepared by [[geocon-consultants]] and transmitted to [[caltrans]] by [[john-e-juhrend]] on February 4, 2020, outlined a characterization program for the Carpenter Road Area:
*   **Number of borings:** 12 borings, each 5 feet deep.
*   **Depth intervals and analytes:**
    *   **0–2 feet:** 3 four-point composite samples for organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) and heavy metals.
    *   **3–5 feet:** 3 four-point composite samples for heavy metals.
*   **Total composite samples:** 6 depth-discrete samples.

The sampling plan was transmitted to the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the Water Board on February 4, 2020, by Richard C. Stewart, Engineering Geologist with [[caltrans]] Central Region Environmental Division.

A subsequent Technical Memorandum dated February 27, 2020, by [[geocon-consultants]], summarized soil characterization testing performed at the proposed clean cap borrow area located adjacent and easterly of Carpenter Road within the SR 132 right-of-way. This memo stated that 12 direct-push borings (B1 through B12) were completed on a grid pattern within the borrow area to a maximum depth of 5 feet. Continuous core soil samples were collected from each boring at depth intervals of 0 to 2 feet and 2 to 5 feet. These samples were composited into depth-discrete 3-part composites, yielding four 3-part composite samples for each sampling depth interval. Each composite sample was analyzed for Title 22 metals and mercury. The four composite samples from the 0 to 2 feet interval were also analyzed for organochlorine pesticide (OCP). The memo reported the following concentration ranges (in milligrams per kilogram [mg/kg]) for Title 22 metals:
*   Arsenic: 1.0 to 1.5 {{quote: "Arsenic: 1.0 to 1.5 (site-specific background: 1.2 mg/kg)"}}
*   Barium: 60 to 110 {{quote: "Barium: 60 to 110 (site-specific background: 72.8 mg/kg)"}}
*   Beryllium: 1.3 to 1.6 {{quote: "Beryllium: 1.3 to 1.6 (site-specific background: 0.2 mg/kg)"}}
*   Chromium: 7.5 to 11 {{quote: "Chromium: 7.5 to 11 (site-specific background: 7.1 mg/kg)"}}
*   Cobalt: 3.2 to 4.9 {{quote: "Cobalt: 3.2 to 4.9 (site-specific background: 4.4 mg/kg)"}}
*   Copper: 5.3 to 9.9 {{quote: "Copper: 5.3 to 9.9 (site-specific background: 7.5 mg/kg)"}}
*   Lead: 1.7 to 7.0 {{quote: "Lead: 1.7 to 7.0 (site-specific background: 2.0 mg/kg)"}}
*   Nickel: 5.6 to 8.2 {{quote: "Nickel: 5.6 to 8.2 (site-specific background: 5.3 mg/kg)"}}
*   Silver: 1.6 to 2.5 {{quote: "Silver: 1.6 to 2.5 (site-specific background: 0.2 mg/kg)"}}
*   Vanadium: 16 to 25 {{quote: "Vanadium: 16 to 25 (site-specific background: 31.3 mg/kg)"}}
*   Zinc: 21 to 41 {{quote: "Zinc: 21 to 41 (site-specific background: 26.3 mg/kg)"}}

Antimony, cadmium, selenium, thallium, mercury, and OCPs were not detected above laboratory reporting limits. {{quote: "The reported metal concentrations in the composite samples are consistent with naturally occurring background concentrations. Based on the data presented herein, the proposed clean cap borrow area appears to be suitable to generate clean cover fill materials for capping BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2."}}

[[geocon-consultants]] tentatively scheduled the drilling of the borings for February 11, 2020.

## Regulatory status

A Variance to Remedial Design Implementation Plan, dated April 9, 2020, prepared by [[geocon-consultants]] for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, states: {{quote: "The highway contractor (Bay Cities Paving and Grading) initially identified an area adjacent and east of Carpenter Road within the SR 132 right-of-way as the clean fill material source for capping Stockpiles 1 and 2. Geocon, in coordination with Caltrans and DTSC, evaluated this area and determined the planned highway underpass excavation area adjacent and east of Carpenter Road would generate soil material suitable for use as clean cover fill material (Geocon February 27, 2020 Technical Memorandum). DTSC subsequently provided approval of the planned Carpenter Road clean fill source area."}}

On June 12, 2020, DTSC and the Waterboard reviewed and approved the "Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan (Plan) dated June 11, 2020 that outlines the proposed sampling and analysis for the soil that will be used to cap the State Route 132 stockpiles." {{quote: "DTSC and the Waterboard have reviewed the Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan (Plan) dated June 11, 2020 that outlines the proposed sampling and analysis for the soil that will be used to cap the State Route 132 stockpiles. The Plan is consistent with the previous borrow area sampling and there are no comments. This e-mail serves as DTSCs approval of the Plan."}} This approval was communicated via email from Dean Wright of DTSC to Richard C. Stewart of Caltrans.
