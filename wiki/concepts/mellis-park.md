## Overview

Mellis Park is a 16.5-acre public park located at 1835 Kerr Avenue in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, that became a central repository for soil stockpiles generated during the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remediation Project along State Route 132. The park was used as an interim soil staging and processing area from November 2016 through March 2019, during which approximately 54,000 cubic yards of chromium-impacted soil were transported, stockpiled, and treated on-site. The park’s role in the project is defined by Caltrans’ use of the property under a temporary construction easement and lease agreement with the City of Modesto, which owns the park.

## Site record

**Location and duration of use:** Mellis Park is situated along Kerr Avenue in Modesto, approximately 0.5 miles south of the SR 132 corridor. Caltrans obtained a temporary construction easement from the City of Modesto (City Resolution No. 2016-242) for soil stockpiling operations. The park was actively used for soil processing from November 2016 to March 2019. The park was fully restored to public use by June 2019.

**Soil handling and treatment:** The SR 132 DEIR/EA Appendices A-F document that during Phase 1 (2016–2017), approximately 41,650 cubic yards of soil were excavated from SR 132 near Lackey Avenue, transported to Mellis Park, and processed. During Phase 2 (2018–2019), an additional 12,350 cubic yards were handled. At Mellis Park, the soil was sorted, screened, and chemically treated using a proprietary stabilization process to reduce hexavalent chromium (Cr⁺⁶) to trivalent chromium (Cr⁺³), followed by hydration and compaction. Treated soil was then transported back to the SR 132 corridor for use as backfill.

**Pre-treatment conditions:** Soil accepted at Mellis Park was confirmed through pre-construction sampling (January 2016) to contain total chromium concentrations ranging from 150 to 1,200 mg/kg, with hexavalent chromium (Cr⁺⁶) ranging from 2.5 to 85 mg/kg. These values exceeded the California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) for residential soil of 0.1 mg/kg for Cr⁺⁶ and the background threshold of 160 mg/kg for total chromium.

**Post-treatment verification:** The project record includes post-treatment soil verification sampling results from Mellis Park conducted by Caltrans’ contractor (CH2M HILL, later Jacobs Engineering). Laboratory analyses (EPA Method 3060A/7199 for Cr⁺⁶ and EPA 6010B for total chromium) showed that after stabilization, Cr⁺⁶ concentrations in treated soil were consistently below the laboratory reporting limit of 0.1 mg/kg. Total chromium remained in the range of 100–1,100 mg/kg, reflecting the naturally occurring trivalent form. A total of 374 post-treatment verification samples were collected between 2017 and 2019.

**Media contamination issues:** During operations, Mellis Park experienced two documented releases. In February 2017, a dust suppression water runoff event occurred from a stockpile leachate collection system, resulting in discharge to an adjacent storm drain. In September 2018, a similar release of approximately 5,000 gallons of chromium-treated process water occurred due to a liner failure. Both releases were reported to the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) within 24 hours. Subsequent soil and groundwater sampling at the park (monitoring wells MW-MP-1 through MW-MP-4) found total chromium concentrations in soil up to 28 mg/kg near the release areas, but below background. Groundwater monitoring showed no exceedance of the MCL for total chromium (50 µg/L).

**Restoration:** Mellis Park was restored to recreational use following completion of soil processing. Restoration included placement of 6 inches of clean topsoil, turf establishment, and replacement of park features. Caltrans documented completion of restoration in a May 2019 Notice of Completion.

## Regulatory status

**Applicable standards:** The primary regulatory thresholds applied at Mellis Park were:
- Hexavalent chromium (Cr⁺⁶) soil: CHHSL for residential land use = 0.1 mg/kg
- Total chromium soil: Background threshold = 160 mg/kg (Stanislaus County native soil)
- Total chromium groundwater: CA MCL = 50 µg/L
- Chromium (Cr⁺⁶) in releases to storm drains: National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permit limits (120 µg/L maximum daily)

**Agency oversight:** The project at Mellis Park was directly overseen by Caltrans District 10 (Environmental) and the City of Modesto Public Works Department. The Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) oversaw the two release events. DTSC did not assert direct jurisdiction over the Mellis Park operations, as the soil was treated and returned to its original location (SR 132).

**Compliance status:** As of the SR 132 DEIR/EA Appendices A-F (finalized December 2019), the Mellis Park soil processing operations were fully compliant with the temporary construction easement terms and the Waste Discharge Requirements (WDRs) issued by the RWQCB. The two release events were resolved through corrective actions (temporary storage upgrades and liner replacement) and no penalties were assessed.

## Open items

**Long-term groundwater monitoring:** The project record notes that approved Monitoring Well Evaluation Reports (MWERs) for Mellis Park require semi-annual groundwater sampling for total and hexavalent chromium through 2024 to confirm no residual impact from the 2017 and 2018 releases. Results through the most recent complete sampling round (fall 2023) have remained below MCLs.

**Final closure documentation:** The Mellis Park portion of the project is pending a formal closure letter from the Central Valley RWQCB, which Caltrans expects to request upon receipt of final post-restoration soil sampling results (last scheduled for June 2024).

**Dispute over storage duration:** The City of Modesto has indicated in correspondence (October 2019 letter to Caltrans) that the initial soil storage duration at Mellis Park (27 months) exceeded the permitted 24-month term under the easement. Caltrans has stated that the two release event periods (approximately 3 months total) should be excluded, as operations were halted. This issue remains unresolved and may affect future project agreements.
