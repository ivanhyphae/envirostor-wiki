## Interim Removal Action Workplan

### Overview

The need for an Interim Removal Action Workplan for the SR 132 ramp safety improvements project in Modesto was determined to be unnecessary. This conclusion was reached following additional investigations and a review of the proposed management of excavated materials.

### Site Record

Caltrans initiated investigations in the area to be excavated at [[stockpile-3-excavation]] in support of an Interim Remedial (Removal) Action Workplan for the ramp project. However, the results of these investigations and the proposed management of the material demonstrated that completion and implementation of an Interim Removal Action Workplan was not needed.

An estimated total of 6,000 cubic yards of material was to be excavated for the widened roadbed, drainage, and retaining wall. Approximately 2800 cubic yards of this material was to be excavated from the north western end of [[stockpile-3-excavation]]. Some of the excavated native material was to be used as backfill for the ramp construction activities. The remainder of the excavated material, including all material excavated from [[stockpile-3-excavation]], was to be transported offsite.

Based on site investigation data presented in the document "Transmittal of Site Investigation Data, Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project SR 99 - Kansas Avenue Northbound Off-Ramp, Modesto California" (Geocon, 4/24/2012), none of the soil samples in the area to be excavated in [[stockpile-3-excavation]] contained total metal concentrations at or higher than the California hazardous waste Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC). With the exception of arsenic, none of the metals were reported at concentrations at or above commercial/industrial screening levels. Arsenic concentrations in samples ranged from less than 1.0 mg/kg to 3.2 mg/kg and were within the range of site-specific background levels. The 95% upper confidence limit concentrations for barium, strontium, and lead in the area to be excavated in [[stockpile-3-excavation]] were below residential and commercial/industrial screening levels.

One soil sample had a barium concentration higher than 10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC), and five soil samples had lead concentrations at or higher than 10 times the STLC. However, the calculated 95% Upper Confidence Level for barium and lead was well below 10 times the respective STLC. These samples were further analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories for solubility levels using the Waste Extraction Test (WET). The results of these analyses fell below the STLC, and therefore the material was not classified as a hazardous waste.

The native soil material proposed for excavation outside the footprint of [[stockpile-3-excavation]] was deemed suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as a non-hazardous soil to an accepting facility.

### Regulatory Status

The determination that an Interim Removal Action Workplan was not needed was communicated by [[caltrans]] in a letter dated August 24, 2012, addressed to Mr. Steven Becker, P.G., Chief of the Site Evaluation and Remediation Unit at the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]], and Mr. Steven W. Meeks, P.E., Senior Water Resources Control Engineer at the [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. This clarification was provided in the context of the ramp safety improvements project.
