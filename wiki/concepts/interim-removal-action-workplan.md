## Interim Removal Action Workplan

### Overview

An Interim Removal Action Workplan (IRAWP) was determined to be unnecessary for the SR 132 ramp safety improvements project in Modesto. This conclusion was reached after Caltrans conducted investigations and reviewed the proposed management of excavated materials.

### Site Record

Caltrans initiated investigations in the area designated for excavation at [[stockpile-3-excavation]] to support a potential Interim Remedial (Removal) Action Workplan for the ramp project. However, the findings from these investigations and the proposed management strategy for the excavated material indicated that the completion and implementation of an IRAWP were not required.

The project anticipated excavating a total of approximately 6,000 cubic yards of material for the widened roadbed, drainage improvements, and retaining wall construction. Of this total, roughly 2,800 cubic yards were to be excavated from the northwestern portion of [[stockpile-3-excavation]]. Some of this excavated native material was planned for reuse as backfill for the ramp construction. The remaining excavated material, including all material from [[stockpile-3-excavation]], was to be transported offsite.

According to the site investigation data presented in the document "Transmittal of Site Investigation Data, Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project SR 99 - Kansas Avenue Northbound Off-Ramp, Modesto California" prepared by [[geocon-consultants]] dated April 24, 2012, none of the soil samples from the excavation area within [[stockpile-3-excavation]] exhibited total metal concentrations at or above the California hazardous waste Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC). With the exception of arsenic, no metals were reported at concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial screening levels. Arsenic concentrations in samples ranged from less than 1.0 mg/kg to 3.2 mg/kg, which were within the range of site-specific background levels. The 95% upper confidence limit concentrations for [[barium]], strontium, and lead in the excavation area of [[stockpile-3-excavation]] were below residential and commercial/industrial screening levels.

One soil sample showed a [[barium]] concentration exceeding 10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC), and five soil samples had lead concentrations at or above 10 times the STLC. However, the calculated 95% Upper Confidence Level for [[barium]] and lead remained well below 10 times the respective STLC. Subsequent analysis of these samples for solubility using the Waste Extraction Test (WET) by Advanced Technology Laboratories confirmed that the results fell below the STLC, thus the material was not classified as hazardous waste.

The native soil material proposed for excavation outside the footprint of [[stockpile-3-excavation]] was deemed suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as non-hazardous soil at an accepting facility.

### Regulatory Status

Caltrans communicated the determination that an Interim Removal Action Workplan was not necessary in a letter dated August 24, 2012. This letter was addressed to Mr. Steven Becker, P.G., Chief of the Site Evaluation and Remediation Unit at the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]], and Mr. Steven W. Meeks, P.E., Senior Water Resources Control Engineer at the [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. This clarification was provided in the context of the ramp safety improvements project.
