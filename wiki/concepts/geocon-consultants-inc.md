Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Geocon) was the environmental consultant for Caltrans on the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. Geocon prepared numerous reports and work plans related to the characterization and remediation of soil stockpiles on Caltrans right-of-way. These stockpiles, originating from former FMC Corporation operations and native soils excavated during SR 99 construction, contained elevated concentrations of barium and, to a lesser extent, lead. Geocon's work supported the regulatory approval process for the SR 132 Project by addressing data gaps and documenting remedial actions.

## Site record

Geocon prepared a Supplemental Site Investigation Report (SSI) dated December 14, 2012, revised March 1, 2013, for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. This SSI involved advancing 68 borings to characterize soil within and around the stockpiles. The purpose was to address potential offsite and vertical migration of contaminants, define lateral stockpile limits, and investigate areas of elevated cadmium. The SSI report noted that barium was detected in all Cadmium Borings at concentrations ranging from 58 to 130,000 mg/kg. Cadmium was not detected above the laboratory reporting limit of 1.0 mg/kg in any soil samples from the Cadmium Borings. Geocon concluded that the SSI data did not suggest lateral or vertical migration of metals exceeding screening levels along the stockpile perimeters.

Geocon also prepared a Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) dated January 30, 2019, for Interim Phase 1 of the SR 132 West Project. This plan outlined the approach for consolidating and capping Barium-Containing Soil (BCS) Stockpiles 1 through 3. The RDIP specified excavation verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg for barium and 80 mg/kg for lead. Geocon submitted two DTSC-approved Variances to the RDIP. The first, dated April 9, 2020, identified clean fill source areas, including MSE retaining wall foundation excavations, and presented site-specific upper-bound background concentrations for barium (120 mg/kg) and lead (3.8 mg/kg). The second Variance, dated September 2, 2020, clarified the use of aggregate base as an alternative clean cover material.

Geocon also prepared a Technical Memorandum on March 13, 2020, summarizing BCS removal verification testing at former Stockpile 3. This memo indicated that 3,075 truckloads of BCS, approximately 38,440 cubic yards, were transported to Stockpile 1. Verification soil samples collected from the base of the Stockpile 3 excavation ranged from 46 to 970 mg/kg for barium and 1.6 to 40 mg/kg for lead, meeting the RDIP verification thresholds.

Geocon submitted a Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) dated December 23, 2021, which DTSC accepted on February 24, 2022. Geocon also prepared an Interim Removal Action Completion Report dated December 1, 2022, documenting the consolidation and clean fill capping of BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3 during Phase 1 construction.

## Regulatory status

Geocon's work was performed under the oversight of the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). DTSC reviewed and commented on Geocon's reports, including the SSI and HHRA Update. DTSC approved Geocon's Final Supplemental Site Characterization Workplan in September 2012. DTSC also approved Geocon's RDIP Variances in April and September 2020.

## Open items

A Final RACR will be submitted by Geocon following the completion of Ultimate Phase 2 construction of the SR 132 West Project, scheduled for 2028.
