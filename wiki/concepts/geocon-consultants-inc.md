Geocon Consultants, Inc. is the environmental and geotechnical consulting firm retained by Caltrans (and later by the City of Modesto as project proponent) to characterize, assess, and manage remediation of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] from at least 2012 through the interim closure of the site in 2022. Geocon's work spans supplemental site investigation, human health risk assessment updates, feasibility study and remedial action planning, remedial design implementation during highway construction, and the interim removal action completion reporting that documented containment of barium- and lead-affected soil beneath the State Route 132 West Project. Its findings and characterizations of [[barium]] and [[chromium]] distribution, and its determination that cadmium was not a chemical of potential concern, shaped the regulatory course of the project under [[dtsc]] and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board oversight.

## Supplemental Site Investigation and reinterpretation of the 2004 cadmium data

Following a July 26, 2012 meeting among Caltrans, DTSC, the Regional Water Quality Control Board, and Geocon, three data gaps were identified: fenceline sampling to assess offsite/vertical migration, perimeter sampling to define lateral stockpile limits, and additional sampling in areas of elevated cadmium detected by Shaw's 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation. Geocon completed 68 soil borings between September 25–28, 2012 (35 Fenceline Borings, 28 Perimeter Borings, and 5 Cadmium Borings at Stockpiles 2 and 3), submitted in the *Supplemental Site Investigation* report dated December 14, 2012 and revised March 1, 2013 in response to DTSC comments dated February 15, 2013.

The Cadmium Borings' results were consequential: cadmium was not detected above the 1.0 mg/kg reporting limit in any of 348 soil samples analyzed across the Geocon (ATL laboratory), Shaw 2006 (Creek Environmental Laboratories), and Shaw 2004 (Sparger Technology) data sets, including 19 samples with associated barium concentrations between 25,000 and 130,000 mg/kg. Because three independent laboratories produced consistent non-detects while only the 2004 Sparger data reported elevated cadmium (up to concentrations corresponding with barium of 25,800–196,000 mg/kg), Geocon concluded the 2004 cadmium results were "not reproducible nor reliable" and likely false positives from sample interference caused by the high barium matrix. This reinterpretation removed cadmium as a chemical of potential concern for the site, a determination Caltrans, DTSC, and CVRWQCB reviewed at a November 16, 2012 project meeting alongside a finding that Fenceline and Perimeter Boring barium concentrations were below residential CHHSLs and decreasing with depth, so no further lateral/vertical assessment of barium was warranted at that time.

One Cadmium Boring sample, CB3-9.0, was analyzed for petroleum hydrocarbons and PAHs based on field staining and odor; DRO was detected at 120 mg/kg (slightly above the residential/industrial ESL of 83 mg/kg) while GRO and ORO were below their respective screening levels, and PAHs detected were well below ESLs.

## HHRA Update and DTSC/HERO review

Geocon prepared the *Human Health Risk Assessment Update* (December 17, 2012), updating Shaw's 2007 HHRA using the 2012 Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Boring data. DTSC's Human and Ecological Risk Office reviewed the update and concluded the stockpiles pose no cancer risk or non-cancer hazard "as long as the stockpiles remain in place and are properly managed," while flagging that elevated chemical concentrations existed at depths greater than one foot and would need evaluation if the stockpiles were later graded, reworked, or removed — a condition that anticipated the later Interim Phase 1 construction.

## Remedial Design Implementation Plan and construction-phase verification (2019–2022)

Geocon's January 2019 *Remedial Design Implementation Plan* (RDIP) translated the Final Remedial Action Plan (2018) into construction-phase procedures for consolidating barium-containing soil (BCS) as highway embankment fill within Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones. The RDIP set BCS removal verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg barium (groundwater protection basis) and 80 mg/kg lead (DTSC residential screening level), against a 2019 construction-worker ESL for barium of 3,000 mg/kg.

Geocon executed this plan directly: it observed and photo-documented excavation and fill placement, performed removal verification and clean-fill-source sampling, conducted worker-zone and perimeter air monitoring, and reported results to Caltrans, DTSC, and CVRWQCB. At Stockpile 3, Geocon's March 13, 2020 technical memorandum documented that 24 verification samples (plus two berm samples) were collected between February 28 and March 12, 2020; three locations (S3 9-0 through S3 11-0) initially exceeded thresholds, were deepened approximately one foot, and resampled. Final excavation samples ranged from 46 to 970 mg/kg barium and 1.6 to 40 mg/kg lead — all below the RDIP thresholds — allowing Geocon to certify the Stockpile 3 excavation suitable for embankment fill without further monitoring.

Two DTSC-approved variances to the RDIP, both prepared by Geocon, addressed clean-cap material: an April 9, 2020 variance identifying clean fill borrow sources (including MSE wall footing excavations along Stockpiles 1 and 2) and establishing site-specific upperbound background concentrations of 120 mg/kg barium and 3.8 mg/kg lead for comparison against clean-fill characterization samples; and a September 2, 2020 variance clarifying that a 4-inch Class 3 aggregate base layer could substitute for the RDIP's specified capping material pending final pavement placement.

Geocon also managed a January 2021 storm-damage response: a record rain event eroded the clean-cap material on the unvegetated north slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2, and Geocon's oversight covered plastic sheeting placed as an interim measure, September 2021 regrading using approved Carpenter Road shoofly clean fill, and October 2021 hydroseed/erosion-blanket revegetation.

## Interim Removal Action Completion Report

Geocon submitted a Draft Interim RACR to DTSC on December 23, 2021; DTSC issued an acceptance letter dated February 24, 2022. The final December 2022 Interim RACR, prepared by Geocon under Project No. S1908-01-01, documented that BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3 (totaling an estimated 160,000 cubic yards) had been consolidated and capped as highway embankment fill ahead of SR 132's opening to traffic on November 3, 2022, and stated that a Final RACR would follow completion of Ultimate Phase 2 construction, then scheduled for 2028. Geocon also performed the April 2019 destruction of six of the ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, MW8) that conflicted with retaining wall and drainage construction, documented in its June 19, 2019 *Groundwater Monitoring Well Destruction Report*.
