# MW-8

MW-8 is one of the original eight monitoring wells installed at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in 2006, located furthest downgradient among the wells and situated within the shallow zone of the upper aquifer beneath the Modesto Formation. Across every sampling round from 2006 through 2019, MW-8's groundwater has consistently classified as sodium-dominant, distinguishing it from the calcium-dominant water found upgradient and beneath Stockpiles 1 and 2 — a pattern Geocon's reports interpret as evidence that MW-8's water type coincides with locations downgradient of the former FMC facility. MW-8 was decommissioned in 2019 as part of a well-reduction program agreed to by DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board after years of monitoring data showed no metals leaching from the stockpiles into groundwater.

## Geochemical character and dissolved metals

MW-8's Stiff diagrams were sodium-dominant in every reporting period identified in the record — 2006, March 2012, May 2012, September/November 2012, January, March, June, September, and December 2013, and February and June 2014 — with no recorded reversal to calcium-dominant water, unlike wells such as MW-5 and MW-9 that shifted between water types over the same period.

Dissolved barium, strontium, arsenic, chromium, and vanadium were reported in MW-8 samples across rounds, but none of the reported concentrations exceeded their numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water in any of the events described in the record. Lead was consistently not detected above the practical quantitation limit of 1.0 µg/l. Manganese was an occasional exception: it was reported above its secondary MCL of 50 µg/l in MW-8's May 2012 sample (identified in the March/May 2012 report as one of the wells, alongside MW-4, exceeding this threshold), though later individual-round reports (e.g., September 2012, November 2012) attribute manganese exceedances to other wells (MW-3, MW-9, MW-8) at different times — the December 2013 report specifically attributes a manganese exceedance to MW-6, not MW-8, indicating the exceeding well varied by round rather than establishing a persistent MW-8 problem.

## General minerals

MW-8's nitrate and TDS results were notable for remaining below their respective MCLs in rounds where many other site wells exceeded them. The September 2012 report gives MW-8 the lowest TDS of any well sampled that round, at 280 mg/l, against a secondary MCL of 500 mg/l — well under the exceedances recorded at MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10. Table data compiled in the February 2014 report show MW-8 nitrate and TDS results generally tracking below the primary and secondary MCLs (10 mg/l and 500 mg/l, respectively) across 2012–2014, in contrast to MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10, which were repeatedly flagged as exceeding one or both thresholds.

|---|---|---|
| Sep 2012 | 280 | none |
| Dec 2013 | 340 | none |

Sulfide was reported intermittently at low concentrations (e.g., 0.010 mg/l range) and was frequently non-detect in MW-8 samples, including the December 2013 and February 2014 rounds.

## Depth to groundwater and well status

MW-8 was purged and sampled using standard three-well-volume protocols (with disposable bailers in later years) through the 2012–2014 monitoring events without reported access or yield problems comparable to MW-5's chronic dry-purging issue. By April 2019, however, MW-8 was reported dry during that sampling round; a duplicate sample was nonetheless collected from MW-8 and mislabeled as non-existent well "MW-11" for QA/QC purposes, and the primary and duplicate results showed similar dissolved metals and general minerals concentrations, supporting the reliability of MW-8 data up to that point.

## Decommissioning

The January 30, 2026 DTSC letter (Site Code 900259) confirms that ten groundwater monitoring wells installed in 2012 were reviewed for years by DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board, which found no evidence of metals leaching from the stockpiles into groundwater and agreed to decommission six of the ten wells in 2019. MW-8 was among the original eight wells and was not one of the four wells addressed in the 2025 Well Destruction Completion Report (which covered the remaining wells still standing at that time), indicating MW-8 was one of the six wells destroyed in the 2019 round rather than surviving to the later destruction phase.
