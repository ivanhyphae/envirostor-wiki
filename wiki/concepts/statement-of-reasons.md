## Overview

The Statement of Reasons (SOR) is a document prepared by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) as part of the [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project. It identifies applicable or relevant and appropriate requirements for implementing the recommended remedial alternative and summarizes remedial investigations. The SOR is intended to be included as Appendix C in the Draft Final RAP.

## Site Record

The SOR summarizes investigations into primary contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in the stockpile soil, including barium, strontium, and lead. Additional COPCs tested for include antimony, arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, chromium, cobalt, copper, mercury, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, silver, thallium, vanadium, and zinc. The soil was also tested for polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, nitrate, sulfate, and sulfide. Underlying groundwater was tested for the same COPCs as the stockpile soil.

Toxicological risk to human health was quantified for individual and collective stockpiles, considering exposure routes of ingestion, inhalation, and dermal contact for current offsite residents and trespassers, future construction workers, future offsite residents, and hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

The 2007 Risk Assessment and the 2013 Risk Assessment Update addressed exposure to COPCs including arsenic, barium, beryllium, chromium (III & IV), cobalt, copper, lead, mercury, molybdenum, nickel, and zinc. Polycyclic aromatic compounds were not included in the risk assessment due to limited detection.

The DTSC prepared the revised Statement of Reasons on May 18, 2015, based on communication with Mr. Richard Stewart on April 23, 2015. The document is to be included as Appendix C in the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan.

## Regulatory Status

Both the 2007 Risk Assessment and the 2013 Risk Assessment Update concluded that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not present an [[no-unacceptable-risk-to-human-health-or-groundwater]]. This conclusion also applied to groundwater analysis.

The Final Feasibility Study (FS) and the Draft Final RAP recommend [[alternative-4]] for remediation. This alternative involves containing approximately [[160000-cubic-yards-of-fill]] of stockpile soil beneath roadway pavement, behind retaining walls, and behind [[bridge-abutments]]. An Operation and Maintenance plan (OMP), referenced in the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP) prepared by [[caltrans]], will include groundwater and surface water monitoring. DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board will review and concur with the RDIP and OMP before the recommended remedial action is implemented.
