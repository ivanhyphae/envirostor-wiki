## Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor

The Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor is a structure associated with the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]] in Modesto, California. Excavations for this foundation generated [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) that required management and assessment as part of the Stockpile 2 BCS Foundation Spoil Workplan Tech Memo.

## Site Record

Excavations for the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor, located at approximately Station 1138+60, were completed in July/August 2021. These excavations were 4.5 feet deep and generated approximately 2 cubic yards of BCS material that was spread on the adjacent ground surface. {{quote: "Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor (approximate Station 1138+60): 4.5 foot-deep foundation excavations completed in July/August 2021 generating approximately 2 cubic yards of BCS material that was spread on the adjacent ground surface"}}

The total estimated BCS material generated from both the weather station and overhead sign foundations is 40 cubic yards. {{quote: "The excavated BCS material generated from the weather station and overhead sign foundations (conservative estimate of 40 cubic yards)"}} This material was to be stockpiled prior to offsite landfill disposal or onsite reuse. The plan involved placing the pre-moistened BCS material on and covering it with minimum 6-millimeter-thick plastic sheeting, surrounded by erosion control measures. The stockpile cover was to be secured with ropes and weights. {{quote: "The stockpile cover will be secured with ropes and weights (i.e. sand bags, tires, etc.) as necessary."}}

Eight discrete soil samples were to be collected from the stockpile on a grid pattern at various depths. These samples would be composited into two 4-part composite samples for analysis. {{quote: "Eight discrete soil samples will be collected from the stockpile on a grid pattern at various depths. The samples will be collected in labelled stainless steel sample tubes, placed in a chilled cooler and transported to California Laboratory Services under chain-of-custody protocol. The laboratory will be instructed to composite the eight discrete soil samples into two 4-part composite samples."}} The analysis would include Title 22 metals, with specific methods for barium (EPA Test Method 6010B) and mercury (EPA Test Method 7471A). {{quote: "The composite soil samples will be analyzed for Title 22 metals following Environmental Protection Agency Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury) under expedited 48-hour turn-around time."}}

Soluble metals testing, including the Waste Extraction Test and Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure, might be required if total metal concentrations were elevated to determine if the BCS material was a California or Federal Resource Conservation and Recovery Act hazardous waste. {{quote: "Soluble metals testing using the Waste Extraction Test and Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure may be required for samples with elevated total metal concentrations to determine if the BCS material is a California or Federal Resource Conservation and Recovery Act hazardous waste."}}

The total barium and lead concentrations would be compared to site-specific background levels: Barium - 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and Lead - 3.8 mg/kg. {{quote: "The total barium and lead concentrations will be compared to the following site-specific background levels: Barium - 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) Lead - 3.8 mg/kg"}} If these concentrations did not exceed background levels, the BCS material could be suitable for onsite reuse with approval from Caltrans and [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]]. {{quote: "In the event the total barium and lead analytical data does not exceed respective site-specific background concentrations, the stockpiled BCS may be suitable for onsite reuse provided Caltrans and DTCS approval of the proposed reuse location."}} If onsite reuse was not approved, the BCS material would be profiled for acceptance at Forward Landfill in Manteca or another accepting landfill. {{quote: "In the event Caltrans and/or DTSC do not approve of onsite reuse of the regulated BCS stockpile soil, the BCS material will be profiled for acceptance at Forward Landfill in Manteca or other accepting landfil"}}

## Regulatory Status

The management and disposal/reuse of BCS material generated from the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor excavations are subject to Caltrans and [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] approval. {{quote: "In the event the total barium and lead analytical data does not exceed respective site-specific background concentrations, the stockpiled BCS may be suitable for onsite reuse provided Caltrans and DTCS approval of the proposed reuse location."}}
