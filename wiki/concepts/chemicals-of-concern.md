## Overview
The Chemicals of Concern (COCs) at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site are barium, lead, arsenic, vanadium, nickel, and chromium+6. These were identified in soil stockpiles generated during the construction of State Route (SR) 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. The excavation traversed a parcel previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC), a company that processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores, which is the basis for their inclusion as COCs.

## Site record
The primary Chemicals of Concern (COC) identified in the soil stockpiles are barium, lead, arsenic, vanadium, nickel, and chromium+6. These were identified in relation to soil stockpiles generated during the construction of State Route (SR) 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings. The excavation traversed a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel purchased from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC), which processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores and other minerals.

The Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Update in 2012 evaluated soil data collected at the fenceline and perimeters of the stockpiles. The maximum detected concentrations of chemicals of concern in these soil data were compared to surface soil concentrations evaluated in the 2007 risk assessment. The update concluded that the calculated risks and hazards from potential exposure to soil in the stockpiles remain virtually unchanged as currently managed, with cumulative cancer risks of less than one-in-a-million for all cancer-causing chemicals detected and a hazard index less than one for all hazardous chemicals detected.

The HHRA update also evaluated maximum concentrations of chemicals of concern detected in onsite shallow groundwater in 2012. These concentrations were found to be similar to those evaluated in the 2007 risk assessment, and the results of the risk assessment remain unchanged. No cancer-causing chemicals were detected in groundwater, and the hazard index was less than one.

Table 2, created by the HERO, lists chemicals detected in the soil stockpiles, identifies those used and manufactured at the FMC facility and considered toxic, and compares maximum concentrations to risk-based soil screening levels and local background concentrations. For arsenic, carcinogenic PAHs (cPAHs), nitrates, and vanadium, the maximum concentrations in the stockpiles are within the range of background concentrations. The maximum concentrations of barium, nickel, and strontium are less than their risk-based screening levels. The chromium+6 concentration is a calculated value that is greater than its risk-based US EPA RSL but less than its CHHSL. The HERO considers it unlikely that chromium+6 is present in the stockpiles due to the lack of historical use at the FMC facility or in the vicinity. Most metals listed in Table 2 exceed local background concentrations, but none were detected at levels approaching their risk-based levels.

## Regulatory status
The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), has reviewed groundwater monitoring reports for the site. DTSC concurs with the Groundwater Monitoring Report dated May 2012, prepared by Geocon Consultants.

DTSC requests that future groundwater monitoring reports include a discussion comparing groundwater monitoring results and concentration trends from previous sampling events, with graphs and maps depicting concentration trends of primary COCs over time. Groundwater monitoring is requested to continue on a quarterly schedule.

## Open items
Future groundwater monitoring reports are required to include a discussion comparing groundwater monitoring results/concentration trends from previous sampling events as more data becomes available. Graphs and maps should be prepared depicting concentration trends of primary COCs over time. All graphics, including existing "stiff" diagrams, should include a legend defining symbols/colors used to depict information. Groundwater monitoring should continue on a quarterly schedule.
