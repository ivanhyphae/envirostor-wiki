The [[draft-final-feasibility-study]] for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Geocon) on behalf of [[caltrans]]. This study evaluated potential remedial actions for the site, focusing on the soil stockpiles located south of the State Route (SR)-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) reviewed the draft and provided comments, which were addressed in the Final Feasibility Study. The [[central-valley-rwqcb]] was consulted during the process.

## Site Record

The Draft Final Feasibility Study was prepared by Geocon and dated December 18, 2013. DTSC provided comments on this draft study on February 11, 2014. The Final Feasibility Study, which incorporated revisions based on DTSC's comments, is titled "Final Feasibility Study Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County" and is dated June 24, 2014.

The Final Feasibility Study identified elevated barium concentrations in stockpile soil samples exceeding commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs). Cadmium concentrations exceeding the commercial/industrial CHHSL were also detected in soil samples collected from 8 of 25 borings in Stockpile 2 and from 2 of 10 borings in Stockpile 3 during a 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI).

A Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) conducted in 2007, and updated in 2012, evaluated potential risks associated with contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in the soil stockpiles. The HHRA concluded that the soil stockpiles, under current management practices, do not pose an unacceptable risk or hazard to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users. Specifically, for Stockpile 2, the HHRA reported a cancer risk of 1E-5 and a non-cancer hazard index (HI) of 0.1 for the offsite resident/trespasser exposed to surface soil. After excluding arsenic (due to similar background concentrations), the revised cancer risk estimate was 1E-7. For Stockpile 3, the estimated non-cancer HI for the offsite resident/trespasser exposed to surface soil was 0.02, with no cancer risk estimated. For future construction workers and offsite residents, the 2007 HHRA calculated cancer risk and non-cancer HI estimates of 9.2E-7 and 0.4, respectively, for exposure to soil at Stockpiles 1 through 3. For the future offsite resident receptor exposed to soil during construction, the calculated cancer risk and non-cancer HI were 6E-10 and 0.017, respectively.

The HHRA Update, using 2012 data, concluded that the 2007 HHRA remained valid. DTSC concurred with the HHRA Update findings in a letter dated April 4, 2013, with the condition that the stockpiles continue to be properly managed and not graded or reworked to expose deeper soil.

The Final Feasibility Study evaluated four alternatives: No Action, Institutional Controls, Removal (excavation and offsite disposal), and Containment. Alternative 4 – Containment, which involves utilizing the stockpiles as embankment material for the SR-132 Project, was recommended. This includes using the soil as fill for embankments, retaining walls, and bridge abutments. For Stockpiles 1 and 2, portions not contained beneath roadways or structures during the interim phase will be graded and capped with a 6- to 12-inch-thick vegetated, clean soil cap. Stockpile 3 is planned to be entirely contained within the interim progress phase of the project, placed in the stockpile fill consolidation zone for the SR-132 bridge over SR-99, and then potentially consolidated within Stockpile 2.

The estimated cost for removal and offsite disposal of Stockpile 3 alone was approximately \$2.65 million, assuming disposal in a Class II (non-hazardous) facility. The total estimated cost for removal and offsite disposal of all stockpiles and replacement with clean fill was approximately \$21.5 million.

## Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] has concurred with the Final Feasibility Study in a letter dated June 30, 2014. DTSC's concurrence was based on the study addressing their comments on the Draft Final Feasibility Study and the HHRA Update. DTSC's memo of December 17, 2009, indicated that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not pose an unacceptable risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or offsite residents. This management includes limiting access, prohibiting excavation/grading, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater monitoring.
