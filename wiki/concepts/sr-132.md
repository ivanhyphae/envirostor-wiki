The SR 132 project refers to a segment of State Route 99 and 132 in Stanislaus County, California, involving the remediation and management of soil stockpiles. These stockpiles originated from the construction of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. The soils were excavated from a parcel previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC), which had an evaporation pond and processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores. The primary chemicals of concern (COC) identified at this site are barium, lead, and strontium.

## Overview

The SR 132 project is intrinsically linked to the caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles-project, focusing on the remediation and management of contaminated soils generated during the construction of State Route 99 and the planned State Route 132 West Expressway. The project involves the characterization, consolidation, and capping of these stockpiles, primarily composed of barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially deposited lead (ADL) impacted soil. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) provides oversight for these activities.

## Site record

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles consist of three stockpiles, SP-1, SP-2, and SP-3, located at State Route 99 and State Route 132 in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. These stockpiles were generated during sub-grade construction of State Route 99 through Modesto, specifically from excavation near Kansas Avenue that removed tailings from a Food and Machinery Corporation (FMC) barite and celestite processing disposal pond. The stockpiles are impacted by hazardous substances, including barium, lead, and strontium. {{quote: "The Site is impacted by hazardous substances (metals), including barium, lead, and strontium."}} The stockpiles are located immediately adjacent to State Route 99 (east and west) and south of Kansas Avenue in Modesto. {{quote: "The stockpiles are immediately adjacent to State Route 99 (east and west) and south of Kansas Avenue in Modesto."}}

In February 2020, a sampling plan was proposed for characterizing soil intended for use as clean fill for the Highway 132 project. This soil was to be excavated from the upper five feet along the Highway 132 alignment, extending east from the [[carpenter-road-area]]. This area has been State right-of-way since the 1960s and was previously agricultural property. The proposed sampling plan involved 12 borings, each 5 feet deep, with analysis of six depth-discrete 4-point composite samples. The planned analyses included organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) and heavy metals for 0-2 feet samples, and heavy metals for 3-5 feet samples. Logs of test borings completed at Carpenter Road in 2017 were appended to document that the material at this location is native soil. Previous investigations, including a 2014 Initial Site Assessment (ISA) and a 2018 Phase II Environmental Site Assessment (ESA), did not report high-risk documented contamination releases in the immediate vicinity of the proposed clean fill source area. Testing of residential backyards adjacent and west of Carpenter Road indicated pesticides and metals at concentrations less than applicable regulatory screening levels.

During the construction of Interim Phase 1 of the SR 132 West Project, BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3 were consolidated and capped. Stockpile 3 was excavated and placed as embankment fill within the designated Stockpile 1 fill containment zone. {{quote: "A total of 3,075 truck-loads of BCS material totaling approximately 38,440 cubic yards (preliminary estimate based on 12.5 cubic yards per truck) was transported to Stockpile 1."}} Verification soil samples collected from the base of the Stockpile 3 excavation met the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) verification thresholds for barium (1,000 mg/kg) and lead (80 mg/kg). {{quote: "Each of the final excavation soil samples contained barium and lead below the RDIP verification thresholds. Barium concentrations ranged from 46 to 970 mg/kg, and lead concentrations from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg."}}

The western and eastern ends of BCS Stockpile 2 were removed between February and March 2020 to facilitate bridge overcrossing abutment construction. Verification soil samples collected from these areas, after initial deepening due to elevated barium concentrations, met the RDIP verification thresholds. {{quote: "Barium concentrations ranged from 54 to 760 mg/kg, and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg."}}

Clean fill cap material sources were evaluated and approved by DTSC. These included an area adjacent and east of Carpenter Road within the SR 132 right-of-way, and MSE wall footing excavations along the southern boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2. {{quote: "With the exception of the Bent 2 excavation stockpiles, each of the identified clean fill source areas was approved by DTSC for use as clean capping material over the BCS Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones."}}

Groundwater monitoring has been conducted in the vicinity of the BCS soil stockpiles. Four groundwater monitoring events were completed for wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10 between November 2019 and March 2021. {{quote: "Reported concentrations of the target analytes for the two groundwater sampling events were below regulatory thresholds consistent with historical data."}} Stormwater sampling events specific to the BCS stockpile areas were completed between December 2019 and December 2020, with results consistent with historical data. {{quote: "Stormwater originating from the BCS stockpiles and completed BCS Containment Zones was not observed to be migrating offsite."}}

## Regulatory status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) has reviewed and concurred with groundwater monitoring reports for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, for May, June, and July 2012. {{quote: "The [[dtsc]] has reviewed and concurred with the groundwater monitoring reports for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, for May, June, and July 2012."}} DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as data becomes available. {{quote: "DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as data becomes available."}}

The RDIP provided verification testing thresholds for BCS removal: Barium - 1,000 mg/kg (groundwater protection) and Lead - 80 mg/kg (residential screening level). {{quote: "Barium1 - 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg, groundwater protection) Lead - 80 mg/kg (residential screening level)"}} Site-specific upper-bound background concentrations for barium and lead were established for clean fill source area characterization: Barium - 120 mg/kg and Lead - 3.8 mg/kg. {{quote: "Barium - 120 mg/kg Lead - 3.8 mg/kg"}}

## Open items

Future groundwater monitoring reports are to be completed as groundwater data becomes available, as requested by DTSC. {{quote: "Future groundwater monitoring reports are to be completed as groundwater data becomes available, as requested by DTSC."}} A supplemental RDIP will be submitted to DTSC for approval prior to construction of Ultimate Phase 2 of the SR 132 West Project. {{quote: "Prior to construction of Ultimate Phase 2 of the SR 132 West Project, a supplemental RDIP will be submitted to DTSC for approval."}} A Final RACR will be submitted following completed construction of Ultimate Phase 2 of the SR 132 West Project. {{quote: "A Final RACR will be submitted following completed construction of Ultimate Phase 2 of the SR 132 West Project."}} Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the interim and final BCS containment measures. {{quote: "Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the interim and final BCS containment measures."}}
