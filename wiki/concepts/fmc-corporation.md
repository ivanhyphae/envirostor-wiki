Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) operated a chemical processing facility roughly 500 feet north of what is now the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site from 1929 to approximately 1985, processing barite and celestite ores and disposing of liquid tailings in unlined evaporation ponds. The stockpiles that are the subject of this remediation exist because the State, in 1961, purchased a portion of the FMC property containing those ponds for SR-99 construction and stockpiled the excavated pond tailings and underlying soil rather than disposing of them offsite. FMC's disposal practices are therefore the source of the contamination — principally barium — that has driven more than fifteen years of investigation, risk assessment, and remedial design at the Site, even though FMC itself is not a named responsible party in the RAP or RACR documents reviewed.

## Corporate and facility history

FMC and its predecessors — identified in the Final RAP as including Barium Products LTD and Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation — occupied property beneath and northeast of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange from the 1930s to the 1970s. The facility processed barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) ores to produce industrial chemicals used in greases, lubricating oil, and pigment blanks, generating sodium sulfide as a by-product that was sold as a caustic and reagent. From the early 1950s (per the RAP executive summary) or 1950s generally (per the site history section) to the late 1970s, liquid tailings from this processing were discharged to seven unlined evaporation ponds at the FMC facility.

## Transfer of contaminated material to Caltrans property

In 1961 the State of California purchased a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility, including a portion of the disposal ponds, to construct the SR-99 freeway through Modesto. During that construction, soil in and around one of the former FMC ponds was excavated and stockpiled within the current Caltrans right-of-way, intended at the time for a planned SR-99/SR-132 interchange. Pond tailings and underlying soil from the FMC property were combined with native soils excavated south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange to form Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3 as they exist today. Aerial photographs from 1963 and 1967 reviewed in the Final RAP confirm that Stockpiles 2 and 3 were in place by 1963 and all three stockpiles existed essentially in their current configuration by 1967.

## Contamination attributable to FMC operations

Because the stockpiles were built substantially from FMC pond tailings, the contaminants of potential concern identified in subsequent investigations — barium (the primary driver of remedial action) and, to a lesser extent, cadmium and PAHs — trace directly to FMC's ore-processing and tailings-disposal history rather than to any activity by Caltrans. The 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation found barium exceeding commercial/industrial CHHSLs in stockpile soil, and cadmium exceeding the commercial/industrial CHHSL in samples from Stockpiles 2 and 3; the 2006 Site Investigation did not confirm elevated cadmium, and cadmium was subsequently excluded as a COPC in the 2007 HHRA. Strontium, also processed at the FMC facility, was detected at a maximum of 231 mg/kg in the 2004 PSI — more than two orders of magnitude below the USEPA residential RSL of 47,000 mg/kg — and was excluded as a COPC; no CHHSL exists for strontium.

The hydrogeology of the FMC facility itself, approximately 1,100 feet (per the RAP's hydrogeologic conditions section) north of the Site, was characterized in a GeoTrans January 2005 report (*Addendum to Comprehensive Remedial Investigations Report*), which describes a shallow-zone/deeper-zone upper aquifer beneath the FMC property separated from a deeper aquifer by a blue clay aquitard, with groundwater flow to the southeast at a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft. The Final RAP notes that shallow aquifer conditions beneath the Caltrans stockpile site and the adjacent FMC facility are similar and considered representative of local hydrogeologic conditions, supporting the use of FMC-area hydrogeologic data to interpret groundwater flow beneath the stockpiles.

## Bearing on the Caltrans remediation

FMC's role in the case file is exclusively historical and causal: it establishes the origin and nature of the contamination (barium- and cadmium-bearing pond tailings, PAHs) that DTSC and CVRWQCB have overseen Caltrans in investigating and ultimately containing through incorporation of the stockpiles into SR-132 embankment fill. The 2018 Final RAP and subsequent Interim RACR documents describe FMC's former operations and the 1961 land transfer as background site history establishing why the material is present, but do not describe any FMC remediation obligation, cost recovery, or liability determination connected to the Caltrans stockpiles; no such action is recorded in the material reviewed.
