# FMC Corporation

## Overview

Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) and its predecessors (Barium Products LTD., Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation) operated chemical processing facilities on property beneath and northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, from the 1930s to 1970s. FMC is the industrial source responsible for the contamination present in the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles ([[stockpile-1]], Stockpile 2, Stockpile 3). The facility processed barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) ores for use in greases, lubricating oil, and pigment blanks, and generated sodium sulfide as a by-product.

From the 1950s to the 1970s, FMC discharged liquid residue ("tailings") to unlined evaporation ponds on its property. In 1961, the State of California purchased a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility — including a portion of the ponds — for construction of the SR 99 freeway. During SR 99 construction, pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the interchange, were placed in lifts to form bridge abutments and embankment fills, creating the three soil stockpiles totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards within current Caltrans right-of-way. The FMC facility is located approximately 1,100 feet north of the stockpile site and less than 500 feet north per the 2018 RAP.

A June 1963 aerial photograph documents the FMC facility, "Former Disposal Pond" areas, and the active placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3 during SR 99 construction. A 1967 aerial photograph confirms the facility and stockpiles in their essentially present-day configuration.

## Site Record

**Operational History and Materials:** An excerpt from the January 2005 *Addendum to the Comprehensive Remedial Investigation Report, FMC Corporation* lists raw materials, intermediates, and agricultural products used at the facility. DTSC considers arsenic, barium, and petroleum coke as the materials with toxic properties among those listed, with barium having "substantially less toxicity than arsenic and petroleum coke." PAHs and metals such as vanadium and nickel are commonly found components of petroleum coke. Sulfate, nitrate, and sulfide were salts used and produced by FMC.

**Chemical Linkage to Stockpiles:** The DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO), in a February 14, 2013 memorandum reviewing the HHRA Update, concluded that the investigation of the soil stockpiles "appears to include all the chemicals used at and produced by the FMC facility and considered toxic by the DTSC." Table 1 of that memorandum lists FMC materials reviewed. Table 2 identifies chemicals detected in the stockpiles and notes which were "used at FMC": specifically, arsenic, barium, nickel, vanadium, and cPAHs (where nickel, vanadium, and cPAHs are presumed components of petroleum coke). Barium is the primary contaminant of concern ([[barium-containing-soil]]).

**Hydrogeology:** The hydrogeology of the FMC facility was characterized in GeoTrans, Inc.'s January 2005 *Addendum to Comprehensive Remedial Investigations Report*, which described first-encountered groundwater at approximately 30 feet below ground surface under confined to semi-confined conditions, a deeper aquifer at 165 feet separated by a blue clay aquitard, and groundwater flow toward the southeast under a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft.

## Regulatory Status

The 2018 *Final Remedial Action Plan* identifies the FMC facility as the source of contamination requiring management. The DTSC-approved remedial alternative for the BCS stockpiles is containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering with structural pavement section and imported clean fill during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project.

A deed restriction will be recorded to preclude use of the property for residential, schools, daycare centers, and hospitals (RAP Section 25356.1(d)(2)).

## Open Items

No open items specific to FMC Corporation are documented in the project sources. The facility's hydrogeologic characterization and materials inventory from the 2005 GeoTrans addendum are treated as complete historical reference documents.
