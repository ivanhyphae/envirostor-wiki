FMC Corporation, formerly Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation, operated a chemical processing facility from 1929 to approximately 1985 on property located less than 500 feet north of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. This facility processed barium and strontium minerals, such as barite and celestite, to produce industrial chemicals. Liquid wastes from this operation were discharged to unlined ponds at the FMC facility from the 1950s to the 1970s. Soil excavated from around one of these former FMC ponds during the construction of SR-99 was used to create the stockpiles.

### Site History and Contaminants

The stockpiles were created in the early 1960s by importing soil from an FMC facility. This soil included pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange. The FMC facility processed barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate). From the 1950s to the 1970s, liquid residue ("tailings") from FMC was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds.

An Initial Site Assessment (ISA) conducted in 2003 identified a potential for the soil stockpiles to contain residual chemicals associated with the former FMC impoundments. A Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) in 2004 characterized the stockpiles and detected barium concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial [[california-chhsl]]s. Cadmium concentrations exceeding the commercial/industrial CHHSL were also detected in soil samples from Stockpile 2 and Stockpile 3.

A Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) in 2007 identified metals, notably [[barium]], and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) as primary contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in the soil stockpiles. Cadmium was not considered a COPC in the HHRA due to the lack of elevated concentrations identified during the 2006 Supplemental Site Investigation (SI). Strontium was also not considered a COPC as its maximum concentration was significantly below the United States Environmental Protection Agency's (USEPA) residential Regional Screening Level (RSL). The HHRA concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose an unacceptable risk to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

In response to the HHRA, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) requested additional toxicological and site information. A Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) was prepared in 2009, and DTSC concluded in December 2009 that the soil stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans, do not pose a risk to human health. DTSC recommended that Caltrans continue to limit access, maintain the perimeter fence, prohibit excavation or removal of soil, and maintain vegetative cover, while also continuing groundwater monitoring.

In 2012, Caltrans entered into a second interagency agreement with DTSC to further address the soil in Stockpiles 1 through 3, which included additional site characterization, risk evaluation, cleanup level determination, and preparation of a Remedial Action Plan (RAP).

Groundwater monitoring has been conducted since 2006, with wells installed adjacent to the stockpiles. Initial results from 2006 indicated that groundwater met drinking water standards (primary and secondary [[california-secondary-mcl]]s) for analyzed constituents. Groundwater monitoring has continued on a bi-monthly, quarterly, and annual basis since then.

### Remedial Actions

The DTSC-approved remedial alternative for the barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles was containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering them with a structural pavement section and imported clean fill during construction of the SR-132/SR-99 interchange. This approach is consistent with the Final RAP and is protective of human health and the environment, including groundwater. A deed restriction will be recorded to preclude the use of the property for residential, schools, daycare centers, and hospitals.

Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the interim Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS containment measures, focusing on monitoring and maintenance of implemented containment measures and potential exposure to contained and capped BCS/aerially deposited lead (ADL) soil materials.
