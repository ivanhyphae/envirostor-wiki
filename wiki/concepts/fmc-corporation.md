# FMC Corporation

## Overview

FMC Corporation, along with its predecessors Barium Products LTD. and Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation, operated chemical processing facilities in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, from the 1930s to the 1970s. {{quote: "Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) and its predecessors (Barium Products LTD., Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation) operated chemical processing facilities on property beneath and northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, from the 1930s to 1970s."}} FMC is identified as the industrial source responsible for contamination found in the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. {{quote: "FMC is the industrial source responsible for the contamination present in the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles ([[stockpile-1]], Stockpile 2, Stockpile 3)."}} The facility processed barite and celestite ores and generated sodium sulfide as a by-product. {{quote: "The facility processed barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) ores for use in greases, lubricating oil, and pigment blanks, and generated sodium sulfide as a by-product."}}

## Site Record

From the 1950s to the 1970s, FMC discharged liquid residue, referred to as "tailings," to unlined evaporation ponds on its property. {{quote: "From the 1950s to the 1970s, FMC discharged liquid residue ("tailings") to unlined evaporation ponds on its property."}} During the construction of SR 99 in 1961, a 4.3-acre parcel of the FMC facility, including a portion of these ponds, was acquired by the State of California. {{quote: "In 1961, the State of California purchased a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility — including a portion of the ponds — for construction of the SR 99 freeway."}} During this construction, tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils, were used to form bridge abutments and embankment fills, creating the three soil stockpiles. {{quote: "During SR 99 construction, pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the interchange, were placed in lifts to form bridge abutments and embankment fills, creating the three soil stockpiles totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards within current Caltrans right-of-way."}} The FMC facility is located approximately 1,100 feet north of the stockpile site. {{quote: "The FMC facility is located approximately 1,100 feet north of the stockpile site and less than 500 feet north per the 2018 RAP."}}

Aerial photographs document the FMC facility and the stockpiles. A June 1963 photograph shows the facility, "Former Disposal Pond" areas, and the active placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3. {{quote: "A June 1963 aerial photograph documents the FMC facility, "Former Disposal Pond" areas, and the active placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3 during SR 99 construction."}} A 1967 aerial photograph shows the facility and stockpiles in their "essentially present-day configuration." {{quote: "A 1967 aerial photograph confirms the facility and stockpiles in their essentially present-day configuration."}}

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) considers arsenic, [[barium]], and petroleum coke as materials with toxic properties among those used and produced at the FMC facility. {{quote: "DTSC considers arsenic, barium, and petroleum coke as the materials with toxic properties among those listed, with barium having "substantially less toxicity than arsenic and petroleum coke."}} PAHs, vanadium, and nickel are commonly found components of petroleum coke. {{quote: "PAHs and metals such as vanadium and nickel are commonly found components of petroleum coke."}} Sulfate, nitrate, and sulfide were salts used and produced by FMC. {{quote: "Sulfate, nitrate, and sulfide were salts used and produced by FMC."}}

A February 14, 2013 memorandum from the DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) concluded that the investigation of the soil stockpiles "appears to include all the chemicals used at and produced by the FMC facility and considered toxic by the DTSC." {{quote: "The DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO), in a February 14, 2013 memorandum reviewing the HHRA Update, concluded that the investigation of the soil stockpiles "appears to include all the chemicals used at and produced by the FMC facility and considered toxic by the DTSC."}} This memorandum lists chemicals detected in the stockpiles that were "used at FMC," including arsenic, barium, nickel, vanadium, and cPAHs. {{quote: "Table 2 identifies chemicals detected in the stockpiles and notes which were "used at FMC": specifically, arsenic, barium, nickel, vanadium, and cPAHs (where nickel, vanadium, and cPAHs are presumed components of petroleum coke)."}} [[barium]] is identified as the primary contaminant of concern. {{quote: "Barium is the primary contaminant of concern ([[barium-containing-soil]])."}}

The hydrogeology of the FMC facility was characterized in GeoTrans, Inc.'s January 2005 *Addendum to Comprehensive Remedial Investigations Report*. This report described first-encountered groundwater at approximately 30 feet below ground surface under confined to semi-confined conditions, a deeper aquifer at 165 feet separated by a blue clay aquitard, and groundwater flow toward the southeast with a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft. {{quote: "The hydrogeology of the FMC facility was characterized in GeoTrans, Inc.'s January 2005 *Addendum to Comprehensive Remedial Investigations Report*, which described first-encountered groundwater at approximately 30 feet below ground surface under confined to semi-confined conditions, a deeper aquifer at 165 feet separated by a blue clay aquitard, and groundwater flow toward the southeast under a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft."}}

## Regulatory Status

The 2018 *Final Remedial Action Plan* identifies the FMC facility as the source of contamination requiring management. {{quote: "The 2018 *Final Remedial Action Plan* identifies the FMC facility as the source of contamination requiring management."}} The DTSC-approved remedial alternative for the stockpiles is containment by incorporating them into the highway embankment fill for the [[sr-132]] West Freeway/Expressway Project, followed by covering with a structural pavement section and imported clean fill. {{quote: "The DTSC-approved remedial alternative for the BCS stockpiles is containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering with structural pavement section and imported clean fill during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project."}} A deed restriction will be recorded to prohibit the use of the property for residential, school, daycare, or hospital purposes. {{quote: "A deed restriction will be recorded to preclude use of the property for residential, schools, daycare centers, and hospitals (RAP Section 25356.1(d)(2))."}}
