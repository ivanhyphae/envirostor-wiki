# Nickel

## Overview

Nickel (Ni) is a metal identified as a presumed component of petroleum coke, a raw material used by the [[fmc-corporation]] facility. Its presence in the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was evaluated as part of the Title 22 metals suite during the Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) and Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA). While not designated as a primary chemical of potential concern (COPC) for the site, nickel is tracked due to its historical association with FMC operations.

## Site record

Nickel was included in the analytical testing for soil samples collected during the 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation. The analytical method employed was EPA Test Method 6010B (or 6020 for ICP-MS), with results reported in milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg).

A DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) memorandum dated February 14, 2013, lists nickel in Table 2, "Chemicals found in Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles." The HERO memorandum notes that nickel, along with vanadium and carcinogenic PAHs, are presumed components of petroleum coke, identified in the FMC Corporation's raw materials and intermediates list.

The HERO evaluation, presented in the February 14, 2013 memorandum, states that the maximum detected concentration of nickel across multiple site investigation events (2004, 2006, and 2012) was less than its risk-based screening levels.

In surface water samples collected on March 10, 2023, nickel was not reported above the MDLs in the background and surface water samples.

## Regulatory status

Applicable screening levels for nickel referenced in project documents include:

*   **California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs)** for residential and industrial land use, developed by the Cal/EPA Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment (OEHHA).
*   **U.S. EPA Regional Screening Levels (RSLs)** for residential properties.

The DTSC HERO memorandum (February 14, 2013) concluded that nickel concentrations in the soil stockpiles were below both the U.S. EPA residential RSL and the Cal/EPA residential CHHSL. The memorandum further states that "none of these chemicals were detected at levels approaching their risk-based levels."

The overall regulatory conclusion, as documented in the HERO memorandum, is that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to persons in the vicinity as long as they remain in place and are properly managed.

## Open items

The DTSC HERO memorandum includes a caveat that the evaluation is based on surface soil concentrations. There are areas in the stockpiles with elevated concentrations of chemicals at depths greater than one foot below ground surface. If there is substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles or if the stockpiles are removed, these elevated concentrations at depth will have to be evaluated with respect to the potential for exposure by residents living adjacent or near the stockpiles during the period when the soil is being moved. This statement applies to nickel and all other evaluated chemicals.

Additionally, the SSI Report notes that an update to the Shaw 2007 HHRA is being prepared, and a Remedial Action Plan (RAP) is under development. Future evaluation of nickel may be part of these ongoing activities, particularly if subsurface disturbance occurs during construction of the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project.
