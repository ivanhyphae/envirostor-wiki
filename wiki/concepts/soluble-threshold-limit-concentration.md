## Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC)

### Overview
The Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) is a regulatory metric under the California Code of Regulations (Title 22) used to classify hazardous waste based on the leachability of contaminants. For the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project (SR 132, Stockpile 2), STLC values were applied to determine whether stockpiled soil met the definition of hazardous waste for off-site disposal. The specific application of STLC to Stockpile 2 is documented in the *SR 132 Stockpile 2 BCS Removal Tech Memo*.

### Regulatory status
In California, STLC values are established for 22 metals and other constituents, with the most commonly cited being:
- Barium: 100 mg/L (as STLC)
- Cadmium: 1 mg/L
- Chromium (total): 5 mg/L
- Lead: 5 mg/L

The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and local Certified Unified Program Agencies (CUPAs) enforce these thresholds for waste classification.
