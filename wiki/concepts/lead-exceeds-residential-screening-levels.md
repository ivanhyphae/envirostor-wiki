## Lead Exceeds Residential Screening Levels

### Overview
Lead was identified as a chemical of concern (COC) in soil stockpiles generated during the [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]] at the [[sr-99-kansas-avenue-northbound-off-ramp]]. These stockpiles originated from native soils and pond tailings excavated in the early 1960s from a 4.3-acre parcel previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) Inc. The presence of lead, along with barium and strontium, was noted due to FMC's historical operations as a chemical manufacturing company processing barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores.

### Site Record
The [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]] involved the excavation of material from Stockpile No. 3, which was generated during the construction of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. This excavation traversed a portion of a parcel formerly owned by FMC Inc. The primary chemicals of concern identified in this material were barium, lead, and strontium.

### Regulatory Status
The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), reviewed information provided by Caltrans' contractors regarding the disposition of excavated material. This review included data from Geocon Consultants Inc. and Teichert Construction. The DTSC's concurrence was requested regarding the disposition of excavated material from the [[sr-99-kansas-avenue-northbound-off-ramp]] project, specifically in the vicinity of Stockpile No. 3. The DTSC's letter dated August 30, 2012, indicates that lead was a COC, but does not explicitly state that lead exceeded residential screening levels in this particular correspondence.
