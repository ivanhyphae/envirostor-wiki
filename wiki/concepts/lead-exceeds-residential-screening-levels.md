## Lead Exceeds Residential Screening Levels

### Overview
Lead was identified as a chemical of concern (COC) in soil stockpiles generated during the [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]]. These stockpiles originated from native soils and pond tailings excavated in the early 1960s from a parcel previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) Inc. The presence of lead, along with barium and strontium, was noted due to FMC's historical operations.

### Site Record
The [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]] involved the excavation of material from Stockpile No. 3, which was generated during the construction of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. This excavation traversed a portion of a parcel formerly owned by FMC Inc. The primary chemicals of concern identified in this material were barium, lead, and strontium.

In an email dated June 18, 2020, Richard C. Stewart of Caltrans informed Dean Wright of DTSC that a revised technical memorandum from Geocon described Stockpile 2 sampling. The email stated that a "120 mg/kg value, which is for barium not lead." This revision did not change the memo's determination that the upper three feet of soil from Stockpile 2 southerly slope MSE wall footing would need to be placed within designated containment zones, while soil below that depth was considered suitable as embankment fill or capping material. DTSC, via email dated June 22, 2020, accepted this memo, agreeing that soil down to 3 feet below ground surface (bgs) must be placed in designated containment zones.

The Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals (primarily barium, lead, and strontium), are located within state right-of-way south of Kansas Avenue. The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) proposed capping these stockpiles as part of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway construction project. If approved, the RAP would allow for the placement of impacted soil beneath roadway pavement, within bridge abutments, and behind retaining walls.

### Regulatory Status
The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC), in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], reviewed information regarding the disposition of excavated material. The DTSC's concurrence was requested regarding the disposition of excavated material from the [[sr-99-kansas-avenue-northbound-off-ramp]] project, specifically in the vicinity of Stockpile No. 3. A DTSC letter dated August 30, 2012, indicates that lead was a COC, but does not explicitly state that lead exceeded residential screening levels in that correspondence.

The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the stockpiles was reviewed by the DTSC and RWQCB and approved for public noticing. The RAP was prepared under the oversight of the DTSC and RWQCB.
