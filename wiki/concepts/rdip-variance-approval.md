# Rdip Variance Approval

The Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles required that clean cover fill sources be sampled and pre-approved by DTSC before placement over the consolidated barium-containing soil (BCS) in Stockpiles 1 and 2. As the SR 132 West Project's construction contractor, Bay Cities Paving and Grading, encountered practical obstacles to sourcing that cover material, Caltrans submitted a series of variance requests departing from the RDIP's original Section 4.7 sourcing and thickness specifications. DTSC and the Regional Water Board reviewed and approved each request, with the second approval imposing additional conditions rather than an unconditional acceptance.

## Carpenter Road source approval

Bay Cities initially identified an area adjacent and east of Carpenter Road, within the SR 132 right-of-way, as the clean fill source for capping Stockpiles 1 and 2. Geocon evaluated the planned highway underpass excavation area in this location and concluded, in a Technical Memorandum dated February 27, 2020, that it would generate soil suitable for use as clean cover fill. DTSC approved the Carpenter Road source area following this evaluation.

## MSE wall footing variance (Stockpile 1 and 2 southern boundaries)

A second clean fill source was identified at the mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall footing excavations, approximately five feet deep, along the southern boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2. Geocon evaluated the Stockpile 1 footing material after excavation of the southern slope BCS material (Technical Memorandum dated April 6, 2020) and found that the upper foot of the planned excavation contained elevated barium concentrations unsuitable for use as clean fill; this material was instead to be placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 permanent fill areas rather than used as cover.

Caltrans submitted the formal variance request letter on April 9, 2020 (transmitted April 13, 2020), addressed to DTSC's Dean Wright, proposing use of the deeper Stockpile 1 footing soil as clean cap material. Richard Stewart's transmittal noted Caltrans's expectation that, if approved, Bay Cities would use this MSE footing soil only as clean cover or embankment fill within the stockpile footprints, and would not use it elsewhere on the project or haul it off-site.

DTSC and the Water Board approved the request by email on April 17, 2020. The soil from 1 to 5 feet below existing grade along the Stockpile 1 southern boundary showed a calculated 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) of 113.1 mg/kg barium, which both agencies determined fell within site-specific naturally occurring background, making it acceptable as clean cap soil. The equivalent material along the Stockpile 2 southern boundary was not yet approved at that time: Dean Wright's email stated that approval or rejection for the Stockpile 2 footing soil would follow once that area's data was submitted and reviewed, and this email did not itself record that outcome.

## Class 3 aggregate subbase variance (second variance)

A second RDIP variance, dated September 2, 2020, proposed using Class 3 aggregate subbase material as clean cap over the BCS/Aerially Deposited Lead (ADL) stockpiles, on the basis that a minimum 4-inch layer would provide adequate cover pending completion of pavement and hardscape. DTSC and the Water Board approved use of Class 3 subbase as an acceptable clean cap alternative but did not accept the proposed 4-inch thickness. A visual inspection of the stockpiled Class 3 material on September 15, 2020 found it to be primarily fine sand, with most grains likely small enough to pass a No. 4 sieve, and both agencies concluded that a minimum 6-inch cap thickness was warranted instead of 4 inches, with increased monitoring potentially necessary — a concern the agencies tied explicitly to the absence of any pavement or hardscaping before the upcoming rainy season.

The approval also imposed a verification sampling condition: a single composite sample from the Class 3 stockpile adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing was to be analyzed for barium and lead by EPA Test Method 6010B, to confirm the material met RDIP standards before its use as subbase cover over the BCS/ADL stockpiles. Dean Wright's September 21, 2020 email served as DTSC's official acceptance of the Class 3 subbase request, conditioned on the revised 6-inch thickness and the Rosemore composite sample; the available record does not show the results of that sample or confirmation that the condition was satisfied.
