Stockpile 3 Excavation refers to two distinct removal actions at the same location on the east side of SR 99 between Kansas Avenue and the Modesto Irrigation District (MID) water canal: a 2012 partial excavation tied to a Kansas Avenue off-ramp safety improvement, and a February–March 2020 complete removal of the remaining barium containing soil (BCS) mass conducted under the SR 132 West Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP). The 2012 action removed and disposed of a limited volume offsite as non-hazardous soil; the 2020 action removed all remaining BCS and relocated it onsite into the Stockpile 1 containment zone, after which DTSC accepted the area as suitable for embankment fill with no further RDIP monitoring. Because the excavation exposed native soil and berm material directly beneath and adjacent to what is now SR 132 right-of-way, the confirmation sampling results here established that this ground could be built on without a cap or continuing institutional controls, distinguishing it from Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2, which were consolidated and remain subject to capping and O&M requirements.

## 2012 Kansas Avenue ramp excavation

A portion of Stockpile 3 was excavated between September 7 and 26, 2012 to allow construction of a retaining wall and laying back of the embankment slope for the Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp safety improvement on SR 99. Approximately 2,800 yd3 of stockpile material was removed over ten days and loaded directly into covered trucks for disposal as non-hazardous soil at the Forward Class II landfill in Manteca, California, under non-hazardous waste manifests. This disposition was based on eight direct-push borings and eleven hand-auger borings within the embankment, which detected barium ranging from 34 to 1,600 mg/kg — all below the residential and commercial/industrial CHHSLs of 5,200 and 63,000 mg/kg, respectively. On this basis, and after Caltrans requested DTSC's concurrence in a letter dated May 29, 2012, DTSC conveyed in an August 30, 2012 letter that offsite management of the Stockpile 3 soil did not pose a threat to human health or the environment. Ambient perimeter air monitoring during excavation, using pDR-1200 real-time particulate counters against a Fence Line Total Dust Action Level of 4.0 mg/m3, along with laboratory air samples, indicated airborne lead and barium remained well below levels of concern. This action is documented in the Stockpile 3 Excavation Summary Report dated March 15, 2013, and left the remainder of the Stockpile 3 mass in place pending the later SR 132 project.

## 2020 complete removal and relocation

Stockpile 3 was completely removed between February and March 2020 to support construction of the SR 132 West Expressway. Excavated BCS material was loaded into transport trucks, decontaminated and tarped before leaving the Stockpile 3 Exclusion Zone, and hauled for placement as embankment fill within the designated Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone. Reported volumes for this haul differ between documents: the Interim RACR narrative states approximately 3,075 truckloads totaling approximately 27,675 yd3 (at 9 yd3/truck), while the March 13, 2020 Technical Memorandum states the same 3,075 truckloads totaled approximately 38,440 yd3 (a preliminary estimate at 12.5 yd3/truck). The excavation reached native soil at approximate elevations of 82.5 to 84 feet above MSL.

## Confirmation sampling and threshold exceedances

Twenty-nine verification soil samples were collected from 26 locations (S3-1 through S3-26) within the removal area. Samples S3 1-0 through S3 24-0 were collected from native soil exposed at the base of the excavation, with the exception of S3 16-0, which came from storm drain trench backfill material adjacent to SR 99 rather than native soil. Two additional samples, S3 SE Berm and S3 SW Berm, were collected from the top of the berm at the southern end of the excavation adjacent to the MID canal. Each sample was analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories for barium and lead by EPA Test Method 6010B, against RDIP verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg for barium (groundwater protection) and 80 mg/kg for lead (residential screening level).

Initial results showed barium above the 1,000 mg/kg threshold at three locations — identified in the technical memorandum as S3 9-0 through S3 11-0 — with concentrations ranging from 2,200 to 7,000 mg/kg, well above the verification level. These three excavation areas were deepened approximately one foot and resampled.

| Sampling stage | Barium range (mg/kg) | Lead range (mg/kg) | Result vs. thresholds (1,000 / 80 mg/kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial (3 exceedance locations) | 2,200 – 7,000 | not separately reported | Barium exceeded threshold |
| Final, all locations after re-excavation | 46 – 970 | 1.6 – 40 | Below both thresholds |

After the one-foot deepening and resampling, all final verification samples fell below both thresholds, with barium ranging from 46 to 970 mg/kg (the highest result, 970 mg/kg, still below the 1,000 mg/kg limit) and lead from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg. This closed out the exceedance without requiring excavation beyond an additional foot at the three affected locations.

## Regulatory review and disposition

The results were documented in the DTSC-approved Stockpile 3 BCS Removal Verification Testing Technical Memorandum, dated March 13, 2020, prepared by Geocon (John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG) and transmitted by Caltrans (Richard C. Stewart, PG) to DTSC, the Central Valley RWQCB, and DTSC staff on March 16, 2020. DTSC's Dean Wright responded by email on March 18, 2020, stating that DTSC had reviewed the memorandum and agreed the Stockpile 3 BCS had been removed, that the remaining soil met the RDIP verification thresholds, and that no further BCS RDIP monitoring requirements were necessary for the area. This determination made the former Stockpile 3 footprint suitable for placement of clean embankment fill and other highway improvements without a cap or ongoing monitoring — a materially different outcome from Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2, which required consolidation, a minimum 12-inch clean soil cover or 4-inch aggregate base layer, and are subject to an Operation and Maintenance Plan and Land Use Covenant. The Stockpile 3 removal and its verification data were incorporated into the Removal Action Completion Report – Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Interim Phase 1 State Route 132 West Project, dated December 1, 2022, which DTSC (in consultation with the Central Valley RWQCB) accepted as final by letter dated January 18, 2023. That acceptance covered the completed removal, consolidation, and capping activities under the approved Remedial Action Plan, RDIP, and two RDIP variance requests approved April 17, 2020 and September 21, 2020, and marked the conclusion of the Interim Phase 1 effort pending final RAP certification after the Ultimate Phase 2 SR 132 build-out.
