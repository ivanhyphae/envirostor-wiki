Institutional controls are administrative and legal measures employed to restrict access to the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] and limit potential exposure to contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in the soil. These controls do not reduce the toxicity, mobility, or volume of contaminants but can be implemented alongside other remedial actions to achieve remedial action objectives (RAOs).

## Site Record
The stockpiles are currently managed under institutional controls, which include:
*   A maintained perimeter chain-link fence.
*   Access restriction signage posted in conspicuous locations.
*   Vegetative cover that is mowed twice yearly to reduce fire danger.
*   Quarterly groundwater monitoring and reporting.
*   Stormwater monitoring performed on a storm-specific basis.

These ongoing management activities and site conditions constitute institutional controls. Discontinuing these would represent a "no action" scenario.

Alternative 2, Institutional Controls, as evaluated in the Feasibility Study (FS), involves:
*   Limiting access to only [[caltrans]]-authorized personnel.
*   Inspecting and maintaining the chain-link fence.
*   Prohibiting any activities involving excavation/grading, off-site removal of soil, or placement of other soil on the Site.
*   Maintaining the current vegetative cover.
*   Continuing to maintain the groundwater monitoring system.
*   Monitoring and maintaining the site perimeter fence to restrict access.
*   Continuing to maintain the vegetative cover to minimize erosion and potential offsite transport via wind or stormwater.
*   Potential use of informational technologies such as public notification via site signage, published notices, and public meetings.
*   Potential use of governmental and administrative controls such as a deed restriction and land use covenant to prevent development for unsuitable uses.
*   Continuing to monitor groundwater quality.

The estimated annual cost for ongoing maintenance and monitoring under this alternative (fence maintenance, mowing, quarterly groundwater monitoring, and weather-dependent stormwater monitoring) is approximately \$50,000. This cost is considered low to moderate and is the second least costly of the four alternatives evaluated. The planned completion of ultimate build-out for the SR-132 Project is in 2028. Some monitoring activities are assumed to be discontinued after the interim progress phase is completed in 2018.

## Regulatory Status
The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) indicated in a memo dated December 17, 2009, that the stockpiles in their current condition do not pose an unacceptable risk to human health for [[caltrans]] workers, trespassers, or offsite residents, based on continued management.

This alternative complies with State and/or Federal regulations under the site's current inactive (but maintained and monitored) use, provided the site remains fenced, its vegetative cover is maintained, and groundwater quality monitoring continues.

The DTSC and [[central-valley-rwqcb]] have accepted this alternative for the short-term, with the understanding that [[caltrans]] is proceeding with the planned construction of the SR-132 project, which will encapsulate the stockpiles (Alternative 4). The [[central-valley-rwqcb]] has indicated that the stockpiles would need to be maintained to protect groundwater quality if the SR-132 Project were not constructed.

## Open Items
A more proactive remedial action is likely preferred by the community due to the perception of some degree of health risk or threat to the environment.
