# Stockpile No. 3

## Overview
Stockpile No. 3 is a soil stockpile generated during the [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]] along SR 99 at Kansas Avenue in Modesto, California. This stockpile, consisting of excess native soils and pond tailings from early 1960s SR 99 construction, was found to contain [[arsenic]] and [[lead]] at concentrations exceeding residential and commercial/industrial screening levels. Its management and disposition were reviewed and approved by the [[dtsc-approval-letter]].

## Site Record
Stockpile No. 3 contained arsenic at a concentration of 15.1 mg/kg and lead at a concentration of 110 mg/kg. These concentrations exceeded the residential and commercial/industrial screening levels.

Additional investigations in the area to be excavated at Stockpile No. 3 for the ramp project indicated that, with the exception of arsenic, none of the metals were reported at concentrations at or above commercial/industrial screening levels. The arsenic concentration in samples ranged from less than 1.0 mg/kg to 3.2 mg/kg. The 95% upper confidence limit concentrations for barium, strontium, and lead in the area to be excavated in Stockpile No. 3 were below residential and commercial/industrial screening levels.

One soil sample from Stockpile No. 3 had a barium concentration higher than 10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC), and five soil samples had lead concentrations at or higher than 10 times the STLC. However, the calculated 95% Upper Confidence Level for barium and lead was well below 10 times the respective STLC. Further analysis using the Waste Extraction Test (WET) showed these samples fell below the STLC, classifying the material as non-hazardous.

Approximately 2800 cubic yards of material were excavated from the northwestern end of Stockpile No. 3 as part of the ramp project. This excavated material, along with other excavated material, was transported offsite.

## Regulatory Status
The [[dtsc-approval-letter]] dated August 30, 2012, provided DTSC's concurrence regarding the disposition of excavated material from Stockpile No. 3. Caltrans had requested DTSC's concurrence on the management of this material, noting that elevated metal concentrations were present but below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels. Based on the site investigation data and the 2009 Preliminary EA, the soil material was deemed suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as a non-hazardous soil.
