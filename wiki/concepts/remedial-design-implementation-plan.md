# Remedial Design Implementation Plan

## Overview
The **Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP)** is the technical document that governed the interim containment of [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) from Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3 during [[interim-phase-1]] construction of the SR 132 West Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and dated January 30, 2019, the RDIP provided the detailed remedial design, BCS management procedures, construction monitoring protocols, and regulatory reporting requirements for implementing the remedy selected in the Final remedial-action-plan (RAP, Geocon, 2018). The RDIP was specific to soil stockpile containment measures for Interim Phase 1; a supplemental RDIP will be prepared for Ultimate Phase 2 construction.

The selected remedial alternative was consolidation and containment by incorporating the three stockpiles as highway embankment fill, then covering them with clean fill and structural pavement section. The RDIP was implemented under the regulatory oversight of the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in cooperation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), under a Caltrans Interagency Agreement.

## Site record

### Verification thresholds
The RDIP established the following soil excavation verification thresholds for BCS removal areas:

- **Barium:** 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg), based on groundwater protection. (Note: the regulatory residential Environmental Screening Level for barium is 3,000 mg/kg, and the construction worker ESL is also 3,000 mg/kg.)
- **Lead:** 80 mg/kg, based on the DTSC residential screening level.

### Clean fill source criteria
The RDIP provided for sampling and analytical testing of planned clean fill source areas within the SR 132 West Project boundaries in accordance with DTSC's *Information Advisory Clean Imported Fill Material* (2001). Two DTSC-approved variances to the RDIP were subsequently issued:

- **Variance #1 (April 9, 2020, approved April 17, 2020):** Identified planned clean fill source borrow areas, including MSE retaining wall foundation excavations adjacent to the southerly boundaries of BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2. This variance established site-specific upper-bound background concentrations for comparison with proposed clean fill source characterization samples:
  - Barium: 120 mg/kg
  - Lead: 3.8 mg/kg

- **Variance #2 (September 2, 2020, approved September 21, 2020):** Clarified the alternative use of a 4-inch-thick layer of Class 3 aggregate subbase over the top of the completed BCS Containment Zones, in lieu of the 4-inch-thick aggregate base (AB) specified in the original RDIP. The structural pavement section for Interim Phase 1 consists of 0.85 feet (10.2 inches) concrete pavement, 0.25 feet (3.0 inches) hot mix asphalt, and 0.70 feet (8.4 inches) Class 3 aggregate subbase. DTSC accepted that the Class 3 aggregate subbase would provide adequate interim clean cover until the remaining pavement section and hardscape are placed.

### Containment design
The RDIP specified that the three BCS Stockpiles would be contained within SR 132 highway embankment fills designated as the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones, located between SR 99 and the west end of Stockpile 1. Excavated BCS for placement came from:

- Complete removal of Stockpile 3 (east side of SR 99)
- Bridge abutment excavations at either end of Stockpile 2
- MSE retaining wall foundation excavations along the southern boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2

The RDIP also provided for placement of shallow soil excavated along the northbound SR 99 shoulder containing hazardous waste concentrations of [[aerially-deposited-lead]] (ADL) within the BCS Containment Zones.

### Clean capping requirements
Section 3.2.2 (and Section 4.7) of the RDIP required capping the surface of completed BCS and ADL consolidation zones with a minimum 12-inch-thick clean fill material consisting of approved imported fill, or alternatively a minimum 4-inch-thick AB layer on the upper stockpile surface pending subsequent placement of structural pavement and hardscape. East- and west-facing temporary slopes were also included in the capping requirement.

### BCS removal and verification results

**Stockpile 3:** Completely removed between February and March 2020. Approximately 3,075 truckloads (27,675 yd³ based on 9 yd³ per truck) of BCS material were transported to the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. Twenty-nine verification soil samples were collected from 26 locations (S3-1 through S3-26). Three initial samples exceeded the barium verification threshold (2,200 to 7,000 mg/kg); those areas were deepened approximately one foot and resampled. Final barium concentrations ranged from 46 to 970 mg/kg, and lead from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg — all below thresholds.

**Stockpile 2 (west and east ends):** Removed between February and March 2020. Ten verification samples from eight locations (S2W-1 through S2W-8) at the west end; fourteen samples from twelve locations at the east end. Four initial samples exceeded the barium threshold (1,100 to 4,100 mg/kg); those areas were deepened and resampled. Final barium concentrations ranged from 54 to 760 mg/kg, and lead from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg.

**MSE wall footing excavations (Stockpiles 1 and 2):** Southerly slopes were excavated (steepened) in March and April 2020. Soil within the footing areas was sampled at one-foot intervals to maximum depths of 3 to 5 feet, at approximate 200-foot intervals. For Stockpile 1, barium was detected in all 15 samples at 42 to 420 mg/kg — above the 120 mg/kg site-specific background but below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold. For Stockpile 2, barium was detected in all 29 samples at 53 to 930 mg/kg; 14 of 29 samples exceeded the 120 mg/kg background, with elevated concentrations generally in the upper 3 feet. Based on these results, all excavated MSE wall footing material was placed in the BCS Containment Zones rather than used as clean cap.

### Air monitoring
The RDIP specified air monitoring protocols including a Fence Line Action Level for total dust of 4.0 milligrams per cubic meter (mg/m³). Air monitoring was performed between February and June 2020 during BCS grading activities. Real-time MIE pDR-1200 monitors were used to verify that "wet methods" dust control was effective. Barium results were compared to an action level of 25 micrograms per cubic meter (µg/m³) from the contractor's Health & Safety Plan (Adanta, November 2019). Lead results were compared to the California Air Resources Board 30-day average ambient standard of 1.5 µg/m³. All monitoring results were below project action and risk levels.

### Preconstruction meeting
An RDIP preconstruction meeting was held on January 29, 2020, with representatives of Caltrans, the City of Modesto, DTSC, WSP, Bay Cities Paving & Grading (BCPG), and Geocon. Discussion items included communication protocols, health and safety, stormwater BMPs, monitoring well protection, decontamination protocols, BCS and ADL excavation/placement/containment, dust mitigation, air monitoring, stop work authority, and tarping of transport trucks.

## Regulatory status
The RDIP was implemented under DTSC regulatory oversight with CVRWQCB cooperation. DTSC accepted the Draft Interim RACR (February 24, 2022) as a technical status report confirming work was accomplished per the approved RDIP and its two variances. A site survey on June 28, 2022 confirmed completed clean capping and project compliance with the RDIP. Certification of the RAP will not occur until after Ultimate Phase 2 is completed.

## Open items
- **Supplemental RDIP for Ultimate Phase 2:** Must be submitted to DTSC for approval prior to Phase 2 construction. The supplemental RDIP will provide monitoring and sampling requirements for any planned BCS disturbance, groundwater monitoring well abandonment/protective measures, and copies of final grading and improvement design plans.
- **Final RACR:** To be submitted following completion of Ultimate Phase 2 construction (scheduled for 2028).
