# MW-4

## Overview
MW-4 is a groundwater monitoring well located at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). It is part of the site's groundwater monitoring network, utilized for quarterly sampling events.

## Site record

### June 2014 sampling event
During the June 2014 sampling event, MW-4 exhibited a calcium-dominant groundwater composition, as indicated by Stiff diagrams. This classification places MW-4 within a group of wells (including [[mw-1]], [[mw-2]], [[mw-7]], [[mw-9]], and MW-10) that consistently show this characteristic. The groundwater from MW-4 is described as bicarbonate (HCO₃) dominant.

Total dissolved solids (TDS) in MW-4 were reported at a concentration exceeding the secondary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 500 mg/l. The specific concentration for MW-4 is not separately tabulated in the source text, but it is listed among wells exceeding this threshold.

Sulfide was detected in MW-4 groundwater during the June 2014 event. A laboratory report (Lab ID: 1401663-03) documents total sulfide at a concentration of ND (Not Detected) with a reporting limit of 0.020 mg/l, analyzed on June 5, 2014.

No dissolved metals concentrations in MW-4 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values during the June 2014 sampling event. Barium and strontium concentrations were reported at levels similar to historical data and significantly below their numeric water quality thresholds.

### September 2013 sampling event
During the September 2013 groundwater monitoring event, MW-4 also exhibited a calcium-dominant composition, consistent with samples from wells [[mw-1]], [[mw-2]], [[mw-7]], and [[mw-9]]. Groundwater from MW-4 had been consistently reported as calcium-dominant since monitoring resumed in March 2012 through the September 2013 event.

Nitrate as nitrogen was reported at a concentration that equaled or exceeded the primary MCL of 10 mg/l. Total dissolved solids (TDS) was reported at a concentration exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l. Specific numerical values for nitrate and TDS in MW-4 are not separately tabulated in the source text for this event; the narrative establishes that MW-4 was among the wells exceeding both thresholds.

Sulfide was detected in MW-4 groundwater during this event. A laboratory report for Lab ID 1302692-03 (Client Sample ID MW-4) documents total sulfide at 0.019 mg/l, reported on September 13, 2013.

No dissolved metals concentrations in MW-4 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values. Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the practical quantitation limit (PQL) of 1.0 µg/l.

## Groundwater chemistry classification
Stiff diagrams for MW-4 consistently indicate bicarbonate (HCO₃) dominant water, with a calcium-dominant cation composition. This ionic composition has shown only very slight changes since monitoring resumed in March 2012 through June 2014. The calcium-dominant classification places MW-4 within the group of wells (along with MW-1, MW-2, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10) that consistently show this signature. This contrasts with the sodium-dominant wells (mw-3, [[mw-6]], mw-8, and, during the 2013 event only, [[mw-5]]).

## Regulatory status
For the June 2014 event, MW-4 groundwater exceeded one applicable standard:
*   **TDS**: Exceeds the California Department of Public Health secondary MCL of 500 mg/l (taste and odor).

For the September 2013 event, MW-4 groundwater exceeded two applicable standards:
*   **Nitrate**: Exceeds the California primary MCL of 10 mg/l.
*   **TDS**: Exceeds the California Department of Public Health secondary MCL of 500 mg/l (taste and odor).

The source notes that [[mw-1]], the upgradient monitoring well, also reported nitrate (15 mg/l in June 2014) and TDS (590 mg/l in June 2014) exceedances. This suggests these concentrations may reflect natural background levels for shallow groundwater in the vicinity of the site rather than site-related impacts. Whether this background interpretation extends to MW-4 is not explicitly stated in the provided text.

No dissolved metals exceedances were identified for MW-4 in either the June 2014 or September 2013 sampling events.
