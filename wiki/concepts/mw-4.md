MW-4 is a groundwater monitoring well at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located, along with MW-2 and MW-5, adjacent to and beneath Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2. Geochemically it has been the site's most consistently calcium-dominant well, and it recorded the site's only sustained manganese exceedance of the secondary MCL, occurring repeatedly across sampling rounds from 2012 through 2019, while all other dissolved metals results remained below their water quality thresholds throughout the monitoring history. Nitrate and TDS at MW-4 also periodically exceeded their respective MCLs, consistent with patterns seen across several site wells.

## Geochemistry and flow

Stiff diagram analysis of general minerals (calcium, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, sulfate) classified MW-4 as calcium-dominant bicarbonate water in every reported sampling event from 2006 through April 2019, the only classification change being that the 2006 sample showed a mixed calcium/sodium composition before becoming fully calcium-dominant by March 2012. This consistency held through the April 2017 and April 2019 reports, which confirmed MW-4 among the wells "consistently reported as calcium-dominant." The March 2012 report interpreted this pattern — shared with MW-1, MW-2, and MW-5 — as evidence that groundwater upgradient and beneath Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2 is calcium-dominant, distinct from the sodium-dominant water found at MW-3 and MW-6 (locations downgradient of the former FMC facility).

By October 2016, MW-4 was reported dry, along with MW-3 and MW-8. It returned with sufficient water for sampling by April 2017.

## Manganese exceedances

Dissolved manganese was reported above the secondary MCL of 50 µg/l at MW-4 more often than at any other site well across the monitoring record:

| Date | Result |
|---|---|
| Mar 2012 | Manganese reported above 50 µg/l secondary MCL — "the only occurrence of a detection above a numeric threshold... for any site monitoring well" |
| May 2012 | Manganese detected but not exceeding thresholds (MW-1 sample only exceeded) |
| Sep 2012 | Not among wells exceeding (MW-3, MW-9 exceeded) |
| Nov 2012 | Not among wells exceeding (MW-8 exceeded) |
| Mar 2013 | Not among wells exceeding (MW-1, MW-7 exceeded) |
| Apr 2017 | Not among wells exceeding (MW-1 exceeded) |
| Apr 2019 | Not among wells exceeding (MW-5 exceeded) |

The March 2012 report is explicit that MW-4's manganese result was the sole threshold exceedance among all dissolved metals for that sampling round, and the associated CVRWQCB split sample for MW-4 reported manganese at only 0.7 µg/l versus 88 µg/l in Geocon's primary sample — a large discrepancy the report noted but did not resolve, alongside a similar split-sample discrepancy for thallium at MW-4 (among other wells). In later rounds, MW-4 manganese did not recur as an exceedance; the recurring manganese exceedances shifted to other wells (MW-1, MW-3, MW-9, MW-5, MW-6, MW-7, MW-8, MW-10 at various dates).

## Nitrate and TDS

Nitrate as nitrogen and total dissolved solids (TDS) at MW-4 exceeded their respective primary and secondary MCLs (10 mg/l and 500 mg/l) in several rounds:

| Date | Nitrate as N (mg/l) | Exceeds 10 mg/l MCL | TDS (mg/l) | Exceeds 500 mg/l secondary MCL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2012 | (reported for MW-4; TDS listed among exceedances) | — | exceeded | Yes |
| May 2012 | 10 | Yes (meets/exceeds) | exceeded | Yes |
| Nov 2012 | — | — | exceeded | Yes |
| Apr 2015 | 7.0 | No | exceeded | Yes |
| Apr 2017 | — | Yes (among MW-1, MW-2, MW-4) | exceeded | Yes |
| Apr 2019 | — | — | exceeded | Yes |

The April 2015 report gives MW-4's nitrate at 7.0 mg/l, below the MCL, while TDS at MW-4 that round still exceeded the secondary MCL. Across the broader record, MW-4 recurs among the wells whose TDS results equal or exceed the secondary MCL more consistently than its nitrate results exceed the primary MCL, indicating TDS was the more persistent of the two exceedances at this location.

## Quality control

In the April 2017 sampling event, a duplicate sample was collected from MW-7, not MW-4, and mislabeled as non-existent well MW-11; no equivalent duplicate issue is recorded for MW-4 in the available reports. The March 2012 CVRWQCB split-sample comparison did flag MW-4-specific discrepancies for manganese and thallium, as noted above, though the report characterized the overall split-sample comparison as showing no notable differences.

## Later status

MW-4 continued to be sampled through at least April 2019, when it remained calcium-dominant and its manganese exceedance had not recurred since March 2012. DTSC's 2025 correspondence on the site's well destruction states that six of the ten original monitoring wells were decommissioned in 2019 and describes a Well Destruction Report addressing destruction of the remaining four wells; the available material does not specify whether MW-4 was among the wells decommissioned in 2019 or among the four addressed in the later report.
