## Overview

MW-4 is a groundwater monitoring well at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. It has been sampled and analyzed as part of various groundwater monitoring events conducted by geocon-consultants-inc. for [[caltrans]].

## Site record

In March 2012, groundwater samples from MW-4 were analyzed for dissolved metals. Antimony, arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, cobalt, copper, lead, selenium, silver, thallium, and zinc were not reported above their respective PQLs. Barium, vanadium, and strontium were reported, with concentrations not exceeding drinking water thresholds. Total chromium and mercury were reported. Manganese was reported at a concentration of 88 µg/l, which exceeded the secondary MCL for taste and odor. The mercury results were considered estimated values due to its presence in the equipment blank. geocon-consultants-inc. noted that the manganese concentration in MW-4 was the only exceedance of a numeric threshold for any site monitoring well. geocon-consultants-inc. also analyzed general minerals, including nitrate as nitrogen, which was reported at 10 mg/l, meeting the primary MCL. Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) were reported at concentrations exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l.

In May 2012, dissolved metals analysis for MW-4 showed barium and strontium were reported, with concentrations below drinking water thresholds. Lead was not reported above its PQL. Arsenic, chromium, and vanadium were reported within ranges that did not exceed drinking water thresholds. Molybdenum and nickel were reported for MW-4. Antimony, copper, and selenium were detected in some samples. Cobalt and manganese were reported for MW-4. All reported concentrations for these metals were below their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water. General minerals analysis for MW-4 showed nitrate as nitrogen at 10 mg/l, meeting the primary MCL. TDS was reported at concentrations exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l.

In November 2012, dissolved metals analysis for MW-4 showed barium and strontium were reported within historical ranges and below water quality thresholds. Lead was not reported above its PQL. Arsenic, chromium, and vanadium were reported within ranges that did not exceed drinking water thresholds. Nickel was reported for MW-4. Molybdenum was not reported for MW-4. Selenium was detected in seven of the ten samples. Copper and manganese were detected in three of the ten samples. Cobalt was detected in the sample from MW-8. All reported concentrations for these metals were below their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water. General minerals analysis for MW-4 showed nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranging from 2.8 to 24 mg/l. TDS concentrations ranged from 340 to 640 mg/l, with TDS exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In January 2013, dissolved metals analysis for MW-4 showed no exceedances of numeric water quality threshold values, with the exception of manganese. General minerals analysis for MW-4 showed nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranging from 2.9 to 30 mg/l. TDS concentrations ranged from 310 to 680 mg/l, with TDS exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In March 2013, dissolved metals analysis for MW-4 showed no exceedances of numeric water quality threshold values, with the exception of manganese detected in samples from MW-1 and MW-7. General minerals analysis for MW-4 showed nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranging from 2.7 to 30 mg/l. TDS concentrations ranged from 290 to 700 mg/l, with TDS meeting or exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In September 2013, dissolved metals analysis for MW-4 showed no exceedances of numeric water quality threshold values. General minerals analysis for MW-4 showed nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranging from 2.5 to 36 mg/l. TDS concentrations ranged from 300 to 750 mg/l, with TDS exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In November 2012, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition. In March 2013, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition. In September 2013, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition. In February 2014, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition. In June 2014, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition. In April 2015, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition. In April 2019, Stiff diagrams indicated that MW-4 had a calcium-dominant composition.

In October 2016, MW-4 was dry. In April 2017, depth to groundwater in MW-4 ranged from 35.52 to 40.73 feet below TOC. In April 2015, depth to groundwater in MW-4 was 36.85 feet below TOC.

## Regulatory status

In March 2012, manganese in MW-4 exceeded the secondary MCL for taste and odor. Nitrate as nitrogen in MW-4 met the primary MCL of 10 mg/l. TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l.

In May 2012, nitrate as nitrogen in MW-4 met the primary MCL of 10 mg/l. TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l.

In November 2012, TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In January 2013, TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In March 2013, TDS met or exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In September 2013, TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In February 2014, TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In June 2014, TDS exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.

In April 2019, TDS was equal to or greater than the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l for MW-4.
