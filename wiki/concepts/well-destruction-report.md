# Well Destruction Report

Two well destruction reports document the sequential decommissioning of all ten groundwater monitoring wells at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site: six wells destroyed in April 2019 ahead of SR 132 highway construction, and four remaining wells destroyed following a 2024 regulatory determination that continued monitoring was no longer useful. Together they mark the end of the site's groundwater monitoring program.

## 2019 destruction of MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, and MW8

[[geocon-consultants-inc]] prepared the first report, dated June 19, 2019 (Project No. S1200-01-01), documenting destruction of six wells required because two lay within future highway travel lanes and four lay within areas of future drainage improvements for [[caltrans]] Contract No. 06A2184, Task Order No. 1, EA No. 10-0X2700. Four other site wells were left in place at that time. Geocon obtained City of Modesto well destruction permit No. ENC2019-50174, marked well locations on April 8, 2019, and provided the required 72-hour Underground Service Alert notifications (Ticket Nos. W910200748, W910200753, W910200756, W910200763, W910200770, W910200773).

Gregg Drilling and Testing, a Caltrans-approved and California-certified subcontractor, destroyed MW1, MW2, and MW5 on April 29, 2019, and MW3, MW7, and MW8 on April 30, 2019, under the supervision of Geocon staff member Lauren Short, with Caltrans personnel present onsite. Each well was destroyed by pressure grouting the casing to its maximum depth: Gregg measured depth to groundwater and total well depth beforehand, filled each well with grout via tremie pipe, and applied approximately 20 to 25 pounds per square inch of pressure for approximately 5 minutes. Grout was prepared at 3 gallons of water per 47-pound sack of neat cement, mixed in 5-gallon plastic buckets. The work was performed in general accordance with California Well Standards Bulletin 74-90, Monitoring Well Standards, Part III, and City of Modesto permit requirements.

## 2025 destruction of the remaining four wells

The second report, dated June 18, 2025 (Project No. S2350-01-02, GeoTracker Case No. SL0609924194), documents Geocon's destruction of the four wells that survived the 2019 round — MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10 — for Caltrans Contract No. 06A2767, Task Order No. 2, EA No. 10-1E7003, at what the report identifies as the Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles site (Stockpiles 1 and 2, encapsulated beneath SR 132, and former Stockpile 3, removed and relocated to Stockpiles 1 and 2). This destruction was driven by Phase II construction of SR 132, scheduled to begin in 2026, which will fully encapsulate the remaining soil stockpiles beneath highway pavement and perimeter retaining walls.

The decision to destroy these four wells rests on a 2024 update to a 2014 comparative evaluation of groundwater data, which found that the encapsulated stockpiles do not have the potential to impact groundwater and that further monitoring at the four remaining wells would not provide useful data. Because the more recent data were consistent with the 2014 evaluation, the water table was declining, and Phase II construction would permanently encapsulate the remaining stockpiles, Geocon recommended decommissioning MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10 and discontinuing groundwater monitoring tied to the soil stockpiles. In correspondence dated May 3, 2024, [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] agreed that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and approved destruction of the four remaining wells, formalizing the [[groundwater-not-impacted]] conclusion that underpins closure of the monitoring program.

## Significance

The two reports bracket the site's entire groundwater monitoring history: the 2019 destructions were driven by physical conflict with highway construction rather than any water-quality finding, while the 2025 destructions were driven by a regulatory determination — concurred in by DTSC and the Central Valley RWQCB — that the stockpiles, once encapsulated, pose no ongoing threat to groundwater. With all ten original monitoring wells (MW1 through MW10) now destroyed, no monitoring wells remain at the site to track constituents such as [[barium]] or [[lead]] that were of interest in earlier site characterization; the regulatory record instead relies on the pre-2025 dataset and the 2014/2024 comparative evaluation to support the no-impact determination.
