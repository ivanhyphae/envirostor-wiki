Well destruction at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site occurred in two rounds, driven in both cases by construction schedule rather than by any contamination finding: six wells (MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, MW8) were destroyed in April 2019 to clear conflicts with SR 132 highway construction, and the four remaining wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, MW10) were destroyed in 2025 after [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] concluded that continued monitoring of the encapsulated stockpiles served no further purpose. The 2025 destruction rested on a groundwater record showing no impact from the barium- and lead-containing soil, so its approval is bound up with the site's [[groundwater-not-impacted]] finding rather than with any exceedance or remedial trigger.

## 2019 destruction (MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, MW8)

Under Caltrans Contract No. 06A2184, Task Order No. 1 (EA No. 10-0X2700), [[geocon-consultants-inc]] destroyed six of the site's ten original groundwater monitoring wells because two wells sat within future highway travel lanes and four sat in areas of planned drainage improvements. Geocon obtained City of Modesto well destruction permit No. ENC2019-50174 and retained Gregg Drilling and Testing, a Caltrans-approved, California-certified subcontractor. Gregg destroyed MW1, MW2, and MW5 on April 29, 2019, and MW3, MW7, and MW8 on April 30, 2019, by pressure-grouting each casing to the maximum well depth (grout mixed at 3 gallons of water per 47-pound sack of neat cement, placed via tremie pipe under approximately 20-25 psi for approximately 5 minutes). Geocon staff member Lauren Short supervised; Caltrans personnel were also present. Depth to groundwater and total well depth were measured for each well prior to destruction. This left four wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, MW10) in place at the site for continued monitoring.

## Basis for destroying the remaining four wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, MW10)

In 2024, Geocon updated a 2014 comparative evaluation of groundwater data for the site. The updated evaluation concluded that the encapsulated soil stockpiles did not have the potential to impact groundwater and that further monitoring from the four remaining wells would not yield useful data, given the consistency of recent results with the 2014 evaluation and the declining water table. On May 3, 2024, DTSC, in consultation with the Central Valley RWQCB, approved the Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data and concurred that the encapsulated soil "is not impacting groundwater and... will not impact groundwater in the future," directing that groundwater monitoring beneath the stockpiles be discontinued and that MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10 be decommissioned per the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan. This directive is the regulatory basis cited in the subsequent well destruction permit application and report — the wells were removed because the groundwater-not-impacted determination made them unnecessary, not because of any water-quality problem requiring their removal.

## 2025 destruction (MW4, MW6, MW9, MW10)

Under Caltrans Contract No. 06A2767, Task Order No. 2 (EA No. 10-1E7003, GeoTracker Case No. SL0609924194), Geocon prepared and submitted a well destruction permit application to the Stanislaus County Department of Environmental Resources (SCDER) on March 3, 2025, referencing the DTSC/RWQCB directive letter of May 3, 2024. SCDER issued Permit Nos. MW25-13 and MW25-14. Geocon marked the work area on March 11, 2025, pulled USA tickets (Nos. 2025031101720, 2025031101731, 2025031101763, 2025031101775), retained Advanced Geological Services (Pleasant Hill, CA) to locate subsurface utilities, and retained PeneCore Drilling (C-57 license #906899, Woodland, CA) to perform the destruction. Caltrans (Adam Inman, PG, Engineering Geologist, District 6) granted Geocon and its subconsultants unrestricted access to the state right-of-way for the project duration and accompanied Geocon during the work, per email correspondence dated March 12, 2025.

The four wells were abandoned by pressure-grouting in general accordance with California Well Standards Bulletin 74-90, Monitoring Well Standards, Part III: each well was filled with neat Portland cement placed via tremie pipe from the bottom under pressure sufficient to penetrate the annular filter pack, with additional grout added under air pressure as needed. PeneCore then removed the well box and concrete apron, over-drilled and removed the top 5 feet of casing, backfilled that interval with Portland cement or sand/cement slurry to grade, and capped the surface with approximately 6 inches of black-dyed concrete or soil matched to the surrounding surface. The completed work is documented in Geocon's Well Destruction Report dated June 18, 2025 (Project No. S2350-01-02), submitted to Caltrans District 6 Hazardous Waste Branch (Attn: Adam Inman, PG).

## DTSC review of the completion report

DTSC's January 30, 2026 letter granted conditional approval of the Well Destruction Completion Report (paired in the same letter with approval of the FY24/25 Storm Water and Surface Water Monitoring Report), subject to one Geological Services Branch comment to be incorporated into a final version. The letter frames the destruction as the closing step in a longer decommissioning sequence: ten wells were installed in 2012, six were decommissioned in 2019 to permit highway construction, and the 2025 report covers destruction of the remaining four "after years of data supporting no metals were leaching into the groundwater from the stockpiles." As of a March 16, 2026 email from DTSC project manager Arielle McLeskey to Adam Inman, DTSC had not yet received the revised final report addressing the conditional-approval comment, indicating that final DTSC sign-off on the well destruction was still outstanding as of that date.
