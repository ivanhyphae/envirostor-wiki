Well destruction, also known as well abandonment, is the process of permanently deactivating groundwater monitoring wells to prevent them from serving as conduits for contaminant migration or pathways for surface water infiltration into the aquifer. This practice is a critical component of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project, ensuring the long-term integrity of groundwater resources.

## Well Destruction Activities and Approvals

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project involved the destruction of several groundwater monitoring wells. A Well Destruction Report dated June 19, 2019, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., documented the destruction of six wells: MW-1, MW-2, MW-3, MW-5, MW-7, and MW-8. These wells were decommissioned due to conflicts with highway construction activities. Later, a subsequent Well Destruction Report, dated June 25, 2025, also prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., detailed the destruction of four additional wells: MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10. This action was taken following a 2014 comparative evaluation of groundwater data, which concluded that the encapsulated soil stockpiles did not impact groundwater and that future monitoring from these wells would not yield useful data.

The destruction of the remaining four wells (MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10) was approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board. This approval was communicated in a DTSC directive letter dated May 3, 2024, which concurred with the assessment that the encapsulated soil was not impacting groundwater. The DTSC subsequently provided a conditional approval of the final Well Destruction Report on January 30, 2026, noting one comment to be incorporated into the final version.

## Permitting and Field Procedures

Well destruction activities required adherence to specific permitting processes. For the wells destroyed in June 2019, a permit (No. ENC2019-50174) was obtained from the City of Modesto. For the later destruction of wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10, permit applications were submitted to the Stanislaus County Department of Environmental Resources (SCDER), specifically Permit Nos. MW25-13 and MW25-14. Pre-field activities included marking proposed work areas, contacting local utilities via Underground Service Alert, and retaining utility locating services. Caltrans granted unrestricted access to Geocon and its subcontractors to State Right of Way for the well destruction project, with Caltrans staff present during activities.

The wells were destroyed by pressure grouting using neat Portland cement, applied via a tremie pipe from the bottom of the well. Following pressure grouting, the well box and concrete apron were removed, and the top 5 feet of the well casing were over-drilled and removed. The over-drilled borings were then filled with Portland cement or sand/cement slurry to the ground surface, capped with approximately 6 inches of black-dyed concrete or soil to match the surrounding surface. PeneCore Drilling, a C-57-licensed contractor, performed the well destruction activities for the latter set of wells.
