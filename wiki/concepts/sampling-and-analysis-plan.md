The Sampling and Analysis Plan (SAP) for the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site details the procedures for evaluating surface water runoff. The primary objective is to determine if this runoff migrates beyond Caltrans' right-of-way and if it has been impacted by chemicals of potential concern present in the stockpiles.

## Site record
The "S9525-06-44A Stormwater SAP Addendum 0213," prepared by [[geocon-consultants]], outlines proposed surface water sampling activities for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. The purpose of this sampling is to assess surface water runoff migration and potential impacts from chemicals of concern within the stockpiles. The scope of services includes surface water sampling, laboratory analysis by a California-certified laboratory, and the preparation of a summary report.

The stockpiles, identified as 1 through 3, originated from soil excavated during the construction of SR 99 around 1961. This soil was sourced from property previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) and contained an evaporation pond. These stockpiles were placed in their current location in anticipation of the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway project.

The "Carpenter Shoofly Area Clean Fill Sampling Plan" specifies vertical transect sampling depths at 2, 5, 10, and 15 feet. This plan is associated with the SR 132 Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Source Testing Sampling Plan.

## Regulatory status
The surface water sampling activities are to be conducted in accordance with protocols approved by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC). The *Final Surface Water Sampling and Analysis Plan*, prepared by Shaw Environmental, Inc. and dated January 25, 2006, established these protocols.
