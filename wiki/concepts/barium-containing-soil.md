# Barium Containing Soil

## Overview

Barium Containing Soil (BCS) is the primary contaminant of concern at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. BCS consists of soil with elevated concentrations of barium, and to a lesser extent, lead. This material originated from historical chemical processing activities at the former [[fmc-corporation]] facility. The BCS was generated in the early 1960s from pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, as well as native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange. These materials were consolidated into three stockpiles, designated [[stockpile-1]], Stockpile 2, and Stockpile 3. The selected remediation strategy involves consolidating the BCS and containing it within highway embankment fill, subsequently covered by clean fill and a structural pavement section as part of the SR 132 West Project's [[interim-phase-1]] and Ultimate Phase 2 construction.

## Site record

### Source and origin

The former FMC facility, situated northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange, was involved in ore and mineral processing, including barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate), from the 1930s to the 1970s. Liquid residue, referred to as "tailings," from these operations was discharged into unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, the State of California acquired a 4.3-acre parcel within the FMC facility, which included a portion of these ponds, for the construction of the SR 99 freeway. Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property along with native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange were subsequently placed to create BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3. {{quote: "From the 1930s to 1970s, property beneath and northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange was occupied by chemical processing facilities operated by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) and its predecessors. Ores and minerals including barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) were processed for use in greases, lubricating oil and pigment blanks. Sodium sulfide was generated as a by-product and sold as a caustic and reagent. From the 1950s to the 1970s, liquid residue ("tailings") generated at this facility was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, the State of California purchased a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility, including a portion of the ponds, for the construction of the SR 99 freeway through Modesto. Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property along with native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange were subsequently placed to create BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3."}}

### Pre-consolidation stockpile configuration and volumes

Prior to the commencement of [[interim-phase-1]] construction, three BCS stockpiles were present within the Caltrans SR 132 right-of-way:

*   **Stockpile 1:** Located approximately 150 feet west of Emerald Avenue, with an estimated fill volume of 34,000 cubic yards. It measured roughly 600 feet long (east-west) by 160 feet wide. {{quote: "BCS Stockpile 1, located approximately 150 feet westerly of Emerald Avenue, was roughly 600 feet long in the east-west direction, 160 feet wide, and had an estimated fill volume of 34,000 cubic yards (yd³)."}}
*   **Stockpile 2:** Situated between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, this stockpile was approximately 1,650 feet long (east-west) by 160 feet wide, with an estimated fill volume of 102,000 cubic yards. {{quote: "BCS Stockpile 2, located between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, was roughly 1,650 feet long in the east-west direction, 160 feet wide, and had an estimated fill volume of 102,000 yd³."}}
*   **Stockpile 3:** Located east of SR 99, it measured approximately 1,100 feet long by 120 feet wide, with an estimated fill volume of 24,000 cubic yards. {{quote: "BCS Stockpile 3, formerly located on the east side of SR 99, was roughly 1,100 feet long, 120 feet wide, and had an estimated fill volume of 24,000 yd³."}}

The BCS material is predominantly composed of layered, poorly graded sand and silty sand, consistent with the underlying native alluvial deposits of the Modesto Formation. The average maximum thickness of the stockpiles was approximately 20 feet. The total estimated volume of BCS across all three stockpiles was 160,000 cubic yards. {{quote: "The BCS Stockpiles are primarily comprised of layered, poorly graded sand and silty sand similar to underlying native alluvial deposits of the Modesto Formation. The average maximum stockpile fill thickness was approximately 20 feet."}} {{quote: "The total estimated volume of BCS across all three stockpiles was 160,000 cubic yards."}}

### Barium concentrations

The [[interim-removal-action-completion-report]] (Interim RACR) documents that the BCS material contains elevated barium concentrations, reaching up to 72,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg), which exceed regulatory screening levels. Site-specific upper-bound background concentrations, as established in the DTSC-approved Variance to the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] dated April 9, 2020, are:

*   Barium: 120 mg/kg {{quote: "Barium - 120 mg/kg"}}
*   Lead: 3.8 mg/kg {{quote: "Lead - 3.8 mg/kg"}}

### Removal and consolidation activities (Interim Phase 1: 2020–2022)

**Stockpile 3 complete removal (February–March 2020):** Stockpile 3 was entirely excavated from the east side of SR 99. Approximately 27,675 cubic yards of BCS material, transported in 3,075 truckloads (assuming 9 yd³ per truck), were moved to the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. {{quote: "Stockpile 3 BCS material located adjacent and east of State Route 99 between Kansas Avenue and the Modesto Irrigation District (MID) water canal was excavated and transported for placement as embankment fill within the designated Stockpile 1 fill containment zone. A total of 3,075 truck-loads of BCS material totaling approximately 38,440 cubic yards (preliminary estimate based on 12.5 cubic yards per truck) was transported to Stockpile 1."}} Twenty-nine verification soil samples were collected from 26 locations (S3-1 through S3-26). Initial results at three locations indicated barium concentrations ranging from 2,200 to 7,000 mg/kg, exceeding the verification threshold of 1,000 mg/kg. These specific areas were excavated an additional foot and resampled. Final verification samples from these areas showed barium concentrations between 46 and 970 mg/kg, and lead concentrations between 1.6 and 40 mg/kg, all below the established thresholds. {{quote: "Following receipt of the analytical data indicating elevated barium concentrations above the verification threshold for sample locations S3 9- 0 through S3 11-0, these excavation areas were deepened approximately one foot and resampled. ... Each of the final excavation soil samples contained barium and lead below the RDIP verification thresholds. Barium concentrations ranged from 46 to 970 mg/kg, and lead concentrations from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg."}}

**Stockpile 2 end removals (February–March 2020):** Excavation occurred at the western end (near the Emerald Avenue bridge abutment, up to approximate Station 1135+35) and the eastern end (near the SR 99 bridge abutment, up to approximate Station 1148+30) of Stockpile 2. Ten verification samples from eight locations at the west end and fourteen samples from twelve locations at the east end were collected. Four initial locations exhibited barium concentrations between 1,100 and 4,100 mg/kg, exceeding the 1,000 mg/kg barium threshold. These areas were deepened by approximately one foot and resampled. Final verification samples from these areas showed barium concentrations ranging from 54 to 760 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg. {{quote: "Following receipt of the initial analytical data indicating elevated barium concentrations above the verification threshold for sample locations S2W 1-0 and S2W 3-0, the western half of the excavation was deepened to an approximate elevation of 81 feet above MSL. Two additional verification soil samples were collected from the deepened excavation on March 6, 2020."}} {{quote: "Each of the final excavation soil samples contained barium and lead below the RDIP verification thresholds. Barium concentrations ranged from 54 to 760 mg/kg, and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg."}}

**MSE wall footing excavations (March–April 2020):** Excavations for Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) wall footings were conducted on the southerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2. Soil within the footing excavation areas was sampled at one-foot intervals to depths of 3 to 5 feet. For Stockpile 1, barium was detected in all 15 samples, with concentrations ranging from 42 to 420 mg/kg. The 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) for barium in these samples was 171.9 mg/kg, which exceeded the 120 mg/kg background concentration but remained below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold. For Stockpile 2, barium was detected in all 29 samples, with concentrations ranging from 53 to 930 mg/kg. Fourteen of the 29 samples exceeded the 120 mg/kg background threshold, predominantly within the upper 3 feet of the excavated material. All excavated MSE wall footing material was placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones. {{quote: "Barium was detected in each of the 15 soil samples analyzed at concentrations ranging from 42 to 420 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg), greater than the maximum site specific background value of 120 mg/kg, but less than the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) removal threshold of 1,000 mg/kg based on groundwater quality protection. The calculated 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) and mean of the entire barium data set are 171.9 and 128.3 mg/kg, respectively"}} {{quote: "Barium was detected in each of the 29 soil samples analyzed at concentrations ranging from 53 to 930 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). Fourteen of the 29 soil samples contained barium at concentrations greater than the maximum site-specific background value for barium of 120 mg/kg."}}

### Containment and capping

BCS from Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3, along with [[aerially-deposited-lead]] (ADL) contaminated soil from the northbound SR 99 shoulder, was consolidated within the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. The Stockpile 1 Containment Zone is situated adjacent to and west of Emerald Avenue, between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50. The Stockpile 2 Containment Zone is located between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, between approximate Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30. {{quote: "The Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones... The Stockpile 1 Containment Zone is situated adjacent to and west of Emerald Avenue, between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50. The Stockpile 2 Containment Zone is located between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, between approximate Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30."}}

Clean fill capping was completed using DTSC-approved material sourced from the Carpenter Road area, Basin 5, and the Carpenter Road shoofly. The majority of the upper surfaces and northern slopes were capped by Fall 2020. A significant rain event in January 2021 caused substantial erosion on the unvegetated northerly slopes. These areas were regraded in September 2021 using clean fill from the Carpenter Road shoofly. The slopes were subsequently hydroseeded with a native plant mix and protected with erosion control blankets and fiber rolls in October 2021. {{quote: "The majority of the upper surface and northern slopes of the completed Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones were capped with DTSC-approved clean fill material by the Fall 2020."}} {{quote: "In January 2021, a record rain event caused significant erosion of the clean cap fill materials placed on the exposed (unvegetated) northerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2. ... The eroded areas regraded in September 2021 using clean fill deposited at the toe of slope and placement of approved clean fill material generated from excavation of the Carpenter Road shoofly."}} {{quote: "The cleaned capped northerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2 were vegetated in October 2021 using a native plant mix hydroseed/hydromulch, and subsequent placement of an erosion control blanket (rolled erosion control product) and fiber rolls."}}

### Verification testing thresholds

The [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) established the following soil excavation verification thresholds for BCS removal areas:

*   **Barium:** 1,000 mg/kg (established for groundwater protection). {{quote: "Barium¹ - 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg, groundwater protection)"}}
*   **Lead:** 80 mg/kg (based on the DTSC residential screening level). {{quote: "Lead - 80 mg/kg (residential screening level)"}}

All verification soil samples were analyzed for barium and lead by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) using EPA Test Method 6010B.

### Air monitoring during BCS disturbance

Air monitoring was conducted between February and June 2020 during BCS grading activities. Real-time pDR-1200 monitors were employed, with a Fence Line Action Level of 4.0 mg/m³ for total dust. Barium results were compared against an action level of 25 µg/m³ specified in the highway contractor's Health & Safety Plan (Adanta, November 2019). All monitored results were below the designated project action levels, indicating effective dust control measures. {{quote: "Real-time MIE pDR-1200 direct reading instrument measurements were compared to the Fence Line Action Level of 4.0 milligrams per cubic meter (mg/m³) to confirm that "wet methods" used during BCS stockpile grading activities were effective in controlling offsite migration of total airborne dust."}} {{quote: "Barium results were compared to an action level of 25 micrograms per cubic meter ( μg/ m³) as presented in the highway contractors' Health & Safety Plan prepared by Adanta, Inc., November 2019."}} {{quote: "The results of air monitoring performed during BCS grading activities for the Project indicate airborne levels of total dust, barium, and lead below designated project action and risk levels, confirming effective dust control measures."}}

## Regulatory status

The project operates under an Interagency Agreement between Caltrans and the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), with collaboration from the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). DTSC serves as the lead regulatory agency.

*   DTSC approved the RDIP in 2019 and subsequent Variances on April 9, 2020, and September 2, 2020. {{quote: "DTSC approved the RDIP in 2019 and subsequent Variances on April 9, 2020, and September 2, 2020."}}
*   DTSC accepted the Draft Interim RACR on February 24, 2022. {{quote: "DTSC accepted the Draft Interim RACR on February 24, 2022."}}
*   A Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded for the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. This covenant will restrict land use to transportation purposes and prohibit unrestricted uses such as residential, schools, daycare, and hospitals. {{quote: "A recorded LUC for the contained and capped soil stockpiles, including the BCS Containment Zone boundary survey, will be prepared by DTSC to restrict the types of land use and future activities that are allowed on the Site."}}
*   Annual Operations and Maintenance (O&M) inspections (scheduled for the third quarter, July–September) and five-year reviews are required. {{quote: "Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the interim and final BCS containment measures."}}
*   Groundwater monitoring (wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10) and stormwater sampling are ongoing. Reported concentrations of target analytes have remained below regulatory thresholds, consistent with historical data. The CVRWQCB will determine if the clean-capped BCS poses a material threat to groundwater quality, after which the remaining wells may be decommissioned. {{quote: "Four groundwater monitoring events were completed for the remaining wells (MW-4, MW-6, MW-9 and MW-10) between November 2019
