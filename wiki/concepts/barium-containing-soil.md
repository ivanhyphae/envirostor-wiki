## Barium Containing Soil (BCS)

### Overview

Barium-containing soil (BCS) is the primary contaminant of concern at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along State Route 132 (SR 132) in Stanislaus County, California. BCS is defined as soil with elevated concentrations of barium, and to a lesser extent, lead. This material originated from historical chemical processing activities at the former [[fmc-corporation]] facility and was consolidated into three stockpiles: [[stockpile-1]], Stockpile 2, and Stockpile 3. The remediation strategy involved consolidating the BCS and incorporating it into highway embankment fill, subsequently covered by clean fill and a structural pavement section as part of the SR 132 West Project.

### Site Record

#### Source and Origin

The former FMC facility, located northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange, processed ores like barite and celestite from the 1930s to the 1970s. Liquid residue, termed "tailings," was discharged into unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, the State of California acquired a portion of this property, including some of the ponds, for SR 99 construction. Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange, were used to create BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3.

#### Pre-Consolidation Stockpile Configuration and Volumes

Before [[interim-phase-1]] construction, three BCS stockpiles were present:

* **Stockpile 1:** Located approximately 150 feet west of Emerald Avenue, with an estimated volume of 34,000 cubic yards (yd³). It measured roughly 600 feet long (east-west) by 160 feet wide.
* **Stockpile 2:** Situated between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, this stockpile was approximately 1,650 feet long (east-west) by 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 102,000 yd³.
* **Stockpile 3:** Located east of SR 99, it measured approximately 1,100 feet long by 120 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 24,000 yd³.

The BCS material is primarily composed of layered, poorly graded sand and silty sand, similar to the underlying native alluvial deposits of the Modesto Formation. The average maximum thickness was approximately 20 feet. The total estimated volume of BCS across all three stockpiles was 160,000 yd³.

#### Barium Concentrations

The [[interim-removal-action-completion-report]] (Interim RACR) indicates that BCS material contains elevated barium concentrations, reaching up to 72,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). Site-specific upper-bound background concentrations, approved by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) in a Variance to the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] dated April 9, 2020, are:

* Barium: 120 mg/kg
* Lead: 3.8 mg/kg

#### Removal and Consolidation Activities (Interim Phase 1: 2020–2022)

* **Stockpile 3 Complete Removal (February–March 2020):** Stockpile 3 was fully excavated. Approximately 27,675 yd³ of BCS material was transported to the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. Twenty-nine verification soil samples were collected. Initial results at three locations showed barium concentrations between 2,200 and 7,000 mg/kg, exceeding the 1,000 mg/kg verification threshold. These areas were deepened and resampled. Final verification samples from these areas showed barium concentrations between 46 and 970 mg/kg, and lead concentrations between 1.6 and 40 mg/kg, all below established thresholds.

* **Stockpile 2 End Removals (February–March 2020):** Excavation occurred at the western end (near Emerald Avenue bridge abutment, up to Station 1135+35) and eastern end (near SR 99 bridge abutment, up to Station 1148+30) of Stockpile 2. Ten verification samples from eight locations at the west end and fourteen samples from twelve locations at the east end were collected. Four initial locations had barium concentrations between 1,100 and 4,100 mg/kg, exceeding the 1,000 mg/kg threshold. These areas were deepened by approximately one foot and resampled. Final verification samples showed barium concentrations ranging from 54 to 760 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg.

* **MSE Wall Footing Excavations (March–April 2020):** Excavations for Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) wall footings were conducted on the southerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2. Soil within these areas was sampled to depths of 3 to 5 feet. For Stockpile 1, barium was detected in all 15 samples, with concentrations ranging from 42 to 420 mg/kg. The 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) for barium was 171.9 mg/kg, exceeding the 120 mg/kg background but below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold. For Stockpile 2, barium was detected in all 29 samples, ranging from 53 to 930 mg/kg. Fourteen samples exceeded the 120 mg/kg background threshold, primarily within the upper 3 feet. All excavated MSE wall footing material was placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones.

#### Containment and Capping

BCS from Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3, along with [[aerially-deposited-lead]] (ADL) contaminated soil from the SR 99 shoulder, was consolidated within the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. These zones are located between SR 99 and Emerald Avenue.

Clean fill capping was completed using DTSC-approved material sourced from the Carpenter Road area, Basin 5, and the Carpenter Road shoofly. Erosion on the northern slopes in January 2021 was regraded in September 2021 using clean fill from the Carpenter Road shoofly and subsequently hydroseeded and protected.

#### Verification Testing Thresholds

The [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) established the following soil excavation verification thresholds for BCS removal areas:

* **Barium:** 1,000 mg/kg (for groundwater protection).
* **Lead:** 80 mg/kg (based on the DTSC residential screening level).

All verification soil samples were analyzed for barium and lead by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) using EPA Test Method 6010B.

#### Air Monitoring During BCS Disturbance

Air monitoring was conducted between February and June 2020 during BCS grading activities. Real-time pDR-1200 monitors were used, with a Fence Line Action Level of 4.0 mg/m³ for total dust. Barium results were compared against an action level of 25 µg/m³ from the contractor's Health & Safety Plan. All monitored results were below project action levels.

### Regulatory Status

The project operates under an Interagency Agreement between [[caltrans]] and the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC), with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) also involved. DTSC is the lead regulatory agency.

* DTSC approved the RDIP in 2019 and subsequent Variances on April 9, 2020, and September 2, 2020.
* DTSC accepted the Draft Interim RACR on February 24, 2022.
* A Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded for the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones, restricting land use to transportation purposes and prohibiting residential or other sensitive uses.
* Annual Operations and Maintenance (O&M) inspections and five-year reviews are required.
* Groundwater monitoring (wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10) and stormwater sampling are ongoing. Reported concentrations have remained below regulatory thresholds. The CVRWQCB will determine if the capped BCS poses a threat to groundwater quality, after which remaining wells may be decommissioned.

### Open Items

* A Final Interim RACR will be submitted following the completion of Ultimate Phase 2 construction of the SR 132 West Project.
* A supplemental RDIP will be submitted to DTSC for approval prior to construction of Ultimate Phase 2.
* The LUC will be finalized and recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder Office.
