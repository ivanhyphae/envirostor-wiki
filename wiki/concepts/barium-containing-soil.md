# Barium Containing Soil

## Overview

Barium Containing Soil (BCS) is the primary contaminated media managed under the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. BCS consists of soil containing elevated barium concentrations — and to a lesser extent elevated lead — originating from historical chemical processing operations at the former Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility. The soil was generated in the early 1960s when pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange, were placed to create BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3. The selected remedial alternative is consolidation and containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering with clean fill and structural pavement section during Interim Phase 1 and Ultimate Phase 2 construction of the SR 132 West Project.

## Site record

### Source and origin

From the 1930s to 1970s, the FMC facility northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange processed ores and minerals including barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) for use in greases, lubricating oil, and pigment blanks. From the 1950s to the 1970s, liquid residue ("tailings") generated at the facility was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, the State of California purchased a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility, including a portion of the ponds, for construction of the SR 99 freeway. Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange, were subsequently placed to create BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3.

### Pre-consolidation stockpile configuration and volumes

Prior to construction of Interim Phase 1, the Site consisted of three BCS stockpiles within Caltrans SR 132 right-of-way:

- **Stockpile 1:** located approximately 150 feet westerly of Emerald Avenue, roughly 600 feet long (east–west) by 160 feet wide, estimated fill volume 34,000 yd³.
- **Stockpile 2:** located between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, roughly 1,650 feet long (east–west) by 160 feet wide, estimated fill volume 102,000 yd³.
- **Stockpile 3:** located east of SR 99, roughly 1,100 feet long by 120 feet wide, estimated fill volume 24,000 yd³.

The BCS material is primarily comprised of layered, poorly graded sand and silty sand similar to underlying native alluvial deposits of the Modesto Formation, with an average maximum stockpile fill thickness of approximately 20 feet. The total estimated volume was 160,000 cubic yards.

### Barium concentrations

The Interim RACR documents that BCS material contains elevated barium concentrations up to 72,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg), exceeding regulatory screening levels. Site-specific upper-bound background concentrations, established in the DTSC-approved Variance to the RDIP dated April 9, 2020, are:

- Barium: 120 mg/kg
- Lead: 3.8 mg/kg

### Removal and consolidation activities (Interim Phase 1: 2020–2022)

**Stockpile 3 complete removal (February–March 2020):** Stockpile 3 was entirely excavated from the east side of SR 99. Approximately 3,075 truckloads totaling approximately 27,675 yd³ (based on 9 yd³ per truck) of BCS material were transported to the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. Twenty-nine verification soil samples were collected from 26 locations (S3-1 through S3-26). Initial results at three locations showed barium concentrations of 2,200 to 7,000 mg/kg, exceeding the verification threshold of 1,000 mg/kg. These areas were deepened approximately one foot and resampled. Final verification samples yielded barium concentrations of 46 to 970 mg/kg and lead concentrations of 1.6 to 40 mg/kg — all below thresholds.

**Stockpile 2 end removals (February–March 2020):** The western end (Emerald Avenue bridge abutment, to approximate Station 1135+35) and eastern end (SR 99 bridge abutment, to approximate Station 1148+30) were excavated. Ten verification samples from eight locations at the west end and fourteen samples from twelve locations at the east end were collected. Four initial locations exceeded the 1,000 mg/kg barium threshold (concentrations of 1,100 to 4,100 mg/kg); these were deepened approximately one foot and resampled. Final barium concentrations ranged from 54 to 760 mg/kg and lead from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg.

**MSE wall footing excavations (March–April 2020):** Southerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2 were excavated for MSE wall construction. Soil within footing areas was sampled at one-foot intervals to maximum depths of 3 to 5 feet. For Stockpile 1, barium was detected in all 15 samples at 42 to 420 mg/kg, with a 95% UCL of 171.9 mg/kg — above the 120 mg/kg background but below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold. For Stockpile 2, barium was detected in all 29 samples at 53 to 930 mg/kg, with 14 of 29 samples exceeding 120 mg/kg, predominantly in the upper 3 feet. All excavated MSE wall footing material was placed in the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones.

### Containment and capping

BCS from Stockpiles 1 through 3, along with ADL-contaminated soil from the northbound SR 99 shoulder, was consolidated within the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. The Stockpile 1 Containment Zone is located adjacent and west of Emerald Avenue between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50. The Stockpile 2 Containment Zone is located between Emerald Avenue and SR 99 between approximate Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30.

Clean fill capping was performed using DTSC-approved material from the Carpenter Road area, Basin 5, and the Carpenter Road shoofly. The majority of upper surfaces and northern slopes were capped by Fall 2020. In January 2021, a record rain event caused significant erosion of the unvegetated northerly slopes; these were regraded in September 2021 using clean fill from the Carpenter Road shoofly. The slopes were hydroseeded with a native plant mix and covered with erosion control blankets and fiber rolls in October 2021.

### Verification testing thresholds

The RDIP established the following soil excavation verification thresholds for BCS removal areas:

- **Barium:** 1,000 mg/kg (based on groundwater protection). Note: the regulatory construction worker 2019 Environmental Screening Level (ESL) for barium is 3,000 mg/kg; the residential ESL for barium is noted elsewhere as 15,000 mg/kg (San Francisco Bay Regional Water Quality Control Board).
- **Lead:** 80 mg/kg (based on DTSC residential screening level).

All verification soil samples were analyzed by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) for barium and lead following EPA Test Method 6010B.

### Air monitoring during BCS disturbance

Air monitoring was performed between February and June 2020 during BCS grading activities. Real-time pDR-1200 monitors were used with a Fence Line Action Level of 4.0 mg/m³ for total dust. Barium results were compared to an action level of 25 µg/m³ from the highway contractor's Health & Safety Plan (Adanta, November 2019). All results were below designated project action levels, confirming effective dust control measures.

## Regulatory status

The project is conducted under an Interagency Agreement between Caltrans and DTSC, with cooperation from the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). DTSC is the lead regulatory agency.

- DTSC approved the RDIP in 2019 and subsequent Variances (April 9, 2020 and September 2, 2020).
- DTSC accepted the Draft Interim RACR on February 24, 2022.
- A Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded for the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones, restricting land use to transportation purposes and prohibiting unrestricted uses (residential, schools, daycare, hospitals, etc.).
- Annual O&M inspections (third quarter, July–September) and five-year reviews are required.
- Groundwater monitoring (wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10) and stormwater sampling continue. Reported concentrations of target analytes were below regulatory thresholds consistent with historical data. Once the CVRWQCB determines the clean-capped BCS does not pose a material threat to groundwater quality, the remaining wells may be destroyed.

## Open items

- A Supplemental RDIP for Ultimate Phase 2 (highway widening to four traffic lanes) must be submitted to DTSC for approval prior to construction.
- A Final RACR will be submitted following completion of Ultimate Phase 2, scheduled for 2028.
- The LUC has not yet been recorded; it must be finalized and recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder Office before DTSC certifies the Site.
- Ongoing annual groundwater and stormwater monitoring will continue until regulatory suspension.
