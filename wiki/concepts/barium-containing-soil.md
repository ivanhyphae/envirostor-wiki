# Barium Containing Soil

## Overview

Barium Containing Soil (BCS) is the primary contaminant of concern at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. BCS consists of soil with elevated concentrations of barium, and to a lesser extent, lead. This material originated from historical chemical processing activities at the former [[fmc-corporation]] facility. The BCS was generated in the early 1960s from pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, as well as native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange. These materials were consolidated into three stockpiles, designated [[stockpile-1]], Stockpile 2, and Stockpile 3. The selected remediation strategy involves consolidating the BCS and containing it within highway embankment fill, subsequently covered by clean fill and a structural pavement section as part of the SR 132 West Project's [[interim-phase-1]] and Ultimate Phase 2 construction.

## Site record

### Source and origin

The former FMC facility, situated northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange, was involved in ore and mineral processing, including barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate), from the 1930s to the 1970s. Liquid residue, referred to as "tailings," from these operations was discharged into unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, the State of California acquired a 4.3-acre parcel within the FMC facility, which included a portion of these ponds, for the construction of the SR 99 freeway. Pond tailings, underlying soils from the FMC property, and native soils excavated south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange were then used to create BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3.

### Pre-consolidation stockpile configuration and volumes

Prior to the commencement of [[interim-phase-1]] construction, three BCS stockpiles were present within the Caltrans SR 132 right-of-way:

*   **Stockpile 1:** Located approximately 150 feet west of Emerald Avenue, with an estimated fill volume of 34,000 cubic yards. It measured roughly 600 feet long (east-west) by 160 feet wide.
*   **Stockpile 2:** Situated between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, this stockpile was approximately 1,650 feet long (east-west) by 160 feet wide, with an estimated fill volume of 102,000 cubic yards.
*   **Stockpile 3:** Located east of SR 99, it measured approximately 1,100 feet long by 120 feet wide, with an estimated fill volume of 24,000 cubic yards.

The BCS material is predominantly composed of layered, poorly graded sand and silty sand, consistent with the underlying native alluvial deposits of the Modesto Formation. The average maximum thickness of the stockpiles was approximately 20 feet. The total estimated volume of BCS across all three stockpiles was 160,000 cubic yards.

### Barium concentrations

The [[interim-removal-action-completion-report]] (Interim RACR) documents that the BCS material contains elevated barium concentrations, reaching up to 72,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg), which exceed regulatory screening levels. Site-specific upper-bound background concentrations, as established in the DTSC-approved Variance to the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] dated April 9, 2020, are:

*   Barium: 120 mg/kg
*   Lead: 3.8 mg/kg

### Removal and consolidation activities (Interim Phase 1: 2020–2022)

**Stockpile 3 complete removal (February–March 2020):** Stockpile 3 was entirely excavated from the east side of SR 99. Approximately 27,675 cubic yards of BCS material, transported in 3,075 truckloads (assuming 9 yd³ per truck), were moved to the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. Twenty-nine verification soil samples were collected from 26 locations (S3-1 through S3-26). Initial results at three locations indicated barium concentrations ranging from 2,200 to 7,000 mg/kg, exceeding the verification threshold of 1,000 mg/kg. These specific areas were excavated an additional foot and resampled. Final verification samples from these areas showed barium concentrations between 46 and 970 mg/kg, and lead concentrations between 1.6 and 40 mg/kg, all below the established thresholds.

**Stockpile 2 end removals (February–March 2020):** Excavation occurred at the western end (near the Emerald Avenue bridge abutment, up to approximate Station 1135+35) and the eastern end (near the SR 99 bridge abutment, up to approximate Station 1148+30) of Stockpile 2. Ten verification samples from eight locations at the west end and fourteen samples from twelve locations at the east end were collected. Four initial locations exhibited barium concentrations between 1,100 and 4,100 mg/kg, exceeding the 1,000 mg/kg barium threshold. These areas were deepened by approximately one foot and resampled. Final verification samples from these areas showed barium concentrations ranging from 54 to 760 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg.

**MSE wall footing excavations (March–April 2020):** Excavations for Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) wall footings were conducted on the southerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2. Soil within the footing excavation areas was sampled at one-foot intervals to depths of 3 to 5 feet. For Stockpile 1, barium was detected in all 15 samples, with concentrations ranging from 42 to 420 mg/kg. The 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) for barium in these samples was 171.9 mg/kg, which exceeded the 120 mg/kg background concentration but remained below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold. For Stockpile 2, barium was detected in all 29 samples, with concentrations ranging from 53 to 930 mg/kg. Fourteen of the 29 samples exceeded the 120 mg/kg background threshold, predominantly within the upper 3 feet of the excavated material. All excavated MSE wall footing material was placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones.

### Containment and capping

BCS from Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3, along with [[aerially-deposited-lead]] (ADL) contaminated soil from the northbound SR 99 shoulder, was consolidated within the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. The Stockpile 1 Containment Zone is situated adjacent to and west of Emerald Avenue, between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50. The Stockpile 2 Containment Zone is located between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, between approximate Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30.

Clean fill capping was completed using DTSC-approved material sourced from the Carpenter Road area, Basin 5, and the Carpenter Road shoofly. The majority of the upper surfaces and northern slopes were capped by Fall 2020. A significant rain event in January 2021 caused substantial erosion on the unvegetated northerly slopes. These areas were regraded in September 2021 using clean fill from the Carpenter Road shoofly. The slopes were subsequently hydroseeded with a native plant mix and protected with erosion control blankets and fiber rolls in October 2021.

### Verification testing thresholds

The [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) established the following soil excavation verification thresholds for BCS removal areas:

*   **Barium:** 1,000 mg/kg (established for groundwater protection).
*   **Lead:** 80 mg/kg (based on the DTSC residential screening level).

All verification soil samples were analyzed for barium and lead by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) using EPA Test Method 6010B.

### Air monitoring during BCS disturbance

Air monitoring was conducted between February and June 2020 during BCS grading activities. Real-time pDR-1200 monitors were employed, with a Fence Line Action Level of 4.0 mg/m³ for total dust. Barium results were compared against an action level of 25 µg/m³ specified in the highway contractor's Health & Safety Plan (Adanta, November 2019). All monitored results were below the designated project action levels, indicating effective dust control measures.

## Regulatory status

The project operates under an Interagency Agreement between Caltrans and the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), with collaboration from the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). DTSC serves as the lead regulatory agency.

*   DTSC approved the RDIP in 2019 and subsequent Variances on April 9, 2020, and September 2, 2020.
*   DTSC accepted the Draft Interim RACR on February 24, 2022.
*   A Land Use Covenant (LUC) will be recorded for the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. This covenant will restrict land use to transportation purposes and prohibit unrestricted uses such as residential, schools, daycare, and hospitals.
*   Annual Operations and Maintenance (O&M) inspections (scheduled for the third quarter, July–September) and five-year reviews are required.
*   Groundwater monitoring (wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10) and stormwater sampling are ongoing. Reported concentrations of target analytes have remained below regulatory thresholds, consistent with historical data. The CVRWQCB will determine if the clean-capped BCS poses a material threat to groundwater quality, after which the remaining wells may be decommissioned.

## Open items

*   A Supplemental RDIP for Ultimate Phase 2 (expansion of the highway to four traffic lanes) must be submitted to DTSC for approval before construction can commence.
*   A Final RACR will be submitted upon completion of Ultimate Phase 2, which is currently scheduled for 2028.
*   The LUC has not yet been recorded. It must be finalized and recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder Office before DTSC can certify the Site.
*   Ongoing annual groundwater and stormwater monitoring will continue until regulatory suspension is granted.
