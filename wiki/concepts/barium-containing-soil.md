Barium-containing soil (BCS) is the fill material comprising Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3 in the former SR 132/99 interchange right-of-way, generated in the early 1960s when pond tailings and underlying soils from the adjacent Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) barite/celestite processing facility, together with native soils from nearby excavations, were placed as stockpile fill. Barium is the primary contaminant of concern at the site, with lead present to a lesser extent. The BCS's fate — excavation, consolidation, and clean-fill capping as permanent highway embankment during Interim Phase 1 of the SR 132 West Project — is the central remedial action documented in the case file, and the verification testing against a fixed barium removal threshold determined which excavated soil could be left in place versus which had to be relocated into the containment zones.

## Removal verification thresholds and results

The 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) set BCS excavation verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg barium (based on groundwater protection) and 80 mg/kg lead (DTSC residential screening level); a footnote to the RDIP noted the regulatory residential Environmental Screening Level for barium is 3,000 mg/kg, well above the more conservative removal threshold actually applied. Separately, a site-specific upperbound background concentration of 120 mg/kg barium (and 3.8 mg/kg lead) was established by the April 9, 2020 RDIP Variance for evaluating whether excavated soil was clean enough to reuse as capping material — a different, lower bar than the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold.

Verification sampling at each removal area followed the same pattern: initial samples at some locations exceeded the 1,000 mg/kg threshold, the excavation was deepened roughly one foot at those locations, and resampling confirmed all final results below threshold.

| Removal area | Initial exceedances | Final barium range (mg/kg) | Final lead range (mg/kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stockpile 3 (26 locations, 29 samples) | 3 locations, 2,200–7,000 mg/kg | 46–970 | 1.6–40 |
| Stockpile 2 west end (8 locations) | 2 locations (S2W-1, S2W-3) | 54–760 | 1.5–35 |
| Stockpile 2 east end (12 locations) | 2 locations (S2E-3, S2E-8) | 54–760 | 1.5–35 |

All final results at Stockpiles 2 and 3 fell below both the 1,000 mg/kg barium and 80 mg/kg lead thresholds, and Geocon determined both removal areas suitable for clean embankment fill and highway improvements without further RDIP monitoring. Stockpile 3 was excavated in its entirety (approximately 3,075 truckloads, on the order of 27,675–38,440 cy depending on the per-truck yardage assumption used in different memos) and relocated into the Stockpile 1 containment zone.

## MSE wall footing soil: a lower bar applied

A different, more consequential result occurred in the MSE wall footing excavations along the southern boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2, where soil was tested not against the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold but against the 120 mg/kg background value, because this material was being evaluated for reuse as clean capping soil rather than for removal completeness. At Stockpile 1 (trenches STK1-1 through STK1-3, 15 samples to 5 feet), barium ranged 42–420 mg/kg — every sample exceeded the 120 mg/kg background value, though all remained far below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold (95% UCL of 171.9 mg/kg, mean 128.3 mg/kg). At Stockpile 2 (trenches STK2-1 through STK2-7, 29 samples to 3–5 feet), barium ranged 53–930 mg/kg, with 14 of 29 samples exceeding 120 mg/kg, 13 of those 14 within the upper 3 feet. Lead at both locations stayed within the range of the 3.8 mg/kg background value and below the 80 mg/kg threshold.

Because the wall footing soil carried elevated barium relative to background — even though below the removal threshold — the decision was made not to use it as clean cap material: at Stockpile 1, the entire excavated footing volume was placed in the BCS containment zones; at Stockpile 2, the upper three feet was required to go into the containment zones, while material below 3 feet was deemed suitable for containment-zone fill, general embankment, or capping pending regulatory approval. This is the point in the record where the 120 mg/kg background figure, rather than the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold, controlled the disposition of soil.

## Clean fill source screening against the same background value

The 120 mg/kg barium background threshold also governed whether outside borrow sources could be used to cap the completed containment zones. Four candidate source areas were sampled: the Carpenter Road underpass excavation and Basin 5 excavation (both February–July 2020, composite Title 22 metals testing) returned barium in the range of 60–110 mg/kg and 66–94 mg/kg respectively, consistent with 2007 background data (72.8 mg/kg), and were DTSC-approved. The Carpenter Road Shoofly excavation (April 2021, 24 discrete samples along six transects, composited to 10 samples) returned barium of 28–96 mg/kg and lead of 1.4–3.8 mg/kg, both below the 120 mg/kg and 3.8 mg/kg background values, and was likewise found to meet the BCS clean cap criteria under the April 9, 2020 variance. The Bent 2 excavation stockpiles were the one identified source area not approved by DTSC for use as capping material; the record does not give the reason.

## Air monitoring for barium during grading

Airborne barium concentrations were monitored at the fence line during BCS mowing, excavation, transport, and fill placement between February and June 2020, using an action level of 25 µg/m³ specified in the contractor's Health & Safety Plan (Adanta, 2019), separate from the 4.0 mg/m³ total-dust Fence Line Action Level and the 1.5 µg/m³ lead action level. Reported results indicated airborne barium, lead, and total dust remained below these action levels throughout the monitored grading activities, supporting the conclusion that dust control ("wet") methods were effective. A prior, smaller-scale BCS excavation in 2012 (removal of roughly 2,800 yd³ from the northern portion of Stockpile 3 for an SR 99 off-ramp safety project) similarly reported airborne barium and lead well below regulatory thresholds.

## Disposition and ongoing controls

BCS from Stockpiles 1–3 was consolidated as permanent highway embankment fill within the Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones and capped with DTSC-approved clean fill, with the northern slopes vegetated in October 2021 after a January 2021 storm eroded the previously placed clean cap. No BCS was removed from the site to landfill during Interim Phase 1 (the 2012 Stockpile 3 partial excavation was the only prior offsite disposal). The remaining BCS is subject to a land use covenant to be recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder restricting the site to transportation use and requiring DTSC notification before any future disturbance of the containment zones, and to groundwater and stormwater monitoring that will continue until the CVRWQCB determines the capped BCS does not pose a material threat to groundwater quality. A supplemental RDIP covering any further BCS disturbance is to precede Ultimate Phase 2 construction, and a Final RACR is to follow completion of that phase, scheduled for 2028.
