## Overview

A Remedial Action Plan (RAP) is a document that outlines the process for addressing contamination at a site. For the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project, the RAP details the characterization of contaminants, assessment of risks, development of remedial alternatives, and the selection and implementation of the final remedy. The RAP is prepared for [[caltrans]] and is reviewed by the [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. The purpose of the RAP is to provide a comprehensive summary of the site's conditions and the proposed cleanup actions to the public for review and comment.

## Site Record

The [[feasibility-study]] for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles evaluated several remedial alternatives, including "no action," "institutional controls," and "removal of stockpiles and offsite disposal." The recommended alternative, Alternative 4 – Containment, involves using the stockpile soil as embankment material for the SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. This containment will be achieved by incorporating the soil into roadway pavements, retaining walls, and bridge abutments.

During the interim progress phase of the SR-132 Project (expected completion by 2018), the southern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 will be covered by the roadway, while the northern portions will be graded for drainage and capped with a 6- to 12-inch-thick vegetated clean soil cap. Stockpile 3 is planned to be entirely contained within the interim progress phase, with portions placed in the consolidation zone of the SR-132 bridge abutment and the remainder in the consolidation zone of Stockpile 2. The ultimate build-out phase (expected completion by 2028) will fully contain all stockpiles beneath roadway pavements and within project structures. Excavation will be limited to reconfiguring the stockpiles to meet project design criteria, not for removal.

The [[dtsc]] issued a letter on December 17, 2009, stating that the soil stockpiles, as currently managed by [[caltrans]], do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. This management includes limiting access, maintaining the perimeter fence, prohibiting soil-disturbing activities, and maintaining vegetative cover and the groundwater monitoring system.

A [[feasibility-study]] was completed by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] in June 2014, which evaluated remedial alternatives. A Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) was prepared by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] in October 2014, in response to comments from the [[dtsc]] dated September 18, 2014.

## Regulatory Status

The [[dtsc]] and [[central-valley-rwqcb]] are the oversight agencies for the RAP. The [[dtsc]] has reviewed and commented on various environmental reports, including the Draft Final RAP. The RAP is intended to be a supplement to the Environmental Impact Report (EIR) being prepared by [[caltrans]] in accordance with the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA).

## Open Items

The Draft Final RAP was made available for public review and comment. Public participation efforts included public notices, fact sheets, public meetings, and the opportunity for public comment on the Draft Final RAP. Comments received during the public comment period are to be responded to in writing and will be included in a responsiveness summary. The [[dtsc]] will approve the revised Final RAP for implementation after addressing comments.
