CEQA governs the environmental review that underlies the containment remedy for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site: [[caltrans]] prepared a combined Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and Remedial Action Plan (RAP) as CEQA Lead Agency, certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018, and [[dtsc]], acting as a CEQA Responsible Agency, relied on that same EIR/EA to satisfy CEQA review for its approval of the RAP on March 14, 2018. The dual-agency structure matters for the file because it means the CEQA record for the remedy is not a DTSC-authored document but a Caltrans one that DTSC adopted for its own purposes — with one significant carve-out.

Caltrans adopted a Statement of Overriding Considerations for the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, but DTSC's [[notice-of-determination]] states explicitly that this statement does not apply to DTSC's approval and that DTSC did not adopt a Statement of Overriding Considerations for its own project. This distinction matters to reviewers: it means DTSC's determination rests on a separate finding that the project, as conditioned, will not have a significant effect on the environment, rather than on an acknowledgment of significant unavoidable impacts accepted for overriding reasons. DTSC's determination further recites that mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval and that findings were made pursuant to CEQA.

The CEQA record — the certified EIR/EA, comments and responses, and the record of project approval — is held at the Caltrans District 10 office in Stockton and at the DTSC file room in Sacramento, and is also referenced online through the Caltrans project page and the DTSC EnviroStor site (global IDs 60001626 and 50280024). The State Clearinghouse number for the project is 2010012010. The DTSC Notice of Determination documenting this CEQA compliance was filed with the Governor's Office of Planning and Research, with an OPR receipt stamp dated 2018.

Because the containment remedy — retaining walls, bridge abutments, roadway cover, clean fill over unpaved areas, and ongoing land use controls, inspections, and groundwater monitoring — was approved through this CEQA pathway, the certified EIR/EA and DTSC's March 14, 2018 determination are the governing environmental-review basis for the remedy as built. Any question about the adequacy of environmental review for the stockpile containment approach traces back to this single certified document and to DTSC's decision to rely on it rather than prepare independent CEQA documentation.
