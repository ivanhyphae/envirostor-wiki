## Overview

The California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) is the statutory framework governing environmental review for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County. For this project, CEQA compliance is documented in the *SR 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment* (DEIR/EA, prepared by Caltrans with concurrence from the Federal Highway Administration). The DEIR/EA serves as both the CEQA document and the NEPA EA. The lead agency under CEQA is Caltrans, District 10. The document identifies the project as a remedial action involving excavation, off-site disposal, and backfill of petroleum‑impacted soil from stockpiles at two locations (Patterson Truck Trail and Yolo Bypass). CEQA review determined that the project may result in significant environmental impacts, requiring an EIR rather than a Negative Declaration. The DEIR/EA was circulated for public comment in [date, not specified in sources – omit]; the final EIR (FEIR) and CEQA findings have not yet been finalized per the record.

## Site record

The DEIR/EA evaluates CEQA significance thresholds for multiple resource areas. Key documented facts from the record:

- **Project actions**: Excavation of up to 55,000 cubic yards of soil (Section 1.0) from two stockpile sites: Patterson Truck Trail (approx. 45,000 CY) and Yolo Bypass (approx. 10,000 CY). Soil is contaminated with total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH) and metals (barium up to 760 mg/kg at MW-5, per DEIR/EA Table 3-1). Off-site disposal at permitted facilities (e.g., Bakersfield landfill) and backfill with clean import material.
- **CEQA environmental review scope**: The DEIR/EA evaluates impacts on air quality, cultural resources, biological resources, hydrology/water quality, noise, traffic, and hazardous materials management. Mitigation measures are proposed for significant impacts, including fugitive dust control (MM-AQ-1), cultural resource monitoring (MM-CR-1), and stormwater pollution prevention (SWPPP). No significant unavoidable impacts are identified.
- **Alternatives analysis under CEQA**: Three alternatives are considered per CEQA Guidelines Section 15126.6: No Project (no action), Reduced Excavation (25,000 CY), and Modified Disposal (on-site treatment). The DEIR/EA identifies the Proposed Action as the environmentally superior alternative, as it fully remediates the stockpiles and reduces risk of off-site migration.
- **Consultation**: The DEIR/EA documents consultation with DTSC (Department of Toxic Substances Control), RWQCB (Regional Water Quality Control Board), and Native American tribes under AB 52 and SB 18. DTSC issued a Ready for Reuse Determination for the Patterson Truck Trail stockpile in 2019 after removal of 40,000 CY during Phase 1 (2019–2020). Phase 2 (remaining soil) is subject to CEQA review in the DEIR/EA.

## Regulatory status

CEQA compliance for this project is currently in draft stage. The DEIR/EA was released for public review (period start/end not specified in provided sources). Key regulatory milestones:

- **Notice of Preparation (NOP)**: Not explicitly mentioned in DEIR/EA but referenced as part of scoping. A public scoping meeting was held [date not given].
- **Lead agency determination**: Caltrans determined that an EIR is required because the project may result in significant impacts to cultural resources (prehistoric archaeological sites) and air quality (PM10 emissions during excavation). No Negative Declaration or Mitigated Negative Declaration was prepared.
- **Status**: The Final EIR (FEIR) has not been filed with the State Clearinghouse (SCH number not specified). CEQA findings (Section 15091) and a Statement of Overriding Considerations (if applicable) are pending. The DEIR/EA states that the project is consistent with all applicable CEQA Guidelines and that all feasible mitigation measures are incorporated.

The project must also comply with the Caltrans CEQA Implementation Procedures and the FHWA NEPA regulations (23 CFR 771). Concurrent NEPA review is performed via the EA, with a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) expected if impacts remain within thresholds.

## Open items

Based on the DEIR/EA, the following CEQA‑related items remain unresolved or pending:

1. **Final CEQA findings and EIR certification**: The FEIR has not been approved; Caltrans must adopt CEQA findings, a mitigation monitoring and reporting program (MMRP), and, if significant unavoidable impacts exist, a Statement of Overriding Considerations. The DEIR/EA does not quantify cumulative impacts beyond the scope of the project – this may be contested by commenters.
2. **Public comments and responses**: The record does not indicate whether comments were received or how they were addressed. Future litigation risk exists if adequate response to significant environmental issues (e.g., tribal cultural resources, cumulative air quality) is not provided.
3. **DTSC concurrence on final remedial action plan (RAP)**: Although DTSC issued a Ready for Reuse Determination for Phase 1, the Phase 2 RAP (incorporated into the DEIR/EA) requires DTSC approval under its VCA (Voluntary Cleanup Agreement). CEQA review must satisfy DTSC’s AB 1482 requirements.
4. **Post‑certification compliance**: After FEIR certification, the project must implement the MMRP and conduct annual reporting. Verification sampling results (e.g., groundwater monitoring at MW-5) must be submitted to RWQCB. No confirmation of compliance with CEQA mitigation measures is yet available.
