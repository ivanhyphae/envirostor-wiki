# Interim Phase 1

## Overview

Interim Phase 1 refers to the first stage of construction for the State Route (SR) 132 West Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The project was designed as an ultimate four-lane expressway build-out; Interim Phase 1 constructed two of the four planned traffic lanes from east of SR 99 to North Dakota Avenue, along with related highway bridge structures and interchanges.

Within the environmental remediation scope, Interim Phase 1 served as the implementation window for consolidating and clean-fill capping [[barium-containing-soil]] Stockpiles 1 through 3 as highway embankment material. The work was performed in accordance with the *Remedial Design Implementation Plan* (RDIP, Geocon, January 30, 2019). SR 132 opened to traffic under this interim configuration on November 3, 2022. Ultimate Phase 2 construction, scheduled for completion in 2028, will widen the highway to the full four-lane configuration.

## Site Record

Interim Phase 1 highway construction encompassed the containment of all three BCS stockpiles within designated Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones located westerly of SR 99. The pre-construction stockpile volumes were: Stockpile 1 at approximately 34,000 cubic yards, Stockpile 2 at approximately 102,000 cubic yards, and Stockpile 3 at approximately 24,000 cubic yards.

### Stockpile 3 Excavation and Relocation

BCS Stockpile 3, formerly located on the east side of SR 99 (roughly 1,100 feet long by 120 feet wide), was completely excavated. Approximately 27,675 cubic yards of soil was transported by truck and placed as embankment fill within the Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone — both on top of Stockpile 1 and between Stockpile 1 and Emerald Avenue. Twenty-nine confirmation soil samples were collected from the bottom of the Stockpile 3 excavation area and analyzed for barium and lead. Three of the confirmation samples showed barium concentrations above the cleanup goal of 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). These three areas were subsequently deepened by approximately one foot and resampled. Final confirmation samples demonstrated remaining soil concentrations below the cleanup goals for both barium and lead (80 mg/kg).

### Stockpile 2 End Excavations

The western and eastern ends of BCS Stockpile 2 were removed using scrapers and placed directly within the Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone as embankment fill. Verification samples for barium and lead were collected from eight sample locations on the western end and twelve sample locations on the eastern end from native soil following removal. Initial confirmation sample results indicated four areas above the 1,000 mg/kg cleanup goal for barium. These four areas were deepened by an additional foot; resulting confirmation samples showed all remaining soil below the verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg for barium and 80 mg/kg for lead.

### Southern Slope Excavations for MSE Walls

Soil was removed from the southern slopes of BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2 to steepen them and allow construction of a mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) retaining wall along the expressway length. Pre-excavation characterization soil samples were collected at 200-foot intervals along the southern slope at one-foot depth intervals to a maximum of five feet below the surface. Results indicated all concentrations for barium and lead were below cleanup goals, and lead was within background concentrations. However, barium was found above the site-specific background concentration of 120 mg/kg, so all material removed was placed in the BCS Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones as embankment fill using scrapers rather than being used as clean cap material.

### ADL Soil Placement

Shallow soil containing hazardous waste concentrations of aerially deposited lead (ADL) was removed from the unpaved shoulder of a portion of existing northbound SR 99 adjacent to the former location of BCS Stockpile 3. ADL-impacted soil was placed in the containment zone in the eastern portion of BCS Stockpile 1.

### Clean Fill Capping

Two DTSC-approved variances to the RDIP addressed clean fill sourcing during Interim Phase 1:

- **Variance No. 1** (April 9, 2020, approved April 17, 2020): Identified clean fill source borrow areas including MSE retaining wall foundation excavations adjacent to the southerly boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2, and established site-specific upper-bound background concentrations of barium at 120 mg/kg and lead at 3.8 mg/kg for comparison with proposed clean fill source area characterization soil samples.

- **Variance No. 2** (September 2, 2020, approved September 21, 2020): Clarified the alternative use of Class 3 aggregate base over the top of completed BCS Containment Zones. The specified structural pavement section for Interim Phase 1 consisted of 0.85 feet (10.2 inches) concrete pavement, 0.25 feet (3.0 inches) hot mix asphalt, and 0.70 feet (8.4 inches) Class 3 aggregate subbase. Areas outside the highway structural pavement section on the upper surface of the stockpiles were capped with hardscape.

### Groundwater Monitoring

Prior to construction, ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) were located adjacent and nearby to the BCS Stockpiles. Depth to groundwater measured in October 2021 ranged between approximately 39 to 43 feet below natural grade. Due to conflicts with planned highway improvements, six of the ten monitoring wells were destroyed in April 2019. Groundwater extraction/dewatering was not required during Interim Phase 1 construction in the immediate vicinity of the BCS Stockpiles.

### Air Monitoring

Air monitoring for potential fugitive dust emissions was implemented during site preparation, BCS Stockpile excavation, and fill placement activities. Results of air monitoring performed during BCS grading activities indicated airborne levels of total dust, barium, and lead were below designated project action and risk levels.

## Regulatory Status

The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) served as the lead regulatory agency providing site mitigation oversight under a [[caltrans]] Interagency Agreement, in cooperation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

DTSC's February 24, 2022 acceptance letter for the Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report stated that the work completed to remediate the Site to date was accomplished as per the approved RDIP or the two approved variance requests. Certification of the Remedial Action Plan will not occur until after Ultimate Phase 2 build-out is completed.

Upon approval of the Final Interim Phase 1 RACR, DTSC will enter into an Operations and Maintenance Agreement with Caltrans that will include the [[operations-and-maintenance-plan]] and complete a land use covenant for the containment areas. The land use covenant/deed restriction will be recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder Office. A legal description and boundary survey of the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones was prepared by Dokken Engineering for this purpose.

No regulatory permits specific to BCS containment and capping were required beyond those already obtained for construction of Interim Phase 1 of the SR 132 West Project.

## Open Items

- A **Final Interim RACR** (distinct from the December 1, 2022 Interim RACR or the earlier draft accepted by DTSC in February 2022) must include completed As-Built Plan and cross section figures, final survey data, and the approved Operations and Maintenance Plan.

- The **Operations and Maintenance Plan** requires a soil management plan, an annual inspection of the pavement and other features of the containment remedy, and groundwater monitoring. The O&M Plan will be submitted for DTSC review such that an approved version can be incorporated into the Final Phase 1 RACR.

- **Ultimate Phase 2** (scheduled for completion in 2028) will include adding additional lanes and completing the final build-out. This effort will require a supplemental RDIP for DTSC review and approval, field oversight, an Ultimate Phase 2 RACR, and revisions to the O&M Plan and O&M Agreement if necessary. Ultimate Phase 2 will be conducted under a separate agreement between DTSC and Caltrans.

- A **Final RACR** will be submitted following Ultimate Phase 2 construction.
