# Interim Phase 1

## Overview

Interim Phase 1 refers to the initial construction phase of the State Route (SR) 132 West Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. This phase focused on constructing two of the four planned traffic lanes, along with associated highway structures and interchanges, from east of SR 99 to North Dakota Avenue. Environmentally, Interim Phase 1 involved the consolidation and capping of [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) Stockpiles 1 through 3, incorporating them as highway embankment material. This work was conducted under the guidance of the *Remedial Design Implementation Plan* (RDIP), prepared by Geocon in January 2019. The SR 132 was opened to traffic in this interim configuration on November 3, 2022.

## Site Record

Interim Phase 1 construction encompassed the containment of all three BCS stockpiles within designated Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones, located west of SR 99. The pre-construction volumes were: Stockpile 1 at approximately 34,000 cubic yards, Stockpile 2 at approximately 102,000 cubic yards, and Stockpile 3 at approximately 24,000 cubic yards.

### Stockpile 3 Excavation and Relocation

BCS Stockpile 3, previously situated on the east side of SR 99, was fully excavated. Approximately 27,675 cubic yards of soil were transported and used as embankment fill within the Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone. Twenty-nine confirmation soil samples from the excavation area were analyzed for barium and lead. Three samples exceeded the barium cleanup goal of 1,000 mg/kg. These areas were deepened by approximately one foot and resampled, with final confirmation samples showing concentrations below the cleanup goals for both barium (1,000 mg/kg) and lead (80 mg/kg).

### Stockpile 2 End Excavations

The western and eastern ends of BCS Stockpile 2 were excavated and placed as embankment fill within the Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone. Verification samples from native soil after removal indicated four areas with barium concentrations above the 1,000 mg/kg cleanup goal. These areas were deepened by an additional foot, and subsequent confirmation samples were below the 1,000 mg/kg barium and 80 mg/kg lead thresholds.

### Southern Slope Excavations for MSE Walls

Soil was removed from the southern slopes of BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2 to facilitate the construction of mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) retaining walls. Pre-excavation sampling indicated all concentrations for barium and lead were below cleanup goals, though barium exceeded the site-specific background concentration of 120 mg/kg. All removed material was placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones as embankment fill.

### ADL Soil Placement

Shallow soil with [[aerially-deposited-lead]] (ADL) concentrations was removed from the unpaved shoulder of a portion of northbound SR 99 adjacent to the former location of BCS Stockpile 3. This ADL-impacted soil was placed in the containment zone in the eastern portion of BCS Stockpile 1.

### Clean Fill Capping

Two DTSC-approved variances to the RDIP addressed clean fill sourcing:

* **Variance No. 1** (April 9, 2020): Identified clean fill source borrow areas, including MSE retaining wall foundation excavations adjacent to the southerly boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2. It also established site-specific upper-bound background concentrations for barium at 120 mg/kg and lead at 3.8 mg/kg for characterization samples.
* **Variance No. 2** (September 2, 2020): Clarified the use of Class 3 aggregate base over the completed BCS Containment Zones. The structural pavement section for Interim Phase 1 included 0.85 feet of concrete pavement, 0.25 feet of hot mix asphalt, and 0.70 feet of Class 3 aggregate subbase. Areas outside the structural pavement section were capped with hardscape.

### Groundwater Monitoring

Prior to construction, ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) were located near the BCS Stockpiles. Groundwater depths in October 2021 ranged from approximately 39 to 43 feet below natural grade. Six wells were destroyed in April 2019 due to conflicts with highway improvements. Groundwater extraction or dewatering was not required during Interim Phase 1 construction in the vicinity of the BCS Stockpiles.

### Air Monitoring

Air monitoring for fugitive dust emissions was conducted during site preparation, BCS Stockpile excavation, and fill placement. Results indicated airborne levels of total dust, barium, and lead were below designated project action and risk levels.

## Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) served as the lead regulatory agency, overseeing site mitigation under a [[caltrans]] Interagency Agreement, in cooperation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

DTSC's February 24, 2022 acceptance letter for the Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report stated that the work completed met the requirements of the approved RDIP or its variance requests. Certification of the Remedial Action Plan will occur after Ultimate Phase 2 construction. Upon approval of the Final Interim Phase 1 [[interim-removal-action-completion-report]], DTSC will enter into an Operations and Maintenance Agreement with Caltrans, which will include the [[operations-and-maintenance-plan]] and a land use covenant recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder Office. No specific permits beyond those for Interim Phase 1 highway construction were required for BCS containment and capping.

## Open Items

*   A **Final Interim RACR** is required, including as-built plans, final survey data, and the approved Operations and Maintenance Plan.
*   The **Operations and Maintenance Plan** needs to be finalized and submitted for DTSC review, incorporating a soil management plan, annual inspections, and groundwater monitoring.
*   **Ultimate Phase 2** construction (scheduled for completion in 2028) will require a supplemental RDIP, field oversight, an Ultimate Phase 2 RACR, and potential revisions to the O&M Plan and Agreement.
*   A **Final RACR** will be submitted following the completion of Ultimate Phase 2 construction.
