## Overview

The Waste Extraction Test (WET) is a laboratory procedure used to determine whether a solid waste exhibits a hazardous characteristic—specifically, toxicity—under California’s hazardous waste regulations (California Code of Regulations, Title 22, Division 4.5, Chapter 11). For the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation (SR 132, Stanislaus County), WET results for **Stockpile 2** were used to classify the material for off‑site disposal or further treatment. The WET simulates leaching conditions in a landfill using a citrate buffer, differing from the federal Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP). The regulatory threshold for WET toxicity (waste extraction limit) is typically lower and more stringent than TCLP thresholds for many metals.

The project record **SR 132 Stockpile 2 BCS Removal Tech Memo** documents the WET testing performed on samples from Stockpile 2 during the 2019–2020 removal action. This testing was required by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) to support waste profiling and disposal facility acceptance.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standard**: California Title 22 waste extraction limit (WET) for each regulated constituent. For metals commonly associated with Caltrans stockpiles, typical limits include:
  - Barium: 100 mg/L
  - Lead: 5 mg/L
  - Chromium (total): 5 mg/L (hexavalent chromium has separate standards)
- **Agency**: DTSC oversees waste classification and disposal for this project, in coordination with the local Certified Unified Program Agency (CUPA) and the landfill operator.
- **Compliance**: As documented in the Tech Memo, Stockpile 2 material met the WET limits for non‑hazardous classification at the time of removal. No subsequent WET exceedances were reported for this stockpile.
