## Barium Containing Soil Aerially Deposited Lead

### Overview
Barium Containing Soil Aerially Deposited Lead (BCS/ADL) refers to stockpiled materials at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] site that were subject to remediation. These stockpiles were addressed through the use of [[class-3-aggregate-subbase]] as a clean cap.

### Site Record
On September 15, 2020, a visual inspection of the Class 3 subbase material proposed as a cap over BCS/ADL stockpiles revealed it to be primarily a fine sand.

Due to the fine-grained nature of this material, a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches was agreed upon, an increase from the initially proposed 4 inches. This adjustment was considered particularly important as no pavement or hardscaping was scheduled to be completed before the upcoming rainy season.

A request was made for a single, composite sample to be collected from the stockpile of Class 3 material adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing. This sample was to be analyzed for barium and lead using EPA Test Method 6010B. The purpose of this sampling was to verify that the material met the standards outlined in the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] for use as a clean cap over BCS/ADL stockpiles.

### Regulatory Status
On September 21, 2020, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) officially accepted the use of Class 3 subbase as an acceptable clean cap alternative for the BCS/ADL stockpiles. This acceptance was contingent upon the condition of a 6-inch minimum thickness and the possibility of increased monitoring. This decision was formalized as DTSC's official acceptance of the request. The acceptance was part of the approval of the Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (Request) dated September 2, 2020.
