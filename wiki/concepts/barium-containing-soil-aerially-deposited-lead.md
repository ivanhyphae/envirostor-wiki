## Barium Containing Soil Aerially Deposited Lead

### Overview
Barium Containing Soil Aerially Deposited Lead (BCS/ADL) refers to stockpiled materials at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] site that were subject to remediation. These stockpiles were addressed through the use of [[class-3-aggregate-subbase]] as a clean cap.

### Site Record
On September 15, 2020, a visual inspection of the Class 3 subbase material proposed as a cap over BCS/ADL stockpiles revealed it to be primarily a fine sand. {{quote: "a visual inspection of this stockpiled material conducted on September 15, 2020 revealed that it is primarily a fine sand with most of the grains likely small enough to pass through a No. 4 sieve."}}

Due to the fine-grained nature of this material, a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches was agreed upon, an increase from the initially proposed 4 inches. {{quote: "Given its abundant fine grained nature, both agencies agree that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches rather than 4 inches is warranted and that increased monitoring may be necessary."}} This adjustment was considered particularly important as no pavement or hardscaping was scheduled to be completed before the upcoming rainy season. {{quote: "This is especially true since no pavement or hard scaping will be completed prior to the upcoming rainy season."}}

A request was made for a single, composite sample to be collected from the stockpile of Class 3 material adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing. This sample was to be analyzed for barium and lead using EPA Test Method 6010B. {{quote: "DTSC and the WB are requesting a single, composite sample be obtained for barium and lead analysis following Environmental Protection Agency Test Method 6010B from the stockpile of Class 3 material adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing."}} The purpose of this sampling was to verify that the material met the standards outlined in the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] for use as a clean cap over BCS/ADL stockpiles. {{quote: "This is necessary to ensure this soil meets the standards imposed in the RDIP since it may be used as Class 3 subbase clean cap over the BCS/ADL stockpiles."}}

### Regulatory Status
On September 21, 2020, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) officially accepted the use of Class 3 subbase as an acceptable clean cap alternative for the BCS/ADL stockpiles. {{quote: "Both DTSC and the WB agree that Class 3 subbase can be used as an acceptable clean cap alternative..."}} This acceptance was contingent upon the condition of a 6-inch minimum thickness and the possibility of increased monitoring. {{quote: "Given its abundant fine grained nature, both agencies agree that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches rather than 4 inches is warranted and that increased monitoring may be necessary."}} This decision was formalized as DTSC's official acceptance of the request. {{quote: "This e-mail serves as DTSCs official acceptance of the Request for use of Class 3 subbase."}} The acceptance was part of the approval of the Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (Request) dated September 2, 2020. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) have reviewed the Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (Request) dated September 2, 2020."}}
