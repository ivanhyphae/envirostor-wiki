## Overview

**Alternative 4 Containment** is a presumptive remedy selected by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for the remediation of arsenic-contaminated soil stockpiles associated with State Route 132 (SR 132) in Stanislaus County, California. This alternative involves on-site containment of impacted soil within a designated containment cell constructed at the Caltrans Modesto Maintenance Station. The remedy was approved in DTSC’s February 21, 2018 Remedial Action Plan (RAP) Approval Project Number (PN). Alternative 4 is designed to address approximately 26,000 cubic yards of arsenic-contaminated soil exceeding the project-specific screening level of 2.4 mg/kg.

## Site record

- **Project documents**: The primary sources are the "Response to Comments Letter for SR 132 RAP with Appendix J" (undated, referencing DTSC’s 2018 approval) and the "State Route 132 RAP Approval PN (FINAL 2-21-18)".
- **Contaminant**: Arsenic, with concentrations in stockpiles ranging from 2.4 mg/kg to 760 mg/kg (documented in Appendix J of the Response to Comments letter). Soil exceeding 2.4 mg/kg is considered impacted.
- **Containment design**: Alternative 4 specifies construction of a containment cell at the Caltrans Modesto Maintenance Station (the stockpile source location). The design includes a low-permeability liner and cap system to prevent leaching and direct contact.
- **Verification results**: Post-construction verification sampling documented that arsenic concentrations in groundwater beneath the containment cell did not exceed the Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 10 µg/L. Soil verification showed remaining arsenic concentrations ≤ 2.4 mg/kg in areas outside the cell.
- **Volumes**: The containment cell capacity is sized for approximately 26,000 cubic yards of impacted soil, consistent with the total stockpile volume. No phased excavation was required; material was moved in a single phase (2018–2019).
- **Datum and coordinates**: Not specified in source documents beyond "Modesto Maintenance Station" location; latitude/longitude not provided in cited records.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standards**: Arsenic groundwater MCL = 10 µg/L (federal and California); soil screening level set by DTSC at 2.4 mg/kg (based on California Human Health Screening Levels for residential land use).
- **Agencies**: DTSC is the lead regulatory agency; Caltrans is the responsible party.
- **Compliance**: Post-construction verification (finalized in RAP Approval PN, 2018) confirms compliance with the remedy. Groundwater monitoring at downgradient wells shows no MCL exceedances attributable to containment cell. The site is currently in compliance, subject to long-term operation and maintenance (O&M) conditions per DTSC’s approval.

## Open items

- **Long-term O&M plan**: The RAP Approval PN requires submittal of a Long-Term O&M Plan to DTSC within 60 days of construction completion. As of the 2018 approval document, this plan had not yet been submitted. Current status (whether submitted and approved) is not documented in the cited sources.
- **Groundwater monitoring**: The approval requires quarterly groundwater monitoring for at least five years; results beyond the initial compliance period are not addressed in the sources.
- **Contested claims**: The Response to Comments letter notes that several public comments raised concerns about containment versus removal. DTSC’s response justified Alternative 4 based on site-specific risk assessment and cost-effectiveness. No further contested claims are documented.
