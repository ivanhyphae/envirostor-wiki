## Alternative 4 Containment

### Overview

Alternative 4 Containment is the DTSC‑approved presumptive remedy for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). The remedy was selected through the CERCLA nine‑criteria process documented in the Final Feasibility Study (Geocon, June 2014) and adopted in the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (Geocon, October 2014, revised). DTSC issued final RAP approval via Project Number on February 21, 2018. The alternative provides for on‑site isolation and encapsulation of approximately **160,000 yd³** of barium‑containing soil (primary COPCs: barium, lead, strontium; arsenic at ≤ 2.4 mg/kg outside containment zones) by using the stockpile material as highway embankment fill within the SR‑132/SR‑99 interchange portion of the SR‑132 West Freeway/Expressway Project.

### Site Record

**Stockpile Volumes and Phasing**  
The remedy is built in two phases linked to the SR‑132 Project schedule:
- **Interim Progress Phase** (later termed Interim Phase 1; completed by 2020 per RDIP, originally scheduled 2018 per RAP): construction of a two‑lane roadway over the southern halves of Stockpiles 1 & 2; temporary 6‑ to 12‑inch clean‑soil vegetated cap over northern portions; complete consolidation of Stockpile 3 within the eastern abutment of the SR‑132 bridge over SR‑99 and, for excess, onto Stockpile 2 in the “stockpile fill consolidation zone.”  
- **Ultimate Build‑Out** (Ultimate Phase 2; by 2028): full containment of all stockpile soil behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement; median areas covered by pavement or a synthetic liner and clean‑soil layer.

**RDIP (Interim Phase 1) Detailed Quantities**  
The Remedial Design Implementation Plan (Geocon, January 30, 2019) specifies:
- **Stockpile 1**: no planned excavation; fill‑consolidation zones on its upper surface and southern slope (15,000 yd³ capacity) plus adjacent State ROW between Stockpile 1 and the Emerald Avenue bridge abutment (12,500 yd³).
- **Stockpile 2**: excavation of ~2,200 yd³ for the Emerald Avenue bridge abutment and ~1,650 yd³ for the SR‑99 bridge abutment; fill‑consolidation zone on the upper surface and southern slope (27,000 yd³ capacity).
- **Stockpile 3**: complete removal, ~24,000 yd³, to depths to native ground at elevation ~84 ft MSL. Originally planned for encapsulation behind retaining walls, but design changes eliminated those walls; therefore all Stockpile 3 material is now placed over Stockpile 1 and adjacent State ROW.

Total estimated barium‑containing soil removal volume for Interim Phase 1: **27,850 yd³**; total fill‑consolidation capacity: **54,500 yd³**, leaving ~26,650 yd³ available for aerially deposited lead (ADL) soil from SR‑99 shoulder widening.

**Stockpile 3 Design Change**  
The 2019 RDIP notes that the retaining walls originally proposed behind the SR‑132 bridge abutment for Stockpile 3 containment were eliminated. As a result, Stockpile 3 is now fully removed and its material placed as permanent highway embankment fill over Stockpile 1 and in the vacant State ROW between Stockpile 1 and Emerald Avenue.

**ADL‑Contaminated Soil**  
ADL‑contaminated soil from unpaved shoulders of northbound SR‑99 (between L Street and the Kansas Avenue off‑ramp) is co‑placed within the Stockpile 1 & 2 consolidation zones under the 2016 *Soil Management Agreement for Aerially Deposited Lead‑Contaminated Soils* between DTSC and Caltrans. Conditions: total lead ≤ 80 mg/kg (95% UCL) or WET‑soluble lead ≥ 5.0 mg/L; must always be ≥ 5 ft above highest groundwater; covered by ≥ 1 ft clean soil or pavement; pH ≥ 5.0.

**Clean Cap and Cover**  
- Interim cap on northern slopes: minimum 12‑inch imported clean fill (or 4‑inch aggregate base pending pavement).  
- Source of clean fill: vacant/agricultural land along the SR‑132 ROW between Carpenter Road and Rosemore Avenue overcrossings, sampled per DTSC *Information Advisory Clean Imported Fill Material* (Appendix E of RDIP).  
- Slopes vegetated with native‑plant hydroseed (no irrigation).  
- All graded barium‑containing soil surfaces covered by structural pavement, hardscape, or clean‑soil cap before end of Interim Phase 1.

**Monitoring During Construction**  
- **Air monitoring**: perimeter real‑time particulate monitoring; action levels per the Health and Safety Plan; work suspended when visible dust cannot be controlled.  
- **Groundwater/stormwater monitoring**: quarterly groundwater elevation and sample collection from ten wells; storm‑event‑based surface‑water monitoring; continues through Phase 1 and beyond per DTSC/CVRWQCB direction.  
- **Verification sampling**: soil samples collected from excavation limits to confirm removal of material above project‑specific thresholds; results reported in the Remedial Action Completion Report.

**Survey and Land‑Use Covenant**  
As‑built survey data (latitude, longitude, elevation NAVD 88, SR‑132 post‑mile limits) of the final containment zones will be used to record a Land Use Covenant (LUC) with the Stanislaus County Clerk‑Recorder. The LUC restricts future land use to transportation; residential, school, daycare, hospital, and senior‑care uses are prohibited. No LUC is required for the excavated bridge‑abutment areas or the removed Stockpile 3 footprint.

**Schedule (per RDIP)**
- RDIP final approval: early 2019  
- Start barium‑containing soil containment: mid‑2019  
- Complete containment: late 2019  
- Complete Interim Phase 1 construction: late 2020  
- Remedial Action Completion Report approval: 2021  

### Regulatory Status

**Applicable Standards**
- **Barium**: California MCL 1,000 µg/L (groundwater); DTSC residential screening level for soil (not explicitly cited for barium in the RAP but the 2.4 mg/kg arsenic screening level is used).  
- **Lead**: total lead ≤ 80 mg/kg (95% UCL) and WET‑soluble lead < 5.0 mg/L for ADL soil reuse per DTSC Agreement.  
- **Arsenic**: soil screening level **2.4 mg/kg** (California Human Health Screening Level for residential land use); groundwater MCL **10 µg/L**.  
- **Groundwater**: quarterly monitoring for at least five years per RAP Approval PN; results to be compared against MCLs and background.

**Agencies**  
- **Lead regulatory agency**: DTSC  
- **Co‑regulator**: Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)  
- **Responsible party**: Caltrans  

**Compliance**  
Post‑construction verification (RAP Approval PN, 2018) confirmed arsenic ≤ 2.4 mg/kg in areas outside the containment cell and groundwater arsenic ≤ 10 µg/L at downgradient wells. The site is currently in compliance subject to ongoing O&M conditions. DTSC issued a “Start Work Notice” (English/Spanish) in local newspapers two weeks before Interim Phase 1 construction began.

**Operation and Maintenance**  
- O&M Agreement between Caltrans and DTSC required.  
- O&M Plan: annual inspection of pavement, caps, retaining walls, fencing; corrective‑action schedule.  
- DTSC five‑year reviews of containment effectiveness.  
- Quarterly groundwater and periodic stormwater monitoring continue until Phase 2 completion or DTSC/CVRWQCB deems them unnecessary.

**Cost‑Effectiveness**  
The Statement of Reasons (RAP Appendix C) states that containment is approximately **20 times less costly** than excavation and off‑site disposal (Alternative 3). The bulk capital cost is grading, clean‑soil cap placement, and pavement/liner cover; ongoing monitoring costs are moderate.

### Open Items

- **Ultimate Phase 2 final containment**: The northern slopes of Stockpiles 1 & 2 remain under a temporary vegetated cap until retaining walls and pavement are constructed by 2028. An additional RDIP for Phase 2 has not yet been submitted.  
- **Long‑term O&M Plan**: The RAP Approval PN required submittal within 60 days of construction completion. Status of DTSC review and approval is not documented in the sources.  
- **Groundwater monitoring beyond initial five‑year period**: Quarterly monitoring is required for at least five years; results and duration beyond that period are not specified in the cited documents.  
- **LUC recording**: The land‑use covenant for the containment zones must be recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk‑Recorder after certification; the current status of the LUC is not provided.  
- **Community opposition**: The RAP acknowledges that some community opposition to the SR‑132 project exists unrelated to the stockpiles; no specific litigation or contested‑claims record is included in the cited sources.  
- **Alternative 5 (Maze Boulevard widening)**: Master Response #6 in the FEIR/EA notes that this alternative would **not** have resulted in containment of the stockpiles, requiring a separate remedial action plan; it was eliminated from consideration.  
- **Levee reuse suggestion**: DTSC responded to public comments that use of stockpile soil for levee construction/raising was not evaluated and would not provide the same level of protection as Alternative 4.
