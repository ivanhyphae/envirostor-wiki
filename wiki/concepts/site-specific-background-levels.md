Site-specific background levels for barium and lead — established from a 2007 Shaw background data summary and extended to other Title 22 metals in later characterization work — function as the operative yardstick throughout the Modesto Soil Stockpiles case, deciding whether excavated soil can be reused as clean cover, embankment fill, or must instead go into the barium-containing soil (BCS) containment zones. The maximum background value of 120 mg/kg for barium and 3.8 mg/kg for lead recur across nearly every clean-fill characterization memo from 2020–2021, each time as the line between soil DTSC will accept as cap material and soil that must be contained.

## Origin and application to background comparisons

The background values trace to a 2007 Shaw background data summary, cited in the February 27, 2020 Carpenter Road borrow area memo, which gave site-specific concentrations for a full Title 22 metals suite (e.g., arsenic 1.2 mg/kg, barium 72.8 mg/kg, chromium 7.1 mg/kg, lead 2.0 mg/kg, vanadium 31.3 mg/kg, zinc 26.3 mg/kg). These figures were used as a comparison basis, not a pass/fail threshold, for that borrow area and for the Basin 5 borrow area tested in June 2020 (revised memo July 2, 2020), where reported ranges (e.g., barium 66–94 mg/kg, lead 5.8–11 mg/kg) were found consistent with background and the material judged suitable to generate clean cover fill.

By contrast, the operative regulatory threshold used repeatedly to classify excavated soil as clean fill versus containment material was a single upper-bound figure: **120 mg/kg for barium** and **3.8 mg/kg for lead**, both described as "maximum site-specific background" values. This pairing appears in the April 9, 2020 Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) approved by DTSC and governs the disposition decisions that follow.

## Characterization results measured against the thresholds

| Source area (date) | Barium range (mg/kg) | Exceeds 120 mg/kg background? | Lead range (mg/kg) | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing (Apr 6, 2020) | 42–420 | Yes, all 15 samples exceeded background (95% UCL 171.9, mean 128.3) | 1.1–6.7 | DTSC required placement in Stockpile 1/2 containment zones, not as cap material, despite Geocon's suggestion it might serve as embankment fill or cap pending approval |
| Stockpile 2 MSE wall footing (Jun 18, 2020) | 53–930 | 14 of 29 samples exceeded 120 mg/kg, mostly in upper 3 feet | 3.5–22 | Upper 3 feet to containment zones; material below 3 feet accepted by DTSC as embankment fill/cap without a new variance |
| Bent 2 excavation stockpiles (Oct 30, 2020) | 31–230 | 4 of 10 samples ≥120 mg/kg | 2.6–13 | Did not meet clean-cap criteria; DTSC/Water Board accepted use only as partial cap (6 inches or less) beneath pavement section |
| Carpenter Road Shoofly (Apr 12, 2021) | 28–96 | No, all below 120 mg/kg | 1.4–3.8 | Met BCS clean-cap criteria; DTSC accepted April 22, 2021 |
| Carpenter Road borrow area (Feb 27, 2020) | 60–110 | No | 1.7–7.0 | Found consistent with background; suitable for clean cap generation |
| Basin 5 borrow area (Jun 16/Jul 2, 2020) | 66–94 | No | 5.8–11 | Found consistent with background; suitable for clean cap generation |

The pattern is consistent: soil from areas immediately adjacent to or within the historical BCS footprint (Stockpile 1 and 2 wall footings, Bent 2 end dumps) repeatedly exceeded the 120 mg/kg barium background threshold, particularly in the upper 1–3 feet, while soil from more distant borrow sources (Carpenter Road, Basin 5, Carpenter Shoofly) consistently met it. Lead concentrations across all these characterization efforts remained near or within the 3.8 mg/kg background range and were not the controlling factor in any disposition decision; barium was.

## Consequences of exceeding background

Each exceedance had a direct, recorded disposition consequence rather than triggering new investigation:

- The April 7, 2020 DTSC email on the Stockpile 1 wall footing memo required containment-zone placement specifically because the 95% UCL (171.9 mg/kg) exceeded the 120 mg/kg background and because the footing area fell outside the RDIP-designated borrow area.
- The June 22, 2020 DTSC email on the revised Stockpile 2 wall footing memo split the excavation by depth: material above 3 feet bgs to containment zones, material below accepted as embankment fill or cap material under the existing April 9, 2020 variance without requiring a new one.
- The November 6, 2020 DTSC/Water Board email on the Bent 2 soils, which failed the clean-cap criteria, allowed a conditional exception — use as a thin (≤6-inch) partial cap under pavement — tied to compliance with the October 22, 2020 Revised SWPPP Amendment during exposure over the winter months.
- The April 22, 2021 DTSC email accepting the Carpenter Shoofly memo found the composite results consistent with background and cleared that source for use as clean cap without qualification.

## Use in earlier site characterization and the 2012 ramp excavation

Background comparisons were also used earlier in the case to support offsite reuse decisions. The May 29, 2012 Caltrans letter to DTSC on the SR 99/Kansas Avenue ramp safety improvement — which intersected Stockpile 3 — reported "elevated metal concentrations above site-specific background levels, but below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels," supporting the conclusion that the excavated material was suitable for reuse as structural backfill or offsite disposal as non-hazardous soil, subject to DTSC concurrence, with the contractor hauling material to the Stanislaus County Fink Road Landfill.

In the Fenceline and Cadmium Boring investigations described in the Final FS Report and related RAP drafts, background comparison served a different function: distinguishing naturally occurring metal concentrations from stockpile-derived contamination. With the exception of barium and lead, metals in Fenceline Boring samples were generally within site-specific background range; arsenic exceedances were also characterized as within background rather than as contamination. Barium in Fenceline surface samples (140–4,300 mg/kg) decreased with depth, and the majority of 5-foot-deep samples fell within background (47–110 mg/kg), supporting the finding that BCS impacts had not migrated laterally or vertically to the stockpile perimeters or adjacent fencelines at concentrations exceeding residential or industrial CHHSLs.

## Significance

The background thresholds are the mechanism by which large volumes of excavated soil were sorted between two very different fates during SR 132 construction: reuse as engineered cap or fill versus permanent consolidation in the Stockpile 1/2 containment zones. Because the 120 mg/kg barium value is treated as a bright-line criterion in DTSC's approval emails — not merely descriptive background — it directly controlled construction sequencing and disposal costs for the MSE wall footing excavations and the Bent 2 and Carpenter Shoofly borrow sources. The repeated finding that lead stayed near background across all these datasets, while barium did not, reinforces the site's central framing of barium (not lead) as the principal soil contaminant of concern requiring containment.
