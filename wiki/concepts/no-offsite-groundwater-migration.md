The determination of "No Offsite Groundwater Migration" is a key finding for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project. This conclusion signifies that groundwater impacted by the soil stockpiles has not moved beyond the project site boundaries to affect adjacent properties, municipal water sources, or other sensitive receptors. This assessment is based on groundwater monitoring data and analyses conducted by [[geocon-consultants]].

## Site record

The assessment of no offsite groundwater migration is primarily supported by the **June 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Report**, prepared by Geocon Consultants for [[caltrans]]. This report analyzed data from six monitoring wells: four onsite (MW-1, MW-2, MW-3, and MW-4) and two offsite (MW-5 and MW-6).

During the second quarter of 2014 monitoring event, **barium** was the only constituent detected above its Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) in an offsite well. However, the report attributes this exceedance to background conditions in the regional groundwater, rather than to impacts originating from the stockpile site. The report states that barium was detected above the MCL in one offsite well, but its presence is consistent with naturally occurring barium in the area's groundwater and not indicative of site-related impacts migrating offsite.

The two offsite wells, MW-5 and MW-6, are designated as sentinel wells to detect potential downgradient migration. The consultant's conclusion that no site-related contaminants have migrated offsite above regulatory thresholds is based on the interpretation that the barium detection in an offsite well is unrelated to stockpile activities.

Geocon Consultants also concluded that groundwater underlying the site is not used as a drinking water source by surrounding land uses, and that no municipal supply wells or private domestic wells are located within proximity that would be at risk, as noted in the "11 29 2012 Caltrans GND Water Monitoring Rpts" document. This further supports the finding that no actionable offsite migration pathway exists under current conditions.

An updated comparative evaluation of groundwater data was performed by Geocon Consultants, with a report dated February 26, 2024, for Project No. S2350-01-02. This evaluation summarized data collected since 2014 and recommended the decommissioning of the remaining four Caltrans monitoring wells and discontinuation of groundwater monitoring related to the soil stockpiles. This recommendation was based on the conclusion that the encapsulated soil stockpiles do not have the potential to impact groundwater.

In correspondence dated May 3, 2024, the Department of Toxic Substances Control and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board agreed that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and approved the destruction of the four remaining groundwater monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10). A Well Destruction Report, dated June 18, 2025, for Project No. S2350-01-02, documents the destruction of six groundwater monitoring wells (MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, and MW8) in 2019 due to conflicts with highway construction activities.

## Regulatory status

The "No Offsite Groundwater Migration" determination is documented in the June 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Report, which was submitted to the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) via the GeoTracker system. The applicable regulatory threshold for barium is the MCL of 1,000 µg/L, as established by California and federal drinking water standards. The consultant's interpretation that the sole offsite MCL exceedance for barium reflects background conditions, not a site-related release, underpins the ongoing compliance status regarding offsite migration.

In correspondence dated May 3, 2024, the Department of Toxic Substances Control and Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board agreed that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and approved the destruction of the four remaining groundwater monitoring wells.

## Open items

The available source record does not contain documentation of the CVRWQCB's formal concurrence with, or response to, the "no offsite migration" conclusion prior to the May 3, 2024, approval for well destruction. While the consultant asserts that the barium exceedance is attributable to background conditions, the source material does not indicate whether the CVRWQCB required or accepted additional geochemical evidence to support this assertion.
