# No Offsite Groundwater Migration

## Overview
No Offsite Groundwater Migration is a key compliance conclusion documented for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). The concept refers to the finding that impacted groundwater associated with the soil stockpiles has remained contained within the project site boundaries and has not migrated laterally to affect neighboring properties, municipal supply wells, or sensitive receptors offsite. This determination was reached by Geocon Consultants, Inc. based on groundwater monitoring data collected through June 2014 and is supported by concentration comparisons between onsite monitoring wells and the nearest municipal well.

## Site record
The determination of no offsite groundwater migration is grounded in the **June 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Report** (dated June 2014), prepared by Geocon Consultants for Caltrans.

The monitoring well network at the site includes six wells: four onsite wells (MW-1, MW-2, MW-3, and MW-4) and two offsite wells (MW-5 and MW-6). Groundwater samples were collected during a monitoring event in the second quarter of 2014 and analyzed for a suite of constituents including total and dissolved metals, with specific focus on arsenic, barium, and chromium.

The key analytical finding supporting the no-offsite-migration conclusion relates to barium concentrations. The report notes that **barium was the only constituent detected above its respective Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL)** in an offsite well during the June 2014 monitoring. However, the report attributes this offsite exceedance to background conditions in the regional groundwater, not to release from the stockpile site. As stated in the findings: barium was detected above the MCL in one offsite well, but its presence is consistent with naturally occurring barium in the area's groundwater — not indicative of site-related impacts migrating offsite.

The two offsite wells, MW-5 and MW-6, serve as sentinel monitoring points to evaluate potential downgradient migration. The report's conclusion — that no site-related contaminants have been detected offsite above regulatory thresholds that would indicate migration of impacted groundwater beyond the property — relies on the interpretation that the barium exceedance is unrelated to stockpile activities.

Geocon further concluded that **groundwater underlying the site is not used as a drinking water source** by surrounding land uses, and that no municipal supply wells or private domestic wells are located within proximity that would be at risk, reinforcing the finding that no actionable offsite migration pathway exists under current conditions.

## Regulatory status
The determination is documented in the June 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Report submitted to the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)** via the **GeoTracker** system. The applicable regulatory threshold referenced is the **barium MCL** (1,000 µg/L under California and federal drinking water standards). The report's interpretation — that the sole offsite MCL exceedance reflects background conditions rather than site-related release — informs the ongoing compliance posture that no offsite groundwater migration of site-derived contaminants has occurred.

No formal regulatory concurrence or dispute with this conclusion is documented in the available source material; the finding stands as the consultant's professional determination submitted under Caltrans oversight and CVRWQCB purview.

## Open items
- No documentation of regulatory response to (or formal concurrence with) the "no offsite migration" conclusion is present in the source record.
- Barium attribution to background conditions is asserted by the consultant; whether the CVRWQCB required or accepted additional geochemical lines of evidence (e.g., ratio analyses, upgradient comparison data, or trend plots) is not reflected in the available report.
