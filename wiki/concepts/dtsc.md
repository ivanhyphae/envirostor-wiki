The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) is the state regulatory agency exercising oversight of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation under the Health and Safety Code, acting jointly with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] throughout the site's investigation, remedy selection, and post-closure phases. DTSC's role spans four decades of the case file: it directed or reviewed the site investigations and human health risk assessment that characterized the stockpiles as containing FMC-derived contaminants (arsenic, barium, strontium, carcinogenic PAHs, vanadium, lead, nickel); it evaluated and ultimately approved Containment (Alternative 4) as the remedy in the [[remedial-action-plan]]; and it continues to approve or comment on the technical deliverables — sampling plans, tech memos, statistical evaluations, and annual inspection reports — that implement and maintain that remedy. DTSC's determinations are consequential because they establish the legal basis for leaving contaminated soil in place beneath a public highway, subject to institutional controls, land use covenants, and recurring agency review.

## Remedy selection and RAP approval

DTSC reviewed the Draft Final RAP prepared by Caltrans (through consultant [[geocon-consultants-inc]]) and, in consultation with the RWQCB, approved it for public noticing ahead of a joint public comment period held January 18 to March 17, 2017, and a public hearing on February 22, 2017, at Mark Twain Junior High School in Modesto. DTSC and Caltrans held a joint comment period for the Draft EIR/EA and Draft Final RAP; of 305 total comments received, 62 addressed the RAP specifically, and DTSC — in consultation with the RWQCB — drafted the responses to those comments, which Caltrans then incorporated into Appendix J of the Final EIR/EA. DTSC's March 13, 2018 letter formally transmitted its Response to Comments for the RAP. DTSC concurred with Alternative 4 (Containment) as the recommended remedy, rejecting Alternative 3 (Removal of 160,000 cubic yards to an off-site disposal facility) as technically feasible but less cost-effective and more disruptive to air quality in the short term.

Under the approved remedy, stockpile soil remains contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath SR 132 West roadway pavement, with clean fill cover on unpaved portions. This determination is consequential because it forecloses removal as the selected path and instead ties long-term protectiveness to the performance of engineered containment — pavement, walls, and cover — built into a public freeway structure.

## Post-approval oversight obligations

DTSC's approval of Containment carries continuing obligations that distinguish this remedy from a closure action. Caltrans must maintain an Operation and Maintenance Agreement and Operation and Maintenance Plan, both subject to DTSC review and approval, requiring annual inspection of the pavement and other containment features and continued groundwater monitoring. DTSC committed to evaluating the containment remedy every five years to confirm it is performing as designed. These are not one-time approvals but recurring compliance touchpoints: DTSC's later correspondence in the record — approval of the Final O&M Agreement, comments on the O&M Plan, review of annual inspection reports (including a 2025 reminder notice and 2026 inspection approval), approval of the groundwater statistical evaluation, and approval of the well destruction report — reflects this ongoing supervisory role rather than a single closure decision.

## Technical review function

Across the case file, DTSC (jointly with the RWQCB in most instances) reviewed and accepted a series of characterization and remediation work products before they could be relied upon in design or construction, including:

- The Supplemental Site Characterization Workplan and DTSC's response to Caltrans' comments on it (2012)
- The Site-Specific Background Levels approach underlying human health risk conclusions (see [[site-specific-background-levels]])
- Tech memos and sampling plans for [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]] wall footings, MSE wall sampling, and confirmation sampling
- The Carpenter Road Shoofly and Basin 5 clean fill sampling plans and results
- The RDIP (Remedial Design Implementation Plan) and multiple RDIP variance requests
- Stormwater sampling and analysis plans and reports (2023–2025)

Each of these approvals functioned as a regulatory gate: work could not proceed to the next phase — excavation, wall construction, fill placement — without DTSC's (and, where applicable, RWQCB's) sign-off on the underlying plan or its results.

## Position on health risk and background levels

DTSC's public-facing responses to comments state that the stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans (perimeter fencing, restricted access, vegetative cover, surface/groundwater monitoring, prohibition on soil placement or removal), do not pose an unacceptable risk to Caltrans workers, trespassers, or nearby residents. DTSC's responses assert that maximum surface soil concentrations of arsenic, carcinogenic PAHs, and vanadium fall within local background ranges, and that barium and nickel maximum surface concentrations are below U.S. EPA residential Regional Screening Levels; strontium and nitrate were reported below levels DTSC considered safe. DTSC also stated that no cancer-causing chemicals were detected in groundwater, characterizing low arsenic concentrations present as naturally occurring rather than site-derived. This position is the evidentiary basis DTSC offered for concurring with Containment over Removal, and it is the position community commenters (including Bruce Frohman, Wes Olsen, and others in Appendix J) directly disputed, alleging DTSC deferred excessively to Caltrans' preferred outcome.

## Site history acknowledged by DTSC

DTSC's response to comments recites that from the 1930s to 1970s, chemical processing facilities operated by Barium Products LTD, Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation, and FMC processed barite and celestite ores on property beneath and northeast of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange, and that the stockpiles derive from material excavated from one of FMC's former evaporation ponds during 1960s construction of SR 99. DTSC also confirmed that a 2007 DTSC-overseen Interim Removal Action Plan cleaned up the separate former FMC property to a commercial-use standard, and that the FMC property itself is now under RWQCB oversight rather than DTSC's.

## Administrative role and public process

DTSC designated a Project Manager (Randy Adams, later subject to a documented PM Change Notice) as the point of contact for the RAP and site correspondence, and maintained the site record on the EnviroStor database under two global IDs (60001626 and 50280024). DTSC co-hosted the February 22, 2017 public hearing with Caltrans, RWQCB, and StanCOG, distributing an English/Spanish Community Update fact sheet, and later issued the formal Response to Comments incorporated into the Final EIR/EA. DTSC's oversight function was also invoked administratively outside the RAP process: Caltrans staff, in responding to a public inquiry, noted an internal agreement that "any question that comes up about the stockpiles, we will send it to [DTSC]," reflecting DTSC's designated authority over stockpile-specific matters even in a project otherwise led by Caltrans.
