# California Secondary MCL

California Secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (Secondary MCLs), promulgated by the California Department of Public Health (later renumbered under the California Environmental Protection Agency), are the taste-and-odor-based drinking water thresholds used throughout groundwater monitoring at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site alongside [[california-primary-mcl]] values. At this site the Secondary MCL is applied almost exclusively to total dissolved solids (TDS), and its 500 mg/L threshold has been exceeded repeatedly and consistently in specific wells since monitoring began in 2012 — making TDS, rather than any of the dissolved metals of concern (barium, strontium), the recurring secondary-standard exceedance at the site.

## TDS exceedances by well and round

Geocon's groundwater monitoring reports (2012–2016) compared results to Primary and Secondary MCLs promulgated by CDPH. Wells MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 have consistently exceeded the 500 mg/L Secondary MCL for TDS across nearly every monitoring round for which they were sampled:

| Date | Wells exceeding TDS Secondary MCL (500 mg/L) | Notable values (mg/L) |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2012 | MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6 | — |
| May 2012 | MW-4, MW-5, MW-6 | — |
| Sep 2012 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | range 280 (MW-8) to 630 (MW-10) |
| Nov 2012 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | range 340 (MW-7) to 640 (MW-5, MW-10) |
| Jan 2013 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | range 310 (MW-7) to 680 (MW-5) |
| Dec 2013 | MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10 | range 270 (MW-3) to 630 (MW-5); MW-1 (upgradient) at 500 |
| Feb 2014 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | range 340 (MW-3, MW-7) to 750 (MW-5) |
| Jun 2014 | MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | range 320 (MW-3) to 680 (MW-5) |
| Apr 2015 | MW-1, MW-4 | range 490 (MW-2, MW-7) to 660 (MW-1) |
| May 2016 | MW-7 | MW-1: 420; MW-7: 510 |

The reports repeatedly flag MW-1 as an upgradient well, so its TDS and nitrate concentrations (e.g., 460 mg/L in September 2012, 500 mg/L in December 2013, 660 mg/L in April 2015) are treated by Geocon as potentially indicative of natural background TDS for shallow groundwater in the vicinity, rather than as evidence of stockpile-related impact. MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10, by contrast, exceed the threshold in nearly every round in which they were sampled, without being characterized as background.

## Relationship to nitrate and dissolved metals

Nitrate as nitrogen is evaluated against the Primary MCL of 10 mg/L (see [[california-primary-mcl]] and [[nitrate]]), not the Secondary MCL, and its exceedances in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 track the same wells that exceed the TDS Secondary MCL, suggesting the two exceedance patterns are linked in the same wells. Dissolved metals — including [[manganese]], [[strontium]], [[chromium]], and barium — are compared against both Primary and Secondary MCLs in the Title 22 metals tables, but the reports' conclusions sections identify manganese, not TDS-related metals, as the recurring dissolved-metals exceedance (e.g., MW-4 in May 2012, MW-6 in January 2013, MW-8 in November 2012, MW-3 and MW-9 in September 2012, MW-6 in December 2013, MW-10 in February 2014). Barium and strontium are consistently reported "significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds" across rounds, meaning the Secondary MCL is not implicated for these metals at this site.

## Significance for site conclusions

Each groundwater report's conclusions section treats the Secondary MCL exceedances for TDS as a persistent but bounded finding: TDS exceeds 500 mg/L in a recurring subset of wells (principally MW-5, MW-6, MW-10, and intermittently MW-1 and MW-4), while dissolved metals of primary concern (barium, strontium) remain well below their thresholds. Because the upgradient well MW-1 also shows Secondary MCL-level TDS, Geocon's reporting repeatedly frames the TDS pattern as potentially reflecting natural background groundwater chemistry rather than a stockpile-derived impact, though this characterization is offered as an interpretive note in the reports rather than as a determination resolving the exceedances.
