## Variance To Remedial Design Implementation Plan

### Overview

A Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP Variance) was prepared for the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles project, located south of the [[sr-132]]/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. This RDIP Variance addresses the identification and approval of clean cover fill material sources required to cap consolidated [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) within [[stockpile-1]] and stockpile-2 during the [[sr-132]] West Project. The RDIP Variance was prepared by [[geocon-consultants]] in accordance with a request from the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC).

### Site Record

The RDIP Variance, dated April 9, 2020, references the *Remedial Design Implementation Plan - Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles,* dated January 30, 2019. Section 4.7 of the RDIP stipulated that the surface of completed Stockpiles 1 and 2 BCS and ADL consolidation zones would be capped with a minimum 12-inch-thick clean fill material. The initial proposed source for this clean fill was vacant and agricultural land within planned excavation areas along the [[sr-132]] Right-of-Way (ROW) between the Carpenter Road and Rosemore Avenue overcrossings. The highway contractor initially identified an area adjacent and east of Carpenter Road within the SR 132 right-of-way as the clean fill material source for capping Stockpiles 1 and 2.

[[geocon-consultants]], in coordination with [[caltrans]] and DTSC, evaluated this area and determined that soil from the planned highway underpass excavation area adjacent and east of Carpenter Road would be suitable. DTSC subsequently approved this planned Carpenter Road clean fill source area.

An additional source of clean fill material was identified at proposed [[mechanically-stabilized-earth-wall]] (MSE) wall footing excavations, approximately five feet deep, along the southern boundaries of Stockpiles 1 and 2. [[geocon-consultants]] evaluated the MSE wall footing material along the southern boundary of [[stockpile-1]] following excavation of BCS material. In coordination with [[caltrans]] and DTSC, it was determined that the upper foot of planned wall footing excavation soil contained elevated barium concentrations, rendering it unsuitable.

A Technical Memorandum dated June 18, 2020, detailed characterization testing of MSE wall footing excavation soil for stockpile-2. Soil samples were collected from one-foot intervals to a maximum planned excavation depth of 3 and 5 feet. Barium was detected in 29 soil samples at concentrations ranging from 53 to 930 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). Fourteen of these samples contained barium concentrations greater than the maximum site-specific background value of 120 mg/kg. Thirteen of the 14 samples with elevated barium were collected at depths between the surface and 3 feet. Lead was detected in all samples at concentrations ranging from 3.5 to 22 mg/kg.

Based on this data, the upper three feet of soil from the stockpile-2 southerly slope MSE wall footing required placement within designated containment zones. Soil excavated from depths greater than 3 feet was deemed suitable for placement within containment zones, as general embankment fill, or as capping material, pending regulatory agency approval.

An email dated June 22, 2020, from Dean Wright of DTSC to Richard Stewart of [[caltrans]] confirmed DTSC and the Water Board's acceptance of the June 18, 2020, Technical Memorandum. DTSC agreed that soil down to 3 feet below ground surface (bgs) from the Stockpile 2 MSE wall footing excavation must be placed in designated containment zones, but soil below that depth is suitable as embankment fill or capping material. This email served as DTSC's official acceptance, and no additional variance was necessary for utilizing the soil below 3 feet bgs.

A subsequent Technical Memorandum dated April 12, 2021, summarized characterization testing of soil from the Carpenter Road Shoofly. Approximately 5,000 cubic yards of native soil were excavated. Soil samples were analyzed for barium and lead. Barium was detected at concentrations ranging from 28 to 96 mg/kg, all less than the maximum site-specific background value for barium of 120 mg/kg. Lead was detected at concentrations ranging from 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg, less than or equal to the maximum site-specific background concentration for lead of 3.8 mg/kg. Based on this data, the soil material from the Carpenter Road Shoofly met the BCS clean cap criteria as approved by DTSC.

### Regulatory Status

The RDIP Variance was prepared in accordance with requests from the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC). DTSC provided approval for the planned Carpenter Road clean fill source area. DTSC also officially accepted the Technical Memorandum regarding Stockpile 2 MSE wall footing excavation characterization testing via email on June 22, 2020, confirming that soil below 3 feet bgs was suitable for use without an additional variance. The Carpenter Road Shoofly soil was deemed to meet BCS clean cap criteria as approved by DTSC, with barium concentrations below the upper-bound background threshold of 120 mg/kg.
