The Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone is a designated area within the State Route 132 Project in Modesto, California, established for the management of barium-containing soil (BCS) spoils generated from foundation excavations. This zone plays a critical role in ensuring that these specific soil materials are handled and disposed of or reused in accordance with regulatory requirements.

### BCS Generation and Management

BCS material is generated from foundation excavations for project structures. Excavations for the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor, located at approximately Station 1138+60, were completed in July/August 2021, reaching a depth of 4.5 feet and producing approximately 2 cubic yards of BCS material. This material was spread on the adjacent ground surface. A planned 5-foot-diameter by 25-foot-deep foundation excavation for Overhead Sign #1, located at approximately Station 1139+30, is anticipated to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of BCS material. A conservative total estimate for BCS material from both foundations is 40 cubic yards.

Prior to offsite disposal or onsite reuse, the BCS material is to be stockpiled. The management plan includes pre-moistening the material and placing it on, then covering it with, minimum 6-millimeter-thick plastic sheeting. This cover is to be secured with ropes and weights and surrounded by erosion control measures.

### Sampling and Analysis

Eight discrete soil samples will be collected from the stockpile on a grid pattern at various depths. These samples will be transported to California Laboratory Services for analysis. The laboratory will create two 4-part composite samples from the eight discrete samples. These composites will be analyzed for Title 22 metals using EPA Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury), with a 48-hour turnaround time. Soluble metals testing may be required if total metal concentrations are elevated, to determine if the BCS material is a California or Federal RCRA hazardous waste.

### Reuse and Disposal Criteria

The total barium and lead concentrations in the BCS material will be compared to site-specific background levels: barium at 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and lead at 3.8 mg/kg. If the total barium and lead analytical data do not exceed these background concentrations, the stockpiled BCS may be suitable for onsite reuse, subject to approval by Caltrans and DTSC. If onsite reuse is not approved, the BCS material will be profiled for acceptance at Forward Landfill in Manteca or another accepting landfill.

### Regulatory Oversight

The management and disposal/reuse of BCS material are subject to approval by Caltrans and DTSC. Site-specific background levels for barium (120 mg/kg) and lead (3.8 mg/kg) serve as thresholds for determining the suitability of the material for onsite reuse.
