The Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone is the southern portion of Stockpile 2 designated for managing barium-containing soil (BCS) spoils generated during structure foundation excavations along SR 132, addressed in Geocon's February 28, 2022 Technical Memorandum to Rick Demi (WSP/City of Modesto). The zone was established to consolidate spoils from two specific excavations — the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor and Overhead Sign #1 — pending sampling to determine whether the material could be reused onsite or required offsite landfill disposal.

## Sources of BCS spoil

Two foundation excavations generated the material managed within this zone:

- **Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor** (approximate Station 1138+60): a 4.5-foot-deep excavation completed in July/August 2021 that generated approximately 2 cubic yards of BCS material, which was spread on the adjacent ground surface rather than contained at the time.
- **Overhead Sign #1** (approximate Station 1139+30): a planned 5-foot-diameter by 25-foot-deep excavation expected to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of BCS material.

Geocon's memo treats the combined volume from both sources as a conservative estimate of 40 cubic yards to be stockpiled within the containment zone.

## Containment and sampling protocol

The workplan specified that pre-moistened BCS material be placed on and covered with minimum 6-millimeter-thick plastic sheeting, surrounded by erosion control measures (straw wattles, hay bales, or similar) as necessary, with the cover secured by ropes and weights (sand bags, tires, or similar). Eight discrete soil samples were to be collected from the stockpile on a grid pattern at various depths, placed in labeled stainless steel sample tubes, and transported under chain-of-custody protocol to California Laboratory Services. The lab was instructed to composite the eight discrete samples into two four-part composite samples, analyzed for Title 22 metals via EPA Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury) under an expedited 48-hour turnaround. The workplan noted that soluble metals testing (Waste Extraction Test and Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure) might be required for samples with elevated total metal concentrations, to determine whether the material would qualify as a California or federal RCRA hazardous waste.

## Disposition criteria

The workplan set the disposition of the stockpiled BCS on a comparison of total barium and lead concentrations against site-specific background levels:

| Analyte | Site-specific background |
|---|---|
| Barium | 120 mg/kg |
| Lead | 3.8 mg/kg |

If analytical results did not exceed these background concentrations, the material was identified as potentially suitable for onsite reuse, contingent on Caltrans and DTSC approval of a specific reuse location. If Caltrans and/or DTSC did not approve onsite reuse, the material was to be profiled for acceptance at Forward Landfill in Manteca or another accepting landfill. The available material does not report the analytical results of the sampling described, so it is not established from this source whether the stockpiled material was ultimately reused onsite or disposed of offsite.
