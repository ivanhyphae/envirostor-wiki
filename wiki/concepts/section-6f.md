## Section 6f: Exceedances of Health-Based Screening Levels – SR 132 Soil Stockpiles

### Overview
Section 6f of the *SR_132_DEIR_EA_Append_A-F* documents the evaluation of soil stockpile materials generated during Caltrans infrastructure improvements along State Route 132 (SR 132) in Stanislaus County, California. This section specifically addresses exceedances of health-based screening levels (SLs) for constituents of potential concern (COPCs) identified in stockpiles related to the Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case. The purpose is to determine whether stockpiled soils exceed California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) or other risk-based thresholds, thereby triggering remedial action or special handling requirements. The analysis supports the environmental impact assessment for the project, overseen by Caltrans and reviewed by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).

### Site Record
The DEIR/EA Appendix A–F, dated October 2021, presents soil sampling data collected during Phase 1 (2019–2020) and Phase 2 (2020–2021) investigations at two primary stockpile locations: the Modesto Stockpile Site (S-1) and the Keyes Stockpile Site (S-2), both adjacent to SR 132. Samples were analyzed for metals, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), semi-volatile organic compounds (SVOCs), pesticides, and polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs). Exceedances were identified by comparing sample concentrations to DTSC-modified CHHSLs for residential and commercial/industrial land use scenarios, as specified in the project’s *Sampling and Analysis Plan* (SAP, 2019).

Key exceedances documented in Section 6f include:

- **S-1 (Modesto Stockpile)**:
  - **Arsenic**: Maximum concentration of 12.5 mg/kg in composite sample S1-COMP-01 (2019), exceeding the residential CHHSL of 0.39 mg/kg and commercial CHHSL of 1.6 mg/kg. However, the background level for arsenic in Stanislaus County is 8.3 mg/kg (from *DTSC Background Study, 2015*); thus, the exceedance was attributed to natural background and not designated a project-specific risk.
  - **Lead**: Concentration of 180 mg/kg in sample S1-WP-03 (2020), exceeding the residential CHHSL of 80 mg/kg but below the commercial CHHSL of 320 mg/kg. Because the stockpile is proposed for reuse on-site in a transportation corridor (non-residential), the commercial threshold applies. No further action was required for lead.
  - **Barium**: Concentration of 760 mg/kg in sample S1-SS-02 (2019), exceeding the residential CHHSL of 700 mg/kg but within commercial CHHSL of 5,000 mg/kg. No exceedance of commercial standard.

- **S-2 (Keyes Stockpile)**:
  - **Chromium VI**: Concentration of 1.1 mg/kg in sample S2-B1-02 (2020), exceeding both residential (0.2 mg/kg) and commercial (0.6 mg/kg) CHHSLs. Confirmation testing via EPA Method 3060A/7196A showed hexavalent chromium was not detected in duplicate re-samples (ND at 0.24 mg/kg), indicating a likely analytical artifact. No action taken.
  - **Total Chromium**: Max 340 mg/kg (S2-SS-04, 2019), exceeding residential CHHSL of 100 mg/kg but not notably above background (170 mg/kg for Stanislaus County). Hexavalent chromium, the more toxic form, was not confirmed.
  - **Mercury**: Concentration of 1.8 mg/kg in sample S2-SS-03 (2019), slightly exceeding the commercial CHHSL of 1.5 mg/kg. Because the exceedance was marginal and the stockpile location is zoned for industrial use, DTSC approved a site-specific risk assessment (SSRA) allowing the material to remain with institutional controls (soil cap). DTSC concurrence letter, *DTSC Concurrence for SSRA*, dated March 2021.

Spatially, exceedances were distributed at isolated sample points within each stockpile, not pervasive across the entire stockpile volume. Volumes documented: Modesto Stockpile = ~15,000 cubic yards (cy), Keyes Stockpile = ~8,000 cy. Verification sampling after removal of hotspots (e.g., arsenic-enriched zone at S-1) in August 2021 reduced concentrations to below commercial CHHSLs in three of four verification samples (V-01, V-02, V-04). One sample (V-03) had barium at 820 mg/kg, still below commercial threshold.

### Regulatory Status
Applicable standards for this project derive from:
- **CHHSLs** (California EPA, 2020 revision) – used for residential and commercial/industrial screening.
- **DTSC-modified screening levels** (Memorandum *DTSC Screening Level Guidance for Caltrans Projects*, 2018) – incorporates background adjustments for arsenic, chromium, and lead in Stanislaus County (DTSC Background Study 2015 values: As 8.3 mg/kg, Cr 170 mg/kg, Pb 55 mg/kg).
- **Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs)** not directly applied because stockpiles are soil, not groundwater; groundwater monitoring (MW-5, MW-7) showed no impact from stockpiles (barium MCL 1,000 µg/L was not exceeded in downgradient wells in 2020–2021 sampling rounds; see Section 5c).

As of the DEIR/EA (October 2021), the project was in compliance with regulatory requirements for soil stockpiles under the *Caltrans Standard Environmental Reference (SER)* and *DTSC Order No. 2020-03*. The DTSC concurrence for keyes stockpile mercury allows use with institutional controls (erosion control, deed notice). The Modesto stockpile is approved for on-site reuse in fill for SR 132 widening, per *Caltrans Design Decision Memo (DDM), January 2022*.

### Open Items
Pending actions documented:
1. **Post-construction confirmation**: Requires soil sampling at four verification points (VP-1 through VP-4) after stockpile placement to confirm no migration of contaminants off-site (CT-STOC-04, due 2023 Q4). Not yet filed as of the case file compilation (2023).
2. **DDT/Pesticide residues**: Initial screening (Section 6d) detected p,p’-DDE at 0.044 mg/kg in one sample, below CHHSLs, but DTSC requested additional testing of five discrete samples from S-1 to rule out persistent organic pollutants (POPs). Results pending in *POP Addendum Report* (due mid-2024).
3. **Worker health and safety plan (WHASP)**: Update required if stockpile reuse involves excavation within 5 feet of groundwater (estimated depth 30 ft bgs, not triggered); but if conditions change, WHASP must be amended.
