Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH) is the constituent that has driven active soil segregation decisions in the SR 132 basin excavations, distinct from the historical PAH and metals contamination associated with the original Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. Where the earlier stockpile characterization and fill-suitability guidance treat TPH as one screening parameter among several, the 2021 Soil Management Plan (SMP) for the SR 132 retention basins made TPH-diesel (TPH-d) the specific trigger for excavation, segregation, and labeling of impacted soil, and left open what happens if impacted soil remains at the maximum design depth of the basins.

## Screening level and its basis

The SMP submitted February 8, 2021 set the TPH-d screening level at 500 mg/kg, described by the submitting consultant (Rincon Consultants) as reflecting the DTSC screening level for commercial/industrial soil. This was a revision from an earlier value, made specifically to satisfy DTSC's prior comments. The consultant noted that the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) had not responded to requests to discuss the appropriate screening level for protection of groundwater, and stated that the outcome of that unresolved discussion would apply to soils left onsite or reused onsite at finished grade — meaning the 500 mg/kg figure was accepted for excavation purposes but was not final for onsite reuse decisions.

## Targeted excavations tied to TPH

The SMP identified two concrete, TPH-specific excavation actions the City of Modesto proposed to proceed with ahead of, or independent of, final resolution of the groundwater-protective screening level:

- Targeted excavation of TPH-d impacted soils at borings RB-10 and RB-11 within Basin 6A, with confirmation soil sampling
- Excavation of approximately 500 cubic yards south of 611 Franklin within Basin 6B, an area with no known TPH-d impacted soils

The distinction between these two locations in the same submittal shows TPH-d results, not general contamination concerns, as the specific basis for selecting where targeted (as opposed to general) excavation was required.

## Regulatory approval and its two open conditions

DTSC and the Water Board issued official acceptance of the revised SMP on February 11, 2021 (email from Dean Wright, DTSC Project Manager, to Caltrans). The acceptance was not unconditional: it identified two comments specific to TPH handling that were to be addressed in the field rather than through further plan revision:

1. The SMP stated that soil with TPH above screening levels would be segregated and covered; DTSC/WB additionally requested that this material be labeled until it is transported offsite.
2. The SMP did not address what would happen if TPH-impacted soil was still present when the basins reached maximum design depth. The acceptance email specified that, should this occur, Caltrans was to be alerted and was to consult with the Water Board and DTSC to determine next steps.

The second condition is an unresolved contingency rather than a closed item: the approval email does not state that this scenario occurred or how it was resolved, only what process was to be followed if it did. Any occurrence of TPH-impacted soil persisting at final basin depth in Basins 6A or 6B would be the point at which this condition becomes operative.

## Health hazard information in the public record

Comment-response material circulated for the SR 132 project (Appendix J responses and hearing summary materials) includes Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry language characterizing TPH health effects — central nervous system effects, headaches and dizziness from one compound, peripheral neuropathy from another, and effects on blood, immune system, lungs, skin, and eyes from other TPH compounds — presented in the context of public comments (e.g., from commenter Maureen Dick) concerning the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles and their handling during the Kansas Avenue off-ramp project closure. This material documents public concern and the administrative record's response to it; it does not report site-specific TPH concentrations or exposure findings.
