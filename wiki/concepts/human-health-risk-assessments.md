Human Health Risk Assessments (HHRAs) are a critical component of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project, evaluating potential risks to human health from identified contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in the soil stockpiles and groundwater. These assessments inform the development of remedial action objectives and cleanup goals to protect human health and the environment.

### HHRA and HHRA Update Findings

An initial HHRA was performed in 2007 by Shaw Environmental, Inc., assessing multiple exposure scenarios for COPCs in the stockpiles and groundwater. Metals, particularly [[barium]], and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were identified as primary COPCs in the soil stockpiles. Metals and general minerals such as nitrate and total dissolved solids were identified as primary COPCs in groundwater. Cadmium and strontium were not considered COPCs in this assessment. The 2007 HHRA concluded that the soil stockpiles did not pose an unacceptable risk or hazard to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

In 2013, Geocon conducted an HHRA Update in response to DTSC requests, incorporating soil analytical data from 2012 Supplemental Site Investigations and groundwater data collected between March 2012 and March 2013. This update compared the 2012 data to the exposure point concentrations (EPCs) used in the 2007 HHRA, concluding that the data was similar and did not significantly increase cancer risk or non-cancer hazard indices. The 2007 HHRA was deemed valid for current resident/trespasser, future construction worker and offsite resident, and hypothetical shallow groundwater user scenarios.

Specific findings from the HHRA and its update include:

*   **Stockpile 1 Current Exposure Assessment:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a cancer risk of 8E-8 and a non-cancer hazard index (HI) of 4E-2 for offsite residents/trespassers. Lead in Stockpile 1 surface soil did not indicate a blood lead concentration greater than 10 µg/dL. The 2012 data confirmed these findings remained valid.
*   **Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a cancer risk of 1E-7 (excluding background arsenic) and a non-cancer HI of 0.1 for offsite residents/trespassers. Lead in Stockpile 2 surface soil did not indicate a blood lead concentration greater than 10 µg/dL. The 2012 data confirmed these findings remained valid.
*   **Stockpile 3 Current Exposure Assessment:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a non-cancer HI of 0.02 for offsite residents/trespassers. Lead in Stockpile 3 surface soil did not indicate a blood lead concentration greater than 10 µg/dL. The 2012 data confirmed these findings remained valid.
*   **Stockpiles 1 through 3 - Future Construction Worker and Offsite Resident:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a cancer risk of 9.2E-7 and a non-cancer HI of 0.4 for construction workers, and a cancer risk of 6E-10 and a non-cancer HI of 0.017 for future offsite residents. The 2012 data confirmed these calculations remained valid.
*   **Onsite Shallow Groundwater:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a non-cancer HI of 0.9 for hypothetical shallow groundwater users. The 2012 data confirmed this calculation remained valid.

### Regulatory Concurrence and Conditions

The DTSC, in letters dated February 15, 2013, and April 4, 2013, concurred with the HHRA Update. The DTSC stated that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or noncancer hazard as long as they remain in place and are properly managed. Proper management includes maintaining fencing and signage, limiting access to authorized personnel, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater and stormwater runoff monitoring.

### Open Items

A DTSC memorandum dated February 14, 2013, noted that areas within the stockpiles with elevated chemical concentrations at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would require evaluation if substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles were to occur.
