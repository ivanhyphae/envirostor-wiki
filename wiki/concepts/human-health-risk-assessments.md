## Overview

Human Health Risk Assessments (HHRAs) are a key component of the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. These assessments evaluate potential risks to human health associated with contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) identified in the soil stockpiles and groundwater. The HHRA process informs the development of remedial action objectives and cleanup goals to protect human health and the environment.

## Site Record

An HHRA was initially performed in 2007 by Shaw Environmental, Inc. This assessment evaluated multiple exposure scenarios for COPCs in the stockpiles and groundwater. Metals, notably [[barium]], and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were identified as primary COPCs in the soil stockpiles. Metals and general minerals (e.g., nitrate, total dissolved solids) were identified as primary COPCs in groundwater. Cadmium and strontium were not considered COPCs in this HHRA. The 2007 HHRA concluded that the soil stockpiles did not pose an unacceptable risk or hazard to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

In response to requests from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), an HHRA Update was performed in 2013 by Geocon. This update incorporated soil analytical data from Supplemental Site Investigations (SSI) conducted in 2012 and groundwater data collected between March 2012 and March 2013. The HHRA Update compared the 2012 data to the exposure point concentrations (EPCs) used in the 2007 HHRA. The update concluded that the 2012 data was similar to the 2006 data used in the 2007 HHRA and did not significantly increase the conservative cancer risk and non-cancer hazard indices. The 2007 HHRA was deemed valid for current resident/trespasser, future construction worker and offsite resident, and hypothetical shallow groundwater user scenarios.

Specific findings from the HHRA and its update include:

*   **Stockpile 1 Current Exposure Assessment:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a cancer risk of 8E-8 and a non-cancer hazard index (HI) of 4E-2 for offsite residents/trespassers. Lead in Stockpile 1 surface soil did not indicate a blood lead concentration greater than 10 micrograms per deciliter (µg/dL). The 2012 data confirmed these findings remained valid.
*   **Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a cancer risk of 1E-7 (excluding background arsenic) and a non-cancer HI of 0.1 for offsite residents/trespassers. Lead in Stockpile 2 surface soil did not indicate a blood lead concentration greater than 10 µg/dL. The 2012 data confirmed these findings remained valid.
*   **Stockpile 3 Current Exposure Assessment:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a non-cancer HI of 0.02 for offsite residents/trespassers. Lead in Stockpile 3 surface soil did not indicate a blood lead concentration greater than 10 µg/dL. The 2012 data confirmed these findings remained valid.
*   **Stockpiles 1 through 3 - Future Construction Worker and Offsite Resident:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a cancer risk of 9.2E-7 and a non-cancer HI of 0.4 for construction workers, and a cancer risk of 6E-10 and a non-cancer HI of 0.017 for future offsite residents. The 2012 data confirmed these calculations remained valid.
*   **Onsite Shallow Groundwater:** The 2007 HHRA calculated a non-cancer HI of 0.9 for hypothetical shallow groundwater users. The 2012 data confirmed this calculation remained valid.

The DTSC, in a letter dated February 15, 2013, concurred with the HHRA Update, stating that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or noncancer hazard as long as they remain in place and are properly managed. Proper management includes maintaining fencing and signage, limiting access to authorized personnel, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater and stormwater runoff monitoring. The DTSC further concurred with the reports in a letter dated April 4, 2013.

## Regulatory Status

The HHRA process informs the determination of acceptable risk levels and cleanup goals. The DTSC has concurred with the findings of the HHRA and its update, establishing that the stockpiles do not pose an unacceptable risk under current management practices. The Remedial Action Objective (RAO) for the site is to protect human health and prevent future impact to groundwater. Cleanup goals are performance-based, ensuring no route of exposure to COPCs in the stockpiles and reducing potential threats to groundwater.

## Open Items

The DTSC's memorandum dated February 14, 2013, noted that areas in the stockpiles with elevated concentrations of chemicals at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would need to be evaluated if substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles were to occur.
