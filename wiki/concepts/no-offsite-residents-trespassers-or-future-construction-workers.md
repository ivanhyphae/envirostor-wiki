## Overview

This concept addresses the project's objective to prevent exposure to contaminants by offsite residents, trespassers, and future construction workers involved with the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. The primary goal is to ensure that the remediation and management of the stockpiled soil do not pose a risk to these populations.

## Site Record

The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] outlines strategies to manage approximately {{quote: "160,000 cubic yards of soil"}} generated from the remediation of the [[fmc-chemical-processing-facility]]. This soil is located at the SR 132 and Highway 99 interchange in Stanislaus County, California. The plan details measures to deter trespassers and to inform future construction workers about the nature of the soil and any necessary precautions. The [[containment-strategy]] is designed to isolate the soil and its constituents, such as [[barium]], from the surrounding environment and potential receptors.

## Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) is overseeing the remediation of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. The RAP was developed to meet DTSC requirements for protecting public health and the environment, detailing how the soil will be managed to prevent offsite migration and exposure.

## Open Items

The long-term management and ultimate disposition of the soil stockpiles are critical to ensuring no future exposure risks. While the RAP details the proposed [[containment-strategy]], the final implementation and verification of these measures are key to confirming their long-term effectiveness in preventing exposure to offsite residents, trespassers, and future construction workers.
