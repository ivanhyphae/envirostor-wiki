Phase 1 Consolidation refers to the initial phase of remediation activities for the [[soil-stockpiles]] associated with the [[sr-132-west-expressway-project]] in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. This phase focused on the containment of barium-containing soils (BCS) and was implemented under the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP). [[caltrans]] is the responsible party for the implementation of the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and RDIP, with oversight from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

### Site Description and Remediation Activities

Prior to remediation, the site contained BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3 within the Caltrans State Route 132 right-of-way. Stockpile 1 was approximately 600 feet long, 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 34,000 cubic yards (yd³). Stockpile 2, located between Emerald Avenue and State Route 99, was approximately 1,650 feet long, 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 102,000 yd³. Stockpile 3, formerly east of State Route 99, was approximately 1,100 feet long, 120 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 24,000 yd³.

In accordance with the approved RAP and the Interim Phase 1 RDIP, Phase 1 remediation involved excavating Stockpile 3, the western and eastern ends of Stockpile 2, and portions of the southern slopes of Stockpile 1 and 2 to facilitate construction of the new State Route 132 Expressway. Approximately 27,675 yd³ of soil from BCS Stockpile 3 was excavated and transported to the BCS Stockpile 1 containment zone as embankment fill. Twenty-nine confirmation soil samples were collected from the excavation area of BCS Stockpile 3 and analyzed for barium and lead. Three samples exceeded the barium cleanup goal of 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). These areas were excavated an additional foot and resampled, with final confirmation samples indicating compliance with cleanup goals.

### Regulatory Approvals and Deliverables

The Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was approved by the DTSC. The Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1, dated January 30, 2019, was prepared for Caltrans by [[geocon-consultants]], Inc. This plan detailed the containment alternative for the stockpiles. The DTSC issued a Conditional Approval Letter for the Draft RDIP on August 20, 2018, pending administrative revisions and review of design plans. A subsequent Conditional Approval Letter was issued on October 12, 2018, after revisions were incorporated. The RDIP was formally approved by the DTSC on April 3, 2019.

An Errata to the January 2019 RDIP was issued post-construction contract award to update roles and responsibilities. This errata clarified that while Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared the RDIP under an on-call contract with Caltrans for hazardous waste services, [[caltrans]] remains the responsible party for ensuring the work is performed in accordance with the RDIP, regardless of the consultant performing the work. A complete set of 100% design plans and final project specifications were to be submitted to the DTSC and CVRWQCB in early 2019.

### Project Team Responsibilities

[[caltrans]] is responsible for the implementation of the RAP and RDIP under regulatory oversight and approval by DTSC, in cooperation with the CVRWQCB. Caltrans is responsible for verifying that highway construction work associated with the soil stockpiles conforms to the RAP and RDIP requirements. This includes supervising the abandonment of impacted groundwater monitoring wells, observing and documenting excavation, fill placement, and capping activities, performing verification soil sampling and analysis for barium-containing soil removal, conducting sampling and analysis of proposed clean soil imports, performing worker zone and perimeter air monitoring, and conducting periodic stormwater monitoring. Caltrans is also responsible for reviewing RDIP requirements with the construction manager and contractor, and coordinating with the Caltrans Environmental Manager and DTSC and CVRWQCB Project Managers.

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), reviewed the Draft Removal Action Completion Report – Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Phase 1 Interim State Route 132 West Project. This report, submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc. on behalf of Caltrans, describes the consolidation and capping activities completed for the barium-containing soils as part of the Interim Phase 1 Construction effort. The DTSC accepted this Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report on February 24, 2022.
