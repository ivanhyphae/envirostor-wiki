Manganese is a dissolved metal that recurs intermittently in Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles groundwater and stormwater monitoring, and it is the constituent most frequently flagged as an isolated exceedance of the California secondary MCL of 50 µg/l across the site's monitoring history. Unlike barium, lead, and strontium — the primary chemicals of concern identified by DTSC — manganese was never designated a COC, but its recurring, well-hopping exceedances mean nearly every quarterly groundwater report through 2016 records at least one manganese-related caveat, and it reappears as a clear, multi-well exceedance in 2018 stormwater sampling tied to the stockpile soils themselves.

## Groundwater results, 2012–2016

Manganese was not detected in every well in every round; when detected, it was usually below the secondary MCL, but nearly every quarterly report identifies one well exceeding it. Reports consistently characterize this as an isolated, single-well occurrence rather than a site-wide condition, and use the language "with the exception of manganese" when otherwise concluding that dissolved metals were below thresholds.

The March 2012 event is the best-documented instance: Geocon's site sample from MW-4 reported manganese at 88 µg/l, above the 50 µg/l secondary MCL, while a Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board split sample from the same well, analyzed by Excelchem, reported only 0.7 µg/l. The March 2012 report treats the split-sample discrepancy as notable but does not resolve it, and the mercury results from the same event were separately flagged as likely biased by equipment-blank contamination — manganese was not implicated in that particular QA/QC finding. Manganese was added to the CVRWQCB's analyte list for that sampling round only after a specific email request from RWQCB's Roberto Cervantes on March 16, 2012.

Across the ten-well network (MW-1 through MW-10), the exceeding well changes almost every round — MW-4 in March 2012, MW-3 and MW-9 in September 2012, MW-8 in November 2012, MW-6 in January and December 2013, MW-10 in February 2014 — with no single well showing a sustained or worsening trend. Every quarterly conclusions section through 2014 repeats the same structure: manganese in one specific well is the sole exception to an otherwise-clean dissolved metals dataset. By April 2015 and May 2016, manganese detections had become less frequent (one of four wells in April 2015, one of two sampled in May 2016) and did not exceed the secondary MCL in either round. The June 2014 groundwater monitoring report recorded no manganese detections above PQLs in any of the ten wells sampled.

The 2007 human health risk assessment, revisited in the 2014 Final Feasibility Study and Draft Final Remedial Action Plan, had already identified manganese as a chemical of potential concern for the hypothetical shallow groundwater user based on 2006 sampling (maximum detected concentrations of 290 µg/l at MW-5 and 260 µg/l at MW-6). The 2012 groundwater dataset showed manganese concentrations similar to or slightly different from those 2007 exposure point concentrations, and the FS/RAP concluded that the 2007 non-cancer hazard index of 0.9 for the hypothetical groundwater user — below the threshold of 1 — remained valid notwithstanding the updated data.

## Stormwater results, 2017–2018

Manganese reappears as a stormwater constituent once sampling of runoff from the stockpiles began. In March 2017, manganese was reported in samples PL2 and PL4, and the report states these exceeded the secondary MCL — the only metal among those detected across samples PL1 through PL5 to do so. The laboratory noted PL2, PL4, and other samples required dilution for manganese analysis due to high concentrations of a non-target analyte.

In January 2018, manganese was reported in sample BG2 at a concentration exceeding its MCL — again the sole exceedance among the metals detected in that round.

The March 2018 event shows the most extensive manganese exceedance recorded at the site: manganese was detected in samples PL2, PL3, PL4, and BG1 at concentrations ranging from 54 to 370 µg/l, all exceeding the 50 µg/l secondary MCL. This is the only stormwater round in the record where manganese exceeded its threshold in multiple samples simultaneously, including a background sample (BG1), and at a magnitude (370 µg/l, more than seven times the secondary MCL) well above anything reported in the groundwater monitoring history. The same March 2018 sampling round also recorded a primary MCL exceedance for lead in sample PL3 (46 µg/l against a 15 µg/l MCL), making it the most significant single sampling event for metals exceedances in the stormwater record.

## Significance

Manganese's secondary MCL is a taste-and-odor/welfare-based standard, and every groundwater report that recorded an exceedance characterized it as an isolated, non-recurring, well-specific event rather than a basis for revising the site's conceptual model or the designation of barium, lead, and strontium as the primary chemicals of concern. No report in the record describes a regulatory response, monitoring change, or remedial action specifically triggered by a manganese exceedance in groundwater. The March 2018 stormwater exceedances — multi-sample, elevated, and including a background location — represent a materially different pattern than the single-well groundwater exceedances, but the available material does not record what, if any, follow-up action was taken specifically in response to the manganese results in that round.
