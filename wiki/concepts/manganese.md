Manganese is a dissolved metal that has been detected in groundwater and stormwater samples collected at the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site. Its presence and concentration are evaluated against state and federal drinking water standards.

### Site Detections and Exceedances

Manganese has been detected in groundwater samples from various monitoring wells. In March 2012, a concentration of 2.9 µg/l was reported in MW-4, exceeding the secondary MCL for taste and odor. In November 2012, the highest concentration detected was 2.1 µg/l in MW-8. September 2012 sampling reported manganese in MW-3, MW-9, and MW-10, with MW-3 showing the highest concentration at 3.9 µg/l. In May 2012, manganese was reported at 0.50 µg/l in MW-1. In December 2013, a concentration of 1.3 µg/l was reported in MW-6. In February 2014, one sample contained 0.78 µg/l, which did not exceed its numeric water quality threshold.

Stormwater sampling has also identified manganese. In March 2017, manganese was reported in samples PL2 and PL4, exceeding primary or secondary MCLs. In January 2018, manganese was detected in some or all samples, with sample BG2 exceeding its primary or secondary MCL. Further sampling in March 2018 found manganese in samples PL2 through PL4 and BG1 at concentrations ranging from 54 to 370 µg/l, all exceeding the secondary MCL for drinking water of 50 µg/l.

### Regulatory Thresholds

The secondary MCL for manganese in drinking water is 50 µg/l, primarily related to taste and odor or welfare-based concerns. Exceedances of this threshold have been documented at the site in groundwater monitoring wells and stormwater sampling locations.
