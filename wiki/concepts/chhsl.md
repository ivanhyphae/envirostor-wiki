California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) are the Cal/EPA soil screening benchmarks used throughout the Modesto Soil Stockpiles record to determine which detected metals warranted treatment as contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) and to justify closing out others. Nearly every investigation phase — the 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI), the 2006 Site Investigation, the 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI), and the resulting Human Health Risk Assessments (HHRAs) — measured stockpile soil concentrations against residential and commercial/industrial CHHSLs, and the outcome of those comparisons shaped which chemicals were carried forward into risk calculations, remedial alternatives, and the eventual Remedial Action Plan (RAP).

## Role in identifying COPCs

The 1963-1967 fill in Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3 originated from excavation near a former FMC Corporation evaporation pond that processed barium and strontium sulfate ores. The 2004 Shaw PSI (51 borings; 194 stockpile soil and 49 native soil samples, plus eight background samples) compared metals, PAHs, nitrate, and pH results to CHHSLs and identified barium as the only metal at concentrations of concern, with maximum detections of 1,730 mg/kg in Stockpile 1, 60,700 mg/kg in Stockpile 2, and 44,900 mg/kg in Stockpile 3 — all measured against residential and commercial/industrial CHHSLs of 5,200 and 63,000 mg/kg, respectively. The same PSI found cadmium exceeding the industrial CHHSL of 7.5 mg/kg in samples from 8 of 25 borings in Stockpile 2 and 2 of 10 borings in Stockpile 3, with corresponding elevated barium (25,800 to 196,000 mg/kg).

That cadmium exceedance did not hold up. The 2006 SI and the 2012 SSI Cadmium Borings — targeting the same locations, including 19 samples with barium between 25,000 and 130,000 mg/kg — found no cadmium above the 1.0 mg/kg reporting limit across 348 soil samples analyzed by two different laboratories (Creek Environmental Laboratories in 2006, Advanced Technology Laboratories in 2012), versus the 2004 PSI data generated by Sparger Technology, Inc. The RAP and FS documents conclude the 2004 cadmium detections were "neither reproducible nor reliable," likely false positives from sample interference caused by the associated high barium concentrations, and cadmium was dropped as a COPC.

## CHHSL comparisons in later investigations

The September 2012 Fenceline and Perimeter Borings, drilled to check for lateral or vertical migration of contaminated soil beyond the stockpile footprints, again used CHHSLs as the decision threshold. None of the reported barium concentrations at any fenceline or perimeter location exceeded the residential (5,200 mg/kg) or industrial (63,000 mg/kg) CHHSLs, despite fenceline surface samples ranging up to 4,300 mg/kg near commercial/industrial development on the north side of Stockpile 2. With the exception of arsenic (attributed to background) and barium/lead, other metals fell within site-specific background. This absence of exceedances at the perimeters supported the conclusion that barium-impacted soil had not migrated off the historic placement footprint shown in 1963 and 1967 aerial photographs.

CHHSLs were also the benchmark used stockpile-by-stockpile in the 2007 HHRA and its 2013 update to confirm that current surface-soil conditions still supported the HHRA's risk conclusions:

| Stockpile | COPC metals compared | 2012 MDC vs. 2007 HHRA EPC (mg/kg) | Residential CHHSL exceeded? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | barium, copper, lead (of 8 metals) | barium 240 vs. 130; copper 24 vs. 13; lead 17 vs. 12 | No |
| 2 | barium, copper, zinc (of 7 metals + Cr) | barium 4,300 vs. 1,100; copper 41 vs. 29; zinc 200 vs. 89 | No |
| 3 | barium, lead (of 3 metals) | barium 1,600 vs. 250; lead 34 vs. 12 | No |

In each stockpile the 2012 maximum detected concentrations were higher than the 2007 EPCs but remained the same order of magnitude and below residential CHHSLs and RSLs, which is the basis stated in the RAP, FS, and HHRA Update for treating the original 2007 HHRA cancer risk and hazard index results as still valid rather than requiring recalculation. Cadmium detections in 2012 surface soil (0.26–0.78 mg/kg across the three stockpiles) also remained below the residential CHHSL of 1.7 mg/kg, reinforcing its exclusion as a COPC.

## Use in screening chemicals with no CHHSL

Where no CHHSL existed, the record substituted other benchmarks and said so explicitly. Strontium was excluded as a COPC because there is no CHHSL for strontium and its maximum detected concentration (231 mg/kg in the 2004 PSI, according to the RAP/FS; 765 mg/kg per the HHRA Update) was orders of magnitude below the USEPA residential RSL of 47,000 mg/kg. Petroleum hydrocarbon and PAH results from a Stockpile 2 Cadmium Boring were likewise compared to San Francisco Bay Area RWQCB Environmental Screening Levels rather than CHHSLs, "because there are no CHHSLs or other regulatory screening levels for petroleum."

The DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office's 2013 review (Table 2, "Chemicals found in Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles") again used CHHSLs alongside USEPA RSLs as the two risk-based screening benchmarks, concluding that maximum concentrations of barium, nickel, and strontium were below their respective risk-based screening levels, and that arsenic, carcinogenic PAHs, nitrates, and vanadium — all FMC-associated chemicals — fell within background ranges. The one flagged exception was a calculated (not measured) hexavalent chromium value that exceeded its USEPA RSL but remained below its CHHSL; HERO considered chromium-VI unlikely to actually be present given no history of its use at the FMC facility.

## Significance

CHHSL comparisons are the recurring evidentiary basis in this record for two connected conclusions: that barium, the dominant stockpile contaminant by concentration, does not exceed residential or industrial screening levels at the fencelines or perimeters and therefore has not migrated off Caltrans right-of-way, and that cadmium — the only chemical that did exceed a CHHSL in the original 2004 PSI — was later shown across two independent laboratories and 348 samples not to be reproducible, removing it as a COPC. Both conclusions underpin the 2009 DTSC finding that the stockpiles, as fenced and managed, pose no risk to workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents, and both were carried forward into the FS and RAP risk narratives without requiring recalculation of the 2007 HHRA's cancer risk and hazard index results.
