The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are a remediation site located south of the State Route (SR) 99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. These stockpiles, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards, were created in the early 1960s by importing soil excavated from evaporation ponds at an FMC facility that had processed barium and strontium minerals. The soil was placed within the Caltrans right-of-way for the planned SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. The primary contaminants of concern (COPCs) identified in the soil are barium, lead, and strontium. Groundwater beneath the stockpiles has also been monitored for barium, strontium, and nitrates. The selected remedial action is containment, involving the incorporation of the stockpile soils into the SR-132 construction as embankment fill, retaining wall backfill, and bridge abutments.

## Site Description and Contaminants

The stockpiles are located south of Kansas Avenue and west of Emerald Avenue, with three distinct areas identified:
*   **Stockpile #1:** South of Kansas Avenue and west of Emerald Avenue. Approximately 600 feet long, 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 34,000 yd³.
*   **Stockpile #2:** South of Kansas Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR-99. Approximately 1,650 feet long, 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 102,000 yd³.
*   **Stockpile #3:** South of Kansas Avenue and east of SR-99. Approximately 1,100 feet long, 120 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 24,000 yd³.

### Soil Contaminants of Potential Concern (COPCs)

*   **Barium:** Detected in stockpile soil samples at concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs). In surface soil samples, concentrations ranged from 140 to 4,300 mg/kg.
*   **Lead:** Total lead concentrations in two stockpile soil samples exceeded 50 mg/kg, with values of 150 and 1,500 mg/kg. Waste Extraction Test (WET) soluble lead was detected at 2.9 and 5.7 mg/l in these samples.
*   **Strontium:** Maximum strontium concentration in soil was 765 mg/kg.
*   **Cadmium:** Detected in soil samples at concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial CHHSLs in Stockpile #2 (8 of 25 borings) and Stockpile #3 (2 of 10 borings) during the 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI). However, cadmium was not identified as a COPC in subsequent assessments.

### Groundwater Constituents of Concern

Groundwater monitoring has identified several constituents of concern:

*   **Barium:** Concentrations in groundwater ranged from 39 to 2,300 µg/l in January 2013 and November 2012. Nine samples exceeded the Title 22 Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) of 100 mg/l.
*   **Strontium:** Concentrations in groundwater ranged from 200 to 1,500 µg/l in January 2013 and November 2012.
*   **Nitrate:** Concentrations in groundwater ranged from 2.9 to 30 mg/l in January 2013 and 2.8 to 24 mg/l in November 2012. Several wells exceeded the primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 10 mg/l.
*   **Total Dissolved Solids (TDS):** Concentrations in groundwater ranged from 310 to 680 mg/l in January 2013 and 340 to 640 mg/l in November 2012. Several wells exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l.
*   **Lead:** Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the practical quantitation limit (PQL) of 1.0 µg/l in samples collected in January 2013 and November 2012.
*   **Manganese:** Detected in MW-6 in January 2013 at a concentration exceeding its numeric water quality threshold.

## Investigations and Remedial Actions

Multiple investigations have been conducted at the site:
*   **2003 Initial Site Assessment (ISA) by Shaw:** Identified potential COPCs.
*   **2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) by Shaw:** Characterized stockpiles and detected elevated barium and cadmium.
*   **2006 Site Investigation (SI) by Shaw:** Further characterized stockpiles and installed groundwater monitoring wells.
*   **2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) by Shaw:** Identified barium and PAHs as primary COPCs in soil and concluded no unacceptable risk.
*   **2009 Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) by Shaw:** DTSC found no risk to human health with proper management.
*   **2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) by Geocon:** Addressed data gaps and updated the HHRA.
*   **2012/2013 Groundwater Monitoring:** Conducted bi-monthly, then quarterly, with additional wells installed.

A partial removal action involved the excavation and offsite disposal of approximately 2,800 yd³ of Stockpile #3 soil between September 7 and 26, 2012, during Caltrans' rehabilitation of the off-ramp to Kansas Avenue.

The **Final Feasibility Study (FS)** by Geocon (June 2014) and the **Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP)** by Geocon (October 27, 2014) recommended **Alternative 4 – Containment**, by incorporating the stockpiles into the SR-132 Project construction as highway embankment fill. This containment strategy is being implemented in phases, with Interim Phase 1 completed by November 3, 2022, and Ultimate Phase 2 planned for future construction.

## Regulatory Oversight and Agreements

*   The **Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)** and the **Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB)** are the lead regulatory agencies.
*   Caltrans is preparing an Environmental Impact Report (EIR) under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA).
*   A **Final Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement** was executed between DTSC and Caltrans on June 20, 2023, for the interim Phase 1 construction. This agreement covers Stockpiles 1 and 2, which were consolidated and capped as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway.
*   A **Land Use Covenant (LUC)** is to be recorded to restrict land use on the Site to transportation purposes, precluding residential, school, daycare, hospital, or senior care uses.
*   **Five-Year Reviews** will be performed by Caltrans to assess the effectiveness of the containment measures. The first review will focus on the interim Stockpile 1 and 2 containment. DTSC will provide project certification after the completion of final containment measures during Ultimate Phase 2.
*   **Annual O&M Inspection Summary Reports** are submitted to DTSC. The Annual Inspection Report dated January 26, 2026, was approved by DTSC on June 11, 2026.

## Fiscal Year Cost Estimates for DTSC Oversight

DTSC has provided annual cost estimates for its oversight activities:
*   **FY 2022-2023:** Estimated costs for reviewing the O&M Plan, fiscal year cost estimate, Interim Phase 1 Remedial Action Completion Report (RACR), completing the O&M Agreement and LUC, and project management.
*   **FY 2023-2024:** Estimated costs for fiscal year cost estimate, completing the LUC, reviewing the Annual Inspection, and project management.
*   **FY 2024-2025:** Estimated costs for fiscal year cost estimate, reviewing the Annual Inspection, amending/reviewing the Interagency Agreement, and project management.
