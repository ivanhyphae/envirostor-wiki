The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are three engineered piles of soil and pond tailings—Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3—placed in the early 1960s within Caltrans right-of-way south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County. The material originated from a 4.3-acre parcel Caltrans purchased in 1961 from fmc-corporation|FMC and its predecessors (Barium Products Ltd., Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation), which had discharged liquid chemical-processing residue to unlined evaporation ponds on the site from the 1950s to the 1970s. Soil excavated from within and around one of those ponds during SR-99 construction was stockpiled at the current location, anticipating future use in the planned SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway interchange. What began as a construction-materials stockpile became, over the following decade, a formal DTSC/[[caltrans]] cleanup site: characterized, risk-assessed, and ultimately closed out not by removal but by incorporation into the highway project itself.

## Site configuration and history

Stockpile 1 (~34,000 yd³) sits south of Kansas Avenue and west of Emerald Avenue; Stockpile 2 (~102,000 yd³) lies between Emerald Avenue and SR-99; Stockpile 3 (~24,000 yd³), a curvilinear pile east of SR-99, is crossed at its southeastern end by the Modesto Irrigation District Lateral #4 canal box culvert. All three are bounded by residential development to the south and commercial/industrial development to the north, enclosed by security fencing. Combined, the stockpiles total approximately 160,000 cubic yards.

The regulatory history moved through two Interagency Agreements between Caltrans and DTSC. The first (2005–2009) drove a Preliminary Site Investigation (2004), a further Site Investigation (2006) that installed eight groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-8), a 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) by Shaw Environmental, and a 2009 Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA). DTSC's December 17, 2009 letter concluded the stockpiles posed no risk to Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents provided Caltrans continued to restrict access, maintain the fence and vegetative cover, avoid disturbing the soil, and continue groundwater monitoring. The second IA (2012) initiated additional characterization, an HHRA Update, a Feasibility Study, and this Remedial Action Plan, running through the 2014 Draft Final RAP and its 2015 appendices.

## Contamination found and its origin

The stockpiles' contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) trace directly to FMC's processing of barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) ores. [[barium]] is the dominant COPC: the 2004 PSI found barium in stockpile soil exceeding commercial/industrial CHHSLs, and Fenceline Borings performed in 2012 detected barium at 140 to 4,300 mg/kg in surface samples (decreasing with depth), with the highest concentrations—above 1,000 mg/kg—along the north side of Stockpile 2. None of these concentrations exceeded the residential (5,200 mg/kg) or industrial (63,000 mg/kg) CHHSLs. Soluble barium testing (WET/DI-WET) told a sharper story: of 33 stockpile samples, 28 exceeded the Title 22 Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) of 100 mg/l for total WET extraction, though only 9 exceeded it under DI-WET. Geocon and Shaw both concluded the title-22-mcl|Title 22 STLC criteria for non-barite barium compounds did not strictly apply, since the site's barium is present as barite, a low-solubility mineral form—a finding that became central to screening out soil treatment and mobility concerns in the Feasibility Study.

Cadmium exceeded commercial/industrial CHHSLs in 8 of 25 Stockpile 2 borings and 2 of 10 Stockpile 3 borings during the 2004 PSI but was not detected at elevated levels in the 2006 SI or later sampling, and was excluded as a COPC in both the 2007 HHRA and the 2013 update. [[strontium]] was excluded on a similar basis: its maximum detected concentration (231 mg/kg in the 2004 PSI, later reported up to 765 mg/kg) remained more than two orders of magnitude below the EPA residential RSL of 47,000 mg/kg, and no CHHSL exists for strontium. [[lead]] was detected above the 50 mg/kg hazardous-waste WET-testing threshold in only two of the stockpile samples (150 and 1,500 mg/kg total lead), with WET soluble lead of 2.9 and 5.7 mg/l—well under hazardous thresholds. DTSC's Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) also flagged a calculated hexavalent [[chromium]] value that exceeded the EPA RSL but not the CHHSL; HERO considered its presence unlikely given no history of hexavalent chromium use at the adjacent FMC facility, treating the total chromium detection as predominantly trivalent given decades of oxidation since disposal.

## Groundwater and stormwater

Ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10, the last two added in June 2012) have tracked shallow groundwater at 31–41 feet below grade, flowing generally southeast to east-southeast at gradients of roughly 0.0005–0.001. Barium, lead, and strontium are DTSC's identified primary groundwater COPCs; across the 2012–2013 bimonthly and later quarterly rounds, none of the ten wells reported these three above their MCLs (barium 1,000 µg/l primary MCL, strontium's higher threshold, lead's 15 µg/l action level)—strontium concentrations in the several-hundred-to-1,500 µg/l range remained well under the roughly 4,000 µg/l threshold plotted in monitoring reports, and lead was frequently non-detect at the 1.0 µg/l PQL. The recurring exceedances instead involve general minerals unrelated to the FMC-derived metals: [[nitrate]] as nitrogen and total dissolved solids (TDS), concentrated in wells MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10.

Because MW-1 is the upgradient well and it too shows nitrate (12 mg/l both events) and TDS (420–460 mg/l) above or near threshold, Geocon's reports treat these exceedances as likely representative of natural background groundwater quality rather than a stockpile-derived impact. A single manganese exceedance recurred at different wells across events (MW-6 in January 2013, MW-8 in November 2012), the only dissolved-metal secondary-MCL exceedance reported. A March 2006 stormwater sampling round found one anomalous barium result (2,000 µg/l at sample SW03, against a 1,000 µg/l MCL) on the northwest side of Stockpile 3, isolated to that location and confined to Caltrans ROW; DTSC concluded stormwater was not a chronic exposure pathway and it was excluded from the HHRA.

## Risk assessment and DTSC's conditional acceptance

The 2007 Shaw HHRA and its 2013 Geocon update both concluded that neither current exposure (offsite resident/trespasser) nor the future construction-worker and offsite-resident scenarios during eventual SR-132 construction posed unacceptable cancer risk or non-cancer hazard, and that a hypothetical shallow-groundwater user's cumulative hazard index (0.9) remained below the threshold of 1. DTSC's HERO office concurred in an April 4, 2013 letter, but its concurrence was explicitly conditional: HERO's February 14, 2013 memorandum warned that the risk evaluation was based on surface soil only, and that "if there is substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles or if the stockpiles are removed," the elevated subsurface concentrations would require separate evaluation for exposure risk during soil movement. That condition directly shaped the remedy: any alternative involving excavation would reopen the risk question that in-place management had closed.

## Remedy selection and disposition

The 2014 Final Feasibility Study screened four alternatives—no action, institutional controls, removal/offsite disposal, and containment—against CERCLA's nine criteria. Removal was calculated to cost roughly 20 times more than containment (once replacement fill costs were included) and would generate an estimated 529,200 pounds of CO2 and require roughly 175 truckloads per day for about 30 days each way. DTSC and the FS selected Alternative 4, Containment: using the stockpile soil itself as embankment fill, retaining-wall backfill, and bridge abutment material for the SR-132/SR-99 interchange, to be built in an interim phase (two-lane roadway, completed by 2018, with northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 temporarily capped with 6–12 inches of clean soil) and an ultimate build-out phase (four lanes, complete containment behind retaining walls and pavement, by 2028). Stockpile 3 was planned for complete containment within the interim phase, consolidated partly into the SR-132 bridge abutment over SR-99 and partly into Stockpile 2's consolidation zone.

One removal action did occur outside this framework: in September 2012, approximately 2,800 yd³ of Stockpile 3 was excavated for a separate Kansas Avenue off-ramp safety improvement, with barium in confirmation samples ranging 34–1,600 mg/kg (below both CHHSLs), disposed as non-hazardous waste at the Forward Class II landfill in Manteca under DTSC's August 30, 2012 non-objection. Because the remaining remedy leaves contaminated soil in place under pavement rather than removing it, DTSC required a land use covenant (LUC) restricting the site to transportation use and barring residential, school, daycare, hospital, or senior-care development; the covenant runs with the land until DTSC terminates it, with Caltrans bearing DTSC's oversight costs under Title 22 Section 67391.1. DTSC's May 2015 Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility assigned Caltrans full responsibility for remediation and ongoing operation and maintenance costs, noting that the arbitration provisions that would normally accompany an NBAR had been repealed by SB 1018 in 2012, leaving the allocation without binding legal effect.
