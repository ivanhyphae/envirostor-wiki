## Overview

Nitrate, reported as nitrate as nitrogen (NO3), is a general mineral constituent monitored in shallow groundwater and surface water at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in Stanislaus County, California. It has repeatedly equaled or exceeded the California primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 10 mg/L in groundwater. The presence of elevated nitrate in upgradient well MW-1 raises questions about whether some portion of the nitrate represents natural background concentrations rather than a release from the stockpiles. Nitrate is analyzed by EPA Test Method 300.0 (ion chromatography) by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) and is included in the general minerals suite for groundwater and surface water geochemistry characterization.

## Site record

### Groundwater Detections

In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.6 mg/L at MW-3 to 29 mg/L at MW-5. Five of the ten monitoring wells equaled or exceeded the primary MCL of 10 mg/L: MW-1 (15 mg/L), MW-5 (29 mg/L), MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10. The analytical results for general minerals, including nitrate, are summarized on Table 3 of the S9800-01-17 Modesto Stockpiles Feb2014 GW Mon 0414 report.

The June 2014 report notes that MW-1 is an upgradient monitoring well, and the reported nitrate concentration of 15 mg/L "may be indicative of natural background nitrate concentrations for the shallow groundwater in the vicinity of the Site." This observation suggests that nitrate exceedances at the site may not be solely attributable to the stockpiled material.

Historical groundwater monitoring data indicates a consistent pattern of nitrate exceedances. In March 2012, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.9 mg/l at MW-3 to 27 mg/l at MW-5. Concentrations in MW-1 (12 mg/l) and MW-6 (18 mg/l) exceeded the primary MCL of 10 mg/l. In November 2012, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.8 mg/l (MW-3) to 24 mg/l (MW-5). The reported concentrations for MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-8, and MW-10 exceeded the primary MCL. In February 2014, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.8 mg/l (MW-3) to 34 mg/l (MW-5). Samples from MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 equaled or exceeded the primary MCL.

### Surface Water Detections

In a March 2006 surface water sampling event, nitrate was among the analytes analyzed. In January 2018, surface water samples were analyzed for nitrate as nitrogen by EPA Test Method 300.0. Data from March 2017 indicates nitrate as nitrogen concentrations in surface water samples, such as BG2 on 3/24/2017, were 10 mg/L.

### General Minerals Context

Nitrate concentrations are evaluated alongside major cations and other anions to characterize groundwater geochemistry. Stiff diagrams for June 2014 indicate bicarbonate-dominant water across all wells, with variations in calcium-dominant and sodium-dominant compositions. In February 2014, samples from MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10 were calcium-dominant, while samples from MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8 were sodium-dominant. Water samples from well MW-5 switched from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013, potentially indicating mixing of different water types.

## Regulatory status

The primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for nitrate as nitrogen, promulgated by the California Department of Public Health (CDPH), is 10 mg/L. In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10 equaled or exceeded this MCL. The S9800-01-17 Modesto Stockpiles Feb2014 GW Mon 0414 report notes these exceedances but does not document specific regulatory directives or enforcement actions related to them. The site is subject to oversight by the [[dtsc]] and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), with quarterly groundwater monitoring.

## Open items

The sources do not identify any open items specifically tied to nitrate, such as pending source investigations, additional delineation wells, or remedial requirements. The question of whether upgradient nitrate at MW-1 represents natural background and its implications for interpreting downgradient exceedances is noted observationally but not formalized as a required follow-up action. Quarterly groundwater monitoring was scheduled to continue.
