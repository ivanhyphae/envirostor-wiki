Nitrate has been the most persistently exceeded groundwater constituent at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, exceeding the [[california-primary-mcl]] of 10 mg/L in the same subset of monitoring wells across nearly every sampling round since 2012. Critically, the wells with the highest and most consistent exceedances include MW-1, an upgradient well, which has led Geocon and later EMKO to treat elevated nitrate as most likely reflecting regional or natural background conditions and, later, the FMC groundwater plume rather than a release from the Caltrans stockpiles themselves.

## Soil and stormwater: not a concern

Nitrate was analyzed in stockpile soil during the Shaw 2004 PSI (maximum 310 mg/kg in 42 of 54 samples) and the 2006 SI, in both cases at concentrations within the range of background and with no regulatory soil screening level applicable. DI-WET soluble nitrate in the 2006 SI ranged from 0.2 to 2.6 mg/l in 28 of 33 samples. The 2007 HHRA and 2013 HHRA Update identified nitrate as a COPC in groundwater (with TDS) but not in soil, and the 2013 HERO memorandum found stockpile nitrate concentrations to be within the range of background, eliminating it as a chemical of concern in that medium. Stormwater monitoring in March 2006, April 2013, and February 2014 analyzed for nitrate but did not identify it as an exceedance driver; the only stormwater exceedances recorded across the file involved barium and strontium.

## Groundwater: recurring exceedances in a consistent well subset

Nitrate as nitrogen has been measured at all ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10, the latter two installed in June 2012) during each groundwater monitoring event since sampling resumed in March 2012. Across every reported round, the primary MCL of 10 mg/L has been exceeded in the same recurring wells — principally MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10, with MW-9 and MW-3/MW-8 appearing intermittently.

By April 2015, only wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, and MW-7 were being sampled, and nitrate exceeded the MCL only at MW-1 and MW-2. By May 2016, only MW-1 and MW-7 were sampled (plus a duplicate labeled MW-11), and nitrate at both fell just below the MCL (9.2 and 8.7 mg/L). This decline in both the number of wells monitored and the reported concentrations reflects a reduction in monitoring frequency — from quarterly to annual starting in 2015 — following the conclusions described below, not a documented decline in underlying groundwater conditions across the full well network.

## Interpretation: upgradient signal and attribution to FMC, not the stockpiles

Because MW-1 is the site's upgradient well, its persistent nitrate exceedances (typically 10–17 mg/L) were repeatedly cited across the 2012–2015 monitoring reports as evidence that elevated nitrate reflects natural background conditions in shallow groundwater rather than a stockpile-derived release — since water reaching MW-1 has not yet passed beneath any stockpile.

The 2024 Updated Statistical Evaluation Report (S2350-01-02) extended this analysis using the full 2012–2023 dataset and comparison to FMC facility monitoring data. It found that nitrate is one of several constituents (with TDS, sulfate, barium, and strontium) known to be elevated in groundwater beneath the FMC facility itself, where liquid wastes were discharged to unlined ponds from the early 1950s to the late 1970s. The report concluded that Caltrans wells MW-1 through MW-4 (Stockpile 1 and western Stockpile 2) show no impact from the stockpiles when compared to upgradient background; that MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 show elevated but declining concentrations attributable to the FMC extraction and treatment system's reduction of source mass rather than to the Caltrans stockpiles (since no change occurred at the stockpiles that would explain a declining trend); and that concentrations in downgradient wells MW-7 and MW-8 are lower than in upgradient MW-6 and MW-10, indicating Stockpile 3 and eastern Stockpile 2 are not impacting groundwater. Based in part on this evaluation, six of the ten Caltrans wells were decommissioned in 2019 prior to highway construction.

## Significance

Nitrate is the constituent most often cited across the monitoring record as exceeding a primary MCL in site groundwater, but the file's own attribution analysis places the source with the adjacent former FMC processing facility and regional background rather than with the Caltrans stockpiles. This distinction underpins the site's overall risk conclusions: the 2007 HHRA and 2013 HHRA Update did not identify an unacceptable groundwater risk from the stockpiles, and the eventual reduction in monitoring frequency and well count relied in part on the argument that nitrate (along with TDS, barium, and strontium) trends in Caltrans wells track FMC-plume behavior rather than any condition attributable to Caltrans-managed soil.
