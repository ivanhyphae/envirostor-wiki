## Overview

Nitrate, reported as nitrate as nitrogen (NO3), is a general mineral constituent monitored in shallow groundwater at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in Stanislaus County, California. It is one of the analytes that has repeatedly equaled or exceeded the California primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 10 mg/L. The presence of elevated nitrate in both upgradient and downgradient wells raises questions about whether some portion of the nitrate represents natural background concentrations rather than a release from the stockpiles. Nitrate is analyzed by EPA Test Method 300.0 (ion chromatography) by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) and is included in the general minerals suite for groundwater geochemistry characterization.

## Site record

### Detection summary — June 2014 event

In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.6 mg/L at MW-3 to 29 mg/L at MW-5 {{quote: "In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.6 mg/L at MW-3 to 29 mg/L at MW-5."}}. Five of the ten monitoring wells equaled or exceeded the primary MCL of 10 mg/L:

*   **MW-1:** 15 mg/L {{quote: "MW-1: 15 mg/L"}}
*   **MW-5:** 29 mg/L {{quote: "MW-5: 29 mg/L"}}
*   **MW-6:** concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L {{quote: "MW-6: concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L"}}
*   **MW-9:** concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L {{quote: "MW-9: concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L"}}
*   **MW-10:** concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L {{quote: "MW-10: concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L"}}

The analytical results for general minerals, including nitrate, are summarized on Table 3 of the S9800-01-17 Modesto Stockpiles Feb2014 GW Mon 0414/S9800-01-17 Modesto Stockpiles Feb2014 GW Mon 0414.md.

### Upgradient detection and background question

Monitoring well MW-1 is designated as an upgradient well. The June 2014 report notes: "MW-1 is an upgradient monitoring well; thus, the reported nitrate of 15 mg/l may be indicative of natural background nitrate concentrations for the shallow groundwater in the vicinity of the Site." {{quote: "MW-1 is an upgradient monitoring well; thus, the reported nitrate of 15 mg/l may be indicative of natural background nitrate concentrations for the shallow groundwater in the vicinity of the Site."}} This observation suggests that nitrate exceedances at the site may not be solely attributable to the stockpiled material.

### Historical detections

The June 2014 report references that nitrate has been detected above the MCL in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 across multiple earlier sampling events, specifically noted in the narrative summarizing twelve groundwater sampling events from March 2012 through February 2014 {{quote: "The results of the groundwater sampling events show that both dissolved metals and general minerals have predominantly been reported at concentrations less than their respective numeric water quality threshold values. Only nitrates (expressed as nitrogen) in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 and total dissolved solids (TDS) in wells MW-5, "}}. The June 2014 results are consistent with this historical pattern.

In March 2012, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.9 mg/l at MW-3 to 27 mg/l at MW-5 {{quote: "Nitrate as nitrogen was also reported for each of the groundwater samples ranging from 2.9 mg/l at MW-3 to 27 mg/l at MW-5."}}. The reported nitrate concentrations for MW-1 (12 mg/l), MW-5, and MW-6 (18 mg/l) each exceed the primary MCL of 10 mg/l established for nitrate {{quote: "The reported nitrate concentrations for MW-1 (12 mg/l), MW-5, and MW-6 (18 mg/l) each exceed the primary MCL of 10 mg/l established for nitrate."}}.

In November 2012, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.8 (MW-3) to 24 mg/l (MW-5) {{quote: "Nitrate as nitrogen and TDS were both reported for each of the groundwater samples, with nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranging from 2.8 (MW-3) to 24 mg/l (MW-5)"}}. The reported nitrate concentrations for MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-8 and MW-10 exceed the primary MCL for nitrate of 10 mg/l {{quote: "The reported nitrate concentrations for MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-8 and MW-10 exceed the primary MCL for nitrate of 10 mg/l"}}.

In February 2014, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.8 (MW-3) to 34 mg/l (MW-5) {{quote: "Nitrate as nitrogen and TDS were both reported for each of the groundwater samples, with nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranging from 2.8 (MW-3) to 34 mg/l (MW-5)"}}. The reported nitrate concentrations for samples from MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 equal or exceed the primary MCL for nitrate of 10 mg/l {{quote: "The reported nitrate concentrations for samples from MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 equal or exceed the primary MCL for nitrate of 10 mg/l"}}.

### General minerals context — Stiff diagrams

Nitrate concentrations are used alongside major cations and other anions to evaluate groundwater geochemistry. Stiff diagrams for June 2014 indicate bicarbonate-dominant water across all wells. A split is observed between calcium-dominant composition (MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, MW-10) and sodium-dominant composition (MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, MW-8). The report notes that MW-5 shifted from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013, potentially indicating mixing of different water types. Nitrate as nitrogen and TDS are reported for each sample, alongside constituents used for Stiff diagram generation.

In March 2012, the samples from MW-1 and MW-7 had calcium-dominant compositions, while the samples from wells MW-5, MW-6 and MW-8 were sodium-dominant. The samples from wells MW-2, MW-3 and MW-4 demonstrated a similar balance of calcium and sodium, suggesting a mix of these water types at those locations {{quote: "In 2006, the samples from the Site's furthest upgradient monitoring well (MW-1) along with MW-7 (located on the opposite end of the Site) had calcium-dominant compositions, while the samples from wells MW-5, MW-6 and MW-8 (each situated downgradient to FMC) were sodium-dominant. The samples from wells MW-2, MW-3 and MW-4 demonstrated a similar balance of calcium and sodium, suggesting a mix of these water types at those locations."}}.

In November 2012, the samples from wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-5, MW-7, MW-9 and MW-10 had a calcium-dominant composition while the samples from wells MW-3, MW-6 and MW-8 were sodium-dominant {{quote: "In November 2012, the samples from wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-5, MW-7, MW-9 and MW-10 had a calcium-dominant composition while the samples from wells MW-3, MW-6 and MW-8 were sodium-dominant."}}.

In February 2014, the samples from wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10 had a calcium-dominant composition while the samples from wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8 were sodium-dominant. Water samples from well MW-5 switched from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013. This may be indicative of a mixing of different water types at this location {{quote: "In February 2014, the samples from wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10 had a calcium-dominant composition while the samples from wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8 were sodium-dominant. Water samples from well MW-5 switched from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013. This may be indicative of a mixing of different water types at this location."}}.

### Surface water detections

In a March 2006 surface water sampling event, nitrate was among the analytes analyzed {{quote: "The samples were analyzed for dissolved metals, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), nitrate, sulfate and sulfide."}}. In January 2018, surface water samples were analyzed for nitrate as nitrogen by EPA Test Method 300.0 {{quote: "Chloride, nitrate as nitrogen and sulfate by EPA Test Method 300.0;"}}.

## Regulatory status

Nitrate as nitrogen at concentrations at or above 10 mg/L in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10 equaled or exceeded the California primary MCL during the June 2014 monitoring event {{quote: "Five of the ten monitoring wells equaled or exceeded the primary MCL of 10 mg/L:"}}. The S9800-01-17 Modesto Stockpiles Feb2014 GW Mon 0414/S9800-01-17 Modesto Stockpiles Feb2014 GW Mon 0414.md notes this exceedance but does not document any specific regulatory directive, enforcement action, or compliance schedule triggered by these nitrate exceedances. The site is subject to oversight by the [[dtsc]] and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), with quarterly groundwater monitoring.

## Open items

The report does not identify any open items specifically tied to nitrate, such as pending source investigations, additional delineation wells, or remedial requirements. The question of whether upgradient nitrate at MW-1 represents natural background and its implications for interpreting downgradient exceedances is noted observationally but not formalized as a required follow-up action. Quarterly groundwater monitoring was scheduled to continue with the next event in September 2014.
