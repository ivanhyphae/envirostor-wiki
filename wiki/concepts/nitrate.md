# Nitrate

## Overview

Nitrate, reported as nitrate as nitrogen, is a general mineral constituent monitored in shallow groundwater at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in Stanislaus County, California. It is one of the analytes that has repeatedly equaled or exceeded the California primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 10 mg/L. The presence of elevated nitrate in both upgradient and downgradient wells raises questions about whether some portion of the nitrate represents natural background concentrations rather than a release from the stockpiles. Nitrate is analyzed by EPA Test Method 300.0 (ion chromatography) by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) and is included in the general minerals suite for groundwater geochemistry characterization.

## Site record

### Detection summary — June 2014 event

In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, nitrate as nitrogen concentrations ranged from 2.6 mg/L at MW-3 to 29 mg/L at MW-5. Five of the ten monitoring wells equaled or exceeded the primary MCL of 10 mg/L:

*   **MW-1:** 15 mg/L
*   **MW-5:** 29 mg/L
*   **MW-6:** concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L
*   **MW-9:** concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L
*   **MW-10:** concentration equal to or exceeding 10 mg/L

The analytical results for general minerals, including nitrate, are summarized on Table 3 of the [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]].

### Upgradient detection and background question

Monitoring well MW-1 is designated as an upgradient well. The June 2014 report notes: "MW-1 is an upgradient monitoring well; thus, the reported nitrate of 15 mg/l may be indicative of natural background nitrate concentrations for the shallow groundwater in the vicinity of the Site." This observation suggests that nitrate exceedances at the site may not be solely attributable to the stockpiled material.

### Historical detections

The June 2014 report references that nitrate has been detected above the MCL in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 across multiple earlier sampling events, specifically noted in the narrative summarizing twelve groundwater sampling events from March 2012 through February 2014. The June 2014 results are consistent with this historical pattern.

### General minerals context — Stiff diagrams

Nitrate concentrations are used alongside major cations and other anions to evaluate groundwater geochemistry. Stiff diagrams for June 2014 indicate bicarbonate-dominant water across all wells. A split is observed between calcium-dominant composition (MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, MW-10) and sodium-dominant composition (MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, MW-8). The report notes that MW-5 shifted from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013, potentially indicating mixing of different water types. Nitrate as nitrogen and TDS are reported for each sample, alongside constituents used for Stiff diagram generation.

### Water quality threshold values applied

The nitrate results are compared against:

*   Primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) promulgated by the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) — nitrate MCL = 10 mg/L

## Regulatory status

Nitrate as nitrogen at concentrations at or above 10 mg/L in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10 equaled or exceeded the California primary MCL during the June 2014 monitoring event. The [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]] notes this exceedance but does not document any specific regulatory directive, enforcement action, or compliance schedule triggered by these nitrate exceedances. The site is subject to oversight by the [[dtsc]] and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), with quarterly groundwater monitoring.

## Open items

The report does not identify any open items specifically tied to nitrate, such as pending source investigations, additional delineation wells, or remedial requirements. The question of whether upgradient nitrate at MW-1 represents natural background and its implications for interpreting downgradient exceedances is noted observationally but not formalized as a required follow-up action. Quarterly groundwater monitoring was scheduled to continue with the next event in September 2014.
