# Updated Comparative Evaluation Of Groundwater Data

The Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, dated February 26, 2024, is the analysis [[geocon-consultants]] submitted on behalf of [[caltrans]] to determine whether the barium- and lead-impacted soil stockpiled beneath the newly constructed State Route 132 Express Way was affecting, or would affect, groundwater. The evaluation compared current conditions against an earlier 2014 evaluation and against the fact that the soil had since been physically encapsulated beneath the Express Way. On May 3, 2024, [[dtsc]], in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], approved the report, concluding that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and will not do so in the future.

## Basis for the conclusion

DTSC's approval letter (Site Code: 900259) states that the finding of no impact rests on two points identified in the Evaluation Report itself: the updated data were consistent with the earlier 2014 evaluation, and the soil stockpiles had since been physically encapsulated beneath the Express Way structure. No concentration values, well identifiers, or sampling dates from the underlying dataset are given in the approval letter; the letter records the agencies' conclusion, not the analytical results that produced it.

## Consequence: end of monitoring and well destruction

Because the data showed no impact, DTSC and the RWQCB approved discontinuing groundwater monitoring beneath the soil stockpiles altogether. The approval also concurs with decommissioning the four remaining monitoring wells — MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10 — consistent with the previously approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan. This makes the evaluation the direct basis for ending the site's groundwater monitoring program and for the associated [[well-destruction]] of the last four wells; no further groundwater sampling obligation is recorded as continuing after this approval.

## Distribution and status

The approval was issued to Rebecca L. Silva (Geocon) by Dean Wright, PG, of DTSC's Site Mitigation and Restoration Program, with copies to Adam Inman (Caltrans District 6, Office of Environmental Engineering), Kyle Cockerham (Central Valley RWQCB Site Cleanup Unit), and Lora Jameson (DTSC Site Evaluation and Remediation Program). The letter is an approval with no further conditions attached beyond the monitoring discontinuation and well decommissioning already described; no follow-up deliverable or additional agency review is called for in the record.
