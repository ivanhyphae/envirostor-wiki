MW-3 is one of the ten original monitoring wells installed at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site in 2006 and is distinguished among the site's wells by its consistent sodium-dominant groundwater chemistry, a signature the site's geochemical reports interpreted as evidence of influence from the former FMC facility rather than from the Caltrans stockpiles themselves. It also became the first well at the site to run dry, going dry by the October 2016 monitoring round and remaining unsampleable through subsequent events. No dissolved metals or general mineral results from MW-3 across any sampling round exceeded numeric water quality thresholds.

## Location and hydrogeologic context

MW-3 sits among the ten wells arrayed across the stockpile area and the upgradient FMC boundary. Repeated Stiff diagram analyses placed MW-3 with wells MW-6 and MW-8 as consistently sodium-dominant, in contrast to the calcium-dominant water found at the upgradient well MW-1 and beneath Stockpiles 1 and 2 (MW-2, MW-4, MW-5). Geocon's reports characterized this pattern as indicating that sodium-dominant water occurs at locations "upgradient to the stockpiles that further coincide with locations downgradient to the FMC facility," grouping MW-3 with MW-6 in that interpretation. This classification held through every reported event from March 2012 through April 2019, the last round in which MW-3 yielded a sample.

## Dissolved metals

Across the March 2012 through April 2019 sampling rounds compiled in the record, MW-3 did not contribute any dissolved-metals exceedance of a numeric drinking water threshold. In the few reports that break out individual well detections for minor metals, MW-3 was specifically noted for below-threshold nickel and molybdenum absence (nickel was reported for each well except MW-3 in November 2012), and for detections of cobalt and manganese at concentrations not exceeding thresholds (September 2012). Mercury was reported as non-detect for MW-3 specifically in the March 2012 round, one of the few site-wide exceptions noted by well.

## General minerals and nitrate

MW-3 consistently returned the lowest nitrate-as-nitrogen concentrations among the site's wells, a pattern repeated across nearly every monitoring round:

The primary MCL for nitrate as nitrogen is 10 mg/l; MW-3's values never approached it, in contrast to wells such as MW-5 and MW-6, which repeatedly exceeded it. MW-3's TDS values (270–340 mg/l where reported) likewise stayed below the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l throughout, again contrasting with several other site wells that exceeded it in the same rounds. Sulfide was reported at MW-3 only intermittently and at low concentrations (e.g., 0.011 mg/l in September 2013 and December 2013 sampling of nearby wells; MW-3 specifically returned 0.011 mg/l in September 2013 and non-detect in February and June 2014).

## Well status and monitoring history

MW-3 was sampled on a bi-monthly and later quarterly basis from March 2012 onward alongside the other original wells. By October 2016, MW-3 was recorded as dry, along with MW-4 and MW-8, and no sample could be collected from it that round. The April 2017 and April 2019 annual reports continued to classify MW-3 as sodium-dominant based on the samples that could still be obtained. By October 2015, an even broader set of wells — MW-3 through MW-6 and MW-8 through MW-10 — were recorded dry, with only MW-1, MW-2, and MW-7 yielding measurable depth-to-groundwater readings that round.

The 2025 DTSC letter response confirms that six of the original ten wells were decommissioned in 2019, following years of monitoring data that the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board and DTSC concluded showed no metals leaching from the stockpiles into groundwater; a subsequent Well Destruction Report addressed the remaining four wells. The source material does not specify by name which six wells were decommissioned in 2019 or whether MW-3 was among that group or among the four addressed in the later destruction report.
