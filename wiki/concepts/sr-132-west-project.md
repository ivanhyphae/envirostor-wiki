# SR 132 West Project

## Overview

The SR 132 West Project, also known as the Modesto Soil Stockpiles project, involves the remediation and containment of barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially deposited lead (ADL) impacted soil. The primary objective is to utilize these soils as highway embankment fill and cap them with clean fill materials as part of the State Route 132 West Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) is the lead regulatory agency overseeing the site mitigation, in cooperation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). The project is managed under a Caltrans Interagency Agreement.

## Site Record

The site comprises three former barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles: Stockpile 1, Stockpile 2, and Stockpile 3.

* **Stockpile 1:** Located approximately 150 feet westerly of Emerald Avenue, it was roughly 600 feet long in the east-west direction, 160 feet wide, and had an estimated fill volume of 34,000 cubic yards (yd³).
* **Stockpile 2:** Situated between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, it was approximately 1,650 feet long in the east-west direction, 160 feet wide, and had an estimated fill volume of 102,000 yd³.
* **Stockpile 3:** Formerly located on the east side of SR 99, it was approximately 1,100 feet long, 120 feet wide, with an estimated fill volume of 24,000 yd³.

Stockpile 3 was completely excavated and placed as highway embankment fill on top of Stockpile 1 and between Stockpile 1 and Emerald Avenue.

Elevated levels of barium and lead were confirmed in stockpile soil samples, exceeding regulatory health-based screening levels.

The Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) for Interim Phase 1 specified the following soil excavation verification thresholds:
* Barium: 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) based on groundwater protection.
* Lead: 80 mg/kg based on DTSC residential screening level.

Site-specific upper-bound background concentrations for proposed clean fill source areas were established as:
* Barium: 120 mg/kg.
* Lead: 3.8 mg/kg.

Interim Phase 1 construction involved the consolidation and clean fill capping of BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3 and ADL impacted soil within designated BCS Containment Zones. Remaining work includes placement of structural pavement, erosion control, drainage, and median barriers.

Groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) were present prior to construction. Depth to groundwater in October 2021 ranged between approximately 39 to 43 feet below natural grade. Groundwater extraction was not required during Interim Phase 1 construction near the BCS stockpiles.

Groundwater and stormwater monitoring activities were conducted by Caltrans' subconsultant, Stantec, during Interim Phase I. Four groundwater monitoring events were completed between November 2019 and March 2021 for wells MW-4, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10. Reported analyte concentrations were below regulatory thresholds. Three stormwater sampling events were completed between December 2019 and December 2020. Stormwater was not observed migrating offsite.

## Regulatory Status

The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) is the lead regulatory agency. The Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) also cooperates in providing site mitigation oversight.

A 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) concluded that the soil and groundwater at the site, as managed, did not pose an unacceptable human risk. This conclusion was based on maintaining a perimeter fence, limiting access to Caltrans workers, maintaining vegetative cover, prohibiting soil excavation/removal, and maintaining groundwater monitoring.

DTSC stated in a letter dated December 17, 2009, that the soil stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans, did not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. DTSC required Caltrans to continue managing the stockpiles by limiting access, inspecting and maintaining the fence, prohibiting excavation/removal or placement of other soil, and maintaining the current grade and vegetative cover, as well as the groundwater monitoring system.

The DTSC-approved remedial alternative for the BCS stockpiles is containment by incorporating them as highway embankment fill and covering with structural pavement section and imported clean fill.

## Open Items

A Final Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) will be submitted following the completed construction of Ultimate Phase 2 of the SR 132 West Project, scheduled for completion in 2028.

Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the interim and final BCS containment measures. DTSC will provide project certification following the completion of final containment measures during Ultimate Phase 2.

A supplemental RDIP will be submitted to DTSC for approval prior to construction of Ultimate Phase 2.
