PL3 is a surface water sample point along the southern edge of Stockpile 2, collected where Bennett Street intersects the alley behind Loletta Street — outside the Caltrans right-of-way, beyond the chainlink fence enclosing the south side of Stockpile 2. Across on-call storm sampling rounds run by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] between 2013 and 2018, PL3 was the location most frequently associated with elevated dissolved metals, including the only reported exceedance of the primary lead MCL at any of the PL locations and repeated exceedances of the secondary manganese MCL.

## Sampling history and results

PL3 was sampled in most storm events beginning April 2013 and continuing through the December 2016, March 2017, January 2018, and March 2018 rounds; it was one of the more reliably wet locations, unlike PL5 and BG2, which frequently went dry.

| Date | Notable dissolved metals results at PL3 | Exceedance |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 28, 2016 | Arsenic, lead, manganese reported (manganese equal to secondary MCL) | Manganese at MCL |
| Dec 15, 2016 | Arsenic, cobalt, lead, manganese reported; manganese exceeded secondary MCL (50 µg/l) | Manganese exceedance |
| Mar 24, 2017 | Antimony, barium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, vanadium, arsenic, lead, zinc reported | None reported above MCLs |
| Jan 8, 2018 | Sampled; results reported in Table 2 of that round | Not detailed in narrative text |
| Mar 22, 2018 | Lead detected at 46 µg/l | Exceeds primary MCL (15 µg/l), ~3× the limit |

The March 22, 2018 event is the only round in the record where a PL3 (or any PL-series) result is described as exceeding a primary MCL: lead at 46 µg/l against the drinking-water primary MCL of 15 µg/l. The same round reported manganese at PL2 through PL4 and BG1, including PL3, in the range of 54 to 370 µg/l, all exceeding the secondary MCL of 50 µg/l. The transmittal letter for that round states that, except for the elevated lead concentration in the sample collected outside the right-of-way at PL3, results were generally consistent with prior events dating to 2013.

In the December 15, 2016 round, manganese at PL3 and PL4 exceeded the secondary MCL; in the October 28, 2016 round, manganese at PL3 was reported at a concentration equal to (not exceeding) the secondary MCL. No consequence, follow-up action, or resampling requirement tied specifically to these PL3 exceedances is described in the available reports.

## Laboratory handling

PL3 samples required dilution in multiple rounds due to high target-analyte concentrations or matrix interference. In the March 2017 round, PL3 was diluted for chloride and sulfate due to possible matrix interference, in addition to the barium, calcium, magnesium, manganese, potassium, sodium, and nitrate dilutions applied across all PL samples that round. In the December 2016 round, PL3 specifically required dilution for barium. In the October 2016 round, PL3 was among the samples diluted for barium, beryllium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, lead, magnesium, manganese, nickel, potassium, sodium, thallium, and vanadium due to failing internal standard or matrix interference.

## Location and containment significance

Every field report through 2018 describes PL3 as located outside the Caltrans right-of-way, on the far side of the chainlink fence enclosing Stockpile 2's south side — meaning water pooling at PL3 has already left the fenced containment area. Despite this, the March 2017 and March 2018 surface water management narratives report no observed migration of stormwater runoff beyond the right-of-way and attribute containment at the Bennett Street/Loletta area to an interceptor berm at the base of the gravel-covered south side access ramp to Stockpile 2, which was reported as functioning as designed by directing runoff back to the area adjacent to the ramp within the Stockpile 2 boundary. The March 2018 report characterizes the March 22, 2018 lead exceedance at PL3 as occurring in a sample collected outside the right-of-way, without describing further investigation or a change in monitoring or containment measures as a result.

By the time of DTSC's January 30, 2026 letter approving the FY24/25 Storm Water and Surface Water Monitoring Report, the agency's summary states that results were all within reporting limits; the letter does not discuss PL3 individually or reconcile that statement with the 2018 PL3 exceedances in the earlier record.
