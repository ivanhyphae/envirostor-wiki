## Overview

The Operations and Maintenance (O&M) Plan is a required deliverable for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project located along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. The plan addresses long-term management of the impacted soil stockpiles following completion of active remediation, providing procedures for ongoing monitoring, maintenance, and reporting to ensure remedy effectiveness and protect human health and the environment.

## Site record

The O&M Plan requirement stems from the Remedial Action Workplan approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for soil stockpiles generated during the SR 132 widening project. The stockpiles contain aerially deposited lead and other heavy metals from historical vehicle emissions and agricultural pesticide applications in the region. Soil from the project corridor was excavated and stockpiled pending characterization and remedial decision-making.

The stockpiles are located at designated staging areas within the Caltrans right-of-way along SR 132 in Modesto and western Stanislaus County. Characterization sampling identified concentrations of lead exceeding the DTSC-specified screening level of 80 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) for residential land use scenarios. Other metals including arsenic and cadmium were detected at concentrations below regulatory thresholds of concern for the site.

The remedy selected for impacted soils includes consolidation, capping, and institutional controls. Following implementation, the O&M Plan will govern inspection and maintenance of the engineered cap, groundwater monitoring (if required), and institutional control verification. The plan duration and specific monitoring frequencies have not yet been finalized in the available record.

## Regulatory status

DTSC is the lead regulatory agency overseeing the project under the California Hazardous Waste Control Law and the Site Mitigation and Restoration Program. The O&M Plan must be prepared consistent with DTSC guidance and approved prior to remedy implementation. The plan is anticipated to be a key component of the final remedy selection documentation, with DTSC retaining authority to modify requirements based on monitoring results and site conditions over time.

The California Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region, may have concurrent jurisdiction over any groundwater monitoring components, though the primary oversight is through DTSC.

## Open items

The specific contents, monitoring schedules, reporting frequencies, and institutional control mechanisms for the O&M Plan are pending development and DTSC approval. No draft O&M Plan is documented in the available record. Key open items include finalization of cap design specifications that will drive inspection protocols, determination of groundwater monitoring requirements if any, and identification of the responsible party for long-term implementation.
