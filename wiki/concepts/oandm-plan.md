The Operations and Maintenance (O&M) Plan is a critical component for the long-term management of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project, located along State Route 132 (SR 132) in Stanislaus County, California. This plan outlines the procedures for ongoing monitoring, maintenance, and reporting to ensure the effectiveness of the implemented remedy and to protect human health and the environment. The O&M Plan is a requirement stemming from the Remedial Action Workplan approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for soil stockpiles generated during the SR 132 widening project. These stockpiles contain soil with elevated levels of lead and barium, originating from historical vehicle emissions and regional agricultural practices. The selected remedy involves consolidation and capping of these impacted soils, with the O&M Plan governing the post-remediation oversight.

## Site record

The O&M Plan specifically addresses barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles that were consolidated, capped with clean fill material, and utilized as highway embankment fill during the construction of Interim Phase 1 of the SR 132 West Project. The stockpiles are located within designated Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones, adjacent to and extending west of SR 99.

The O&M Plan details procedures for managing BCS material within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones should it be disturbed. Caltrans is responsible for providing a minimum of ten working days advance notification to DTSC before any planned activities that may disturb BCS material or associated clean cover fill. No disturbance is permitted without prior written authorization from DTSC.

Annual inspections are a key component of the O&M Plan. The O&M Site Inspection Form is used by field crews to document the condition of the cap/SR 132 Freeway. DTSC recommends that inspections also occur after events such as fires, substantial rainfall (1 inch in 24 hours), significant seismic events, or traffic accidents that could damage the cap. Periodic patrols should also note any unauthorized access, such as homeless encampments, that could impact the cap.

The O&M Plan was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of Caltrans and submitted in December 2022. Comments from DTSC dated August 1, 2022, on the Draft O&M Plan dated March 2022, were addressed in the final version.

Annual inspection reports are submitted to DTSC for approval. For example, the Annual Operation and Maintenance Inspection Report dated January 26, 2026, which documented an inspection at State Route 132 West Expressway at the State Route 132/99 Interchange, was approved by DTSC. The report noted that vegetation was still sparse along the road, and that leftover erosion control materials were limiting erosion.

## Regulatory status

The O&M Plan is prepared in accordance with the requirements of the DTSC, which is the lead regulatory agency overseeing the project. The plan is a deliverable for the Remedial Action Completion Report. DTSC retains the authority to modify O&M requirements based on monitoring results and site conditions.

The Land Use Covenant (LUC) also mandates annual inspections and reporting. The owner is required to conduct an annual inspection verifying compliance with the Covenant and submit a report to the Department by January 18th of each year.

## Open items

The duration and specific monitoring frequencies for the O&M Plan were not fully finalized in the initial documentation. While annual inspections are standard, DTSC recommends additional inspections after specific events like fires, significant rainfall, seismic events, or traffic accidents.
