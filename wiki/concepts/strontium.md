# Strontium

## Overview
Strontium is identified as one of three primary chemicals of concern (COCs) in groundwater at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. Its presence is linked to historical operations of Barium Products Ltd., which processed celestite (strontium sulfate) at the adjacent FMC property. Soil excavated from the FMC evaporation pond area during SR 99 construction in approximately 1961 was used to create the stockpiles that are the subject of ongoing groundwater monitoring. Strontium is analyzed as a dissolved metal using EPA Method 6020 in semiannual and quarterly groundwater sampling events.

## Site record
In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, strontium was reported in all ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10). Concentrations remained below regulatory thresholds. The June 2014 groundwater monitoring report, prepared by [[geocon-consultants]], Inc. (Project No. S9800-01-17, dated August 2014), noted that strontium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds."

A time-series plot (Figure 6) of strontium concentrations from February 25, 2012, through June 14, 2014, shows all plotted data points falling below the Water Quality Threshold of 4,000 µg/L. Historical groundwater sampling events included in the record are: March, May, June, July, September, and November 2012; January, March, June, September, and December 2013; February 2014; and the June 2014 event. DTSC representatives observed the March 2012 sampling and collected split samples, finding no notable differences in reported concentrations.

Strontium is analyzed as part of the Title 22 dissolved metals suite by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) using EPA Method 6020 under chain-of-custody protocol.

The spatial distribution of strontium is not separately tabulated, but Stiff diagram analysis indicates two groundwater types: calcium-dominant in wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10, and sodium-dominant in wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8. Well MW-5 transitioned from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013, suggesting water type mixing at that location.

## Regulatory status
The applicable numeric water quality threshold for strontium cited in the June 2014 report is the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Notification Level for Drinking Water, set at 4,000 µg/L. No exceedances of this threshold were reported for any monitoring well through the June 2014 sampling event. The report concluded that "none of the reported dissolved metals concentrations for the groundwater samples collected in June 2014 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values." Strontium is not subject to a promulgated Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) under CDPH regulations; the Notification Level serves as the benchmark for regulatory evaluation.

## Open items
Groundwater monitoring continued on a quarterly basis following the June 2014 event, with the next sampling event scheduled for September 2014. No specific open items related to strontium are identified in the provided source material.
