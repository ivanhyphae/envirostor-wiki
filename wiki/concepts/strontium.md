Strontium (Sr) is identified as a chemical of potential concern (COPC) at the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site, primarily due to its presence in soil excavated from the former FMC evaporation pond area. This soil was used to create the stockpiles during the construction of SR 99 around 1961. Strontium has been detected in soil samples from all three stockpiles, as well as in groundwater and stormwater runoff.

## Site record

In the Shaw 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI), strontium was detected in soil samples with a maximum concentration of 231 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). In the Geocon 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI), strontium was detected in surface soil samples from Stockpile 1 with a maximum concentration of 61 mg/kg, from Stockpile 2 with a maximum concentration of 110 mg/kg, and from Stockpile 3 with a maximum concentration of 100 mg/kg. For future construction workers and offsite residents, strontium was detected in soil samples with a maximum concentration of 270 mg/kg.

Strontium has also been detected in groundwater. In the 2012 groundwater samples, strontium was detected in all samples with a maximum concentration of 1,400 µg/l. In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, strontium was reported in all ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) at concentrations below regulatory thresholds. The June 2014 groundwater monitoring report noted that strontium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds."

In stormwater runoff monitoring conducted by Geocon on February 28, 2014, strontium was found to be higher than background samples in all four stormwater samples collected adjacent to the stockpiles.

## Regulatory status

The applicable numeric water quality threshold for strontium cited in the June 2014 report is the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Notification Level for Drinking Water, set at 4,000 µg/L. No exceedances of this threshold were reported for any monitoring well through the June 2014 sampling event. The report concluded that "none of the reported dissolved metals concentrations for the groundwater samples collected in June 2014 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values." Strontium is not subject to a promulgated Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) under CDPH regulations; the Notification Level serves as the benchmark for regulatory evaluation.

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) has approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]], State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. The approved RAP includes containment of the stockpiles during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project.

## Open items

No specific open items related to strontium are identified in the provided source material beyond the ongoing monitoring and maintenance requirements outlined in the approved RAP.
