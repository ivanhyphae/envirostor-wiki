Strontium is one of three primary contaminants of concern (COPCs) at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, alongside [[barium]] and [[lead]]. Unlike barium and lead, strontium was screened out of both the 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) and the 2013 HHRA Update as a human-health COPC because its maximum detected soil concentration fell more than two orders of magnitude below the USEPA residential Regional Screening Level (RSL) of 47,000 mg/kg — yet it was retained throughout as one of the three constituents DTSC required to be tracked in groundwater, and it is the constituent for which the largest gap opened between soil testing scope and the earliest groundwater/HHRA work.

## Source in stockpile soil

Strontium entered the stockpile material because celestite (strontium sulfate) was one of the two principal ores — the other being barite, or barium sulfate — processed at the adjacent FMC (Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation) facility and its predecessors (Barium Products Ltd., 1930s; Westvaco Chlorine Products Corp., 1943; merged into FMC, 1948). Liquid tailings from that processing were discharged to unlined evaporation ponds from the 1950s to 1970s. When the State purchased a 4.3-acre parcel containing part of the ponds in 1961 for SR-99 construction, pond tailings and underlying soil were excavated and placed into what are now Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3.

Strontium was included in the metals suite analyzed in Shaw's 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (194 stockpile and 49 native soil samples from 50 borings), but the maximum reported concentration was 231 mg/kg (later reports also cite 765 mg/kg from the same PSI dataset). It was **not** included in Shaw's 2006 site investigation laboratory analysis — the dataset that became the primary basis for the 2007 HHRA — so the original HHRA could not and did not evaluate strontium as a soil COPC at all; the exclusion in 2007 was a data-availability gap, not a risk-based finding.

Geocon's September 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation closed part of that gap by adding strontium to the analyte list for 35 "Fenceline Borings" placed at the stockpile perimeters. Results by stockpile/area, compared against the HHRA's screening basis (no CHHSL exists for strontium):

| Area | 2012 Maximum Detected Concentration (mg/kg) |
|---|---|
| Stockpile 1 (surface) | 61 |
| Stockpile 2 (surface) | 110 |
| Stockpile 3 (surface) | 100 (detected in all but one sample) |
| Stockpiles 1–3 combined (Fenceline + Cadmium Borings) | 270 (detected in all but one sample) |

All of these remain far below the 47,000 mg/kg RSL used to screen strontium out of concern in both the 2004 PSI and the 2013 HHRA Update, and DTSC's Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) review of the HHRA Update Table 2 likewise concluded that the maximum strontium concentration in stockpile soil was less than its risk-based screening level. No further soil action was directed for strontium specifically; the Final FS and Draft Final RAP's Statement of Reasons list strontium as one of the three primary soil COPCs addressed by the remedy (Alternative 4, in-place containment of ~160,000 cubic yards beneath roadway pavement, retaining walls, and bridge abutments) alongside barium and lead, but the driver for excavation/containment decisions was barium, not strontium.

## Groundwater monitoring

DTSC identified barium, lead, and strontium as the three primary chemicals of concern in groundwater for the site, and Geocon's Title 22 dissolved-metals groundwater analyses (EPA Methods 6020/7470) have included strontium in every monitoring round from 2012 onward, plotted against a stated water quality threshold of 4,000 µg/l on each report's "Strontium Concentrations vs. Time" figure.

Strontium concentrations across all ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10, the latter two installed in June 2012) have consistently stayed in the low hundreds to roughly 1,500 µg/l range — well under the 4,000 µg/l threshold — across every reported round:

| Monitoring event | Strontium range/notes |
|---|---|
| Jan 2013 | Reported in all 10 wells; "similar to historical levels," significantly less than threshold |
| Sept 2012 | Reported in all 10 wells; maximum ~1,400 µg/l per HHRA Update citation of Sept 2012 dataset |
| Nov 2012 | Reported in all 10 wells; consistent with prior rounds |
| Feb 2014 | Reported in all 10 wells; consistent with prior rounds |
| June 2014 | Reported in all 10 wells; consistent with prior rounds |
| Dec 2013 | Reported in all 10 wells; consistent with prior rounds |
| Apr 2015 | Reported in all 4 wells sampled that round; consistent with prior rounds |
| May 2016 | Reported in both wells sampled that round; consistent with prior rounds |

No groundwater report in the record identifies a strontium exceedance of the 4,000 µg/l threshold at any well in any round. The 2007 HHRA's hypothetical shallow-groundwater-user scenario used the maximum detected concentration of strontium from the 2012 dataset (1,400 µg/l, from wells MW-5/MW-6/MW-10) alongside eleven other metals as an update input, and concluded the calculated non-cancer hazard index (0.9) remained below the threshold of 1 — meaning strontium's contribution did not change the HHRA's conclusion that the hypothetical groundwater pathway posed no unacceptable hazard.

## Stormwater and surface water

Strontium was not part of Shaw's original 2006 stormwater sampling and analysis plan, which targeted dissolved metals, PAHs, nitrate, sulfate, and sulfide without flagging strontium as a concern (that round's sole reported exceedance was barium in one sample). Strontium first appears as a stormwater analyte in Geocon's resumed sampling program. The April 7, 2014 report on the February 28, 2014 stormwater event — four locations adjacent to the stockpiles plus two background locations — found that results were "generally consistent with background values" for most analytes, **except** for barium at one location adjacent to Stockpile 2's south side, and for strontium, which was reported higher than background at all four stockpile-adjacent stormwater locations. This is the one point in the record where strontium is identified as elevated relative to a comparison baseline (background) rather than merely below a regulatory numeric threshold; no further characterization or response specific to that strontium finding is described in the available material.

Later stormwater rounds (March 2017, January 2018, March 2018) continued to report strontium among the dissolved metals detected at the site's stormwater/background locations, but the pass/fail determinations in those reports turn on lead and manganese exceedances (e.g., lead at 46 µg/l in sample PL3, March 2018, exceeding the 15 µg/l primary MCL; manganese exceeding its 50 µg/l secondary MCL in several samples) — strontium is listed among the reported analytes without any stated exceedance of an MCL in these rounds.

## Significance

Strontium's role in this file is narrower than barium's or lead's: it was named a primary COPC in groundwater from the outset and has been tracked in every groundwater round without ever approaching its 4,000 µg/l comparison threshold, supporting the recurring conclusion that groundwater beneath the site meets drinking water standards. In soil, strontium was effectively absent from the 2007 HHRA's evaluation not because it was assessed and cleared, but because the 2006 dataset underlying that HHRA never analyzed for it; the 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation and the 2013 HHRA Update retroactively confirmed maximum concentrations far below the RSL, closing that gap without altering the site's risk conclusions. The one unresolved thread is the February 2014 stormwater finding of above-background strontium at all four stockpile-adjacent sampling locations — a finding the record reports but does not trace to any further investigation or remedial response.
