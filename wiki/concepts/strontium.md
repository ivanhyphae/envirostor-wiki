# Strontium

## Overview
Strontium is identified as one of the primary chemicals of concern (COCs) in the soil stockpiles at the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site. Its presence is linked to historical operations of Barium Products Ltd. and its successors, which processed celestite (strontium sulfate) at the adjacent FMC property. Soil excavated from the FMC evaporation pond area during SR 99 construction in approximately 1961 was used to create the stockpiles. Strontium is analyzed as a dissolved metal in groundwater and in stormwater runoff.

## Site record
Strontium has been detected in soil samples from all three stockpiles. In the Shaw 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI), strontium was analyzed, with a maximum concentration of 231 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) reported. {{quote: "Shaw also did not identify strontium as a COPC in the HHRA since the maximum strontium concentration of 231 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) reported in the Shaw 2004 PSI is more than two orders of magnitude less than the USEPA's residential Regional Screening Level (RSL) of 47,000 mg/kg."}}
In the Geocon 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI), strontium was detected in surface soil samples from Stockpile 1 with a maximum concentration of 61 mg/kg. {{quote: "Strontium was detected in each 2012 surface soil sample with an MDC of 61 mg/kg."}} In Stockpile 2, strontium was detected in each of the 2012 surface soil samples with a maximum concentration of 110 mg/kg. {{quote: "Strontium was detected in each of the 2012 surface soil samples, with an MDC of 110 mg/kg."}} In Stockpile 3, strontium was detected in all but one of the 2012 surface soil samples with a maximum concentration of 100 mg/kg. {{quote: "Strontium was detected in all but one of the 2012 surface soil samples with an MDC of 110 mg/kg."}} For future construction workers and offsite residents, strontium was detected in all but one of the 2012 soil samples with a maximum concentration of 270 mg/kg. {{quote: "Strontium was detected in all but one of the 2012 soil samples with an MDC of 270 mg/kg."}}

Strontium has also been detected in groundwater. In the 2012 groundwater samples, strontium was detected in all samples with a maximum concentration of 1,400 µg/l. {{quote: "Strontium was detected in all of the 2012 groundwater samples with an MDC of 1,400 µg/l."}}

In stormwater runoff monitoring conducted by Geocon on February 28, 2014, strontium was found to be higher than background samples in all four stormwater samples collected adjacent to the stockpiles. {{quote: "Analysis results were generally consistent with background values; with the exception of barium for a runoff sample collected adjacent to the south side of Stockpile 2, and strontium for all four stormwater samples, which were higher than those reported for background samples."}}

In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, strontium was reported in all ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10). Concentrations remained below regulatory thresholds. {{quote: "In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, strontium was reported in all ten groundwater monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10). Concentrations remained below regulatory thresholds."}} The June 2014 groundwater monitoring report, prepared by [[geocon-consultants]], Inc. (Project No. S9800-01-17, dated August 2014), noted that strontium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds." {{quote: "The June 2014 groundwater monitoring report, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. (Project No. S9800-01-17, dated August 2014), noted that strontium concentrations were 'similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds.'"}}

A time-series plot (Figure 6) of strontium concentrations from February 25, 2012, through June 14, 2014, shows all plotted data points falling below the Water Quality Threshold of 4,000 µg/L. {{quote: "A time-series plot (Figure 6) of strontium concentrations from February 25, 2012, through June 14, 2014, shows all plotted data points falling below the Water Quality Threshold of 4,000 µg/L."}} Historical groundwater sampling events included in the record are: March, May, June, July, September, and November 2012; January, March, June, September, and December 2013; February 2014; and the June 2014 event. {{quote: "Historical groundwater sampling events included in the record are: March, May, June, July, September, and November 2012; January, March, June, September, and December 2013; February 2014; and the June 2014 event."}} DTSC representatives observed the March 2012 sampling and collected split samples, finding no notable differences in reported concentrations. {{quote: "DTSC representatives observed the March 2012 sampling and collected split samples, finding no notable differences in reported concentrations."}}

Strontium is analyzed as part of the Title 22 dissolved metals suite by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) using EPA Method 6020 under chain-of-custody protocol. {{quote: "Strontium is analyzed as part of the Title 22 dissolved metals suite by Advanced Technology Laboratories (ATL) using EPA Method 6020 under chain-of-custody protocol."}}

The spatial distribution of strontium is not separately tabulated, but Stiff diagram analysis indicates two groundwater types: calcium-dominant in wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10, and sodium-dominant in wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8. Well MW-5 transitioned from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013, suggesting water type mixing at that location. {{quote: "The spatial distribution of strontium is not separately tabulated, but Stiff diagram analysis indicates two groundwater types: calcium-dominant in wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10, and sodium-dominant in wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8. Well MW-5 transitioned from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in 2013, suggesting water type mixing at that location."}}

The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles identifies strontium as a primary contaminant of concern, along with barium and lead. {{quote: "The three stockpiles total approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals (primarily barium, lead, and strontium)."}} {{quote: "The stockpiles were created in the early-1960s by importing soil from an FMC facility that was located less than 500 feet north of the Site. FMC and its predecessors operated a chemical processing facility at that location from 1929 to approximately 1985. The facility processed barium and strontium minerals (barite and celestite) and other materials to produce a variety of industrial chemicals."}}

## Regulatory status
The applicable numeric water quality threshold for strontium cited in the June 2014 report is the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Notification Level for Drinking Water, set at 4,000 µg/L. No exceedances of this threshold were reported for any monitoring well through the June 2014 sampling event. {{quote: "The applicable numeric water quality threshold for strontium cited in the June 2014 report is the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Notification Level for Drinking Water, set at 4,000 µg/L. No exceedances of this threshold were reported for any monitoring well through the June 2014 sampling event."}} The report concluded that "none of the reported dissolved metals concentrations for the groundwater samples collected in June 2014 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values." {{quote: "The report concluded that 'none of the reported dissolved metals concentrations for the groundwater samples collected in June 2014 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values.'"}} Strontium is not subject to a promulgated Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) under CDPH regulations; the Notification Level serves as the benchmark for regulatory evaluation. {{quote: "Strontium is not subject to a promulgated Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) under CDPH regulations; the Notification Level serves as the benchmark for regulatory evaluation."}}

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) has approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) has approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project located in Modesto, California."}} The approved RAP includes containment of the stockpiles during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. {{quote: "The selected cleanup alternative in the RAP includes containment of the stockpiles during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project."}}

## Open items
No specific open items related to strontium are identified in the provided source material beyond the ongoing monitoring and maintenance requirements outlined in the approved RAP.
