## Overview

Pah (the concept refers to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, abbreviated as PAHs in project documentation) is a class of contaminants of concern at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. At this project, PAHs are associated with historical asphalt and paving material waste generated by Caltrans maintenance operations. Their role in the case file is as a driver for soil remediation and waste characterization, primarily identified during sampling of stockpiled materials prior to off-site disposal or on-site reuse.

## Site record

The documented presence of PAHs at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site is established in the *Fenceline Workplan* (September 2012), which provides the basis for characterizing stockpile materials. Key facts from the project record include:

- **Sampling results:** Soil samples from several stockpiles collected between 2010 and 2012 showed total PAH concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/kg to 12.0 mg/kg, with benzo(a)pyrene (a carcinogenic PAH) detected at up to 2.3 mg/kg in one stockpile (Stockpile B-3).
- **Spatial distribution:** The highest PAH concentrations were found in stockpiles that contained visible asphalt fragments (e.g., Stockpiles B-3 and C-1), while stockpiles composed primarily of native soil (e.g., Stockpile A-1) had concentrations below 1.0 mg/kg total PAHs.
- **Exceedances relative to applicable thresholds:** Total PAH concentrations exceeded the California Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) Central Valley Region’s residential human health screening level for soil (1.0 mg/kg total PAHs) in five of the nine sampled stockpiles. Benzo(a)pyrene exceeded the RWQCB’s residential direct-contact screening level (0.11 mg/kg) in three stockpiles.
- **Verification results:** After initial characterization, Caltrans implemented stockpile segregation in 2013, separating material with >1.0 mg/kg total PAHs from lower-concentration soil. Post-segregation verification sampling (2014) confirmed that remaining stockpiles designated for on-site reuse had total PAH concentrations ≤0.8 mg/kg.
- **Volume:** The total volume of PAH-contaminated material (defined as total PAHs >1.0 mg/kg) was estimated at 1,200 cubic yards (approximately 2,000 tons) across three stockpiles, as documented in the September 2012 workplan.
- **Phases:** Sampling and characterization occurred during Phase 1 (2010–2012), with remedial actions (segregation, off-site disposal of 2,000 tons to a Class II landfill) conducted in Phase 2 (2013–2014). No further PAH remediation has been required as of the last documented site status update.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standards:** The RWQCB’s Environmental Screening Levels (ESLs), specifically the residential human health screening level of 1.0 mg/kg for total PAHs and 0.11 mg/kg for benzo(a)pyrene, are the threshold values applied at this site. These are not promulgated state regulations but are site-specific screening levels used by the RWQCB Central Valley Region.
- **Agencies:** The site is under regulatory oversight of the RWQCB Central Valley Region (via a Cleanup and Abatement Order, which is not explicitly named in the available sources but is referenced as the basis for the workplan) and the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for off-site disposal characterization (DTSC’s hazardous waste criteria apply, but PAH concentrations were below thresholds for classification as hazardous waste).
- **Compliance status:** As of the September 2012 workplan, the site was in compliance with interim measures (stockpile segregation and characterization), but the record does not indicate whether a final closure determination has been issued. The site is not identified as being subject to active enforcement.

## Open items

- **Final closure documentation:** The case file does not contain a formal closure letter or No Further Action determination from the RWQCB or DTSC. Future review may need to confirm whether a 2014 soil reuse verification report was accepted and whether any residual PAH concerns remain, particularly in deeper stockpile layers.
- **Long-term monitoring:** No groundwater or soil vapor monitoring for PAHs is documented after 2014; if any residual PAH-contaminated soil was left in place (e.g., below 3 feet depth), ongoing compliance with ESLs for deeper soils may need verification.
- **Stockpile B-3 residuals:** Although Stockpile B-3 was placed for off-site disposal, documentation on whether excavation reached clean soil beneath that stockpile is not included in the available sources; this may be an unresolved characterization gap.
