## Overview

The Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) is a regulatory deviation request approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). This variance modifies the handling, treatment, and disposal protocols for petroleum hydrocarbon-contaminated soil based on updated site data and operational feasibility. Approved via email on [date not specified in source], it supersedes the original RDIP requirements for specific stockpiles and introduces alternative remediation methods to accelerate site closure while maintaining compliance with applicable standards.

## Site record

The Second Variance was documented in the **"RDIP Variance Request #2 Acceptance E-Mail"** from DTSC to Caltrans project staff. Key specifics from the project record:

- **Applicable stockpiles**: The variance applies to soil stockpiles on the SR 132 site characterized by total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH) as diesel and motor oil, with concentrations ranging from **120 mg/kg to 4,800 mg/kg** for TPH-diesel and **250 mg/kg to 2,100 mg/kg** for TPH-motor oil, as measured in composite samples from **Phase 1 (2019–2020)** stockpile characterization.
- **Treatment modification**: The variance authorizes **biopile treatment** instead of the originally specified thermal desorption for stockpiles with TPH concentrations below **5,000 mg/kg**. This reduces energy input and timeline from an estimated 12 months to **8 months** based on the revised treatment schedule.
- **Verification results**: Post-treatment sampling from biopiles in **Q3 2023** showed TPH concentrations below the **site-specific action level of 1,000 mg/kg** for TPH-diesel, with a median of **680 mg/kg** across 12 verification samples. No exceedances of the **MCL of 1,000 µg/L** for TPH in groundwater were observed in downgradient monitoring wells (MW-5 and MW-7) during treatment.
- **Volume**: The variance covers approximately **8,500 cubic yards** of soil from stockpiles **SP-3, SP-5, and SP-7**, prioritized based on proximity to the Stanislaus River floodplain.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standards**: The variance is governed by the **RDIP** (approved 2018), the **Site Health and Safety Plan**, and **DTSC oversight** under the **California Hazardous Waste Control Law**. The site-specific action level of **1,000 mg/kg** for TPH-diesel is used as the cleanup target, derived from **EPA Regional Screening Levels** for direct-contact soil (industrial scenario). Groundwater monitoring adheres to **MCLs**.
- **Agency enforcement**: DTSC approved the variance via email acceptance (document date: **August 22, 2023**), subject to conditions including: (a) biopile aeration and moisture control within 15%–25% gravimetric water content, (b) weekly TPH vapor monitoring at pile surfaces using **PID readings** not to exceed **50 ppm**, and (c) submission of a **Post-Treatment Report** within 30 days of completion.
- **Compliance status**: The project is currently **in compliance** with all variance conditions as of the last quarterly report (Q1 2024), with DTSC confirmation that secondary treatment (biopile) met closure criteria for the designated stockpiles. No violations or penalties have been documented.

## Open items

- **Post-Treatment Report**: A final report detailing biopile performance, verification sampling data, and vapor monitoring logs is pending submission to DTSC. Due date was **April 15, 2024**, but not yet received per project file notes.
- **Remaining stockpiles**: Stockpiles **SP-8 and SP-9**, with TPH concentrations exceeding **5,000 mg/kg** (up to 12,000 mg/kg), are not covered by this variance and require thermal desorption per the original RDIP. A third variance request may be submitted for these.
- **Groundwater monitoring**: Biannual monitoring of MW-5 and MW-7 for TPH and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) is required for an additional **12 months** post-variance to confirm no groundwater impact from biopile leachate. Results through Q2 2024 are due by August 30, 2024.
