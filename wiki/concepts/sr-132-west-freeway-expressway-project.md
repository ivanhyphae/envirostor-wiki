## Overview
The [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]] is a Caltrans transportation improvement project in Modesto, California, involving the construction of a four-lane freeway/expressway. A significant component of this project is the remediation and containment of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. These stockpiles contain metals, primarily [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]]. The remediation plan, known as the [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] (RAP), was developed under the oversight of the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. The RAP was approved by the DTSC on February 21, 2018.

## Site Record

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] are located within state right-of-way south of Kansas Avenue, occupying three areas: between Carpenter Avenue and Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99. The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for these stockpiles was approved by the DTSC on February 21, 2018.

The RAP's selected cleanup alternative involves the containment of the stockpiles during the construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. This containment will be achieved by placing the stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavements. Unpaved portions will be covered with clean fill.

Specific excavations for structures within the southern portion of the Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone have generated [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) spoils. For the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor (approximate Station 1138+60), approximately 4.5 feet deep excavations in July/August 2021 generated about 2 cubic yards of BCS material, which was spread on the adjacent ground surface. A planned excavation for Overhead Sign #1 (approximate Station 1139+30) is expected to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of BCS material. The total estimated BCS material from these [[bcs-foundation-excavations]] is 40 cubic yards.

Eight discrete soil samples are to be collected from these BCS stockpiles for analysis. The samples will be composited into two 4-part composite samples and analyzed for Title 22 metals using EPA Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury). Soluble metals testing may be required if total metal concentrations are elevated.

The total [[barium]] and [[lead]] concentrations will be compared to site-specific background levels: Barium - 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and Lead - 3.8 mg/kg. If these concentrations do not exceed background levels, the BCS material may be suitable for on-site reuse with approval from Caltrans and DTSC.

## Regulatory Status

The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on February 21, 2018. The DTSC determined that the selected containment remedy will not have a significant effect on the environment with the implementation of project controls (mitigation measures). Land use controls and annual inspections of the containment features, along with groundwater monitoring, will be conducted to ensure the long-term integrity of the remedy.

The Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and Draft Final RAP were circulated for public review from January 18, 2017 through March 17, 2017.
