The SR 132 West Freeway Expressway Project is a Caltrans transportation improvement project in Modesto, California, that involves the construction of a four-lane freeway/expressway. A key component of this project is the remediation and containment of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]], which contain metals, primarily [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]]. The remediation plan, known as the [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] (RAP), was developed under the oversight of the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB). The RAP was approved by the DTSC on February 21, 2018.

## Site Record

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] are located within state right-of-way south of Kansas Avenue, occupying three areas: between Carpenter Avenue and Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99. These stockpiles total approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, primarily [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]]. {{quote: "The three stockpiles total approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals (primarily barium, lead, and strontium)."}} The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for these stockpiles was approved by the DTSC on February 21, 2018. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) has approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project located in Modesto, California."}}

The RAP's selected cleanup alternative involves the containment of the stockpiles during the construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. This containment will be achieved by placing the stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavements. Unpaved portions will be covered with clean fill. {{quote: "The selected cleanup alternative in the RAP includes containment of the stockpiles during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. The stockpiles will be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway. Unpaved portions will have clean fill cover."}}

Specific excavations for structures within the southern portion of the Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone have generated [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) spoils. For the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor (approximate Station 1138+60), approximately 4.5 feet deep excavations in July/August 2021 generated about 2 cubic yards of BCS material, which was spread on the adjacent ground surface. {{quote: "Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor (approximate Station 1138+60): 4.5 foot-deep foundation excavations completed in July/August 2021 generating approximately 2 cubic yards of BCS material that was spread on the adjacent ground surface"}} A planned excavation for Overhead Sign #1 (approximate Station 1139+30) is expected to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of BCS material. {{quote: "Overhead Sign #1 (approximate Station 1139+30): planned 5-foot-diameter by 25 foot-deep foundation excavation that will generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of BCS material."}} The total estimated BCS material from these [[bcs-foundation-excavations]] is 40 cubic yards. {{quote: "The excavated BCS material generated from the weather station and overhead sign foundations (conservative estimate of 40 cubic yards) will be stockpiled prior to offsite landfill disposal or onsite reuse."}}

Eight discrete soil samples are to be collected from these BCS stockpiles for analysis. The samples will be composited into two 4-part composite samples and analyzed for Title 22 metals using EPA Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury). Soluble metals testing may be required if total metal concentrations are elevated. {{quote: "Eight discrete soil samples will be collected from the stockpile on a grid pattern at various depths. The samples will be collected in labelled stainless steel sample tubes, placed in a chilled cooler and transported to California Laboratory Services under chain-of-custody protocol. The laboratory will be instructed to composite the eight discrete soil samples into two 4-part composite samples. The composite soil samples will be analyzed for Title 22 metals following Environmental Protection Agency Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury) under expedited 48-hour turn-around time. Soluble metals testing using the Waste Extraction Test and Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure may be required for samples with elevated total metal concentrations to determine if the BCS material is a California or Federal Resource Conservation and Recovery Act hazardous waste."}}

The total [[barium]] and [[lead]] concentrations will be compared to site-specific background levels: Barium - 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and Lead - 3.8 mg/kg. {{quote: "The total barium and lead concentrations will be compared to the following site-specific background levels: Barium - 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) Lead - 3.8 mg/kg"}} If these concentrations do not exceed background levels, the BCS material may be suitable for on-site reuse with approval from Caltrans and DTSC. {{quote: "In the event the total barium and lead analytical data does not exceed respective site-specific background concentrations, the stockpiled BCS may be suitable for onsite reuse provided Caltrans and DTCS approval of the proposed reuse location."}}

## Regulatory Status

The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on February 21, 2018. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) has approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project located in Modesto, California."}} The DTSC determined that the selected containment remedy will not have a significant effect on the environment with the implementation of project controls (mitigation measures). {{quote: "DTSC has completed an independent analysis and determined that the selected containment remedy will not have a significant effect to the environment with implementation of the project controls (mitigations measures)."}} Land use controls and annual inspections of the containment features, along with groundwater monitoring, will be conducted to ensure the long-term integrity of the remedy. {{quote: "Land use controls and inspection of the pavement and other features of the containment remedy and groundwater monitoring will be conducted annually to ensure the integrity of the remedy into the future."}}

The Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and Draft Final RAP were circulated for public review from January 18, 2017, through March 17, 2017. {{quote: "The Draft EIR/EA with attached Draft Final RAP was circulated for public review from January 18, 2017 through March 17, 2017."}}
