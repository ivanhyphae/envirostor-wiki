# Human Health Risk Assessment

## Overview

The Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site was conducted to evaluate potential chronic health risks and hazards to human receptors from contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in soil and groundwater. The assessment was performed in accordance with Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) guidance. The HHRA concluded that the stockpiles, as managed, do not pose an unacceptable cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

## Site record

### 2007 HHRA (Shaw Environmental, Inc.)

Shaw Environmental, Inc. prepared the baseline HHRA dated May 14, 2007, using soil and groundwater data from the 2006 Site Investigation and groundwater monitoring events. This HHRA is included as Appendix A of the Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) (Shaw, June 30, 2009).

*   **Contaminants of Potential Concern (COPCs):** Metals, notably [[barium]], and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were identified as primary COPCs in soil stockpiles. Metals and general minerals (e.g., nitrate, total dissolved solids) were primary COPCs in groundwater. Cadmium was excluded as a COPC due to a lack of elevated detections. Strontium was excluded because its maximum reported concentration (231 mg/kg) was more than two orders of magnitude below the USEPA's residential [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] of 47,000 mg/kg.
*   **Exposure Scenarios Evaluated:**
    *   Current offsite resident/trespasser (combined as a conservative residential exposure scenario).
    *   Future construction worker.
    *   Future offsite resident (during construction).
    *   Hypothetical future shallow groundwater user (using residential exposure assumptions).
*   **Exposure Pathways:** Incidental ingestion, dermal contact, and inhalation of dust for soil. For groundwater, ingestion and dermal contact. Surface water was not evaluated due to infrequent rain events and lack of significant exposure potential.
*   **Exposure-Point Concentrations (EPCs):** Maximum detected concentrations (MDCs) were used for COPCs in each exposure scenario, except for Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment, which used 95th percentile upper confidence limits (UCLs).
*   **Key Risk Characterization Findings (2007 HHRA):**
    *   **Stockpile 1 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** Cancer risk of 8E-8 (below 1E-6 criterion); non-cancer Hazard Index (HI) of 0.04 (below threshold of 1).
    *   **Stockpile 2 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** Revised cancer risk of 1E-7 (after excluding background arsenic risk); non-cancer HI of 0.1.
    *   **Stockpile 3 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** No excess cancer risk calculated; non-cancer HI of 0.02.
    *   **Construction Worker (Stockpiles 1–3 combined, 0–20 ft bgs):** Cancer risk of 9.2E-7; non-cancer HI of 0.4.
    *   **Future Offsite Resident (during construction):** Cancer risk of 6E-10; non-cancer HI of 0.017.
    *   **Hypothetical Shallow Groundwater User:** Non-cancer HI of 0.9. No cancer risk calculated.

### 2013 HHRA Update (Geocon Consultants, Inc.)

Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared the HHRA Update, revised March 1, 2013, under Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order No. 44. This update incorporated soil analytical data from the September 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) and groundwater data from bi-monthly monitoring events between March 2012 and March 2013. The update compared 2012 EPCs against the 2007 HHRA EPCs to determine if earlier risk and hazard estimates remained valid.

*   **Comparison Methodology:** 2012 EPCs were compared to newly collected fenceline, perimeter, and cadmium boring samples. If 2012 concentrations were within the same order of magnitude and did not exceed residential CHHSLs or RSLs, the 2007 HHRA conclusions were deemed valid.
*   **Stockpile 1 — Current Exposure (2012 vs. 2007 EPCs):** Barium MDC of 240 mg/kg (vs. 130 mg/kg in 2007); copper MDC of 24 mg/kg (vs. 13); lead MDC of 17 mg/kg (vs. 12); zinc MDC of 120 mg/kg detected, exceeding background MDC of 44 mg/kg. The 2007 risk and hazard calculations (cancer risk 8E-8, HI 0.04) remained valid.
*   **Stockpile 2 — Current Exposure (2012 vs. 2007):** Barium MDC of 4,300 mg/kg (vs. 1,100 mg/kg), copper MDC of 41 mg/kg (vs. 29), zinc MDC of 200 mg/kg (vs. 89). The 2007 risk and hazard calculations (cancer risk 1E-7, HI 0.1) remained valid.
*   **Stockpile 3 — Current Exposure (2012 vs. 2007):** Barium MDC of 1,600 mg/kg (vs. 250), lead MDC of 34 mg/kg (vs. 12). The 2007 non-cancer HI of 0.02 remained valid.
*   **Construction Worker and Future Offsite Resident (2012 vs. 2007):** Barium MDC of 130,000 mg/kg (vs. 72,000 mg/kg), but the 95% UCL for 2012 barium data was 7,556 mg/kg. The 2007 risk and hazard calculations (cancer risk 9.2E-7 and 0.4 for construction worker; 6E-10 and 0.017 for future offsite resident) remained valid.
*   **Onsite Shallow Groundwater (2012 vs. 2007):** Slightly higher concentrations of cobalt (5.3 vs. 3.0 μg/L), copper (7.4 vs. 6.2), manganese (290 vs. 260), nickel (9.6 vs. 7.1), selenium (4.4 vs. 3.0), vanadium (42 vs. 34), and zinc (120 vs. 15), primarily from upgradient well MW-10. The 2007 non-cancer HI of 0.9 remained valid.
*   **Update Summary:** The 2007 HHRA remained valid with respect to exposure potential for all receptor scenarios.

### Cadmium Data Resolution

The 2012 SSI addressed earlier cadmium detections from the Shaw 2004 PSI, which had reported concentrations exceeding the [[commercial-industrial-screening-level]] (7.5 mg/kg) in Stockpiles 2 and 3. In 348 soil samples analyzed during the Shaw 2006 SI and Geocon 2012 SSI, cadmium was not detected above the laboratory reporting limit of 1.0 mg/kg. The 2004 PSI cadmium data were determined to be neither reproducible nor reliable and are considered false positives, possibly due to sample interference or dilution effects from high [[barium]] concentrations. Cadmium is therefore not a COPC for the Site.

## Regulatory status

*   **DTSC Concurrence — December 17, 2009:** Following the Final PEA, DTSC determined that the soil stockpiles, as currently managed by Caltrans on Caltrans property, do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. DTSC required continued management practices: limiting access, maintaining fencing, prohibiting excavation/grading/offsite soil removal, and maintaining vegetative cover and groundwater monitoring.
*   **DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) Memorandum — February 14, 2013:** HERO concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to persons in the vicinity as long as the stockpiles remain in place and are properly managed. HERO cautioned that areas with elevated concentrations at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would require evaluation during grading, reworking, or removal activities.
*   **DTSC Concurrence — April 4, 2013:** DTSC concurred with the findings of the HHRA Update and the March 1, 2013, SSI report.
*   **Cleanup Goals (Performance-Based):** The [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] established that a numerical cleanup goal was not necessary to protect human health. Instead, a performance-based cleanup goal was adopted: ensuring no route of exposure to COPCs in the stockpiles and reducing the potential threat to groundwater.
*   **Remedial Action Objective:** Protect the health of neighboring residents, onsite trespassers, and Caltrans-authorized personnel and prevent future impact to groundwater by managing the stockpiles either in-place or by removing them from the Site.

## Open items

The HERO memorandum of February 14, 2013, identifies a conditional future obligation: "if there is substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles or if the stockpiles are removed, these elevated concentrations at depth will have to be evaluated with respect to the potential for exposure by residents living adjacent or near the stockpiles during the period when the soil is being moved." This evaluation has not yet been documented as completed in the available sources.
