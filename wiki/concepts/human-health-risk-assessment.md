# Human Health Risk Assessment

## Overview

The Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site was originally prepared by Shaw Environmental, Inc. in 2007 and subsequently updated by Geocon Consultants, Inc. in 2013. The assessment evaluated potential chronic health risks and hazards to human receptors from contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in soil and groundwater associated with three stockpiles located adjacent to SR 99 and Kansas Avenue in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The HHRA was conducted in accordance with Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) guidance. The assessment concluded that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not pose an unacceptable cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

## Site record

### 2007 HHRA (Shaw Environmental, Inc.)

Shaw prepared the baseline HHRA dated May 14, 2007, using soil and groundwater data from the 2006 Site Investigation and groundwater monitoring events. The HHRA is included as Appendix A of the Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) (Shaw, June 30, 2009).

**Contaminants of Potential Concern:** Metals — notably barium — and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were identified as the primary COPCs in the soil stockpiles. Metals and general minerals (e.g., nitrate, total dissolved solids) were the primary COPCs in groundwater. Cadmium was excluded as a COPC due to a lack of elevated detections during the 2006 SI. Strontium was excluded because the maximum reported concentration of 231 mg/kg (from the Shaw 2004 PSI) was more than two orders of magnitude below USEPA's residential Regional Screening Level (RSL) of 47,000 mg/kg.

**Exposure Scenarios Evaluated:** Three receptor groups — a current offsite resident/trespasser (combined as a conservative residential exposure scenario), a future construction worker, and a future offsite resident (during construction). A hypothetical future shallow groundwater user was also evaluated using residential exposure assumptions, though no "current" groundwater exposure scenario was considered since shallow groundwater is not used as a drinking water resource.

**Exposure Pathways:** Incidental ingestion, dermal contact, and inhalation of dust for soil. For groundwater, ingestion and dermal contact. Surface water was not evaluated as an exposure pathway due to infrequent rain events and the lack of surface water bodies or significant exposure potential.

**Exposure-Point Concentrations:** The HHRA conservatively used maximum detected concentrations (MDCs) for COPCs in each exposure scenario, except for the Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment, which used 95th percentile upper confidence limits (UCLs).

**Key Risk Characterization Findings (2007 HHRA):**

- **Stockpile 1 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** Cancer risk of 8E-8 (well below the 1E-6 criterion); non-cancer Hazard Index (HI) of 0.04 (well below the threshold of 1). Lead exposure modeled using LeadSpread did not indicate blood lead concentrations exceeding 10 μg/dL at the 95th or 99th percentile.

- **Stockpile 2 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** Total cancer risk initially estimated at 1E-5, driven by arsenic in surface soil. The arsenic cancer risk of 1.45E-5 (based on the 95th percentile UCL of 1.63 mg/kg) was removed from the total because background arsenic (95th percentile UCL of 1.15 mg/kg) produced a nearly identical risk of 1.15E-5. With arsenic excluded, the revised cancer risk was 1E-7. Non-cancer HI was 0.1.

- **Stockpile 3 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** No COPCs were considered carcinogens; therefore, no cancer risk was calculated. Non-cancer HI was 0.02.

- **Construction Worker (Stockpiles 1–3 combined, 0–20 ft bgs):** Cancer risk of 9.2E-7; non-cancer HI of 0.4.

- **Future Offsite Resident (during construction):** Cancer risk of 6E-10; non-cancer HI of 0.017.

- **Hypothetical Shallow Groundwater User:** Non-cancer HI of 0.9. No COPCs were considered carcinogens; no cancer risk was calculated.

### 2013 HHRA Update (Geocon Consultants, Inc.)

Geocon prepared the HHRA Update, revised March 1, 2013, under Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order No. 44. The update incorporated soil analytical data from the September 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) and groundwater data from bi-monthly monitoring events conducted between March 2012 and March 2013. The document compared 2012 EPCs against the 2007 HHRA EPCs and evaluated whether the earlier risk and hazard estimates remained valid.

**Comparison Methodology:** The EPCs from the 2007 HHRA were compared to newly collected fenceline, perimeter, and cadmium boring samples. Where 2012 concentrations were within the same order of magnitude and none exceeded residential CHHSLs or RSLs, the 2007 HHRA conclusions were deemed valid.

**Stockpile 1 — Current Exposure (2012 vs. 2007 EPCs):** Barium MDC of 240 mg/kg (vs. 130 mg/kg in 2007); copper MDC of 24 mg/kg (vs. 13); lead MDC of 17 mg/kg (vs. 12); zinc MDC of 120 mg/kg detected, exceeding the background MDC of 44 mg/kg. The 2007 risk and hazard calculations (cancer risk 8E-8, HI 0.04) remained valid.

**Stockpile 2 — Current Exposure (2012 vs. 2007):** Barium MDC of 4,300 mg/kg (vs. 1,100 mg/kg), copper MDC of 41 mg/kg (vs. 29), zinc MDC of 200 mg/kg (vs. 89). The 2007 risk and hazard calculations (cancer risk 1E-7, HI 0.1) remained valid.

**Stockpile 3 — Current Exposure (2012 vs. 2007):** Barium MDC of 1,600 mg/kg (vs. 250), lead MDC of 34 mg/kg (vs. 12). The 2007 non-cancer HI of 0.02 remained valid.

**Construction Worker and Future Offsite Resident (2012 vs. 2007):** Barium MDC of 130,000 mg/kg (vs. 72,000 mg/kg), but the 95% UCL for the 2012 barium data was 7,556 mg/kg — significantly less than the MDC and the 2007 EPC. The 2007 risk and hazard calculations (cancer risk 9.2E-7 and 0.4 for construction worker; 6E-10 and 0.017 for future offsite resident) remained valid.

**Onsite Shallow Groundwater (2012 vs. 2007):** Slightly higher concentrations of cobalt (5.3 vs. 3.0 μg/L), copper (7.4 vs. 6.2), manganese (290 vs. 260), nickel (9.6 vs. 7.1), selenium (4.4 vs. 3.0), vanadium (42 vs. 34), and zinc (120 vs. 15), primarily from upgradient well MW-10. The 2007 non-cancer HI of 0.9 remained valid.

**Update Summary:** The 2007 HHRA remained valid with respect to exposure potential for all receptor scenarios.

### Cadmium Data Resolution

The 2012 SSI specifically addressed earlier cadmium detections from the Shaw 2004 PSI, which had reported concentrations exceeding the industrial CHHSL (7.5 mg/kg) in Stockpiles 2 and 3. In 348 soil samples analyzed during the Shaw 2006 SI and Geocon 2012 SSI, cadmium was not detected above the laboratory reporting limit of 1.0 mg/kg, including in 19 samples with barium concentrations between 25,000 and 130,000 mg/kg. The 2004 PSI cadmium data were determined to be neither reproducible nor reliable and are considered false positives, possibly due to sample interference or dilution effects from high barium concentrations. Cadmium is therefore not a COPC for the Site.

## Regulatory status

**DTSC Concurrence — December 17, 2009:** Following the Final PEA, DTSC determined that the soil stockpiles, as currently managed by Caltrans on Caltrans property, do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers conducting routine activities, trespassers, or adjacent residents. DTSC required continued management practices: limiting access to Caltrans-authorized personnel, maintaining fencing, prohibiting excavation/grading/offsite soil removal, and maintaining vegetative cover and groundwater monitoring.

**DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) Memorandum — February 14, 2013:** HERO concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to persons in the vicinity as long as the stockpiles remain in place and are properly managed. HERO cautioned that areas with elevated concentrations at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would require evaluation during grading, reworking, or removal activities with respect to potential exposure to adjacent residents.

**DTSC Concurrence — April 4, 2013:** DTSC concurred with the findings of the HHRA Update and the March 1, 2013, SSI report.

**Cleanup Goals (Performance-Based):** The RAP established that, based on HHRA and HHRA Update findings, a numerical cleanup goal was not necessary to be protective of human health. Instead, a performance-based cleanup goal was adopted: ensuring no route of exposure to COPCs in the stockpiles and reducing the potential threat to groundwater.

**Remedial Action Objective:** Protect the health of neighboring residents, onsite trespassers, and Caltrans-authorized personnel and prevent future impact to groundwater by managing the stockpiles either in-place or by removing them from the Site.

## Open items

While the HHRA and HHRA Update evaluations are complete and regulatory concurrence has been obtained, the HERO memorandum of February 14, 2013, identifies a conditional future obligation: "if there is substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles or if the stockpiles are removed, these elevated concentrations at depth will have to be evaluated with respect to the potential for exposure by residents living adjacent or near the stockpiles during the period when the soil is being moved." This evaluation has not yet been documented as completed in the available sources.
