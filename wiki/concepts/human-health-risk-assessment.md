## Overview

The Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) is a critical component of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case file. It evaluates potential risks to human health from exposure to contaminants in the soil stockpiles and associated groundwater. The HHRA process, involving multiple updates and reviews by agencies like the [[dtsc]] and [[central-valley-rwqcb]], aims to determine if current or future land uses pose unacceptable health risks. The HHRA has been conducted by Shaw Environmental, Inc. and updated by [[geocon-consultants-inc]].

## Site Record

*   **2007 HHRA by Shaw Environmental, Inc.:** Evaluated three stockpiles (SP#1, SP#2, SP#3) and groundwater for exposure to chemicals of potential concern (COPCs), including metals (notably [[barium]]) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). The HHRA concluded that COPCs in soil and groundwater did not pose unacceptable risks to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, or construction workers. Cadmium was not identified as a COPC in this assessment due to a lack of elevated concentrations in the 2006 site investigation. Strontium was also excluded as a COPC because its maximum concentration was significantly below the EPA residential Regional Screening Level (RSL).
*   **2009 Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) by Shaw:** Prepared in response to DTSC comments, summarizing previous findings and providing additional information.
*   **DTSC Letter (December 17, 2009):** DTSC found that the soil stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans, did not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. Caltrans was directed to continue managing the stockpiles by limiting access, maintaining the fence, prohibiting excavation/grading/removal/addition of soil, and maintaining the current grade and vegetative cover, as well as the groundwater monitoring system.
*   **2012-2013 HHRA Update by Geocon Consultants, Inc.:** Incorporated new soil and groundwater data collected in 2012. This update compared exposure point concentrations (EPCs) from the 2007 HHRA with the new data.
    *   **Stockpile 1 Current Exposure Assessment:** Barium, copper, and lead showed slightly higher concentrations in 2012 surface soil samples compared to 2007 HHRA EPCs. Zinc exceeded background MDC. Cadmium was detected slightly above the reporting limit but below the residential [[california-chhsl]]. Strontium was detected. The 2007 HHRA risk and hazard calculations were deemed valid.
    *   **Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment:** Barium, copper, and zinc showed higher concentrations in 2012 surface soil samples compared to 2007 HHRA EPCs. Cadmium was detected below the residential CHHSL. Strontium was detected. The 2007 HHRA risk and hazard calculations were deemed valid.
    *   **Stockpile 3 Current Exposure Assessment:** Barium and lead showed higher concentrations in 2012 surface soil samples compared to 2007 HHRA EPCs. Copper and zinc exceeded background MDCs. Cadmium was detected below the residential CHHSL. Strontium was detected. The 2007 HHRA risk and hazard calculations were deemed valid.
    *   **Stockpiles 1-3 Future Construction Worker and Offsite Resident:** Barium, copper, and zinc showed higher concentrations in 2012 soil samples compared to 2007 HHRA EPCs. The 95% UCL for 2012 barium data was significantly less than the 2007 HHRA EPC. Strontium was detected. The 2007 HHRA risk and hazard calculations for future conditions were deemed valid.
    *   **Onsite Shallow Groundwater:** Cobalt, copper, manganese, nickel, selenium, vanadium, and zinc showed slightly higher concentrations in 2012 groundwater samples compared to 2007 HHRA EPCs. Strontium was detected. The 2007 HHRA risk and hazard calculations for a hypothetical groundwater user were deemed valid.
*   **DTSC Letter (February 15, 2013):** DTSC, in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], reviewed the Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) and HHRA Update reports.
*   **HERO Memorandum (February 14, 2013):** The Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard as long as they remain in place and are properly managed. HERO agreed with the HHRA update's conclusion that risks and hazards remain virtually unchanged and insignificant. HERO also agreed that recent groundwater concentrations are similar to those in the 2007 HHRA, posing no cancer risk and a hazard index less than one. HERO noted that elevated concentrations at depths greater than one foot would need evaluation if grading or removal occurs.
*   **DTSC Letter (April 4, 2013):** DTSC concurred with the findings of the SSI and HHRA Update reports.

## Regulatory Status

*   **California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs):** Elevated barium concentrations in stockpile samples exceeded commercial/industrial CHHSLs in the 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI). Cadmium also exceeded commercial/industrial CHHSLs in Stockpile 2 and Stockpile 3 in the 2004 PSI.
*   **DTSC Findings (December 17, 2009):** DTSC determined that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not pose a risk to human health for specific receptor groups (Caltrans workers, trespassers, adjacent residents).
*   **DTSC Concurrence (April 4, 2013):** DTSC concurred with the findings of the Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) and Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Update.
*   **Remedial Action Plan (RAP):** The RAP process, including the [[feasibility-study]], aims to address COPCs and ensure protection of human health and water quality. The DTSC and [[central-valley-rwqcb]] require concurrence on the RAP.

## Open Items

*   DTSC noted that elevated concentrations of chemicals at depths greater than one foot below ground surface within the stockpiles would need to be evaluated if substantial grading or reworking of the stockpiles occurs, or if the stockpiles are removed.
*   The potential for the stockpiles to impact groundwater from a water quality degradation standpoint remains a concern of the [[central-valley-rwqcb]].
