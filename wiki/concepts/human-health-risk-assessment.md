## Human Health Risk Assessment

### Overview

The Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site was conducted to evaluate potential chronic health risks and hazards to human receptors from contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) in soil and groundwater. The assessment was performed in accordance with Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) guidance. The HHRA concluded that the stockpiles, as managed, do not pose an unacceptable cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.

### Site Record

#### 2007 HHRA (Shaw Environmental, Inc.)

Shaw Environmental, Inc. prepared the baseline HHRA dated May 14, 2007, using soil and groundwater data from the 2006 Site Investigation and groundwater monitoring events. This HHRA is included as Appendix A of the Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) (Shaw, June 30, 2009).

*   **Contaminants of Potential Concern (COPCs):** Metals, notably [[barium]], and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) were identified as primary COPCs in soil stockpiles. Metals and general minerals (e.g., nitrate, total dissolved solids) were primary COPCs in groundwater. Cadmium was excluded as a COPC due to a lack of elevated detections. Strontium was excluded because its maximum reported concentration (231 mg/kg) was more than two orders of magnitude below the USEPA's residential [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] of 47,000 mg/kg. {{quote: "Shaw also did not identify strontium as a COPC in the HHRA since the maximum strontium concentration of 231 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) reported in the Shaw 2004 PSI is more than two orders of magnitude less than the Unites States Environmental Protections Agency's (USEPA) residential Regional Screening Level (RSL) of 47,000 mg/kg."}}
*   **Exposure Scenarios Evaluated:**
    *   Current offsite resident/trespasser (combined as a conservative residential exposure scenario).
    *   Future construction worker.
    *   Future offsite resident (during construction).
    *   Hypothetical future shallow groundwater user (using residential exposure assumptions).
*   **Exposure Pathways:** Incidental ingestion, dermal contact, and inhalation of dust for soil. For groundwater, ingestion and dermal contact. Surface water was not evaluated due to infrequent rain events and lack of significant exposure potential. {{quote: "Shaw prepared a Conceptual Site Exposure Model (CSEM) as part of their HHRA (Shaw, 2007c). The CSEM identifies primary sources of COPCs, exposure routes, receptor scenarios, and identifies whether they are "complete" or "incomplete." The CSEM concluded that the offsite resident and trespasser were the current human receptors. Future receptors during the project would include the future construction worker and future offsite resident. Potential exposure routes for the current resident/trespasser exposure scenario include incidental ingestion, inhalation of dust, and dermal contact. Exposure routes for the future land-use scenario would include incidental ingestion, dermal contact, and inhalation of dust for the construction worker."}}
*   **Exposure-Point Concentrations (EPCs):** Maximum detected concentrations (MDCs) were used for COPCs in each exposure scenario, except for Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment, which used 95th percentile upper confidence limits (UCLs). {{quote: "The EPCs utilized in the 2007 HHRA are the MDCs for the selected COPCs for each exposure scenario with the exception of the Stockpile 2 Current Exposure Assessment which utilized the 95% UCLs for the selected COPCs."}}
*   **Key Risk Characterization Findings (2007 HHRA):**
    *   **Stockpile 1 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** Cancer risk of 8E-8 {{quote: "The calculated cancer risk and non-cancer HI for the current offsite resident and trespasser receptors exposed to surface soil on Stockpile 1 is 8E-8 and 4E-2, respectively."}}; non-cancer Hazard Index (HI) of 0.04 {{quote: "The calculated cancer risk and non-cancer HI for the current offsite resident and trespasser receptors exposed to surface soil on Stockpile 1 is 8E-8 and 4E-2, respectively."}} (below threshold of 1).
    *   **Stockpile 2 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** Revised cancer risk of 1E-7 {{quote: "The revised cancer risk estimate, with arsenic excluded, is 1E-7."}}; non-cancer HI of 0.1 {{quote: "Additionally, the estimated HI for non-cancer effects is below the threshold of 1."}}.
    *   **Stockpile 3 — Current Offsite Resident/Trespasser:** No excess cancer risk calculated; non-cancer HI of 0.02 {{quote: "The estimated non-cancer HI for the offsite resident/trespasser receptor exposed to surface soil on Stockpile 3 was 0.02, which is well below the threshold of 1."}}.
    *   **Construction Worker (Stockpiles 1–3 combined, 0–20 ft bgs):** Cancer risk of 9.2E-7 {{quote: "The 2007 HHRA calculated current cancer risk and non-cancer hazard estimates of 9.2E-7 and 0.4, respectively, for the construction worker receptor exposed to soil at Stockpiles 1 through 3."}}; non-cancer HI of 0.4 {{quote: "The 2007 HHRA calculated current cancer risk and non-cancer hazard estimates of 9.2E-7 and 0.4, respectively, for the construction worker receptor exposed to soil at Stockpiles 1 through 3."}}.
    *   **Future Offsite Resident (during construction):** Cancer risk of 6E-10 {{quote: "The calculated current cancer risk and non-cancer HI were 6E-10 and 0.017, respectively, for the future offsite resident receptor exposed to soil at Stockpiles 1 through 3."}}; non-cancer HI of 0.017 {{quote: "The calculated current cancer risk and non-cancer HI were 6E-10 and 0.017, respectively, for the future offsite resident receptor exposed to soil at Stockpiles 1 through 3."}}.
    *   **Hypothetical Shallow Groundwater User:** Non-cancer HI of 0.9 {{quote: "The 2007 HHRA calculated a current non-cancer HI for the hypothetical shallow groundwater user at 0.9."}}. No cancer risk calculated.

Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared the HHRA Update, revised March 1, 2013, under Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order No. 44. This update incorporated soil analytical data from the September 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) and groundwater data from bi-monthly monitoring events between March 2012 and March 2013. The update compared 2012 EPCs against the 2007 HHRA EPCs to determine if earlier risk and hazard estimates remained valid. {{quote: "Geocon updated the 2007 HHRA by incorporating soil analytical data generated from the fenceline, perimeter, and stockpile sampling as presented in the revised Supplemental Site Investigation dated March 1, 2013, and groundwater analytical data generated from bi-monthly sampling events."}}

*   **Comparison Methodology:** 2012 EPCs were compared to newly collected fenceline, perimeter, and cadmium boring samples. If 2012 concentrations were within the same order of magnitude and did not exceed residential CHHSLs or RSLs, the 2007 HHRA conclusions were deemed valid. {{quote: "Because the 2012 metal concentrations are of the same order of magnitude as those used in the 2007 HHRA and that none of the 2012 metal detections exceeded respective residential CHHSLs or RSLs, the 2007 HHRA risk and hazard calculations for the current resident/trespasser remain valid for Stockpile 1."}}

*   **Update Summary:** The 2007 HHRA remained valid with respect to exposure potential for all receptor scenarios. {{quote: "The HHRA Update concluded that the 2007 HHRA remains valid with respect to exposure potential for the current resident/trespasser, future construction worker and offsite resident, and hypothetical shallow groundwater user at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpile Site."}}

#### Cadmium Data Resolution

The 2012 SSI addressed earlier cadmium detections from the Shaw 2004 PSI, which had reported concentrations exceeding the [[commercial-industrial-screening-level]] (7.5 mg/kg) in Stockpiles 2 and 3. In 348 soil samples analyzed during the Shaw 2006 SI and Geocon 2012 SSI, cadmium was not detected above the laboratory reporting limit of 1.0 mg/kg. The 2004 PSI cadmium data were determined to be neither reproducible nor reliable and are considered false positives, possibly due to sample interference or dilution effects from high [[barium]] concentrations. Cadmium is therefore not a COPC for the Site. {{quote: "Based on the cumulative cadmium data, it appears the Shaw 2004 PSI cadmium data is neither reproducible nor reliable and represents false positives possibly as result of sample interference/dilution effects due to the associated high barium concentrations. Cadmium is therefore not considered a COPC for the project site."}}

### Regulatory Status

*   **DTSC Concurrence — December 17, 2009:** Following the Final PEA, DTSC determined that the soil stockpiles, as currently managed by Caltrans on Caltrans property, do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. DTSC required continued management practices: limiting access, maintaining fencing, prohibiting excavation/grading/offsite soil removal, and maintaining vegetative cover and groundwater monitoring. {{quote: "In a letter dated December 17, 2009, the DTSC responded to the Final PEA stating that: 'DTSC finds that the soil stockpiles, as currently managed by Caltrans on Caltrans property, do not pose a risk to human health for: 1) Caltrans workers who access the fenced site to conduct mowing operations, conduct fence repairs, or other routine act'"}}
*   **DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) Memorandum — February 14, 2013:** HERO concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard to persons in the vicinity as long as the stockpiles remain in place and are properly managed.
