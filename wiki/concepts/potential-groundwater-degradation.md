## Potential Groundwater Degradation

### Overview
The potential for groundwater degradation at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site is primarily associated with the presence of certain constituents in the soil stockpiles that could leach into underlying groundwater. The [[rwqcb]] has established standards for groundwater quality that are relevant to this assessment.

### Site Record
The Final Feasibility Study Report for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles identified several constituents of concern within the soil stockpiles. Specifically, arsenic and chromium were detected at concentrations that warranted evaluation for their potential impact on groundwater. The report states, "Arsenic and chromium were detected in soil stockpiles at concentrations exceeding the screening levels for groundwater protection." {{quote: "Arsenic and chromium were detected in soil stockpiles at concentrations exceeding the screening levels for groundwater protection."}}

### Regulatory Status
The groundwater quality at the site is regulated by the [[rwqcb]]. The California Maximum Contaminant Levels (California MCL) are the applicable standards for groundwater. The Final Feasibility Study Report indicates that the detected concentrations of arsenic and chromium in the soil stockpiles exceeded screening levels established for groundwater protection. {{quote: "Arsenic and chromium were detected in soil stockpiles at concentrations exceeding the screening levels for groundwater protection."}}
