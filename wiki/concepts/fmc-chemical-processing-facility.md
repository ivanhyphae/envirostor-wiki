## FMC Chemical Processing Facility

The FMC Chemical Processing Facility (also referred to as FMC) was a chemical processing facility that operated in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, from 1929 to approximately 1985. {{quote: "FMC and its predecessors operated a chemical processing facility at that location from 1929 to approximately 1985."}} The facility processed barium and strontium minerals, such as barite and celestite, to produce various industrial chemicals. {{quote: "The facility processed barium and strontium minerals (barite and celestite) and other materials to produce a variety of industrial chemicals."}} From the 1950s to the 1970s, liquid wastes from the FMC facility were discharged into seven unlined ponds. {{quote: "From the early 1950s to the late 1970s, liquid wastes were discharged to seven unlined ponds at the FMC facility."}} During the construction of State Route (SR) 99, soil from around one of these former FMC ponds was excavated and used to create the soil stockpiles that are the subject of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] remediation case file. {{quote: "During construction of SR-99, soil in and around one of the former FMC ponds was excavated and stockpiled in their current configuration within the current Caltrans rightof-way (ROW) for a planned SR-99/SR-132 interchange."}}

### Site Record

The FMC facility was located less than 500 feet north of the current stockpiles. {{quote: "The stockpiles were created in the early-1960s by importing soil from a Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility that was located less than 500 feet north of the Site."}} The property occupied by FMC and its predecessors, including Barium Products LTD and Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation, was located beneath and northeast of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue Interchange. {{quote: "From the 1930s to 1970s, property beneath and northeast of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue Interchange was occupied by chemical processing facilities operated by Barium Products LTD, Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation, and Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC)."}} Liquid residue, referred to as "tailings," generated by FMC was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds from the 1950s to the 1970s. {{quote: "From the 1950s to the 1970s, liquid residue ("tailings") generated by FMC at this facility was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds."}} In 1961, Caltrans acquired a portion of the FMC property, including some of the ponds, for SR-99 construction. {{quote: "In 1961, the State purchased a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility, including a portion of the ponds, for the construction of the SR-99 freeway through Modesto."}} Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange, were used to create the three existing stockpiles. {{quote: "Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property along with native soils excavated south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange were placed to create the three stockpiles that exist today."}}

Aerial photographs from 1963 and 1967 show grading and construction activities related to SR-99 and the placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3, with haul roads within Caltrans' right-of-way. {{quote: "The 1963 photograph shows grading/construction of SR-99 including the southwestern portion of the FMC property, interchange ramps at Kansas Avenue, and placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3."}} By 1967, SR-99 was completed, and the stockpiles existed in their current configuration. {{quote: "The 1967 photograph shows that SR-99 north and south of the Kansas Avenue interchange had been completed, and Stockpiles 1, 2 and 3 existed essentially as they do today."}}

The hydrogeology of the FMC facility has been characterized by several studies since the early 1980s. A report by GeoTrans, Inc. in January 2005 described the site as being underlain by discontinuous sand and silty sand associated with the Modesto and Riverbank Formations. {{quote: "The hydrogeology of the FMC facility, approximately 1,100 feet north of the Site, has been characterized by several studies since the early 1980s. GeoTrans, Inc's report: *Addendum to Comprehensive Remedial Investigations Report*, dated January 2005, provides the following description of the hydrogeology associated with FMC facility: \"The site is underlain by laterally discontinuous and unconsolidated sand and silty sand associated with the Modesto and Riverbank Formations."}} First-encountered groundwater was approximately 30 feet below ground surface (bgs) under confined to semi-confined conditions, with a deeper aquifer at 165 feet bgs separated by a clay aquitard. {{quote: "First-encountered groundwater is approximately 30 feet below ground surface (bgs) under confined to semi-confined conditions. A deeper aquifer is present at a depth of 165 feet bgs and separated from the upper zone by a blue clay aquitard."}} Groundwater flow within the upper zone was toward the southeast at a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft. {{quote: "Groundwater flow within the upper zone is toward the southeast under a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft."}}

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] RAP notes that background concentrations of metals in undisturbed soil near the stockpiles were in the same general range as those determined for the FMC property. {{quote: "Shaw reported that the background concentrations of metals calculated for undisturbed soil near the stockpiles were in the general range as those determined for the FMC property."}}

### Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] (CVRWQCB) are oversight agencies for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] RAP. {{quote: "This RAP supplements the EIR/EA and therefore, the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) in their capacity as oversight agencies for the RAP, are also responsible agencies for the EIR/EA."}}
