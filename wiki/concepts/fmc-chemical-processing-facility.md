## Overview

The FMC Chemical Processing Facility, operated by FMC and its predecessors from 1929 to approximately 1985 in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, is a key historical site relevant to the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] remediation case file. This facility processed barium and strontium minerals, generating liquid wastes (tailings) that were discharged into unlined evaporation ponds. Soil excavated from around one of these former ponds was used to create the soil stockpiles that are the subject of the remediation. The FMC facility was located less than 500 feet north of the current stockpiles.

## Site Record

The FMC Chemical Processing Facility, also known as FMC, operated from 1929 to approximately 1985. The facility processed barium and strontium minerals, such as barite and celestite, to produce various industrial chemicals. Between the early 1950s and late 1970s, liquid wastes, referred to as "tailings," were discharged into seven unlined evaporation ponds at the facility.

The FMC facility was situated less than 500 feet north of the current stockpiles. The property occupied by FMC and its predecessors, including Barium Products LTD and Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation, was located beneath and northeast of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue Interchange. In 1961, Caltrans acquired a 4.3-acre parcel in the southwestern portion of the FMC facility, which included some of the ponds, for the construction of State Route (SR) 99. Pond tailings and underlying soils from the FMC property, along with native soils excavated south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange, were used to create the three existing stockpiles.

Aerial photographs from 1963 and 1967 document grading and construction activities related to SR-99 and the placement of Stockpiles 2 and 3, with haul roads within Caltrans' right-of-way. By 1967, SR-99 was completed, and the stockpiles existed in their current configuration.

The hydrogeology of the FMC facility has been characterized by several studies since the early 1980s. A report by GeoTrans, Inc. in January 2005 described the site as being underlain by discontinuous sand and silty sand associated with the Modesto and Riverbank Formations. First-encountered groundwater was approximately 30 feet below ground surface (bgs) under confined to semi-confined conditions, with a deeper aquifer at 165 feet bgs separated by a clay aquitard. Groundwater flow within the upper zone was toward the southeast at a gradient of 0.002 ft/ft.

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] RAP notes that background concentrations of metals in undisturbed soil near the stockpiles were in the same general range as those determined for the FMC property.
