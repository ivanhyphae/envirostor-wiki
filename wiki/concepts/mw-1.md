MW-1 is one of the eight original monitoring wells installed by Caltrans in 2006 to bracket the three Modesto soil stockpiles, and it has served as the site's designated upgradient background well through nearly every subsequent round of groundwater monitoring. Its principal significance to the case file is that it is the well against which downgradient impacts from the stockpiles and the adjacent former FMC facility are judged — but MW-1 itself has consistently shown nitrate and, at times, TDS concentrations exceeding drinking water thresholds, a pattern the consulting geologist has repeatedly characterized as likely reflecting natural background conditions rather than site-related contamination.

## Role as background well and hydrogeologic setting

MW-1 was installed in 2006 along with MW-2 through MW-8, before MW-9 and MW-10 were added in June 2012 to provide additional upgradient and adjacent coverage. MW-1 is completed in the shallow zone of the upper aquifer within the unconsolidated sand, silty sand, and silt of the Modesto Formation, with shallow groundwater historically encountered at approximately 35 feet below ground surface (elevation approximately 50 feet). Depth to groundwater at MW-1 has consistently been the shallowest of the network — for example, 32.73 feet below top of casing in September 2012 and 33.46 feet in December 2013, compared to over 40 feet at MW-5 in the same rounds — consistent with its upgradient position relative to the stockpiles. Regional groundwater flow has been characterized as generally toward the south to southeast, with historical gradients ranging from 0.0006 to 0.004 ft/ft depending on the monitoring event.

By October 2015 and May 2016, MW-1 was one of only a small number of wells (with MW-7, and briefly MW-2) still yielding sufficient water to sample, as MW-3 through MW-6 and MW-8 through MW-10 went dry. This persistence is notable: MW-1 has remained one of the two most reliably sampled wells at the site into the later, less-frequent monitoring rounds.

## Geochemistry: consistently calcium-dominant

Stiff diagram analysis has classified MW-1 as calcium-dominant water in every reported sampling event from 2006 through at least April 2019, without the shifts seen at other wells (several of which, such as MW-5 and MW-9, changed between calcium- and sodium-dominant classifications over time, interpreted by Geocon as possible mixing of water types). This consistency is one basis for treating MW-1 as representative of unimpacted background groundwater rather than water influenced by the former FMC evaporation ponds, which are associated with sodium-dominant signatures at wells such as MW-3, MW-6, and MW-8.

## Nitrate and TDS: recurring exceedances treated as background

Despite its role as the reference well, MW-1 has repeatedly returned nitrate-as-nitrogen concentrations at or above the California primary MCL of 10 mg/L, and TDS concentrations at or above the secondary MCL of 500 mg/L in several rounds. In each report where this occurs, Geocon notes explicitly that because MW-1 is upgradient, the exceedance "may be indicative of natural background nitrate and TDS concentrations for the shallow groundwater in the vicinity of the Site" rather than a site-related impact.

Across these rounds, MW-1's nitrate values cluster in the low-to-mid teens, occasionally dipping just under the 10 mg/L MCL (as in May 2016) but exceeding it in most reported events. TDS has hovered at or just above the 500 mg/L secondary MCL rather than showing a strong trend in either direction. No consequence beyond this repeated notation as probable background is recorded in the available reports; the exceedances have not been described as triggering a separate investigation or regulatory response specific to MW-1.

## Dissolved metals

MW-1 has not been a location of concern for the site's three primary chemicals of concern — barium, lead, and strontium — which DTSC identified as the key groundwater constituents to track. Barium and strontium have been reported at MW-1 at concentrations similar to other wells and well below their respective thresholds (1,000 µg/L and 4,000 µg/L) across all rounds. Lead has typically not been detected above the practical quantitation limit of 1.0 µg/L at MW-1; exceptions include the March 2013 event, when lead was reported at 2.2 µg/L, still well under its numeric threshold.

Manganese has been the most frequently flagged metal at MW-1 among the non-COC dissolved metals: it exceeded its secondary MCL in the March 2012 sampling (the first resumed round), and again in March 2013 and April 2017. These were isolated detections rather than a sustained trend, and no threshold exceedance for manganese at MW-1 is recorded as prompting any specific follow-up action.

## Quality control role

MW-1 was one of the wells sampled under DTSC oversight when groundwater monitoring resumed in March 2012: DTSC representatives observed sample collection and collected split samples (including from MW-1) submitted to an alternate laboratory, with no notable differences in reported analyte concentrations between the two labs.
