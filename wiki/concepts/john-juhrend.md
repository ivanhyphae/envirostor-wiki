John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG, CEM, is a Geocon Consultants, Inc. project manager and senior engineer who served as the principal technical author and point of contact for soil characterization work supporting reuse and capping decisions for [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]] on the SR 132 project. He drafted the sampling plans and technical memoranda that DTSC and the Central Valley RWQCB reviewed to decide whether excavated soils could be reused on site, and his memos are the documents of record for two of the project's clean-fill classification decisions.

## MSE wall footing sampling plan

On March 31, 2020, Juhrend sent Caltrans (Richard Stewart) a sampling plan for the Stockpile 1 and 2 mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall footing excavations, to be forwarded to DTSC and the Water Board for approval. The plan called for sampling borings or trenches at the midpoint of the wall footing excavation at ten locations spaced at approximately 200-foot intervals as the Stockpile 1 southern slope zone barium containing soil (BCS) was removed, with discrete samples collected at one-foot intervals to the planned footing depth (roughly 4 to 5 feet) and analyzed for barium and lead on 48-hour turnaround. Juhrend's email set out the screening framework the resulting data would be compared against: a maximum site-specific background of 120 mg/kg for barium (against an RDIP BCS removal threshold of 1,000 mg/kg, a construction worker ESL of 3,000 mg/kg, residential ESL of 15,000 mg/kg, and commercial ESL of 220,000 mg/kg), and a maximum site-specific background of 3.8 mg/kg for lead (against a DTSC residential level of 80 mg/kg).

## Stockpile 1 wall footing tech memo and DTSC's condition

On April 6, 2020, Juhrend submitted the resulting technical memorandum (dated the same day) to Stewart for transmittal to DTSC's Dean Wright and the Water Board's Steven Meeks, seeking approval to use the Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing excavation soil as embankment fill and/or clean cover material within Stockpiles 1 and 2. He noted that once the Stockpile 2 BCS southerly slope material was removed, Geocon would complete the wall footing excavation sampling in that area as well.

DTSC and the Water Board accepted the memo on April 7, 2020, but rejected the specific proposed use: because the 95% Upper Confidence Limit of the sampled soil exceeded the 120 mg/kg background maximum, and because the MSE wall footing area fell outside the borrow area designated in the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP), Dean Wright required that the material instead be placed within the Stockpiles 1 and 2 fill containment zones or used as general bridge embankment fill — not as capping material. This is a case where sampling data disqualified the intended reuse pathway rather than confirming it.

## Carpenter Road Shoofly clean fill characterization

Juhrend authored a second technical memorandum, dated April 12, 2021, characterizing approximately 5,000 cubic yards of native soil excavated from the Carpenter Road Shoofly for use as clean capping material over portions of the BCS in Stockpiles 1 and 2, pending DTSC approval, under the April 9, 2020 Variance to Remedial Design Implementation Plan. Geocon collected 24 discrete soil samples along six vertical transects (CSF1–CSF6) at 2, 5, 10, and 15 feet from the top of the slope, composited by the laboratory (California Laboratory Services) into eight three-part composites analyzed for barium and lead by EPA Method 6010B on 48-hour turnaround.

Barium was detected in all 10 analyzed samples at 28 to 96 mg/kg, and lead in 9 of 10 at 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg — both ranges below or at the site-specific background thresholds of 120 mg/kg (barium) and 3.8 mg/kg (lead). Unlike the MSE wall footing soil, the Shoofly material met the BCS clean cap criteria established in the DTSC-approved variance, and Juhrend's memo states this data would be included in the Removal Action Completion Report to follow completion of the Stockpile 1 and 2 clean fill capping activities.
