Three fixed storm water monitoring points — SW-East, SW-West, and BG-West — are sampled at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site during qualifying rain events to check whether the containment system encapsulating barium- and lead-contaminated soil beneath SR 132 is holding. SW-East and SW-West sit within or adjacent to the encapsulated stockpile footprint and Ponding Basin 5; BG-West is a background/upgradient location at a drop inlet on North Carpenter Road, used for comparison rather than as a compliance point. Sampling is performed by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] under a Storm Water Sampling and Analysis Plan dated December 13, 2023, itself built on a 2006 Shaw Environmental plan and a 2013 Geocon addendum approved under [[dtsc]] protocols.

## Locations and purpose

- **SW-East** — the storm water discharge point on the north side of SR 132, west of SR 99.
- **SW-West** — within the east side of Ponding Basin 5, north of SR 132 and east of North Carpenter Road.
- **BG-West** — a drop inlet on the east side of North Carpenter Road, approximately 700 feet south of the Kansas Avenue intersection; serves as the background station.

The stated purpose of the program is to evaluate the integrity of the stockpile containment system approved by DTSC and built into the SR 132 West Expressway project, and to determine whether storm water has been affected by chemicals of potential concern from the encapsulated stockpiles.

## Sampling method

At each event, samples are collected with a pressure bailer and suction pump into laboratory-supplied containers, field-filtered through 0.45-micron filters for dissolved metals, then chilled and shipped to ASSET Laboratories under chain-of-custody. QA/QC includes disposable bailers and full chain-of-custody documentation for each sample.

## Results by round

**February 1, 2024** (reported May 2024): all three stations were sampled and yielded water. TDS ranged 42–88 mg/L across the three stations; sulfate was reported only at SW-West and BG-West, at 3.6 and 6.5 mg/L. Barium and zinc were detected at all three stations, and copper at SW-East, all below their respective primary or secondary drinking water MCLs. Strontium was detected at SW-East and SW-West, but no MCL exists for it. No discharge or runoff from the Caltrans right-of-way was observed during this event.

**February 4, 2025** (reported June 2025): BG-West was dry and not sampled; only SW-East and SW-West yielded results. TDS was 210 mg/L (SW-East) and 180 mg/L (SW-West); sulfate was 21 mg/L and 14 mg/L respectively — both TDS and sulfate roughly two to four times the 2024 values at the same two stations, though still reported as not exceeding secondary MCLs. Barium, copper, and zinc were again detected at both stations without exceeding primary or secondary MCLs. Molybdenum, vanadium, and strontium were detected at both stations, but none of these has an established MCL. No discharge or runoff migrating away from the Caltrans right-of-way was observed.

Across the two rounds for which values are available, no [[barium]] or [[lead]] exceedance is reported at any of the three stations, and no runoff leaving the Caltrans right-of-way was observed in either event. This is the evidentiary basis, so far as these two rounds go, for treating the SR 132 containment system as intact: the stations closest to the encapsulated stockpiles (SW-East, SW-West) show the same non-exceedance pattern as the background station (BG-West) when it could be sampled, and the primary chemicals of concern named for the site — barium and lead — are not reported above MCLs at any station in either round.

## Continuity and gaps

Sampling depends on rainfall: BG-West could not be sampled in February 2025 because it was dry, illustrating that the three-station network does not produce a complete dataset in every qualifying event. Both the 2024 and 2025 reports state that Caltrans planned additional storm water sampling during qualifying rain events in the following year, and the December 2023 SAP that governs the current round of sampling was itself built on protocols going back to the 2006 Shaw plan and 2013 Geocon addendum, indicating the three-station layout has been in continuous use across multiple programs rather than being reconstituted anew for each report.
