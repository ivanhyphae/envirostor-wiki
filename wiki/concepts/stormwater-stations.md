## Stormwater Stations

### Overview
Stormwater stations are locations where samples of stormwater are collected to evaluate the integrity of the containment system for encapsulated soil stockpiles. These stockpiles, identified as Stockpiles 1 and 2, were encapsulated beneath State Route (SR) 132 in Stanislaus County, California, due to contamination with barium and lead. The sampling aims to determine if stormwater has been impacted by chemicals of potential concern from these encapsulated materials.

### Site record
Stormwater sampling activities have been conducted in accordance with protocols approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). These protocols were established in the *Storm Water Sampling and Analysis Plan (SAP)* prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., dated December 13, 2023, which references earlier plans by Shaw Environmental, Inc. and Geocon Consultants, Inc.

Sampling events have occurred on the following dates:
*   February 1, 2024
*   February 4, 2025

The approximate site location is south of the intersection of State Route (SR) 99 and Kansas Avenue in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The approximate site boundaries, Stockpiles 1 and 2, and storm water sampling locations are shown on the Site Plan (Figure 2) referenced in the SAP.

### Regulatory status
The sampling activities are performed in accordance with protocols approved by the DTSC and the CVRWQCB. The purpose of the sampling is to gauge the effectiveness of the containment system for barium and lead-contaminated soil.
