Stormwater stations are designated locations where samples of stormwater are collected to assess the effectiveness of the containment system for encapsulated soil stockpiles at the State Route (SR) 132 project in Stanislaus County, California. These stations play a crucial role in monitoring potential impacts from barium and lead-contaminated soils that have been encapsulated beneath the roadway.

### Stormwater Sampling Activities

Stormwater sampling has been conducted to evaluate the integrity of the containment system for the Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles, located south of the intersection of State Route (SR) 99 and Kansas Avenue in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. Stockpiles 1 and 2, containing barium and lead-contaminated soil, were encapsulated beneath SR 132. The sampling protocols are established in the *Storm Water Sampling and Analysis Plan (SAP)* prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., dated December 13, 2023, which references earlier plans by Shaw Environmental, Inc. and Geocon Consultants, Inc.

Sampling events have occurred on the following dates:
*   February 1, 2024
*   February 4, 2025

The approximate site boundaries, Stockpiles 1 and 2, and the storm water sampling locations are depicted on the Site Plan (Figure 2) referenced in the SAP.

### Regulatory Oversight and Approval

The stormwater sampling activities are conducted in accordance with protocols approved by the California Environmental Protection Agency, Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). The purpose of this monitoring is to ensure that stormwater has not been impacted by chemicals of potential concern from the encapsulated stockpiles.

The DTSC reviewed and approved the Storm Water Sampling Report dated June 18, 2025, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of Caltrans. This report detailed the results of stormwater sampling conducted on February 4, 2025. The DTSC's review was coordinated by their Project Manager and included input from technical staff, specifically geologists from the Geological Services Branch (GSB) and toxicologists from the Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO). The DTSC noted that the results of the Storm Water Sampling Report were all within reporting limits.
