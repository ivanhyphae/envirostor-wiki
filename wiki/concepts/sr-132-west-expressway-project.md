The SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project is the highway construction project through Modesto that provided the physical remedy for the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles: barium- and lead-affected soil left over from the former FMC facility was consolidated, capped with clean fill, and buried as highway embankment fill beneath the new roadway rather than removed off-site. Regulatory approval of the remedy under [[dtsc]] and [[central-valley-rwqcb]] oversight was tied directly to the project's construction schedule and design documents, and the highway's Interim Phase 1 opening on November 3, 2022 marked the point at which the interim containment was substantially complete.

## Project phasing and the stockpiles

The project was built in two phases. Interim Phase 1 constructed two of four planned traffic lanes from east of SR 99 to North Dakota Avenue, along with related bridge structures and interchanges, and contained BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3 by consolidating them into two Containment Zones built as highway embankment fill: the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone (adjacent and west of Emerald Avenue, roughly SR 132 Stations 1125+50 to 1133+50) and the Stockpile 2 Containment Zone (between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, roughly Stations 1135+35 to 1148+30). Stockpile 3, located east of SR 99, had been partially removed in 2012 to allow an off-ramp widening; its remaining barium-containing soil (BCS) material was entirely removed and placed within the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone during Interim Phase 1. Aerially-deposited lead (ADL) soil from the SR 99 northbound shoulder, with lead concentrations exceeding California hazardous waste thresholds, was also placed within the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone rather than the Stockpile 2 zone. Ultimate Phase 2, which will widen the highway to four lanes, is still to come and will complete the final containment measures; DTSC has stated it will provide project certification only following completion of Ultimate Phase 2.

## Regulatory approvals tied to construction

The remedy implemented through the SR 132 project followed a defined document chain: the 2018 Final RAP selected consolidation/containment as the remedy, and the 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) translated that selection into construction-specific technical requirements. DTSC gave conditional approval to the draft RDIP on August 20, 2018, and again on October 12, 2018 after Geocon incorporated administrative revisions, with both conditional approvals held pending review of the 95% and 100% design plans. DTSC and the RWQCB reviewed the complete 100% design plans and the "City of Modesto Book 2 of 3 100% Special Provisions" specifications, and the RDIP-Interim Phase 1 (dated January 30, 2019) received final approval on April 3, 2019 — meaning the remedy could not proceed to construction until the highway's own design and specification package had cleared regulatory review. A Post-Construction Contract Award Errata was later issued to update the RDIP's project organization section, striking Geocon Consultants as "Caltrans' agent" and clarifying that Caltrans, not its consultant, remains the responsible party for RDIP conformance regardless of which contractor performs the work; DTSC accepted this errata sheet on February 5, 2020.

Because the RDIP anticipated on-the-fly design changes, two field variance requests were processed during construction, each requiring DTSC/RWQCB sign-off before soil could be diverted from its planned disposition:

- **April 2020** — Bay Cities requested using the upper foot of Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing excavation soil as clean fill was rejected due to elevated barium; DTSC and the Water Board instead approved use of soil from 1–5 feet below grade in that footing area (95% UCL barium of 113.1 mg/kg, within site background) as clean cap material, with the parallel request for Stockpile 2 footing soil held pending submission of its own data.
- **September 2020** — DTSC and the Water Board approved Class 3 aggregate subbase as an interim clean cap alternative over the BCS/ADL stockpiles ahead of pavement placement, but increased the required minimum thickness from the requested 4 inches to 6 inches after a September 15, 2020 visual inspection found the material to be predominantly fine sand passing the No. 4 sieve, and required a composite barium/lead sample from the Class 3 stockpile near the Rosemore Overcrossing before it could be used.

Clean fill sourced for the project was also subject to source testing and DTSC review, including sampling for the SR 132 roadway cut east of Carpenter Road (see [[carpenter-road-area]]) and, in June 2020, a companion sampling plan for a proposed clean fill borrow location at "Basin 5," which Caltrans forwarded to DTSC and the Water Board for approval on the premise that conditions there would mirror the previously tested Carpenter Road cut.

## Post-construction operations and maintenance

With Interim Phase 1 substantially built and SR 132 opened to traffic on November 3, 2022, the remedy shifted from active construction control to long-term operation and maintenance under an O&M Plan prepared by Geocon for the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones. DTSC's August 1, 2022 comments on the March 2022 draft O&M Plan required, among other items, a minimum 10-working-day advance notice to DTSC before any BCS disturbance, explicit reference to Health and Safety Code 25123.3(b)(4)(A) cap-thickness requirements, post-event inspections after fires, heavy rainfall (1 inch in 24 hours), significant seismic events, or traffic accidents, and inspection reporting on unauthorized access such as encampments on the north vegetated slope — that slope will remain unpaved and vegetated, rather than paved, until Ultimate Phase 2 is completed. The final O&M Plan incorporating these comments was issued in December 2022. DTSC's November 7, 2023 annual cost-estimate letter for fiscal year 2023–2024 lists completion of a Land Use Covenant, review and approval of the annual inspection (due by January 31, 2024), and continued project management as the scheduled oversight tasks for that period, confirming that regulatory obligations tied to the highway project continue past construction completion into a standing five-year-review and annual-inspection cycle.
