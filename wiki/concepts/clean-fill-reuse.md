## Clean Fill Reuse

### Overview

Clean Fill Reuse refers to the practice of utilizing excavated soil from the project site as clean fill material in other locations, thereby avoiding off-site disposal and associated costs. This concept is central to the management of soil generated during remediation activities at the SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA site, particularly concerning material excavated from [[basin-5]]. The viability of Clean Fill Reuse is contingent upon the soil meeting specific chemical and physical criteria, as determined through sampling and analysis.

### Site Record

The [[basin-5-clean-fill-sampling-plan]] outlines the strategy for evaluating excavated soil for Clean Fill Reuse. This plan was developed in response to the need to characterize material from Basin 5. The sampling plan details the locations and methods for collecting samples to assess suitability for reuse. Specific analytical parameters are identified to ensure the material meets reuse criteria.

### Regulatory Status

The suitability of soil for Clean Fill Reuse is governed by regulatory standards and project-specific requirements. The [[basin-5-clean-fill-sampling-plan]] was developed to address these requirements. The ultimate decision on whether excavated soil can be reused as clean fill rests with regulatory agencies.

### Open Items

The successful implementation of Clean Fill Reuse depends on the analytical results from the sampling conducted under the [[basin-5-clean-fill-sampling-plan]]. If the results indicate that the excavated soil does not meet the criteria for reuse, alternative disposal methods will be required.
