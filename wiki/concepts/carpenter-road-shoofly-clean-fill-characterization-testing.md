The Carpenter Road Shoofly Clean Fill Characterization Testing involved sampling and analysis of native soil within the State Route 132 right-of-way. This testing was performed to determine if the excavated soil material could be used as clean capping material for barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles. Approximately 5,000 cubic yards of soil were excavated from the Carpenter Road Shoofly for this purpose.

### Soil Sampling and Analysis

Twenty-four discrete soil samples were collected from the Carpenter Road Shoofly along six vertical transects (CSF1 through CSF6). Samples were taken from the surface of the exposed soil at depths of 2, 5, 10, and 15 feet from the top of the slope. These discrete samples were then composited into eight 3-part composite samples. The composite samples were analyzed for barium and lead using EPA Test Method 6010B.

### Analytical Results

Barium was detected in all 10 analyzed soil samples, with concentrations ranging from 28 to 96 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). These concentrations are below the maximum site-specific background value for barium of 120 mg/kg. Lead was detected in 9 of the 10 samples, with concentrations ranging from 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg. These lead concentrations are less than or equal to the maximum site-specific background concentration for lead of 3.8 mg/kg.

### Clean Fill Determination

Based on the analytical results for barium and lead, the soil material excavated from the Carpenter Road Shoofly meets the clean cap criteria established in the referenced variance document, approved by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). Specifically, the barium concentrations are below the upper-bound background threshold of 120 mg/kg. This information will be included in the Removal Action Completion Report following the completion of the capping activities for Stockpile 1 and 2.
