Fenceline stations are storm water sampling locations placed at the perimeter of the encapsulated Stockpile 1 and 2 footprint beneath SR 132, established under the December 2023 Storm Water Sampling and Analysis Plan (Geocon, Project No. S2350-01-02) to test whether storm water leaving the containment system carries chemicals of potential concern from the encapsulated soil. Their purpose is narrower than general site groundwater monitoring: they are a check on the physical integrity of the retaining walls, bridge abutments, and pavement cap that isolate the barium- and lead-contaminated stockpile soil, not a measure of ambient conditions.

## Origin and purpose

The fenceline sampling program grew out of a chain of prior storm water work at the site: the Final Surface Water Sampling and Analysis Plan (Shaw Environmental, January 25, 2006), the Addendum to Surface Water Sampling and Analysis Plan (Geocon, February 20, 2013), and the Surface Water Report, Modesto Soil Stockpile Encapsulation Project (Stantec, March 10, 2023). The December 2023 SAP, prepared by Geocon for [[caltrans]] District 6 under Contract No. 06A2767, Task Order No. 2, EA No. 10-1E7003, carried forward the sampling protocols approved by [[dtsc]] in the 2006 Shaw plan and the 2013 Geocon addendum. The stated purpose is to evaluate the integrity of the containment system approved by DTSC and built into the SR 132 West Expressway project, which isolated barium- and lead-contaminated soil behind retaining walls and bridge abutments and beneath roadway pavement and clean soil between SR 99 and Carpenter Avenue. Sampling results are meant to show whether that containment system is holding or whether storm water has been impacted by constituents from the encapsulated stockpiles.

## Relationship to other monitoring at the site

Fenceline stations are distinct from the groundwater monitoring well network discussed in the March 2012 Groundwater Monitoring Report (Geocon, May 2012), which tracks barium, lead, and strontium in wells installed in 2006 around the same former FMC/Barium Products Ltd. parcel and evaporation pond. That groundwater program is reviewed by DTSC in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board and, as of the June 27, 2012 DTSC letter, was to continue on at least a quarterly schedule with trend graphs and legended Stiff diagrams for future reports. The fenceline storm water stations instead sample runoff at the encapsulation perimeter and are a separate line of evidence going to whether the cap and walls are containing the source material, rather than to groundwater quality beneath or downgradient of the site.

The available material does not give sampling dates, analytes, or results specific to the fenceline stations themselves — only the plan establishing them and their stated purpose.
