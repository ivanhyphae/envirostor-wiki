## Overview

Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., is the Senior Engineering Geologist within the Department of Toxic Substances Control ([[dtsc]]) Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program who authored the DTSC response letter dated November 29, 2012, regarding groundwater monitoring reports for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. In this capacity, Adams served as the DTSC signatory concurring with the May, June, and July 2012 groundwater monitoring reports submitted by the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) for the State Route ([[sr-99]]) 99 and 132 soil stockpiles site in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California.

## Site record

Adams reviewed and concurred with three groundwater monitoring reports prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012:

1. "Groundwater Monitoring Report - May 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"
2. "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"
3. "Groundwater Monitoring Report - July 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"

The letter documents that these reports present analytical results of groundwater monitoring well sampling implemented in May, June, and July 2012, respectively. Adams specifically notes that the June 2012 report also discusses the installation of monitoring wells [[mw-9]] and [[mw-10]]. [[mw-9]] is located south of [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] and west of [[sr-99]], while [[mw-10]] is located south of [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] and east of [[sr-99]].

Adams's letter reiterates the site history as documented in DTSC's project file: the soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings generated when Caltrans constructed a segment of [[sr-99]] north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. Excavating that segment traversed a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel purchased from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC). An evaporation pond was located in the southernmost corner of that parcel. FMC and its predecessors operated as a chemical manufacturing company that processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores and other minerals. The primary chemicals of concern for the site are identified as barium, lead, and strontium.

## Regulatory status

As the DTSC reviewer, Adams conveyed the agency's concurrence with the subject groundwater monitoring reports. His letter also contained a directive: DTSC requested "that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available."

The letter was issued under the letterhead of Deborah O. Raphael, Director of DTSC, and was copied to multiple parties including Sam Haack, Caltrans District 10 Project Manager in Stockton; Nicole Damin, Senior Hazardous Materials Specialist with the Stanislaus County Health Agency in Modesto; and Steven Meeks, Senior Water Resources Control Engineer (affiliation truncated in the source document).

## Open items

The source document does not identify any unresolved issues, contested claims, or disputes associated with Adams's review.
