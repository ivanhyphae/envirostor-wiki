Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., is the Senior Engineering Geologist within the [[dtsc]] Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program. He authored the DTSC response letter dated November 29, 2012, regarding groundwater monitoring reports for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. In this role, Adams served as the DTSC signatory concurring with the May, June, and July 2012 groundwater monitoring reports submitted by the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) for the State Route 132 and SR 99 soil stockpiles site in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California.

## Site record

Adams reviewed and concurred with three groundwater monitoring reports prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012:

1.  "Groundwater Monitoring Report - May 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"
2.  "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"
3.  "Groundwater Monitoring Report - July 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"

These reports presented analytical results from groundwater monitoring well sampling conducted in May, June, and July 2012, respectively. Adams specifically noted that the June 2012 report detailed the installation of monitoring wells [[mw-9]] and [[mw-10]]. [[mw-9]] is situated south of the [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] and west of [[sr-99]], while [[mw-10]] is located south of the [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] and east of [[sr-99]].

Adams's letter also reiterated the site history as documented in DTSC's project file. The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings generated during Caltrans's construction of a segment of [[sr-99]] north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. This excavation traversed a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel acquired from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC). An evaporation pond was situated in the southernmost corner of this parcel. FMC and its predecessors operated as a chemical manufacturing company that processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores, among other minerals. The primary chemicals of concern identified for the site are barium, lead, and strontium.

## Regulatory status

As the DTSC reviewer, Adams conveyed the agency's concurrence with the reviewed groundwater monitoring reports. His letter also included a directive: DTSC requested "that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available."

The letter was issued under the letterhead of Deborah O. Raphael, Director of DTSC, and was copied to Sam Haack (Caltrans District 10 Project Manager), Nicole Damin (Senior Hazardous Materials Specialist with the Stanislaus County Health Agency), and Steven Meeks (Senior Water Resources Control Engineer).
