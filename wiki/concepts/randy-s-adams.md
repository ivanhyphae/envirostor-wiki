Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., served as the Senior Engineering Geologist within the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program. He was the DTSC Task Order Manager for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project. In this capacity, Adams reviewed and concurred with several groundwater monitoring reports submitted by Caltrans for the State Route 132 and SR 99 soil stockpiles site in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California.

## Site record

Adams reviewed and concurred with three groundwater monitoring reports prepared by Geocon Consultants, all dated November 2012:

1.  "Groundwater Monitoring Report - May 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route (SR) 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"
2.  "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"
3.  "Groundwater Monitoring Report - July 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California"

These reports presented analytical results from groundwater monitoring well sampling conducted in May, June, and July 2012, respectively. Adams specifically noted that the June 2012 report detailed the installation of monitoring wells [[mw-9]] and [[mw-10]]. [[mw-9]] is situated south of the [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] and west of [[sr-99]], while [[mw-10]] is located south of the [[kansas-avenue-off-ramp]] and east of [[sr-99]].

Adams's review also encompassed the "Groundwater Monitoring Report, California Department of Transportation Modesto Soil Stockpiles - State Route 99 and 132, March 2012, Modesto, California," prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated May 2012. This report presented analytical results from groundwater monitoring well sampling conducted on March 12, 2012, October 5, 2006, and June 14, 2006. The monitoring wells were installed in 2006 to monitor groundwater quality in the vicinity of three soil stockpiles. The Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) collected "split" water samples during the March 12, 2012 sampling event.

Adams's role as DTSC's Task Order Manager is further evidenced by his authorship of the DTSC response letter dated November 29, 2012, regarding groundwater monitoring reports for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project. This letter was issued under the authority of the "Caltrans/Department of Toxic Substances Control Interagency Agreement No. 43A0260 Task Order No. 6."

The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings generated during Caltrans's construction of a segment of [[sr-99]] north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. This excavation traversed a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel acquired from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC). An evaporation pond was situated in the southernmost corner of this parcel. FMC and its predecessors operated as a chemical manufacturing company that processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores, among other minerals. The primary chemicals of concern identified for the site are [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]].

On May 18, 2015, Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist for the Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program at DTSC, authored a cover letter to Ms. Grace Magsayo, P.E., Project Manager for Caltrans. This letter transmitted revised documents for inclusion as appendices in the [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]] for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project. The enclosed documents were Appendix B, Administrative Record; Appendix C, Statement of Reasons; and Appendix D, Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility. Adams indicated that following the addition of these appendices, the Draft Final RAP would be referenced in the Caltrans Draft Environmental Impact Report (EIR) and made available for public review and comment concurrently with the Draft EIR.

## Regulatory status

As the DTSC reviewer, Adams conveyed the agency's concurrence with the reviewed groundwater monitoring reports. His letter also included a directive: DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available. DTSC concurred with the Groundwater Monitoring Report, dated May 2012, and requested that future groundwater monitoring reports include a discussion comparing groundwater monitoring results/concentration trends from previous sampling events as more data becomes available. At a minimum, groundwater monitoring should continue on a quarterly schedule.

The letter was issued under the letterhead of Deborah O. Raphael, Director of DTSC, and was copied to Sam Haack (Caltrans District 10 Project Manager), Nicole Damin (Senior Hazardous Materials Specialist with the Stanislaus County Health Agency), and Steven Meeks (Senior Water Resources Control Engineer).
