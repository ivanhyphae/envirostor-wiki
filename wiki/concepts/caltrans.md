The California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) is the state agency responsible for planning, designing, constructing, operating, and maintaining the state highway system. In the context of the Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Caltrans acted as the responsible party for the remediation and management of the stockpiles, integrating them into the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway project.

## Site Description

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles consist of three distinct areas, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil, located south of Kansas Avenue in Modesto, California. These stockpiles originated from soil excavated during the construction of the State Route 99 Modesto bypass in the early 1960s. The soil was sourced from unlined ponds on the Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) Modesto Processing Plant property, where residual liquids from chemical processing were discharged. The primary contaminants of concern identified in the stockpiles are barium, strontium, and lead.

*   **Stockpile 1:** Approximately 2.5 acres, located south of Kansas Avenue between Carpenter and North Emerald Avenue. Estimated fill volume of 34,000 cubic yards.
*   **Stockpile 2:** Approximately 7.6 acres, located south of Kansas Avenue between North Emerald Avenue and State Route 99. Estimated fill volume of 102,000 cubic yards.
*   **Stockpile 3:** Approximately 2.5 acres, located south of Kansas Avenue and east of State Route 99. This stockpile was completely excavated and placed as highway embankment fill on top of Stockpile 1 and between Stockpile 1 and Emerald Avenue during Interim Phase 1 construction.

## Contaminants of Concern

The primary contaminants of concern identified in the stockpiles are barium, strontium, and lead.

*   **Barium Concentrations:**
    *   Stockpile 1: Ranges from 37 to 1,700 parts per million (ppm), with an average of 82 ppm. Higher concentrations were found deeper than 6 feet below ground surface.
    *   Stockpile 2: Ranges from 49 to 64,000 ppm, with an average of 5,440 ppm. Higher concentrations were found deeper than 6 feet below ground surface.
    *   Stockpile 3: Ranges from 35 to 126,000 ppm, with an average of 4,310 ppm. Higher concentrations were found deeper than 6 feet below ground surface.
    *   State action levels for Barium are 5,200 ppm for residential uses and 63,000 ppm for commercial uses.
    *   Modesto ambient environment barium concentrations range from 17 to 120 ppm.
*   **Lead Concentrations:** Elevated lead concentrations were also identified. Aerially Deposited Lead (ADL)-contaminated soil, with lead concentrations exceeding regulatory criteria, was excavated from SR 99 shoulder areas and placed in the eastern end of the Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone in June and July 2020, in accordance with a DTSC-Caltrans Soil Management Agreement.

## Remedial Action and Containment

The selected remedial alternative was consolidation and containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering them with clean fill and structural pavement.

*   **Interim Phase 1 (2019-2022):** Stockpiles 1 and 2 were consolidated and capped as highway embankment fill during the construction of the SR 132 West Project. SR 132 was opened to traffic on November 3, 2022.
*   **Ultimate Phase 2 (Scheduled for completion in 2028):** This phase will involve highway widening to four traffic lanes, completing the final containment of Barium-Containing Soil (BCS).

Containment measures include:
*   Incorporation of stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath highway pavement.
*   Covering unpaved portions with clean fill, vegetated ground cover, or erosion control blankets.
*   Maintenance of perimeter fencing to restrict access.

## Regulatory Oversight and Approvals

The project was conducted under the oversight of the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and in cooperation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

*   **Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA):** A 2007 HHRA and a 2013 HHRA Update indicated that the soil stockpiles did not pose an unacceptable risk to current or future offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical future shallow groundwater users.
*   **DTSC Letter (December 17, 2009):** DTSC stated that the stockpiles, as managed, did not pose a risk to human health and directed Caltrans to continue managing the stockpiles by limiting access, maintaining fencing, prohibiting excavation/removal, and maintaining vegetative cover and grade. Groundwater monitoring was also to be maintained.
*   **Remedial Action Plan (RAP):** The Final RAP, approved by DTSC, designated containment as the selected remedy.
*   **Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP):** This plan provided the technical approach for the interim implementation of the selected remedy.
*   **Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR):** Submitted by Geocon Consultants, Inc. in December 2022, documenting completed consolidation and clean fill capping. This report was accepted by DTSC on February 24, 2022.
*   **Land Use Covenant (LUC):** A LUC will be recorded to restrict land use to transportation purposes and prohibit unrestricted uses on the contained stockpiles.
*   **Operation and Maintenance (O&M):** Caltrans will enter into an O&M Agreement with DTSC, requiring annual inspections of containment measures and periodic five-year reviews by DTSC.

## Groundwater and Stormwater Monitoring

Groundwater monitoring wells historically present near the stockpiles were sampled periodically. Six wells were destroyed due to construction conflicts, while four were protected in place. Groundwater monitoring continued through March 2022, with reported analyte concentrations below regulatory thresholds. Stormwater sampling events conducted between December 2019 and December 2020 showed results consistent with historical data, with no observed offsite migration. Additional stormwater sampling will continue until Ultimate Phase 2 construction is complete.

## Open Items

*   A Final RACR will be submitted after the completion of Ultimate Phase 2 construction in 2028.
*   Groundwater monitoring will continue until the CVRWQCB determines the clean-capped material poses no material threat to groundwater quality.
*   Caltrans will finalize and record the LUC.
*   Caltrans will enter into an O&M Agreement with DTSC.
