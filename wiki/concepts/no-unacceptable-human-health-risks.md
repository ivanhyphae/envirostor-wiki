## No Unacceptable Human Health Risks

### Overview
The determination of "no unacceptable human health risks" is a conclusion reached by Caltrans and regulatory agencies regarding the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. This conclusion is based on the evaluation of potential risks posed by contaminants identified in the soil stockpiles, considering various exposure scenarios and the implementation of specific remedial actions.

### Site Record
The Final Feasibility Study Report for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, dated June 2014, evaluated potential human health risks. The report concluded that with the implementation of the selected [[containment-remedial-action]], no unacceptable human health risks would remain. This conclusion was based on the assumption that the soil stockpiles would be managed and disposed of in accordance with the proposed remedial action plan, which included measures to prevent direct contact and migration of contaminants.

### Regulatory Status
The determination of no unacceptable human health risks is a key component in the regulatory closure process for the site. This determination allows for the approval of the selected remedial action by regulatory agencies, such as the [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. The selected remedy, which involves the management and potential reuse of approximately [[160000-cubic-yards-of-fill]], is designed to ensure that residual contaminants, such as [[arsenic]], [[barium]], and [[chromium]], do not pose a threat to human health or the environment. The [[california-mcl]]s were considered in the risk assessment process.
