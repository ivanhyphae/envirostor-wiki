## Overview

[[stanislaus-county]], California, is the location of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project, a component of the larger SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. The county is involved as a project partner, and key infrastructure and facilities within its jurisdiction are part of the project area. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and [[caltrans]] are the primary regulatory and oversight agencies for the soil stockpiles.

## Site Record

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are situated within Stanislaus County, south of Kansas Avenue and west of State Route (SR) 99. The stockpiles occupy a portion of Caltrans' right-of-way (ROW) approximately 350 feet south of the Kansas Avenue overcrossing of SR-99 in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The stockpiles extend approximately 2,500 feet west of SR-99 and approximately 500 feet east of SR-99. The three stockpiles total approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, primarily [[barium]], [[lead]], and strontium, as well as aromatic hydrocarbons. The primary contaminant of concern identified for metals in the soil stockpiles and groundwater under the soil stockpiles is barium. Other metals in the soil stockpiles include lead, cadmium, strontium, and arsenic. The primary contaminant of concern for organics identified in the soil stockpiles is aromatic hydrocarbons.

The stockpiles were created in the early 1960s by importing soil from a Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility that was located less than 500 feet north of the Site.

The selected remedial alternative to mitigate the barium-containing (and other contaminants of concern) soil stockpiles is containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering with pavement and/or clean fill during construction of the SR-132/SR-99 interchange portion of the planned SR-132 Project. This action would avoid potential future impacts to human health and the environment.

## Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) have reviewed and approved the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the stockpiles.

The Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Update, prepared by Geocon Consultants Inc. and revised March 1, 2013, concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or noncancer hazard to persons in the vicinity of these stockpiles as long as the stockpiles remain in place and are properly managed. Proper management implies that Caltrans would continue current management practices, including maintaining fencing and signage, limiting access, avoiding soil disturbance, maintaining vegetative cover to reduce erosion, mowing to minimize fire danger, and continuing groundwater and stormwater runoff monitoring. DTSC concurred with the findings of the HHRA Update in a letter dated April 4, 2013.
