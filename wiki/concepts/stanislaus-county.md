## Stanislaus County

Stanislaus County is the location of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project, a component of the larger SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. The county is involved as a project partner, and key infrastructure and facilities within its jurisdiction are part of the project area. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and [[caltrans]] are the primary regulatory and oversight agencies for the soil stockpiles.

## Site Record

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are situated within Stanislaus County, south of Kansas Avenue and west of State Route (SR) 99.{{quote: "The stockpiles occupy a portion of Caltrans' right-of-way (ROW) approximately 350 feet south of the Kansas Avenue overcrossing of SR-99 in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The stockpiles extend approximately 2,500 feet west of SR-99 and approximately 500 feet east of SR-99."}} The total volume of the three stockpiles is approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil.{{quote: "The three stockpiles total approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals (primarily barium, lead, and strontium)."}} These stockpiles contain metals, primarily [[barium]], [[lead]], and strontium, as well as aromatic hydrocarbons.{{quote: "The primary contaminant of concern identified for metals in the soil stockpiles and groundwater under the soil stockpiles is barium. Other metals in the soil stockpiles include, lead, cadmium, strontium, and arsenic. The primary contaminant of concern for organics identified in the soil stockpiles is aromatic hydrocarbons."}}

The stockpiles were established in the early 1960s using soil imported from a [[fmc-corporation]] facility.{{quote: "The stockpiles were created in the early–1960s by importing soil from a Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility that was located less than 500 feet north of the Site."}}

The proposed remediation strategy, detailed in the Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP), involves containment by integrating the stockpiles into the highway embankment fill and covering them with pavement and/or clean fill.{{quote: "The selected remedial alternative to mitigate the barium-containing (and other contaminants of concern) soil stockpiles is containment by incorporating the stockpiles as highway embankment fill and covering with pavement and/or clean fill during construction of the SR-132/SR-99 interchange portion of the planned SR-132 Project."}} This approach is intended to prevent future impacts to human health and the environment.{{quote: "This action would avoid potential future impacts to human health and the environment."}}

## Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) have reviewed and approved the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the stockpiles.{{quote: "As required by state law, the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) have reviewed the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the stockpiles and approved it for public noticing."}}

The [[human-health-risk-assessment]] (HHRA) conducted for the site concluded that the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or noncancer hazard to nearby individuals, provided they remain in place and are managed appropriately.{{quote: "The HERO memorandum stated: '*the soil stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or noncancer hazard to persons in the vicinity of these stockpiles as long as the stockpiles remain in place and are properly managed.'"}} Proper management entails maintaining fencing and signage, restricting access, avoiding soil disturbance, preserving vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater and stormwater runoff monitoring.{{quote: "Being "properly managed" implies that Caltrans would continue the current management which includes: maintaining fencing and signage around the stockpiles thereby limiting access to the stockpiles, not disturbing or exposing soil in the stockpiles, maintaining vegetative cover to reduce potential wind and rain soil erosion and transport offsite (i.e. soil dust transport from wind and sediment laden surface water runoff), mowing the vegetative cover to minimize fire danger, and groundwater and stormwater runoff monitoring."}} DTSC concurred with the findings of the HHRA Update.{{quote: "In a letter dated April 4, 2013, DTSC stated their concurrence with the findings of the HHRA Update as follows: '*DTSC concurs with reports titled "SSI, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County, California" (Geocon, March 1, 2013) and "HHRA Update, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County, California."* "}}

## Agencies Involved

Key agencies involved in the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation include:
*   [[caltrans]] (Lead Agency for CEQA/NEPA Compliance)
*   [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (Responsible Agency for Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles)
*   Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) (Responsible Agency for Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles)
*   [[fmc-corporation]] (Originator of the soil)
*   [[geocon-consultants]] (Prepared the Final Remedial Action Plan)
*   Stanislaus Council of Governments (StanCOG) (Project Sponsor)
*   City of Modesto (Project Partner)
