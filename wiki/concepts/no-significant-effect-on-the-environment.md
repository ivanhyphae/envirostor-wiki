## No Significant Effect On The Environment

### Overview
The determination of "No Significant Effect On The Environment" is a finding made under the [[california-environmental-quality-act]] (CEQA) for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project. This signifies that the project, as proposed and implemented, is not expected to cause substantial or potentially significant adverse environmental impacts. This conclusion is documented within the CEQA review process and leads to the issuance of a [[notice-of-determination]].

### Site Record
For ramp safety improvements along State Route (SR) 99 at Kansas Avenue in Modesto, [[caltrans]] conducted additional investigations at Stockpile No. 3. The results of these investigations, along with the proposed management of the excavated material, demonstrated that an Interim Removal Action Workplan was not necessary for the ramp project.

An estimated 6,000 cubic yards of material were to be excavated for the widened roadbed, drainage, and retaining wall. Approximately 2,800 cubic yards of this material were to be sourced from the north-western end of Stockpile No. 3. Some of the excavated native material was designated for reuse as backfill for the ramp construction. The remaining excavated material, including all material from Stockpile No. 3, was to be transported offsite.

Based on site investigation data presented in the document "Transmittal of Site Investigation Data, Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project SR 99 - Kansas Avenue Northbound Off-Ramp, Modesto California," dated April 24, 2012, soil samples from the area to be excavated in Stockpile No. 3 did not contain total metal concentrations at or above the California hazardous waste Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC). With the exception of arsenic, metals were not reported at concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial screening levels. Arsenic concentrations in samples ranged from less than 1.0 mg/kg to 3.2 mg/kg, which is within the range of site-specific background levels. The 95% upper confidence limit concentrations for barium, strontium, and lead in the excavation area of Stockpile No. 3 were below residential and commercial/industrial screening levels.

While one soil sample showed a barium concentration greater than 10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) and five soil samples had lead concentrations at or above 10 times the STLC, the calculated 95% Upper Confidence Level for barium and lead was well below 10 times the respective STLC. Further analysis of these samples by Advanced Technology Laboratories using the Waste Extraction Test (WET) indicated solubility levels below the STLC, meaning the material was not classified as hazardous waste.

The native soil material proposed for excavation outside the footprint of Stockpile No. 3 was deemed suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as non-hazardous soil at an accepting facility.

### Regulatory Status
The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC), acting as a CEQA Responsible Agency, approved the project on March 14, 2018, and determined that the project would not have a significant effect on the environment. This determination allows for the issuance of a [[notice-of-determination]].

[[caltrans]], as the Lead Agency under CEQA, certified the [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) on March 2, 2018. DTSC utilized this EIR/EA for its CEQA analysis related to the [[remedial-action-plan]].
