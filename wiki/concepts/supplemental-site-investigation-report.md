The **Supplemental Site Investigation Report** (SSI Report) is a document prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. This report was developed under Caltrans Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order Number 44, and Expense Authorization 10-403500. The primary purpose of the SSI Report was to conduct further soil characterization, specifically to delineate the lateral and vertical extent of metals, particularly [[barium]], within the soil stockpiles. The data collected was intended to support an update to the 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA).

## Site record
The SSI investigation involved three types of borings: Fenceline Borings, Perimeter Borings, and Cadmium Borings. Soil samples from these borings were analyzed for Title 22 metals. A project meeting held on November 16, 2012, with representatives from Caltrans, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), and Geocon, reviewed preliminary analytical findings.

Key findings from the preliminary data review included:
*   **Barium**: Concentrations in all soil samples from the Fenceline and Perimeter Borings were reported below the residential California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL). The data also indicated that barium concentrations decreased with depth. Based on these results, the meeting participants determined that no further environmental assessment of the lateral and vertical extent of barium-impacted soil in the stockpiles was necessary at that time. {{quote: "Based on reported barium concentrations below the residential CHHSL for all of the soil samples obtained from the Fenceline and Perimeter Borings and decreasing barium concentrations with depth, additional environmental assessment of the lateral and vertical extent of barium-impacted soil stockpile is not warranted at this time."}}
*   **Cadmium**: Concentrations in all soil samples from the Fenceline and Cadmium Borings were reported below the residential CHHSL of 1.7 mg/kg. Consequently, [[cadmium]] was removed as a chemical of potential concern (COPC) for the project site. {{quote: "Based on reported cadmium concentrations below the residential CHHSL of 1.7 mg/kg in all of the soil samples obtained from the Fenceline and Cadmium Borings, cadmium is not considered a COPC for the project site."}}

The soil analytical data from these borings were designated for use in updating the Shaw 2007 HHRA, a task that Geocon was undertaking. The SSI Report also included a review of historical information to assess the potential for barium-impacted pond tailings and soil from the [[fmc-corporation]] evaporation ponds to have been placed outside the Caltrans right-of-way (ROW) during the construction of the soil stockpiles. This historical review is detailed in Section 2.2 and presented on Figure 3 of the report. Unused soil samples from the Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Borings were retained by Geocon and the analytical laboratory (ATL) under chain-of-custody protocol, within the six-month holding time for metals, for potential further analysis.

## Regulatory status
The preliminary findings and proposed actions from the November 16, 2012, meeting were documented pending the formal submittal and regulatory review of the SSI Report by DTSC and CVRWQCB. The SSI Report served as a basis for the subsequent HHRA update, which was reviewed by DTSC's Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO). DTSC identified a discrepancy in report titles: the HHRA report referenced "State Routes 99 and 132," while the SSI Report title was "State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project." DTSC requested that the HHRA title be revised for consistency with the SSI Report. {{quote: "DTSC notes that the title of the HHRA report is slightly different than the title of the SSI report (e.g., the SSI includes "Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County" in the title and the HHRA includes "Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Routes 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California" in the title). Please revise the HHRA title to be consistent with the SSI."}}

HERO concluded that the investigation of the soil stockpiles appeared to encompass all toxic chemicals associated with the FMC facility, including [[arsenic]], [[barium]], petroleum coke constituents (such as [[nickel]] and [[vanadium]]), and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Based on surface soil concentrations, HERO's overall conclusion was that the stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard provided they remain in place and are managed appropriately. {{quote: "The overall HERO conclusion, based on surface soil concentrations, was that the stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard as long as they remain in place and are properly managed."}}

## Open items
*   The title of the HHRA update needed to be revised to align with the title of the SSI Report, as directed by DTSC. {{quote: "Please revise the HHRA title to be consistent with the SSI."}}
*   HERO noted that if significant grading, reworking, or removal of the stockpiles were to occur, the elevated chemical concentrations found at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would require evaluation concerning potential exposure to nearby residents during soil disturbance activities. {{quote: "HERO identified that if substantial grading, reworking, or removal of the stockpiles occurs, the elevated concentrations of chemicals at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would require evaluation with respect to potential exposure to adjacent or nearby residents during soil disturbance activities."}}
