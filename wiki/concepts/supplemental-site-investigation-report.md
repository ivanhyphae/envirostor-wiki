# Supplemental Site Investigation Report

## Overview
The **Supplemental Site Investigation Report** (SSI Report) is a document prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. under caltrans-district-6|Caltrans Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order Number 44, and Expense Authorization 10-403500, to further characterize soil conditions at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California). The report presents additional soil sampling and analytical data collected to evaluate the lateral and vertical extent of metals—particularly barium—in the stockpile-1|soil stockpiles and to support an update of the 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA).

The SSI Report is formally titled: *Supplemental Site Investigation Report, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County*.

## Site record
The SSI investigation included three categories of borings: **Fenceline Borings**, **Perimeter Borings**, and **Cadmium Borings**. Soil samples were analyzed for Title 22 metals. Key preliminary analytical findings reviewed at a project meeting on November 16, 2012, attended by representatives of Caltrans, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), and Geocon, include:

- **Barium**: Concentrations were reported below the residential California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) for all soil samples obtained from the Fenceline and Perimeter Borings. Barium concentrations were observed to decrease with depth. Based on these findings, the meeting participants concluded that additional environmental assessment of the lateral and vertical extent of barium-impacted soil in the stockpiles was not warranted at the time.

- **Cadmium**: Concentrations were reported below the residential CHHSL of 1.7 mg/kg in all soil samples obtained from the Fenceline and Cadmium Borings. As a result, [[cadmium]] was removed from consideration as a chemical of potential concern (COPC) for the project site.

The soil analytical data generated from the Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Borings were designated for use in updating the Shaw 2007 HHRA, a task Geocon was preparing at the time of the SSI Report.

The SSI Report also includes a review of historical information to determine the potential for barium-impacted pond tailings and soil excavated from the fmc-corporation|FMC Corporation evaporation ponds to have been deposited outside the Caltrans right-of-way (ROW) during placement of the soil stockpiles. This information is presented in Section 2.2 and on Figure 3 of the report.

Unused soil samples from the Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Borings were retained by Geocon and the analytical laboratory (ATL) under chain-of-custody protocol, within the six-month holding time for metals, pending potential additional laboratory analysis.

## Regulatory status
The preliminary findings and action items from the November 16, 2012, meeting were documented pending submittal and regulatory review of the SSI Report. The regulatory agencies involved in the review included DTSC and CVRWQCB. The SSI Report was subsequently used as a basis for the HHRA update, which was reviewed by DTSC's Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO). DTSC noted a discrepancy in the report titles—the HHRA report referenced "State Routes 99 and 132" while the SSI Report referenced "State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project"—and requested that the HHRA title be revised to match the SSI Report title.

HERO concluded that the investigation of the soil stockpiles appeared to include all chemicals used at and produced by the FMC facility that DTSC considers toxic, including arsenic, barium, petroleum coke constituents (such as [[nickel]] and vanadium), and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). The overall HERO conclusion, based on surface soil concentrations, was that the stockpiles do not pose a cancer risk or non-cancer hazard as long as they remain in place and are properly managed.

## Open items
- The HHRA update title was required to be revised to match the SSI Report title per DTSC direction.
- HERO identified that if substantial grading, reworking, or removal of the stockpiles occurs, the elevated concentrations of chemicals at depths greater than one foot below ground surface would require evaluation with respect to potential exposure to adjacent or nearby residents during soil disturbance activities.
