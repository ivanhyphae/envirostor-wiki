The Supplemental Site Investigation Report (SSI Report, Rev. 0313) was prepared by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] under Caltrans Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order 44, EA 10-403500, to close open questions about the lateral and vertical extent of metals-impacted soil in the stockpiles at the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site along SR 132. It followed a round of Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium borings and was reviewed at a joint project meeting with Caltrans, [[dtsc]], and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] on November 16, 2012, before the report itself was finalized, so the report documents findings that regulators had already previewed and accepted in preliminary form.

## Findings on barium and cadmium

The November 16, 2012 meeting reviewed preliminary soil analytical data from the Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Borings and reached two determinations that the SSI Report carried forward: barium concentrations in all Fenceline and Perimeter Boring samples were below the residential CHHSL and decreased with depth, so no additional investigation of the lateral or vertical extent of barium-impacted soil was considered warranted; and cadmium concentrations in all Fenceline and Cadmium Boring samples were below the residential CHHSL of 1.7 mg/kg, so cadmium was dropped as a contaminant of potential concern (COPC) for the site. These findings narrowed the scope of further investigation by removing cadmium from consideration entirely and closing out barium delineation as an open task.

## Historical deposition review

As an action item from the same meeting, the report examined historical information on whether barium-impacted pond tailings and soil excavated from the FMC evaporation ponds could have been deposited outside the Caltrans right-of-way during stockpile placement. This review is presented in Section 2.2 and on Figure 3 of the report, tying the stockpiles' contamination history to the adjacent FMC ponds as a potential off-ROW source question.

## HHRA update and sample retention

The soil analytical data from the Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Borings were designated for use in updating the 2007 Shaw human health risk assessment (HHRA), which Geocon was preparing concurrently. DTSC's review of the resulting HHRA update flagged a title inconsistency: the SSI Report's title refers to "State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project," while the HHRA title referred to "State Routes 99 and 132," and DTSC directed that the HHRA title be revised to match the SSI Report. As an additional action item, Geocon and the analytical laboratory (ATL) retained unused soil samples from the Fenceline, Perimeter, and Cadmium Borings under chain-of-custody protocol, preserving them for further metals analysis within the six-month holding time.
