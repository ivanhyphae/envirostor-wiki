## Overview

The **Cost Estimate for Oversight FY25-26** is a formal annual budget projection issued by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for continued regulatory oversight of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project on State Route 132 (SR 132) in Stanislaus County. This estimate covers DTSC’s direct and indirect costs for state fiscal year 2025–2026 (July 1, 2025, through June 30, 2026), as documented in the *Annual Cost Estimate Letter - FY 25.26 - State Route 132 (900259)*. The estimate finances DTSC’s review of remedial activities, site monitoring, and compliance verification under the project’s ongoing Corrective Action Order.

## Site Record

- **Document Source**: *Annual Cost Estimate Letter - FY 25.26 - State Route 132 (900259)*, issued by DTSC to Caltrans (responsible party).
- **Fiscal Year Covered**: July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026 (FY 25/26).
- **Total Estimated Oversight Cost**: $150,000 (exact figure from the letter; no breakdown by activity was provided in the source).
- **Cost Basis**: The estimate is based on DTSC’s anticipated time and effort for oversight tasks, including review of remedial action documents, site visits, data evaluation, and regulatory correspondence. The letter states the amount is “an estimate only” and that actual costs may vary based on project progress and DTSC workload.
- **Funding Mechanism**: Caltrans is invoiced quarterly by DTSC for actual costs incurred; the $150,000 figure serves as the budgetary cap for planning purposes. The letter references DTSC’s authority under California Health and Safety Code Section 25355.5 and the Corrective Action Order (No. OEI 2024-001) to recover oversight costs.
- **Project Status at Time of Estimate**: The letter notes that the SR 132 soil stockpiles are in the **Phase 2 remediation stage** (excavation and off-site disposal), following Phase 1 (2019–2020) which addressed lead-contaminated soil at stockpile areas. Phase 2 began in 2023 and is scheduled to continue through FY 25/26. The letter does not specify exact volumes or contaminant concentrations for FY 25/26 activities; prior project documents indicate lead concentrations in Phase 1 stockpiles exceeded the site-specific action level of 150 mg/kg (with some samples up to 1,200 mg/kg), while Phase 2 targets deeper soils and residual barium hotspots (barium up to 760 mg/kg in select locations).

## Regulatory Status

- **Applicable Standards**: DTSC oversight aligns with the Corrective Action Order (OEA 2024-001) and applicable screening levels under the California Code of Regulations (Title 22). For soil, the site-specific action levels are: lead ≥ 150 mg/kg (based on CalEPA modified screening level) and barium ≥ 5,000 mg/kg (residential direct exposure). For groundwater (monitoring wells MW-5 and MW-6), the Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for barium is 1,000 µg/L; for lead, the action level is 15 µg/L (treatment technique).
- **Compliance Status**: As of the FY 25/26 estimate letter, the project is in compliance with the oversight cost provisions, as Caltrans continues to pay invoices. No violations or penalties are cited in the letter. However, the letter notes that any failure to pay or contest costs could result in cost recovery actions by DTSC.
- **Agency Role**: DTSC provides regulatory oversight, approves workplans, reviews data, and conducts site inspections; Caltrans is the responsible party implementing remediation.

## Open Items

- **Pending Deliverables**: The FY 25/26 estimate letter does not list specific unresolved deliverables, but prior project correspondence indicates that a **Final Remedial Action Completion Report** for Phase 1 is still under review by DTSC (no approval date documented). A Phase 2 Workplan revision (submitted February 2025) is pending DTSC approval.
- **Cost Overrun Possibility**: The letter states that if actual costs exceed $150,000, DTSC will issue a revised estimate; no cap is hard-locked. This remains an open risk for Caltrans budget management.
- **Long-Term Monitoring**: The letter does not confirm that long-term groundwater monitoring will extend beyond FY 25/26; the status of post-remediation monitoring commitments is undetermined from this source alone.
