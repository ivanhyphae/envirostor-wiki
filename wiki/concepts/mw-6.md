# MW-6

## Overview
MW-6 is a groundwater monitoring well at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). It is one of ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10) installed to assess groundwater conditions adjacent to three soil stockpiles generated during SR 99 construction around 1961. The well is located south of Kansas Avenue, in the vicinity of Stockpile 2 (south of Kansas Avenue, between North Emerald Avenue and SR 99). MW-6 was installed as part of the original well network (MW-1 through MW-8) in 2006. The well monitors shallow groundwater in the area downgradient of the stockpiles, and its geochemical signature is consistently sodium-dominant, distinguishing it from calcium-dominant wells such as MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10.

## Site record

### Well condition and field observations
During the September 4, 2013 pre-sampling inspection, the concrete and well box for MW-6 appeared damaged. The September 2013 monitoring report notes: "We believe that the damage occurred when the well box was run over with a lawnmower. The well casing did not appear damaged." The well box was replaced on September 23, 2013, following authorization. Locks on the well were also replaced as part of the September 2013 sampling event.

### Groundwater geochemistry
Groundwater sampled from MW-6 in the September 2013 event was classified as sodium-dominant based on Stiff diagram analysis. This is consistent with historical patterns — water samples from MW-6, along with those from MW-3 and MW-8, have consistently been reported as sodium-dominant across the 2012 through September 2013 sampling events. This contrasts with calcium-dominant wells (MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, MW-10) and with MW-5, which shifted from historically calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant in September 2013.

### Nitrate as nitrogen
Nitrate as nitrogen concentrations in MW-6 equaled or exceeded the primary MCL for nitrate of 10 mg/L during the September 2013 sampling event. MW-6 is one of five wells (along with MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, and MW-10) where this exceedance was reported.

### Total dissolved solids
TDS concentrations in MW-6 exceeded the secondary MCL of 500 mg/L in September 2013. MW-6 is one of five wells (along with MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, and MW-10) where TDS exceeded this threshold.

### Sulfide
Sulfide was reported in the MW-6 sample at a concentration of 0.062 mg/L, based on laboratory analysis by EPA method.

### Dissolved metals
No dissolved metals concentrations in the MW-6 groundwater sample collected in September 2013 exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values. Barium and strontium were reported at concentrations similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than numeric water quality thresholds. Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the practical quantitation limit of 1.0 µg/L. The laboratory analytical report from the September 2013 sampling event (Lab ID: 1302710-03, Client Sample ID: MW-6) includes dissolved metals analysis by ICP-MS (EPA 6020) and dissolved mercury by cold vapor AA (EPA 7470A).

### Cation-anion balance
A cation-anion balance analysis was performed on the MW-6 sample as part of the laboratory reporting for the September 2013 event.

## Regulatory status
The September 2013 sampling event compared analytical results to Primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) promulgated by the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) and Secondary MCLs promulgated by CDPH. For MW-6, nitrate as nitrogen was reported at concentrations equaling or exceeding the primary MCL of 10 mg/L, and TDS was reported at concentrations exceeding the secondary MCL of 500 mg/L. Dissolved metals remained below all applicable numeric water quality threshold values. The exceedances for nitrate and TDS are documented but have not been attributed to the stockpiles; the September 2013 report notes that MW-1, an upgradient well, also showed nitrate and TDS exceedances, suggesting natural background concentrations for shallow groundwater in the Site vicinity.
