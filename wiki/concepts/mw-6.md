# Mw 6

MW-6 is one of the eight original 2006-era groundwater monitoring wells at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, positioned downgradient of the former FMC facility and consistently the sodium-dominant, highest-TDS, highest-nitrate well in the network from the resumption of quarterly monitoring in March 2012 through at least 2016. It is also the only well in the network that repeatedly produced dissolved manganese exceedances of the secondary MCL and the well most frequently affected by low water yield, going dry during purging in multiple sampling rounds and eventually running dry outright by mid-2015.

## Water type and general minerals

Stiff diagram analyses classified MW-6 as sodium-dominant, bicarbonate-type water in every round from March 2012 through June 2014 — consistently, unlike several other wells (MW-5, MW-9, MW-10) that shifted classification over time. Geocon's geochemical interpretation associated the sodium-dominant signature at MW-6 with locations coinciding with the area downgradient of the former FMC facility, distinguishing it from the calcium-dominant water beneath Stockpiles 1 and 2.

MW-6 was also one of the wells most frequently exceeding the secondary MCL for TDS (500 mg/l) and the primary MCL for nitrate as nitrogen (10 mg/l) across nearly every monitoring round for which well-specific data are reported:

| Date | Nitrate as N (mg/l) | TDS (mg/l) |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2012 | 18 | — |
| May 2012 | 18 | — (exceeded 500) |
| Nov 2012 | — | exceeded 500 |
| Jan 2013 | — | exceeded 500 |
| Mar 2013 | — | exceeded 500 |
| Jun 2013 | 20 | 620 |
| Sep 2013 | — | exceeded 500 |
| Dec 2013 | — | exceeded 500 |
| Feb 2014 | — | exceeded 500 |
| Jun 2014 | — | exceeded 500 |

Both thresholds were exceeded or equaled at MW-6 in nearly all rounds where it could be sampled, in contrast to the upgradient well MW-1, whose comparable exceedances were treated by Geocon as possible natural background. No such background caveat was applied to MW-6 in the reports reviewed.

## Dissolved metals and manganese exceedances

MW-6 was the site's recurring source of dissolved manganese detections above the secondary MCL of 50 µg/l, an exceedance pattern not shared by most other wells in the network:

| Date | Manganese finding |
|---|---|
| Jan 2013 | Detected; exceeded secondary MCL (only well with a manganese exceedance that round) |
| Dec 2013 | Detected; exceeded secondary MCL (only well with a manganese exceedance that round) |

In the March 2012 baseline round, manganese was detected at MW-4 rather than MW-6, but by January 2013 and again in December 2013, MW-6 had become the well reporting the manganese exceedance. Aside from manganese, dissolved metals at MW-6 — including barium, strontium, arsenic, chromium, vanadium, and other Title 22 metals — were consistently reported at concentrations similar to historical levels and below their numeric water quality thresholds. Lead was reported once at MW-6, at 1.3 µg/l in December 2013, below its water quality threshold.

In the March 2012 round, mercury was reported at MW-6 among seven of eight wells, but the report qualified all mercury results site-wide as estimated and likely biased high because mercury was also detected at a similar concentration (0.31 µg/l) in the equipment blank. Molybdenum reported for the MW-6 sample that round was similarly qualified as estimated because molybdenum also appeared in the associated laboratory method blank.

## Well yield problems

MW-6 was one of the two wells (with MW-5) that most frequently went dry during purging, requiring recovery and re-purging before sampling could proceed:

- **September 2012:** went dry after purging 1.5 gallons; allowed to recover, purged an additional gallon, recovered again, then sampled.
- **November 2012:** went dry after purging 1 gallon; recovered, purged an additional 1.5 gallons, recovered again, then sampled.
- **January 2013:** went dry after purging 1.5 gallons; recovered, purged an additional 2 gallons, recovered again, then sampled.
- **March 2013:** went dry after purging 1.5 gallons; recovered, purged an additional 1.7 gallons, recovered again, then sampled.
- **June 2013:** went dry after purging 1.75 gallons; recovered, purged an additional 2.5 gallons, sampled; unlike MW-5, MW-6 was not subject to the surge-block redevelopment performed that round.

By September 2013, the well box and concrete pad at MW-6 were found damaged — attributed by Geocon to having been run over by a lawnmower — though the well casing itself was undamaged; the well box was replaced on September 23, 2013, along with a new lock.

By the October 2015 and May 2016 depth-to-groundwater rounds, MW-6 was reported dry, and Geocon explicitly attempted but was unable to purge or sample it in May 2016 due to insufficient water. This marks a transition from a well that could be purged dry and would recover within a monitoring visit (2012–2014) to one that no longer yielded sufficient water to sample at all.

## Later monitoring and decommissioning

By April 2019, MW-6 was still being sampled and continued to be classified as sodium-dominant, consistent with its behavior since 2012. According to DTSC's January 2026 letter respecting the Well Destruction Completion Report and Storm Water and Surface Water Monitoring Report (FY24/25), six of the original ten groundwater monitoring wells were decommissioned in 2019 to allow continued highway construction, and the remaining four were addressed in a subsequent Well Destruction Report reviewed by DTSC's Geological Services Branch. The letter does not identify MW-6 by name among either the six wells decommissioned in 2019 or the four addressed later, so its specific decommissioning date cannot be confirmed from the available material.
