## Class 3 Aggregate Subbase

### Overview
Class 3 aggregate subbase material was approved for use as a temporary clean cap alternative over [[barium-containing-soil-aerially-deposited-lead]] (BCS/ADL) stockpiles at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. This material is intended to remain in place until final pavement and hardscape are installed.

### Site Record
On September 15, 2020, a visual inspection of the Class 3 subbase material indicated that it was primarily a fine sand. {{quote: "a visual inspection of this stockpiled material conducted on September 15, 2020 revealed that it is primarily a fine sand with most of the grains likely small enough to pass through a No. 4 sieve."}}

### Regulatory Status
The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) agreed to the use of Class 3 subbase as an acceptable clean cap alternative. {{quote: "Both DTSC and the WB agree that Class 3 subbase can be used as an acceptable clean cap alternative"}} However, due to the fine-grained nature of the material observed during a visual inspection on September 15, 2020, a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches was mandated, an increase from the initially proposed 4 inches. {{quote: "Given its abundant fine grained nature, both agencies agree that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches rather than 4 inches is warranted"}}

DTSC and the WB requested that a single, composite sample be obtained for barium and lead analysis from the Class 3 material stockpile adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing, following Environmental Protection Agency Test Method 6010B. {{quote: "In addition, DTSC and the WB are requesting a single, composite sample be obtained for barium and lead analysis following Environmental Protection Agency Test Method 6010B from the stockpile of Class 3 material adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing."}} This analysis is required to ensure the material meets the standards set in the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) for use as a clean cap. {{quote: "This is necessary to ensure this soil meets the standards imposed in the RDIP since it may be used as Class 3 subbase clean cap over the BCS/ADL stockpiles."}}

DTSC officially accepted the request for the use of Class 3 subbase on September 21, 2020. {{quote: "This e-mail serves as DTSCs official acceptance of the Request for use of Class 3 subbase."}} This decision was documented in an e-mail regarding [[rdip-variance-request]] #2, which reviewed the Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan dated September 2, 2020. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) have reviewed the Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (Request) dated September 2, 2020."}}

### Open Items
The need for increased monitoring of the Class 3 subbase material was identified as a potential necessity. {{quote: "and that increased monitoring may be necessary."}} This is particularly relevant given that no pavement or hardscaping will be completed prior to the upcoming rainy season. {{quote: "This is especially true since no pavement or hard scaping will be completed prior to the upcoming rainy season."}}
