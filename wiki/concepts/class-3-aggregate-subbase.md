## Overview

**Class 3 Aggregate Subbase** is a granular construction material used in the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project (State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California). Under RDIP Variance #2 (Document S1908-01-01), Class 3 aggregate subbase was specified as a clean fill alternative to on-site soils for backfilling excavated areas after remediation of contaminated stockpiles. It meets California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) Standard Specifications (Section 26) for aggregate subbase, with quality control requirements to ensure no additional contamination is introduced to the site.

## Site record

- **Document reference**: RDIP Variance #2, dated January 2020, approved by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) on February 10, 2020.
- **Purpose**: Used as backfill material in remediated areas of Stockpiles 2, 3, and 4 (SR 132 right-of-way), following excavation of soils containing elevated arsenic, lead, and barium concentrations.
- **Specifications**: Class 3 aggregate subbase per Caltrans Standard Specifications Section 26, requiring maximum particle size of 1.5 inches, 100% passing the 1.5-inch sieve, and a plasticity index not exceeding 6. Sourced from Caltrans-approved quarries in Stanislaus County.
- **Volume employed**: Approximately 4,200 cubic yards of Class 3 aggregate subbase imported between March and May 2020 for backfilling after remediation of Stockpile 3 (arsenic up to 280 mg/kg, lead up to 450 mg/kg) and Stockpile 4 (barium up to 760 mg/kg).
- **Verification results**: Post-placement compaction testing (ASTM D1556) on May 15, 2020, showed 95% relative compaction at all five test locations (TP-1 through TP-5). No evidence of contamination in the aggregate subbase—grab samples collected on April 3, 2020, from truck deliveries showed arsenic <2.0 mg/kg, lead <5.0 mg/kg, and barium <50 mg/kg, per laboratory reports from Alpha Analytical (case file number 20200403-1).
- **Spatial distribution**: Applied as a 12-inch-thick base layer across 2.3 acres of backfilled areas, overlain by 6 inches of 3/4-inch crushed stone (Class 2 aggregate base) and 6 inches of imported topsoil.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standards**: Caltrans Standard Specifications Section 26 (Class 3 aggregate subbase), as incorporated by reference into the RDIP Variance #2. No California Title 22 hazardous waste criteria apply to the aggregate subbase itself (classified as inert material).
- **Agency oversight**: DTSC approved the variance on February 10, 2020, with condition that aggregate subbase be sampled (one per 500 cubic yards) for arsenic, lead, barium, and total petroleum hydrocarbons prior to placement. All 8 validation samples (from four quarry batches) were below DTSC screening levels: arsenic <12 mg/kg (SL 12 mg/kg), lead <80 mg/kg (SL 80 mg/kg), barium <670 mg/kg (SL 670 mg/kg), and TPH <10 mg/kg.
- **Compliance status**: Verified by DTSC in the Interim Remedial Action Completion Report (July 2020), confirming no exceedances of applicable standards for the aggregate subbase.

## Open items

- **Pending documentation**: The Final Remedial Action Report (due December 2021) must include a certificate of compliance for the Class 3 aggregate subbase from the quarry operator (Stanislaus Materials, Inc.). As of the last available document (Case File Review Memo, October 2021), this certificate was not yet submitted.
- **Potential issue**: Deep soil samples taken in Stockpile 4 in September 2020 detected barium at 1,200 mg/kg below the aggregate subbase layer (at 18–24 inches depth), exceeding the DTSC direct-exposure screening level (670 mg/kg). DTSC noted in a November 2020 meeting memo that the aggregate subbase may not have been thick enough to isolate this contamination from surface contact.
