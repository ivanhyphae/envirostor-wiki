## Class 3 Aggregate Subbase

### Overview
Class 3 aggregate subbase was approved as a temporary clean cap alternative for [[barium-containing-soil-aerially-deposited-lead]] (BCS/ADL) stockpiles at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. This material was intended to remain in place until final pavement and hardscape installation.

### Site Record
On September 15, 2020, a visual inspection of the Class 3 subbase material indicated it was primarily a fine sand, with most grains likely small enough to pass through a No. 4 sieve.

The specified structural pavement section for the Interim Phase I of the SR 132 West Project included 0.70 feet (8.4 inches) of Class 3 aggregate subbase.

### Regulatory Status
The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) agreed to the use of Class 3 subbase as an acceptable clean cap alternative. However, due to the fine-grained nature of the material observed during a visual inspection on September 15, 2020, a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches was mandated, an increase from the initially proposed 4 inches.

DTSC and the WB requested a single, composite sample be obtained for barium and lead analysis from the Class 3 material stockpile adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing, following Environmental Protection Agency Test Method 6010B. This analysis was required to ensure the material met the standards set in the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) for use as a clean cap.

DTSC officially accepted the request for the use of Class 3 subbase on September 21, 2020. This decision was documented in an e-mail regarding [[rdip-variance-request]] #2, which reviewed the Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan dated September 2, 2020.

### Open Items
The need for increased monitoring of the Class 3 subbase material was identified as a potential necessity, particularly since no pavement or hardscaping would be completed prior to the upcoming rainy season.
