# Barium

## Overview
Barium is the primary chemical of potential concern (COPC) at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. The soil stockpiles were generated in the early 1960s from excavation of evaporation ponds on property purchased from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC), where barite (barium sulfate) had been processed since the 1930s. Elevated barium concentrations were identified in Stockpiles 2 and 3 during a Preliminary Site Investigation conducted by Shaw Environmental, Inc. in 2004. Barium, along with lead and strontium, was designated by DTSC as a primary chemical of concern in groundwater for the site. Barium is tracked in both soil and groundwater across all three stockpile areas.

## Site record

### Source history
From the 1930s to 1970s, Barium Products Ltd. (later purchased by Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation, which merged with FMC in 1948) occupied property at 1200 Barium Road (now Graphics Drive) in Modesto, processing ores and minerals including barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate). From the 1950s to the 1970s, liquid residue from processing operations was discharged to unlined evaporation ponds along the western portion of the FMC site. In 1961, a 4.3-acre parcel including a portion of these ponds was purchased by the State of California for construction of SR 99. Soil in and around the ponds was excavated and placed in lifts to form bridge abutments and embankment fills, resulting in three distinct stockpiles totaling approximately 121,000 cubic yards.

### Soil concentrations

**Stockpile 3 – April 2012 Geocon investigation:**
Geocon Consultants, Inc. conducted 8 direct-push borings and 11 hand-auger borings within the northern end of Stockpile 3 on April 16, 2012. A total of 59 soil samples were analyzed for Title 22 metals using EPA Method 6010B. Barium was reported in each sample at concentrations ranging from 34 to 1,600 mg/kg. These concentrations were below both the residential CHHSL for barium of 5,200 mg/kg and the commercial/industrial CHHSL of 63,000 mg/kg.

One sample (DP1-14.5, collected at 14.5 feet depth) contained barium at 1,600 mg/kg, which equaled or exceeded ten times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) for barium of 1,000 mg/kg — the threshold at which waste extraction test (WET) soluble testing may be required for waste stream characterization. However, the calculated 95% upper confidence limit for barium was significantly less than ten times the STLC, and therefore WET soluble testing was not performed. No soil samples contained total barium concentrations at or above the California hazardous waste Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC). Soil materials excavated from the investigation area were determined not to require management or disposal as hazardous waste.

**Stockpiles 1 and 2 – 2019 remedial design:**
The January 2019 "Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1" by Geocon Consultants, Inc. identifies designated "Barium-Containing Soil" (BCS) stockpiles and a "Barium-Containing Soil and ADL Consolidation Zone." Grading plan figures show Stockpile 1, Stockpile 2, and a "BCS Stockpile" labeled as Stockpile 61. The figures reference a "BCS Removal Sampling Plan" with proposed sampling locations near the retaining wall/fence line. Specific concentrations from the 2019 sampling plan are not provided in the available source documents.

### Groundwater concentrations

Groundwater monitoring wells MW-1 through MW-10 were installed in the unconsolidated sand, silty sand, and silt layers within the Modesto Formation underlying the site. Wells are completed within the shallow zone of the upper aquifer at approximately 35 feet below ground surface. Groundwater flow direction is generally toward the south-southeast.

**June 2014 monitoring event:**
Geocon collected groundwater samples from wells MW-1 through MW-10 on June 4–5, 2014. Barium was reported in each of the ten groundwater samples. Specific barium concentrations from this event are summarized in Table 2 of the monitoring report; the source documents note that barium concentrations remained significantly less than the numeric water quality threshold. A time-series plot (Figure 4, "Barium Concentrations vs. Time") displays barium data for all ten monitoring wells from May 2006 through June 2014, with all data points plotting well below the 1,000 µg/L water quality threshold line.

The June 2014 report stated that barium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds."

**Long-term groundwater trends:**
Twelve groundwater sampling events were conducted between March 2012 and February 2014 (March, May, June, July, September, and November 2012; January, March, June, September, and December 2013; and February 2014). Across these events, dissolved metals including barium were predominantly reported at concentrations less than their respective numeric water quality threshold values. Barium concentrations over time are presented on Figure 4 of the August 2014 monitoring report, with data extending from May 27, 2006, to August 13, 2014.

## Regulatory status

### Soil thresholds and findings
Barium concentrations in Stockpile 3 soil (34–1,600 mg/kg) were below the California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) for both residential (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial (63,000 mg/kg) exposure scenarios. No soil samples exceeded the TTLC. The 95% UCL for barium was below ten times the STLC (barium STLC = 100 mg/L; ten times STLC = 1,000 mg/kg), so excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was determined suitable for offsite disposal as non-hazardous waste following disclosure and review of site characterization data.

### Groundwater thresholds
The California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for barium in drinking water is 1,000 µg/L. In the June 2014 groundwater sampling event, none of the reported dissolved barium concentrations exceeded this threshold. This finding was consistent with all prior groundwater monitoring events.

### DTSC determinations
In a letter dated December 17, 2009, DTSC responded to the Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) prepared by Shaw in 2009, stating that "the stockpiles as currently managed by Caltrans on Caltrans property do not pose a risk to human health for: 1) Caltrans workers who access the fenced site…, 2) trespassers; and 3) residents adjacent to the stockpiles."

### Air monitoring thresholds
For the Stockpile 3 excavation (Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project, June 2012), Geocon and [[douglas-s-krause]] determined that barium at its Cal/OSHA 8-hour TWA Permissible Exposure Limit of 10 mg/m³ represents less of a potential exposure hazard than inorganic lead. Air monitoring focused primarily on measurement of inorganic lead as a surrogate for dust control effectiveness; barium was analyzed on pre-weighed 5µ PVC filters as a secondary parameter.

## Open items

### Long-term management
DTSC's December 17, 2009, letter directed Caltrans to continue managing the stockpiles until the SR 99/Highway 132 Interchange construction proceeds, at which time the stockpiled soil would be used as embankment fill or disposed of offsite. The available source documents do not indicate whether this condition has been fully satisfied.

### Stockpiles 1 and 2 consolidation
The January 2019 remedial design plan documents a "Barium-Containing Soil and ADL Consolidation Zone," identifying an interim remediation phase. The specific barium concentrations within Stockpiles 1 and 2 at the time of that plan, and verification sampling results, are not detailed in the available sources.

### Groundwater monitoring continuation
As of the August 2014 monitoring report, groundwater monitoring was being performed quarterly, with the next event scheduled for September 2014. The long-term monitoring schedule and any subsequent regulatory determinations are not documented in the available source materials.
