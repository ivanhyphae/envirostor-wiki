## Overview
Barium is a primary chemical of potential concern (COPC) at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. It was identified in soil stockpiles generated from the excavation of evaporation ponds on property formerly owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC). These ponds received liquid residue from barite (barium sulfate) processing that occurred from the 1930s to the 1970s. Elevated barium concentrations were found in Stockpiles 2 and 3, and it is also tracked in groundwater.

## Site record

### Source history
The property where the stockpiles originated was occupied by Barium Products Ltd. (later FMC) from the 1930s to the 1970s. Liquid residue from barite and celestite processing was discharged into unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, Caltrans purchased a portion of this property for SR 99 construction. Soil from these ponds was excavated and used to form three distinct stockpiles, totaling approximately 121,000 cubic yards.

### Soil concentrations
*   **Stockpile 3 – April 2012 Geocon investigation:**
    *   Geocon Consultants, Inc. collected 59 soil samples from Stockpile 3.
    *   Barium concentrations ranged from 34 to 1,600 mg/kg.
    *   These concentrations were below the residential CHHSL (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial CHHSL (63,000 mg/kg).
    *   One sample (DP1-14.5) had 1,600 mg/kg of barium, which equaled or exceeded ten times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) for barium (1,000 mg/kg). However, the 95% upper confidence limit for barium was below this threshold, so Waste Extraction Test (WET) soluble testing was not performed.
    *   No soil samples exceeded the Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC) for hazardous waste.
    *   Excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was determined not to require management or disposal as hazardous waste.

*   **Stockpiles 1 and 2 – January 2019 Remedial Design:**
    *   The "Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1" by [[geocon-consultants]] identified designated "Barium-Containing Soil" (BCS) stockpiles and a "Barium-Containing Soil and ADL Consolidation Zone."
    *   Grading plan figures show Stockpile 1, Stockpile 2, and a "BCS Stockpile" labeled as Stockpile 61.
    *   Proposed sampling locations were identified near the retaining wall/fence line for a "BCS Removal Sampling Plan." Specific concentrations from this plan are not detailed in the available sources.

### Groundwater concentrations
Groundwater monitoring wells MW-1 through MW-10 were installed in the Modesto Formation, completed in the shallow zone of the upper aquifer at approximately 35 feet below ground surface. Groundwater flow is generally toward the south-southeast.

*   **June 2014 monitoring event:**
    *   Geocon collected groundwater samples from wells MW-1 through MW-10.
    *   Barium was reported in all ten samples.
    *   Barium concentrations were significantly less than the numeric water quality threshold of 1,000 µg/L. Time-series plots (Figure 4 in the August 2014 report) show all data points well below this threshold.
    *   The June 2014 report stated that barium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds."

*   **Long-term groundwater trends (March 2012 – February 2014):**
    *   Twelve groundwater sampling events were conducted.
    *   Dissolved barium was predominantly reported at concentrations less than its numeric water quality threshold.

## Regulatory status

### Soil thresholds and findings
*   Barium concentrations in Stockpile 3 soil (34–1,600 mg/kg) were below the California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) for both residential (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial (63,000 mg/kg) exposure scenarios.
*   No soil samples exceeded the Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC).
*   The 95% UCL for barium was below ten times the STLC (1,000 mg/kg), so excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was deemed suitable for offsite disposal as non-hazardous waste.

### Groundwater thresholds
*   The California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for barium in drinking water is 1,000 µg/L.
*   In the June 2014 groundwater sampling event, no reported dissolved barium concentrations exceeded this threshold. This was consistent with all prior monitoring events.

### DTSC determinations
*   In a letter dated December 17, 2009, the DTSC stated that the stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans, did not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents.

### Air monitoring thresholds
*   For the Stockpile 3 excavation (June 2012), barium at its Cal/OSHA 8-hour TWA Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL-TWA) of 10 mg/m³ was considered less of a potential exposure hazard than inorganic lead.
*   Air monitoring focused on inorganic lead, with barium analyzed as a secondary parameter on filters.

## Open items

### Stockpiles 1 and 2 consolidation
*   The January 2019 remedial design plan identified a "Barium-Containing Soil and ADL Consolidation Zone" for an interim remediation phase.
*   Specific barium concentrations within Stockpiles 1 and 2 at the time of the plan, and verification sampling results, are not detailed in the available sources.

### Groundwater monitoring continuation
*   As of the August 2014 monitoring report, groundwater monitoring was scheduled quarterly, with the next event in September 2014.
*   The long-term monitoring schedule and subsequent regulatory determinations are not documented in the available source materials.
