Barium is a contaminant of potential concern (COPC) identified in the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. It was found in soil stockpiles originating from the excavation of evaporation ponds at a former FMC facility. These ponds received liquid residue from barite (barium sulfate) processing between the 1930s and 1970s. Elevated barium concentrations were detected in Stockpiles 2 and 3, and it is also monitored in groundwater.

## Site record

### Source history
The property where the stockpiles originated was occupied by Barium Products Ltd. (later FMC) from the 1930s to the 1970s. Liquid residue from barite and celestite processing was discharged into unlined evaporation ponds. In 1961, Caltrans purchased a portion of this property for SR 99 construction. Soil from these ponds was excavated and used to form three distinct stockpiles, totaling approximately 121,000 cubic yards. {{quote: "The Site consists of three soil stockpiles in excess of 120,000 yd<sup>3</sup> of contaminated soil."}} {{quote: "The stockpiles were generated during sub-grade construction of State Route 99 through Modesto, Stanislaus County. Excavation near Kansas Avenue removed tailings from a portion of a Food and Machinery Corporation (FMC) barite (BaSO<sub>4</sub>) and celestite (SrSO<sub>4</sub>) processing disposal pond that operated on a parcel purchased by the state."}} {{quote: "The Site is impacted by hazardous substances (metals), including barium, lead, and strontium."}} {{quote: "The stockpiles are immediately adjacent to State Route 99 (east and west) and south of Kansas Avenue in Modesto."}}

### Soil concentrations
*   **Stockpile 3 – April 2012 Geocon investigation:**
    *   Geocon Consultants, Inc. collected 59 soil samples from Stockpile 3. {{quote: "Geocon Consultants, Inc. collected 59 soil samples from Stockpile 3."}}
    *   Barium concentrations ranged from 34 to 1,600 mg/kg. {{quote: "Barium concentrations ranged from 34 to 1,600 mg/kg."}}
    *   These concentrations were below the residential CHHSL (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial CHHSL (63,000 mg/kg). {{quote: "These concentrations were below the residential CHHSL (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial CHHSL (63,000 mg/kg)."}}
    *   One sample (DP1-14.5) had 1,600 mg/kg of barium, which equaled or exceeded ten times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) for barium (1,000 mg/kg). {{quote: "One sample (DP1-14.5) had 1,600 mg/kg of barium, which equaled or exceeded ten times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) for barium (1,000 mg/kg)."}} However, the 95% upper confidence limit for barium was below this threshold, so Waste Extraction Test (WET) soluble testing was not performed. {{quote: "However, the 95% upper confidence limit for barium was below this threshold, so Waste Extraction Test (WET) soluble testing was not performed."}}
    *   No soil samples exceeded the Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC) for hazardous waste. {{quote: "No soil samples exceeded the Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC) for hazardous waste."}}
    *   Excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was determined not to require management or disposal as hazardous waste. {{quote: "Excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was determined not to require management or disposal as hazardous waste."}}

*   **Stockpiles 1 and 2 – January 2019 Remedial Design:**
    *   The "Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1" by [[geocon-consultants]] identified designated "Barium-Containing Soil" (BCS) stockpiles and a "Barium-Containing Soil and ADL Consolidation Zone." {{quote: "The "Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remedial Design Implementation Plan – Interim Phase 1" by Geocon Consultants, Inc. identified designated "Barium-Containing Soil" (BCS) stockpiles and a "Barium-Containing Soil and ADL Consolidation Zone."}}
    *   Grading plan figures show Stockpile 1, Stockpile 2, and a "BCS Stockpile" labeled as Stockpile 61. {{quote: "Grading plan figures show Stockpile 1, Stockpile 2, and a "BCS Stockpile" labeled as Stockpile 61."}}
    *   Proposed sampling locations were identified near the retaining wall/fence line for a "BCS Removal Sampling Plan." Specific concentrations from this plan are not detailed in the available sources.

### Groundwater concentrations
Groundwater monitoring wells MW-1 through MW-10 were installed in the Modesto Formation, completed in the shallow zone of the upper aquifer at approximately 35 feet below ground surface. Groundwater flow is generally toward the south-southeast. {{quote: "Groundwater was encountered in the project vicinity at depths between 30 and 40 feet (below natural grade) with flow toward the southeast."}} {{quote: "Shaw determined that groundwater flow is toward the southeast at a gradient of approximately 0.001."}} {{quote: "In June 2013, depth to groundwater at the Site ranged from 31.73 (MW-1) to 40.11 (MW-5) feet below TOC. Based on the groundwater elevation data, the groundwater flow is toward the east-southeast at an average gradient of 0.0005, which is generally consistent with historical flow."}}

*   **June 2014 monitoring event:**
    *   Geocon collected groundwater samples from wells MW-1 through MW-10. {{quote: "Geocon collected groundwater samples from wells MW-1 through MW-10."}}
    *   Barium was reported in all ten samples. {{quote: "Barium was reported in all ten samples."}}
    *   Barium concentrations were significantly less than the numeric water quality threshold of 1,000 µg/L. {{quote: "Barium concentrations were significantly less than the numeric water quality threshold of 1,000 µg/L."}} Time-series plots (Figure 4 in the August 2014 report) show all data points well below this threshold. {{quote: "Time-series plots (Figure 4 in the August 2014 report) show all data points well below this threshold."}}
    *   The June 2014 report stated that barium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds." {{quote: "The June 2014 report stated that barium concentrations were "similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds."}}

*   **Long-term groundwater trends (March 2012 – February 2014):**
    *   Twelve groundwater sampling events were conducted. {{quote: "Twelve groundwater sampling events were conducted."}}
    *   Dissolved barium was predominantly reported at concentrations less than its numeric water quality threshold. {{quote: "Dissolved barium was predominantly reported at concentrations less than its numeric water quality threshold."}}

## Regulatory status

### Soil thresholds and findings
*   Barium concentrations in Stockpile 3 soil (34–1,600 mg/kg) were below the California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) for both residential (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial (63,000 mg/kg) exposure scenarios. {{quote: "Barium concentrations ranged from 34 to 1,600 mg/kg. These concentrations were below the residential CHHSL (5,200 mg/kg) and commercial/industrial CHHSL (63,000 mg/kg)."}}
*   No soil samples exceeded the Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC). {{quote: "No soil samples exceeded the Total Threshold Limit Concentration (TTLC) for hazardous waste."}}
*   The 95% UCL for barium was below ten times the STLC (1,000 mg/kg), so excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was deemed suitable for offsite disposal as non-hazardous waste. {{quote: "The 95% upper confidence limit for barium was below this threshold, so Waste Extraction Test (WET) soluble testing was not performed."}} {{quote: "Excavated soil from Stockpile 3 was determined not to require management or disposal as hazardous waste."}}

### Groundwater thresholds
*   The California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for barium in drinking water is 1,000 µg/L. {{quote: "Barium was reported at a concentration of 2,000 micrograms per liter (µg/l) in sample SW03 exceeding the MCL of 1,000 µg/l."}} {{quote: "Barium concentrations were significantly less than the numeric water quality threshold of 1,000 µg/L."}}
*   In the June 2014 groundwater sampling event, no reported dissolved barium concentrations exceeded this threshold. {{quote: "In the June 2014 groundwater sampling event, no reported dissolved barium concentrations exceeded this threshold."}} This was consistent with all prior monitoring events. {{quote: "This was consistent with all prior monitoring events."}}

### DTSC determinations
*   In a letter dated December 17, 2009, the DTSC stated that the stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans, did not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. {{quote: "In a letter dated December 17, 2009, the DTSC stated that: "DTSC finds that the soil stockpiles, as currently managed by Caltrans on Caltrans property, do not pose a risk to human health for: 1) Caltrans workers who access the fenced site to conduct mowing operations, conduct fence repairs, or other routine act"}}

### Air monitoring thresholds
*   For the Stockpile 3 excavation (June 2012), barium at its Cal/OSHA 8-hour TWA Permissible Exposure Limit (PEL-TWA) of 10 mg/m³ was considered less of a potential exposure hazard than inorganic lead. {{quote: "Direct read (pDR-1200) and laboratory air sample results for the project indicated that airborne levels of lead and barium were well below levels of concern during excavation activities at Stockpile 3."}}
*   Air monitoring focused on inorganic lead, with barium analyzed as a secondary parameter on filters. {{quote: "Direct read (pDR-1200) and laboratory air sample results for the project indicated that airborne levels of lead and barium were well below levels of concern during excavation activities at Stockpile 3."}}

## I. Task Order Purpose

The purpose of this Task Order is for payment by the State of California, Department of Transportation (Caltrans) of costs incurred by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for oversight and consultative services, review of investigative data and reports, and other information necessary to prepare written correspondence related to remedy evaluation/decision documents (e.g. Human Health Risk Assessment, Feasibility Study, Remedial Action Plan, etc.) and other regulatory instruments as appropriate to regulate and remediate the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles – State Route 132/99 (Site) prior to construction of the future Route 132 West Expressway.

The Site consists of three stockpiles in excess of 120,000 yd<sup>3</sup> of contaminated soil. The stockpiles were generated during sub-grade construction of State Route 99 through Modesto, Stanislaus County. Excavation near Kansas Avenue removed tailings from a portion of a Food and Machinery Corporation (FMC) barite (BaSO<sub>4</sub>) and celestite (SrSO<sub>4</sub>) processing disposal pond that operated on a parcel purchased by the state. The Site is impacted by hazardous substances (metals), including barium, lead, and strontium. The stockpiles are

immediately adjacent to State Route 99 (east and west) and south of Kansas Avenue in Modesto.

Under direction of DTSC through a preceding 2006 Interagency Agreement task order, Caltrans submitted a Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment in 2009. The DTSC letter dated 12/7/2012 to Caltrans discussed the need to address the use of soil stockpile material in the State Route 132 West Expressway project and/or its final disposition as it relates to human health and water quality prior to design and construction of the State Route 132 West Expressway project. DTSC requests that the Human Health Risk Assessment be updated, along with the collection and development of other technical data necessary for Site remediation decisions and approvals.

Caltrans agrees to reimburse DTSC for the costs they incur for review, consultation, decision document approval, and/or processing applications(s) or request(s) made by Caltrans. The details of the Scope of Work are described below under the "Scope of Services."

## TASK 2. Additional Site Characterization as needed.

- 2.1. <u>Sampling and Analysis Workplan</u>. Caltrans shall submit a workplan that describes the activities proposed to further characterize soil, surface water and/or groundwater. The workplan should also include a Site health and safety plan, quality assurance/quality control plan, sampling plan, and implementation schedule.
- 2.2. Caltrans shall begin implementation of the approved workplan in accordance with the approved implementation schedule. DTSC may provide oversight of workplan implementation.
- 2.3. <u>Site Characterization Report</u>. Caltrans shall submit a Site Characterization Report that, at a minimum, presents the data, summarizes the findings of the investigation, validates the data, and includes recommendations and conclusions.
- TASK 3. <u>Risk Evaluation and Cleanup Level Determination</u>. Caltrans shall update the existing risk evaluation consistent with U. S. EPA Risk Assessment Guidance for Superfund (EPA/540/1-89/002) and DTSC Supplemental Guidance for Human Health Multimedia Risk Assessments of Hazardous Waste Sites and Permitted Facilities. This evaluation will identify chemicals of concern and potential routes of exposure; characterize the potential risk; evaluate potential risks to environmental receptors; consider existing and contemplated uses of the site; and identify site cleanup goals. This information may be submitted as part of the Remedy Selection Document.
- TASK 4. Feasibility Study (FS). Caltrans shall evaluate feasibility of appropriate remediation and response alternatives. Reasonable potential alternatives for the remediation of the Site should be evaluated, including the "no action" alternative. Such an evaluation may be incorporated in the Remedial Action Plan, or may, if the analysis is complex, be addressed in a separate study or report. The evaluation will (a) identify the goals for the cleanup based upon current and projected future land uses; (b) evaluate feasible alternatives to meet these goals, including their effectiveness, ability for implementation and cost; and (c) recommend a preferred alternative.
- TASK 5. Remedial Action Plan. Caltrans shall prepare a Remedial Action Plan (RAP) in accordance with applicable standards and requirements set forth in Health and Safety Code section 25356.1. The RAP will summarize the results of site characterization, risk evaluation, and other technical studies that shall set forth in detail appropriate steps to remedy soil, surface water and groundwater contamination attributed to the Site. In addition, the RAP shall contain a schedule for implementation of all proposed removal and remedial actions.
