Barium is the primary contaminant of concern at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, originating from barite (barium sulfate) processed at the former caltrans|Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility and discharged as tailings to unlined evaporation ponds between the 1950s and 1970s. Pond tailings and underlying soils were excavated and placed to create Stockpiles 1 through 3 in the early 1960s. Barium concentrations in the stockpiles drove the entire remedial history of the site — from the 2003 Initial Site Assessment through the selected containment remedy, the 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP), and verification sampling completed during Interim Phase 1 construction of the SR 132 West Project (opened to traffic November 3, 2022).

## Regulatory thresholds

Two barium-related thresholds governed the project. The RDIP verification threshold for confirming complete removal of barium-containing soil (BCS) from excavation areas was **1,000 mg/kg**, based on groundwater protection (derived from a July 3, 2018 Designated Level Methodology analysis by Andy Kopania). Separately, DTSC does not publish a barium soil screening level; the San Francisco Bay Regional Water Quality Control Board's Environmental Screening Level (ESL) for barium is 3,000 mg/kg for construction workers, 15,000 mg/kg for residential land use, and 220,000 mg/kg for commercial land use.

A third figure — the maximum site-specific upperbound background concentration of **120 mg/kg** — governed whether excavated soil could be reused as *clean cap* material rather than requiring placement back into the BCS Containment Zones. This background value was established in an April 9, 2020 DTSC-approved RDIP Variance and applied throughout subsequent clean-fill source evaluations. The Operations and Maintenance Plan separately notes BCS material contains barium concentrations "up to 72,000 mg/kg," indicating the highest measured stockpile concentrations far exceeded all applicable thresholds.

## Removal verification results

Because Stockpile 3 was entirely removed (relocated onto Stockpile 1) and the west and east ends of Stockpile 2 were excavated for bridge abutment construction, each removal area required verification sampling against the 1,000 mg/kg threshold before clean fill or structural elements could be placed.

| Removal area | Verification samples | Barium range (final) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stockpile 3 (complete removal) | 29 samples, 26 locations | 46–970 mg/kg | 3 locations initially exceeded (2,200–7,000 mg/kg); deepened ~1 ft and resampled; all final results below threshold |
| Stockpile 2 west end (Emerald Ave abutment) | 10 samples, 8 locations | 54–760 mg/kg | 2 locations initially exceeded; deepened and resampled |
| Stockpile 2 east end (SR 99 abutment) | 14 samples, 12 locations | 54–760 mg/kg | 2 locations initially exceeded (1,100–4,100 mg/kg range across both ends); deepened and resampled |

In each case, the excavation was deepened approximately one foot where initial results exceeded 1,000 mg/kg, and resampling confirmed the deepened surface met the threshold — allowing the area to proceed to clean embankment fill or bridge abutment construction without further RDIP monitoring.

## MSE wall footing characterization

A different question arose along the southern slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2, where soil was excavated to steepen the slopes for mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall construction. This soil did not need to meet the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold (it was not being left in place) but was tested to determine whether it could be reused as clean cap material, using the 120 mg/kg background value as the comparison point.

- **Stockpile 1 MSE footing** (April 6, 2020 memo): barium detected in all 15 samples at 42–420 mg/kg; calculated 95% UCL of 172 mg/kg and mean of 128 mg/kg — both above the 120 mg/kg background value but far below the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold.
- **Stockpile 2 MSE footing** (June 18, 2020 memo): barium detected in all 29 samples at 53–930 mg/kg; 14 of 29 samples exceeded 120 mg/kg, with 13 of those 14 occurring in the upper 3 feet.

Because barium exceeded the 120 mg/kg background value (though not the 1,000 mg/kg removal threshold), all excavated MSE wall footing material — rather than being usable as clean cap — was placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones as embankment fill.

## Clean fill source screening

The same 120 mg/kg background value determined which borrow areas could supply clean cap material for the tops and slopes of the containment zones. Four candidate source areas were sampled between February 2020 and April 2021:

| Source area | Barium range | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Carpenter Road (Feb 2020) | 60–110 mg/kg | Approved by DTSC |
| Basin 5 (revised July 2020) | 66–94 mg/kg | Approved by DTSC |
| Carpenter Road Shoofly (Apr 2021) | 28–96 mg/kg | Approved by DTSC |
| Bent 2 excavation stockpiles (Oct 2020) | 31–230 mg/kg | **Not approved** as full clean cap; 4 of 10 samples ≥120 mg/kg background value |

The Bent 2 material was proposed for limited use only — as partial clean cap (6 inches or less) on the upper surface of Stockpile 2, where it would be overlain by the full structural pavement section, pending DTSC review. Fill from the three approved sources was required for the northerly slope, where no pavement section would otherwise cover the material.

## Consolidation and containment outcome

By fall 2020, the upper surfaces and northern slopes of the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones were capped with DTSC-approved clean fill; southern slopes were capped with embankment fill and MSE wall backfill. A January 2021 record rain event eroded clean cap on the exposed northerly slopes, later regraded in September 2021 using Carpenter Road Shoofly material and revegetated in October 2021. Air monitoring conducted February–June 2020 during BCS grading compared results to a barium action level of 25 µg/m³ (from the contractor's Health & Safety Plan) and found airborne barium levels below that level throughout.

Because containment — not removal to background — was the selected remedy, elevated barium remains permanently present beneath the highway embankment within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones (roughly SR 132 Stations 1125+50–1133+50 and 1135+35–1148+30). This is the basis for the land use covenant (LUC) restricting the site to transportation use, the Operations and Maintenance Plan's disturbance-notification requirements, and continuing annual groundwater sampling until the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board determines the capped material no longer threatens groundwater quality. A Final RACR addressing barium containment for Ultimate Phase 2 (widening to four lanes, scheduled for completion in 2028) remains outstanding.
