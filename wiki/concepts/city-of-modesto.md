## Overview

The **City of Modesto** is an incorporated municipality in Stanislaus County, California, and a key stakeholder in the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project along State Route 132. Its role is primarily as a land-use authority and potential future site manager for remediated parcels. In the specific context of Stockpile 2, the city is referenced as the entity that will acquire legal ownership of the remediated property, contingent on verification that cleanup goals have been met. The City of Modesto is not the project proponent—that is the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans)—but it is the receiving agency whose acceptance criteria partly define the project’s remedial objectives.

## Site record

The project record (specifically the *SR 132 Stockpile 2 BCS Removal Tech Memo*) documents that Stockpile 2 is located within the City of Modesto’s municipal boundaries, on a parcel adjacent to SR 132. The city is named as the future landowner of this parcel after remediation is complete. The Tech Memo states that Caltrans is performing the removal action “to facilitate transfer of the property to the City of Modesto.” No specific city-published concentrations, dates of city council decisions, or city-originated correspondence are cited in the source document. The city’s involvement is limited to the property transfer transaction, with no documented role in oversight, sampling, or regulatory decision-making for the soil removal itself.

## Regulatory status

The City of Modesto is not a regulatory agency in this project. It does not set cleanup standards for the Stockpile 2 removal. Applicable thresholds for the arsenic, lead, and other contaminants of concern at Stockpile 2 are set by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), per the *SR 132 Stockpile 2 BCS Removal Tech Memo*. The city’s regulatory relevance is as a future property owner that must accept the property once concentrations are below these DTSC/EPA residential or commercial screening levels. The source does not indicate that the city has imposed additional or more stringent standards.

## Open items

The source document does not record any unresolved issues involving the City of Modesto. However, based on the stated requirement for property transfer, an open item can be inferred: verification that post-removal confirmation sampling meets thresholds acceptable to the city’s acquisition procedures. The Tech Memo does not specify whether the city has formally accepted the final remediation report or granted approval for the transfer. This is not documented in the source and should be confirmed with project records outside the scope of this single memo.
