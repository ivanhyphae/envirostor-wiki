## Overview

The [[national-environmental-policy-act]] (NEPA) is a federal law that mandates the evaluation of potential environmental effects of proposed federal activities and programs. For the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]], [[caltrans]] acts as the lead agency under NEPA, assuming responsibility on behalf of the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA). NEPA requires consideration of indirect effects, which can include changes in land use, economic vitality, and population density, as well as aesthetic values, community cohesion, and public facilities and services. The act also mandates the identification of all potentially significant impacts, including energy impacts and the protection of paleontological resources.

## Site Record

NEPA requires the evaluation of potential environmental effects, including indirect effects, which may occur beyond the immediate project area or at a future time. {{quote: "The Council on Environmental Quality regulations, which established the steps necessary to comply with NEPA, require evaluation of the potential environmental effects of all proposed federal activities and programs. This includes a requirement to examine indirect effects, which may occur in areas beyond the immediate influence of a proposed action or at some time in the future."}}

NEPA also mandates consideration of aesthetic values and the protection of human-made resources, community cohesion, and public facilities and services. {{quote: "NEPA of 1969 as amended established that the federal government use all practicable means to ensure that all Americans have safe, healthful, productive, and aesthetically and culturally pleasing surroundings (42 U.S. Code 4331[b][2]). The Federal Highway Administration in its implementation of NEPA (23 U.S. Code 109[h]) directs that final decisions regarding projects are to be made in the best overall public interest. This requires taking into account adverse environmental impacts, such as destruction or disruption of human-made resources, community cohesion, and the availability of public facilities and services."}}

Paleontological resources are also addressed under NEPA. {{quote: "Several federal statutes address paleontological resources, their treatment, and funding for mitigation as a part of federally authorized projects: ... 16 U.S. Code 431-433 (the \"Antiquities Act\") prohibits appropriating, excavating, injuring, or destroying any object of antiquity situated on federal land without the permission of the Secretary of the Department of Government having jurisdiction over the land. Fossils are considered \"objects of antiquity\" by the Bureau of Land Management, the National Park Service, the Forest Service, and other federal agencies."}} Construction activities for the proposed project could impact the sensitive Pleistocene Modesto Formation, potentially leading to adverse impacts under NEPA if important paleontological vertebrate fossil resources are present. {{quote: "If important paleontological vertebrate fossil resources are present at the project site then construction activities could cause adverse impacts under NEPA and significant impacts under CEQA, such as destruction and loss of scientifically significant paleontological vertebrate fossil resources."}}

Energy impacts are also subject to NEPA review. {{quote: "NEPA (42 U.S. Code 4332) requires the identification of all potentially significant impacts to the environment, including energy impacts."}}

Wildlife impacts are regulated under NEPA, alongside other federal and state laws. {{quote: "Federal laws and regulations relevant to wildlife include the following: - NEPA - Migratory Bird Treaty Act - Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act"}}

Invasive species are also a consideration under NEPA. {{quote: "On February 3, 1999, President Bill Clinton signed Executive Order 13112 requiring federal agencies to combat the introduction or spread of invasive species in the U.S. ... The Federal Highway Administration guidance issued August 10, 1999 directs the use of the State's invasive species list, maintained by the California Invasive Species Council to define the invasive species that must be considered as part of NEPA analysis for a proposed project."}}

Cumulative impacts are assessed under NEPA. {{quote: "A definition of cumulative impacts, under NEPA, can be found in 40 Code of Federal Regulations Part 1508.7 of the Council on Environmental Quality regulations."}}

## Regulatory Status

[[caltrans]] serves as the lead agency under NEPA for the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]], fulfilling the responsibilities of the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) through an assumption of responsibility. {{quote: "Caltrans is the lead agency under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) and the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA)."}} The project documentation is prepared in compliance with both NEPA and CEQA. {{quote: "Project documentation, therefore, has been prepared in compliance with both CEQA and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA)."}} The determination of significance under NEPA dictates whether an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) or a lower level of documentation, such as an Environmental Assessment (EA), is required. {{quote: "Under NEPA, significance is used to determine whether an Environmental Impact Statement, or a lower level of documentation, would be required."}} If the project is determined not to have a significant impact on the environment, Caltrans would issue a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI). {{quote: "If Caltrans, as assigned by the Federal Highway Administration, further determines that the NEPA action does not significantly impact the environment, Caltrans would issue a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) in accordance with NEPA."}}
