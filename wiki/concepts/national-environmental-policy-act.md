## Overview

The [[national-environmental-policy-act]] (NEPA) is a federal law that mandates the evaluation of potential environmental effects of proposed federal activities and programs. For the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]], [[caltrans]] acts as the lead agency under NEPA, assuming responsibility on behalf of the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA). NEPA requires consideration of indirect effects, which can include changes in land use, economic vitality, and population density, as well as aesthetic values, community cohesion, and public facilities and services. The act also mandates the identification of all potentially significant impacts, including energy impacts and the protection of paleontological resources.

## Site Record

NEPA requires the evaluation of potential environmental effects, including indirect effects, which may occur beyond the immediate project area or at a future time.

NEPA also mandates consideration of aesthetic values and the protection of human-made resources, community cohesion, and public facilities and services.

Paleontological resources are also addressed under NEPA. Construction activities for the proposed project could impact the sensitive Pleistocene Modesto Formation, potentially leading to adverse impacts under NEPA if important paleontological vertebrate fossil resources are present.

Energy impacts are also subject to NEPA review.

Wildlife impacts are regulated under NEPA, alongside other federal and state laws.

Invasive species are also a consideration under NEPA.

Cumulative impacts are assessed under NEPA.

## Regulatory Status

[[caltrans]] serves as the lead agency under NEPA for the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]], fulfilling the responsibilities of the Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) through an assumption of responsibility. The project documentation is prepared in compliance with both NEPA and CEQA. The determination of significance under NEPA dictates whether an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) or a lower level of documentation, such as an Environmental Assessment (EA), is required. If the project is determined not to have a significant impact on the environment, Caltrans would issue a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI).
