## DTSC Residential Screening Level

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] is a benchmark used by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) to evaluate potential risks to residents from contaminants in soil. In the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, this screening level is applied to assess whether concentrations of certain chemicals, such as [[barium]], exceed acceptable levels for residential land use.

### Site Record

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] for barium was considered in the evaluation of soil stockpiles. Specifically, in a letter dated May 29, 2012, Caltrans indicated that elevated metal concentrations were found in soil stockpiles, but these were "below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels" {{quote: "The final analysis does indicate the presence of elevated metal concentrations above site-specific background levels, but below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels."}}. This assessment was based on soil analysis performed by [[geocon-consultants]], Inc. for the ramp safety improvement project located in Modesto along State Route (SR) 99 at Kansas Avenue, which intersected Stockpile #3 of the soil stockpiles intended for the future [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]].

### Regulatory Status

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] is a regulatory tool employed by the DTSC to guide cleanup decisions. When contaminant concentrations in soil exceed the [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]], further investigation or remediation may be required to protect public health and the environment. In the case of the soil stockpiles discussed in the May 29, 2012 letter, the concentrations of metals, including barium, were determined to be below these screening levels, allowing for the material to be managed as non-hazardous {{quote: "Based on this analysis, the contractor for the ramp project will haul the material to Stanislaus County Fink Road Landfill."}}.
