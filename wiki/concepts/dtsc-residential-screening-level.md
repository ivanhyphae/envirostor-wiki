## DTSC Residential Screening Level

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] is a benchmark used in the [[human-health-risk-assessment]] process to evaluate potential risks to residents from contaminants in soil. In the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, this screening level is applied to assess whether concentrations of certain chemicals, such as [[barium]], exceed acceptable levels for residential land use.

### Site Record

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] for barium was considered in the evaluation of soil stockpiles. For instance, in the context of the [[draft-final-remedial-action-plan]], the [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] for barium was identified as a threshold for assessing potential risks.

### Regulatory Status

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] is a regulatory tool employed by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) to guide cleanup decisions. When contaminant concentrations in soil exceed the [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]], further investigation or remediation may be required to protect public health and the environment. The project documents indicate that some soil samples may have concentrations that warrant consideration against these screening levels.
