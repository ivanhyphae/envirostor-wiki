## DTSC Residential Screening Level

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] is a benchmark established by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) for evaluating potential risks to residents from contaminants in soil. This screening level is applied to assess whether contaminant concentrations in soil exceed acceptable levels for residential land use.

### Site Record

In the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, the [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] has been applied to specific contaminants. For instance, the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP) for the interim containment of soil stockpiles during Phase 1 construction of the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]] provided a [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] for [[lead]] of 80 mg/kg. This threshold was used for soil excavation verification during the removal of Stockpile 3, bridge abutment excavations, and MSE retaining wall foundation excavations.

### Regulatory Status

The [[dtsc-residential-screening-level]] serves as a regulatory threshold for determining the need for further action. When contaminant concentrations exceed this level, it may trigger requirements for additional investigation or remediation to protect public health. The DTSC has approved variances to the RDIP, indicating their review and acceptance of project-specific approaches to managing soil based on these screening levels and [[site-specific-background-levels]]. For example, a DTSC-approved Variance to the RDIP dated April 9, 2020, prepared by [[geocon-consultants]], identified planned clean fill source borrow areas and presented [[site-specific-background-levels]] for barium (120 mg/kg) and lead (3.8 mg/kg) for comparison with soil samples.
