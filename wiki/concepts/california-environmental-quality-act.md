The [[california-environmental-quality-act]] (CEQA) is a state law requiring public agencies to evaluate the environmental impacts of their proposed actions and identify mitigation measures. For the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project, CEQA compliance was a critical component, involving the preparation of an [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) and subsequent approvals.

## Overview

CEQA mandates that public agencies assess potential environmental effects of projects that could cause significant impacts, typically through an EIR/EA. The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project underwent CEQA review to evaluate the environmental consequences of remediating soil stockpiles at the SR 132 site in Stanislaus County. [[caltrans]] served as the Lead Agency for CEQA, preparing the EIR/EA and a [[remedial-action-plan]]. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) acted as a CEQA Responsible Agency, utilizing the EIR/EA for its own CEQA analysis related to the RAP.

## Site record

[[caltrans]], as the Lead Agency under CEQA, certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018. DTSC, in its role as a CEQA Responsible Agency, used this EIR/EA for the RAP's CEQA analysis.

The CEQA process for the project involved categorizing potential impacts into four levels: "potentially significant impact," "less than significant impact with mitigation," "less than significant impact," and "no impact."

The project description within the CEQA documentation details the containment of three stockpiles, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, including [[barium]], strontium, and lead. The containment strategy involves retaining walls, bridge abutments, and covering unpaved portions with clean fill. Long-term protection is to be ensured through land use controls, inspections, and groundwater monitoring.

## Regulatory status

DTSC, acting as both Lead Agency and a Responsible Agency under CEQA, approved the project on March 14, 2018. DTSC determined that the project would not have a significant effect on the environment. DTSC also certified that an EIR was prepared and that mitigation measures were incorporated as conditions of project approval. A Statement of Overriding Considerations was not adopted for this project by DTSC. Findings were made pursuant to CEQA provisions.

The final environmental document, comments and responses, and the record of project approval are publicly available at the [[caltrans]] District 10 Office, DTSC's File Room, and online via the [[caltrans]] SR 132 West project website. Information is also accessible via the DTSC EnviroStor website.

The State Clearinghouse Number for the project is 2010012010.
