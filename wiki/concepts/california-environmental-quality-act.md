The [[california-environmental-quality-act]] (CEQA) is a state law that requires state and local agencies to consider the environmental implications of their actions before approving projects. For the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]], CEQA compliance involved determining the potential environmental impacts of the proposed remediation activities and ensuring that any significant impacts were mitigated.

## Overview

CEQA requires public agencies to prepare an [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) for projects that may have a significant effect on the environment. If a project is determined to have no significant effect, a Negative Declaration may be issued. The process culminates in a [[notice-of-determination]] (NOD). The [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] underwent CEQA review to assess the environmental consequences of soil remediation.

## Site record

The CEQA review for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] involved evaluating potential impacts related to soil contamination and remediation activities. The project aimed to address soil contamination at the SR 132 site in Stanislaus County. The [[dtsc]] (DTSC) was involved in overseeing the remediation process, which is subject to CEQA requirements. The [[public-resources-code]] outlines the procedures for CEQA compliance.

## Regulatory status

The [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] was subject to CEQA review to ensure that potential environmental impacts were identified and addressed. The outcome of this review would determine the project's regulatory path forward, potentially leading to a finding of [[no-significant-effect-on-the-environment]] or requiring a more comprehensive EIR. The issuance of a [[notice-of-determination]] signifies the completion of the CEQA process for a project. The [[brownfields-and-environmental-restoration-program]] and the [[remedial-action-plan]] are components that would be considered within the CEQA framework.
