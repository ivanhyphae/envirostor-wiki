The [[california-environmental-quality-act]] (CEQA) is a state law that requires public agencies to evaluate the environmental impacts of their proposed actions and to identify ways to mitigate those impacts. For the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]], CEQA compliance was a critical component, involving the preparation of an [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) and subsequent approvals by regulatory agencies.

## Overview

CEQA mandates that public agencies assess potential environmental effects of projects that could cause significant impacts. This process typically results in the preparation of an EIR/EA. The [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] underwent CEQA review to evaluate the environmental consequences associated with the remediation of soil stockpiles at the SR 132 site in Stanislaus County. Caltrans served as the Lead Agency for CEQA purposes, preparing the EIR/EA and a [[remedial-action-plan]] (RAP). The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) acted as a CEQA Responsible Agency, utilizing the EIR/EA for its own CEQA analysis related to the RAP.

## Site record

Caltrans, as the Lead Agency under CEQA, certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018. {{quote: "Caltrans, acting as the Lead Agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment(EIR/EA) and Remedial Action Plan RAP). Caltrans certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018."}} DTSC, in its role as a CEQA Responsible Agency, used this EIR/EA for the RAP's CEQA analysis. {{quote: "DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, is utilizing the EIR/EA for the RAP CEQA analysis."}}

The CEQA process for the project involved categorizing potential impacts into four levels: "potentially significant impact," "less than significant impact with mitigation," "less than significant impact," and "no impact." {{quote: "The CEQA impact levels include 'potentially significant impact,' 'less than significant impact with mitigation,' 'less than significant impact,' and 'no impact.'"}}

The project description within the CEQA documentation details the containment of three stockpiles, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, including barium, strontium, and lead. {{quote: "The project consists of containment of three stockpiles totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals (primarily barium, strontium, and lead) during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project."}} The containment strategy involves retaining walls, bridge abutments, and covering unpaved portions with clean fill. {{quote: "The stockpiles will be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway. Unpaved portions will have clean fill cover."}} Long-term protection is to be ensured through land use controls, inspections, and groundwater monitoring. {{quote: "This containment remedy provides long-term protection of people and the environment by preventing exposure and minimizing the infiltration of surface water into groundwater under the stockpiles. Land use controls and inspection of the payement and other features of the containment remedy and groundwater monitoring will be conducted annually to ensure the integrity of the remedy into the future."}}

## Regulatory status

DTSC, acting as both Lead Agency and a Responsible Agency under CEQA, approved the project on March 14, 2018. {{quote: "As Lead Agency a Responsible Agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), DTSC approved the above-described project on March 14, 2018 and has made the following determinations:"}} DTSC determined that the project would not have a significant effect on the environment. {{quote: "- 1. The project will not have a significant effect on the environment."}} DTSC also certified that an EIR was prepared and that mitigation measures were incorporated as conditions of project approval. {{quote: "- 2. An Environmental Impact Report that included this project was prepared and certified by the California Department of Transportation pursuant to the provisions of CEQA. - 3. Mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval."}} A Statement of Overriding Considerations was not adopted for this project by DTSC. {{quote: "- A *Statement of Overriding Considerations was not adopted for this project."}} Findings were made pursuant to CEQA provisions. {{quote: "- 5. Findings were made pursuant to the provisions of CEQA."}}

The final environmental document, comments and responses, and the record of project approval are publicly available at the Caltrans District 10 Office, DTSC's File Room, and online via the Caltrans SR 132 West project website. {{quote: "This is to certify that the final environmental document, comments and responses, and the record of project approval are available to the public at the following locations: Caltrans District 10 Office 1976 Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. Blvd. Stockton, California 95205 (209) 948-7543 DTSC - File Room 8800 Cal Center Drive Sacramento, California 95826 (916) 255-3758 Caltrans - Online at: http://www.dot.ca.gov/d10/x-project-sr132west.html"}} Information is also accessible via the DTSC EnviroStor website. {{quote: "DTSC - EnviroStor website: http://www.envirostor.dtsc.ca.gov/public/profile report?global id=60001626 http://www.envirostor.dtsc.ca.gov/public/profile report?global id=50280024"}}

The State Clearinghouse Number for the project is 2010012010. {{quote: "State Clearing House Number: 2010012010"}}
