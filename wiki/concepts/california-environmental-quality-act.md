The [[california-environmental-quality-act]] (CEQA) is a state law requiring public agencies to consider the environmental implications of their actions before approving projects. For the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]], CEQA compliance involved assessing potential environmental impacts of remediation activities and ensuring significant impacts were mitigated.

## Overview

CEQA mandates that public agencies prepare an [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) for projects that may significantly affect the environment. If no significant effects are anticipated, a Negative Declaration may be issued. The process concludes with a [[notice-of-determination]] (NOD). The [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] underwent CEQA review to evaluate the environmental consequences of soil remediation.

## Site record

The CEQA review for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] involved assessing potential impacts related to soil contamination and remediation. The project aimed to address soil contamination at the SR 132 site in Stanislaus County. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) oversaw the remediation process, which is subject to CEQA requirements. The [[public-resources-code]] outlines the procedures for CEQA compliance.

Caltrans, acting as the Lead Agency under CEQA, prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and a Remedial Action Plan (RAP). Caltrans certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018. {{quote: "Caltrans, acting as the Lead Agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment(EIR/EA) and Remedial Action Plan RAP). Caltrans certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018."}} DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, utilized this EIR/EA for the RAP's CEQA analysis. {{quote: "DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, is utilizing the EIR/EA for the RAP CEQA analysis."}}

The CEQA process for the project included determining significance levels for various environmental factors. These levels are categorized as "potentially significant impact," "less than significant impact with mitigation," "less than significant impact," and "no impact." {{quote: "The CEQA impact levels include 'potentially significant impact,' 'less than significant impact with mitigation,' 'less than significant impact,' and 'no impact.'"}}

## Regulatory status

The [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] was subject to CEQA review to ensure potential environmental impacts were identified and addressed. The outcome of this review determined the project's regulatory path, potentially leading to a finding of no significant effect or requiring a comprehensive EIR. The issuance of a [[notice-of-determination]] signifies the completion of the CEQA process for a project. {{quote: "The issuance of a [[notice-of-determination]] signifies the completion of the CEQA process for a project."}} The [[brownfields-and-environmental-restoration-program]] and the [[remedial-action-plan]] are components considered within the CEQA framework.

DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, approved the project on March 14, 2018, determining that the project would not have a significant effect on the environment. {{quote: "As Lead Agency a Responsible Agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), DTSC approved the above-described project on March 14, 2018 and has made the following determinations: - 1. The project will not have a significant effect on the environment."}} DTSC also certified that an EIR was prepared and that mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval. {{quote: "2. An Environmental Impact Report that included this project was prepared and certified by the California Department of Transportation pursuant to the provisions of CEQA. - 3. Mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval."}}
