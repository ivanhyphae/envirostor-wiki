## Overview

The "No Action Alternative" is a baseline remedial alternative evaluated as part of the Feasibility Study (FS) for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] remediation case file. It represents the scenario where no active remediation or management measures are implemented beyond existing, minimal site controls. This alternative is required by the National Oil and Hazardous Substances Pollution Contingency Plan (NCP) for comparison against other potential remedial actions.

## Site Record

Under a no-action scenario, the stockpiles would remain in place without any active excavation, alteration, or removal of soil. Site control, maintenance, and monitoring activities would be discontinued. However, the sources note that as long as [[caltrans]] owns and controls the property as State right-of-way, they would maintain the perimeter fence and continue restricting access to Caltrans-authorized personnel. Therefore, the most likely site occupant would be a trespasser.

The 2007 HHRA and its recent update concluded that the concentrations of COPCs in the stockpiles do not pose an unacceptable level of health risk to an onsite trespasser. The no-action alternative can therefore be considered protective of human health as long as land use remains the same and access is restricted.

This alternative would be the least protective of the environment, as it would not reduce the contaminant mass or the potential for COPCs to impact surface or groundwater quality. It would not reduce the toxicity, mobility, and/or volume of COPCs in the stockpiles. Mobility via erosion from wind or stormwater infiltration is limited by the vegetative cover. COPC concentrations in groundwater samples collected from monitoring wells adjacent to and downgradient of, and native soil samples collected from beneath, the stockpiles are inconclusive with respect to COPC migration from the stockpiles.

The no-action alternative is readily implementable because it requires no labor, materials, or equipment. There is no cost associated with this alternative.

## Regulatory Status

This alternative would not meet State or Federal regulations with respect to hazardous waste levels of COPCs in soil on the Site because of the lack of site control and public notification. It would have the lowest level of regulatory acceptance because of the lack of site controls and monitoring and maintenance. This alternative would not be acceptable to the regulatory agencies because access to the Site would not be controlled, and groundwater quality monitoring would not continue.

## Community Acceptance

This alternative would likely have the lowest level of community acceptance due to the perceived threat to human health and the environment. Although the presence of the stockpiles has been generally acceptable to the community for five decades, this alternative would likely not remain acceptable to the public due to an increased perception of risk to human health and the environment associated with the stockpiles.
