Geocon Consultants, Inc. was the environmental consulting firm retained under a Caltrans on-call hazardous waste services contract to author the core regulatory deliverables governing remediation of the barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles at SR 132: the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP), its variances and errata, groundwater monitoring reports, tech memoranda for individual construction disturbances, and the final Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) and Operations and Maintenance Plan. Its role shifted over the life of the project from Caltrans' named field agent responsible for implementing the RDIP to, later, simply the author of technical submittals reviewed and accepted by DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) — a distinction DTSC required be documented in the record.

## Role and change in project organization

The January 30, 2019 RDIP identified Geocon as Caltrans' agent responsible for ensuring the stockpiles were contained in accordance with the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and RDIP. A Post-Construction Contract Award Errata to that RDIP subsequently struck Geocon from that role, stating that "regardless of the consultant who will perform the work, Caltrans is the responsible party to make sure the work will be performed according to the RDIP." The Errata's Project Organization section lists Caltrans as Site Owner/Responsible Party, with Dokken Engineering as the project proponent's civil engineer, WSP USA as construction manager, and Bay Cities Paving and Grading, Inc. as contractor — none of them Geocon. This formally removed Geocon from a position of implementation responsibility while it continued to author technical deliverables for Caltrans.

## Deliverables authored and their regulatory disposition

Geocon prepared and submitted, on Caltrans' behalf, the following documents accepted or approved by DTSC in consultation with the RWQCB:

| Stockpile 2 BCS Foundation Spoil Assessment Workplan Tech Memo | February 28, 2022 | Approved | March 4, 2022 |
| Draft O&M Plan | March 2022 | Comments issued (spelling, notification period, HSC citation, inspection triggers) | August 1, 2022 |

The April 3, 2019 RDIP approval followed two prior DTSC conditional approvals of Geocon drafts — an August 20, 2018 conditional approval of the July 18, 2018 draft, and an October 12, 2018 conditional approval of a revised September 14, 2018 version — each keyed to specific administrative text revisions and to DTSC's review of 95% and 100% design plans, which Geocon incorporated before final approval issued.

## Field characterization work

Geocon performed discrete sampling events tied to specific construction disturbances and reported results directly to Caltrans and its construction manager rather than through a full workplan-and-approval cycle in every case. In the Bent 2 Excavation Soil Stockpiles Characterization Technical Memorandum (October 30, 2020), Geocon reported that of ten soil samples collected from Bent 2 end-dump stockpiles placed on Stockpile 2, four of ten exceeded the site-specific background value for barium (120 mg/kg), with detections ranging from 31 to 230 mg/kg; lead ranged from 2.6 to 13 mg/kg, within background range in the samples where detected. Geocon's own conclusion was that the material did not meet clean-cap criteria under the RDIP Variance but could be used, "pending DTSC review and approval consideration," as partial clean cap (6 inches or less) given the additional structural pavement section planned over Stockpile 2's upper surface — an example of Geocon proposing a disposition for out-of-criteria material rather than the material triggering an independent DTSC directive.

Geocon also drafted the Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan, submitted through Caltrans' Richard Stewart in June 2020; Caltrans staff noted at the time that Geocon's submittal included only a sample-location map, without the technical memorandum and background-comparison tables that had accompanied the earlier Carpenter Road-area submittal, and that Geocon was relying on that earlier tech memo as still applicable.

## Groundwater monitoring and closure of monitoring obligations

Geocon authored groundwater monitoring reports dating back at least to the May 2012 report on the March 12, 2012, October 5, 2006, and June 14, 2006 sampling rounds, which DTSC concurred with while directing that future reports add trend comparisons and standardized legends. Geocon's role culminated in the February 26, 2024 Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, prepared under Project Manager Rebecca L. Silva, which concluded — consistent with an earlier 2014 evaluation — that the encapsulated soil was not impacting groundwater and would not do so in the future. DTSC and the RWQCB approved this conclusion on May 3, 2024 and concurred with Geocon's recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring and decommission the four remaining wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, MW10), ending the monitoring obligation that Geocon's reporting had supported since 2012.

## Continuing obligations under the O&M Plan

Geocon's December 2022 O&M Plan, prepared for DTSC under Project Manager John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG, remains the operative document governing inspection and maintenance of the clean cap over Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones through Ultimate Phase 2. It addressed DTSC's August 1, 2022 comments on the March 2022 draft, including a 10-working-day advance notice requirement for any BCS disturbance and inspection triggers tied to fires, substantial rainfall, seismic events, and traffic accidents. Final certification of the RAP will not occur until after Ultimate Phase 2 construction, meaning Geocon's O&M Plan — and any successor revisions — governs an interim period that DTSC's January 18, 2023 acceptance letter describes as lasting an anticipated 8 to 10 years before Phase 2 begins.
