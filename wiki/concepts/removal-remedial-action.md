## Removal Remedial Action

A [[removal-remedial-action]] involves the excavation, loading, transportation, and off-site disposal of contaminated soil in a permitted landfill. This action aims to reduce or eliminate the mobility, toxicity, and volume of contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) at a site, thereby mitigating exposure pathways for future land use. For the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project, removal was considered as a potential remedial alternative.

### Site Record

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project file details "Removal" as a General Response Action (GRA) that consists of excavating and removing all or portions of the stockpiles and disposing of them at an off-site permitted disposal facility. {{quote: "Removal consists of excavating and removing all or portions of the stockpiles and disposal at an off-site permitted disposal facility."}} This process would typically involve mechanical equipment such as backhoes, front-end loaders, or bulldozers, with transportation by truck. {{quote: "Excavation would typically be performed using mechanical equipment such as a backhoe, front-end loader, or bulldozer and transportation would be by truck."}}

A portion of Stockpile 3 was previously excavated and disposed of offsite as part of the Caltrans rehabilitation of the off-ramp to Kansas Avenue. {{quote: "To date, the only removal action taken on the Site has been excavation and landfill disposal of a portion of Stockpile 3 as part of Caltrans' rehabilitation of the off-ramp to Kansas Avenue to improve traffic safety and meet current design standards."}} Approximately 2,800 cubic yards (yd<sup>3</sup>) of Stockpile 3 soil were excavated between September 7 and 26, 2012, and transported to the Forward Class II landfill facility in Manteca, California, under non-hazardous waste manifests. {{quote: "Approximately 2,800 yd3 of the Stockpile 3 soil embankment were excavated over ten days between September 7 and 26, 2012. The excavated stockpile material was directly loaded into covered trucks for transport to the Forward Class II landfill facility in Manteca, California, under non-hazardous waste manifests."}} Dust suppression was provided by pre-soaking and water spray during these activities. {{quote: "Dust suppression provided by the Caltrans contractor during the stockpile excavation and loading activities consisted of pre-soaking and water spray during the stockpile excavation activities."}} Air monitoring during this excavation indicated that airborne levels of lead and barium were well below levels of concern. {{quote: "Direct read (pDR-1200) and laboratory air sample results for the project indicated that airborne levels of lead and barium were well below levels of concern during excavation activities at Stockpile 3."}}

The Feasibility Study (FS) evaluated a comprehensive removal of all stockpiles. This alternative was estimated to cost approximately $21.5 million, including the replacement of removed soil with imported clean fill. {{quote: "Removal of the stockpiles through excavation, loading, transportation, and disposal at an offsite landfill is the most costly of the alternatives at approximately \$21.5 million (Table 4)."}} The cost of removing Stockpile 3 only was estimated at $2.65 million. {{quote: "The cost of removal of Stockpile 3 only was estimated at \$2.65 million (Table 7)."}} This alternative was deemed technically implementable but faced significant constraints due to prohibitively high costs with no available funding. {{quote: "With implementation of best management practices (BMPs) such as dust control (water spray application) and air monitoring, soil track-off controls, and transportation planning (e.g., route planning, load tarping, etc.) during soil handling activities (excavation, loading, and transportation), removal would be effective in the short-term. However, under this alternative, truck traffic on roads in the site vicinity would increase dramatically for both removal of the material and replacement with imported fill material."}} {{quote: "This alternative is technically implementable. However, other constraints to this alternative exist that decrease its implementability. Those constraints include a significant increase in truck traffic on adjacent and nearby roads for a period of approximately 60 days, an increased potential for offsite exposure due to generation of airborne dust from truck loads or spillage, and prohibitively high cost with no funding source."}}

### Regulatory Status

The removal of Stockpile 3 was designated for offsite disposal as non-hazardous soil. {{quote: "Based on this data, data previously presented in the PEA, and review by DTSC, the excavated soil stockpile materials were designated for offsite disposal as non-hazardous soil to an accepting licensed landfill facility."}} The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) conveyed that offsite management of soil from Stockpile 3 did not pose a threat to human health or the environment in a letter dated August 30, 2012. {{quote: "The DTSC conveyed their finding that offsite management of the soil from Stockpile 3 did not pose a threat to human health or the environment in a letter dated August 30, 2012."}}

The comprehensive removal alternative was assessed to comply with State and Federal regulations, including the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District's (SJVAPCD) Rule 8021 regarding fugitive dust emissions, provided adequate dust suppression measures were implemented. {{quote: "This alternative would comply with State and Federal regulations as the soil would be removed from the Site and potential for exposure to COPCs and threat to the environment would be mitigated. This alternative would comply with the SJVAPCD's Rule 8021 regarding fugitive dust emissions during construction as long as dust suppression (water spray) was adequately performed during earthmoving activities."}}

### Open Items

The primary open item regarding the removal remedial action is the lack of funding for a comprehensive removal of all stockpiles. {{quote: "Removal of the stockpiles and replacement with clean fill is estimated to require 175 truckloads per day over an approximate 30-day period. A similar number of loads and time would be required to import clean fill material to replace the stockpiles. Air emissions from heavy equipment (e.g., graders, excavators, loaders) and trucking will be significantly increased for this alternative relative to all other alternatives and the work would fall under the SJVAPCD's Indirect Source Review Rule 9510."}} {{quote: "Although technically implementable, it is the least implementable of the four alternatives due to the cost for which there is no funding."}}
