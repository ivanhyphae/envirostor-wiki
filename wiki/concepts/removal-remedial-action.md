A [[removal-remedial-action]] is a cleanup strategy involving the excavation, transport, and off-site disposal of contaminated soil. This approach aims to reduce the mobility, toxicity, and volume of contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) at a site. For the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project, removal was evaluated as a potential remedial alternative.

## Site Record

The [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project file describes "Removal" as a General Response Action (GRA) that entails excavating and disposing of all or portions of the stockpiles at an off-site permitted facility. This process would typically utilize mechanical equipment like backhoes and front-end loaders, with soil transported by truck.

A portion of Stockpile 3 was previously excavated and disposed of off-site as part of Caltrans' rehabilitation of the off-ramp to Kansas Avenue. Between September 7 and 26, 2012, approximately 2,800 cubic yards (yd<sup>3</sup>) of Stockpile 3 soil were excavated and transported to the Forward Class II landfill facility in Manteca, California, under non-hazardous waste manifests. Dust suppression was managed through pre-soaking and water spray during these activities. Air monitoring conducted during this excavation indicated that airborne levels of lead and barium remained well below levels of concern.

The Feasibility Study (FS) evaluated a comprehensive removal of all stockpiles. This alternative was estimated to cost approximately $21.5 million, including the cost of importing clean fill to replace the removed soil. The cost for removing only Stockpile 3 was estimated at $2.65 million. This alternative was deemed technically implementable but faced significant constraints due to its prohibitively high cost and the lack of identified funding. The FS noted that while removal would be effective in the short term with best management practices (BMPs) like dust control and air monitoring, it would lead to a significant increase in truck traffic for approximately 60 days, both for removal and replacement fill, increasing the potential for off-site exposure.

## Regulatory Status

The removal of Stockpile 3 was designated for off-site disposal as non-hazardous soil. The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) confirmed in a letter dated August 30, 2012, that the off-site management of soil from Stockpile 3 did not pose a threat to human health or the environment.

The comprehensive removal alternative was assessed to comply with State and Federal regulations, including the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District's (SJVAPCD) Rule 8021 regarding fugitive dust emissions, provided adequate dust suppression measures were implemented.

## Open Items

The primary open item regarding the removal remedial action is the lack of funding for a comprehensive removal of all stockpiles. The FS estimated that removal and replacement would require approximately 175 truckloads per day over a 30-day period for both removal and import of clean fill, significantly increasing air emissions from heavy equipment and trucking. This alternative was identified as the least implementable due to its cost, for which no funding is available.
