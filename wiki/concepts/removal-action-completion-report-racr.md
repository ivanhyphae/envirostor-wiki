The Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) documents the remediation activities undertaken for barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles at the State Route (SR) 132 West Project in Modesto, California. This report, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of Caltrans, details the consolidation and capping of these stockpiles as highway embankment fill during Interim Phase 1 construction, under the oversight of the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).

### Acceptance of Interim Phase 1 Remediation

The DTSC, in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), accepted the Interim Removal Action Completion Report for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Interim Phase 1 State Route 132 West Project. This acceptance was communicated via electronic mail on January 18, 2023, from Meredith Williams, Ph.D., Director of DTSC, to John E. Juhrend, P.E., CEG, Project Manager at Geocon Consultants, Inc. The RACR, dated December 1, 2022, documented the soil removal, consolidation, and capping activities completed for the barium-containing soils (BCS) as part of the Interim Phase 1 construction. DTSC found that these efforts were performed in accordance with the approved Remedial Action Plan (RAP), Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP), and two approved variance requests to the RDIP (dated April 17, 2020, and September 21, 2020). The accepted RACR included As-Built Plans, cross-section figures, final survey data, and the final Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Plan.

Following the acceptance of the Interim RACR, DTSC committed to preparing a draft O&M Agreement for Caltrans' review within 30 days and to complete a Land Use Covenant (LUC) for the remediated area for recording with the Stanislaus County Assessor's office. This acceptance marks the conclusion of the Interim Phase 1 effort. However, final certification of the RAP will not occur until after the completion of the Ultimate Phase 2 State Route 132 build-out, which is anticipated to begin in 8 to 10 years and will involve adding additional lanes. The Ultimate Phase 2 work will require a supplemental RDIP for DTSC review and approval, field oversight, an Ultimate Phase 2 RACR, and potential revisions to the O&M Plan and O&M Agreement.

### Operation and Maintenance Plan Comments

Prior to the acceptance of the RACR, DTSC provided comments on the Draft Operation and Maintenance Plan (Draft O&M Plan), dated March 2022, which was intended to be included as Appendix G of the RACR. These comments, issued on August 1, 2022, from DTSC to Geocon Consultants, Inc., addressed various aspects of the plan for inspecting, maintaining, and repairing the portion of SR 132 used as a clean cap cover over two BCS stockpiles.

Key comments included:
*   Correction of a misspelling of "Maintenance" in the Subject line.
*   Inclusion of a minimum advance notification period (DTSC recommended 10 working days) for any BCS disturbance in Section 2.1.
*   Revision of Section 2.4 to explicitly state that Health and Safety Code (HSC) 25123.3(b)(4)(A) requirements will be met, clarifying that the minimum thickness of 6 millimeters would satisfy HSC requirements, and correcting a misspelling of "millimeter."
*   Expansion of Section 3.1 to include inspections after events such as fires, substantial rainfall (1 inch in 24 hours), significant seismic events, or traffic accidents that could damage the cap, in addition to annual inspections.
*   Inclusion in Section 2.7 of periodic patrols to note if unauthorized access, such as homeless encampments, has impacted the cap, particularly on the north vegetated slope.
*   A recommendation to expand the O&M Site Inspection Form.

### Site Record and Verification

The Interim RACR covers the consolidation and clean fill capping of BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3. These stockpiles, along with aerially deposited lead (ADL) impacted soil from the unpaved shoulder of northbound SR 99, were consolidated and capped with DTSC-approved clean fill materials. Remaining work includes the placement of structural pavement, erosion control, drainage, and median barriers.

Specific removal verification testing was conducted for BCS Stockpiles 2 and 3. For Stockpile 3, excavation reached approximate elevations between 82.5 and 84 feet above mean sea level (MSL). A total of 24 verification soil samples were collected between February 28 and March 12, 2020. Following initial results, areas with elevated barium concentrations were deepened and resampled. Final excavation soil samples from Stockpile 3 contained barium concentrations ranging from 46 to 970 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg, meeting the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) verification thresholds.

For Stockpile 2, excavation at the west end reached an approximate elevation of 82 feet MSL, with subsequent deepening to 81 feet MSL. The east end was excavated to an approximate elevation of 83 feet MSL, with deepening to 82 feet MSL and then 81 feet MSL in areas with elevated barium. Verification soil samples from Stockpile 2 contained barium concentrations ranging from 54 to 760 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg, meeting the RDIP verification thresholds.

Characterization testing was also performed for potential clean cap borrow areas. The Carpenter Road Shoofly borrow area, sampled on February 21, 2020, yielded soil with barium concentrations ranging from 60 to 110 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.7 to 7.0 mg/kg, consistent with background concentrations and suitable for clean cover fill. The Basin 5 borrow area, sampled on June 16, 2020, showed barium concentrations from 66 to 94 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 5.8 to 11 mg/kg, also consistent with background.

Testing of Bent 2 excavation soil stockpiles, conducted on October 30, 2020, revealed barium concentrations ranging from 31 to 230 mg/kg. Four of these samples exceeded the upperbound background threshold for barium of 120 mg/kg. Lead concentrations ranged from 2.6 to 13 mg/kg. This material was deemed potentially suitable for partial clean cap (6 inches or less) pending DTSC review. Carpenter Shoofly clean fill characterization testing, performed on April 12, 2021, showed barium concentrations ranging from 28 to 96 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg, meeting the clean cap criteria.

Air monitoring performed during BCS grading activities indicated airborne levels of total dust, barium, and lead below designated project action and risk levels, confirming effective dust control.

### Regulatory Thresholds

The RDIP verification thresholds for BCS removal are:
*   Barium: 1,000 mg/kg (groundwater protection)
*   Lead: 80 mg/kg (residential screening level)

The upperbound background threshold for barium, as approved by DTSC for clean cap material, is 120 mg/kg.
