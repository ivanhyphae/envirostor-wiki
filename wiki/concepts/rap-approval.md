## Rap Approval

DTSC approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, formalized in a public notice dated February 21, 2018. The approval selected containment as the cleanup remedy for approximately 160,000 cubic yards of metals-affected soil (primarily barium, strontium, and lead) held in three stockpiles within the state right-of-way south of Kansas Avenue, and it set the regulatory framework — land use controls, inspection, and groundwater monitoring — that governed all subsequent work at the site, including the later Stockpile 3 barium-containing soil (BCS) removal.

### What the RAP approved

The selected remedy contains the stockpiles in place during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project rather than removing them wholesale: material is contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway, with clean fill cover placed over unpaved portions. DTSC's public notice states this containment approach provides long-term protection by preventing exposure and minimizing infiltration of surface water into groundwater beneath the stockpiles, and that land use controls, inspection of pavement and other containment features, and groundwater monitoring would be conducted annually to confirm the remedy's integrity going forward.

### CEQA/NEPA basis for the approval

Caltrans, as CEQA/NEPA lead agency, prepared an EIR/EA for the SR 132 project; after certifying it, Caltrans issued a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) under NEPA and a Notice of Determination (NOD) under CEQA. DTSC, acting as a CEQA responsible agency, conducted an independent analysis and concluded that the selected containment remedy would not have a significant environmental effect once the project's mitigation measures were implemented, and found that the Final EIR/EA contained all necessary mitigation measures to keep impacts less than significant. DTSC prepared its own Statement of Findings and NOD consistent with that conclusion. This dual sign-off — Caltrans' EIR/EA/FONSI/NOD and DTSC's independent CEQA findings — is the documented basis on which the RAP was allowed to proceed to implementation.

### Consequence for Stockpile 3: departure from pure containment

Although the approved remedy was containment, the record shows Stockpile 3 was instead subject to physical removal. A March 13, 2020 technical memorandum from Geocon Consultants, Inc. documented removal of Stockpile 3 BCS and confirmation sampling against cleanup levels set in the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP). DTSC (Dean Wright, Project Manager) accepted the memorandum by email on March 18, 2020, agreeing that the sampling results showed the BCS had been removed, that remaining soil met RDIP verification thresholds, and that no further RDIP monitoring was required for that area, which was then deemed suitable for clean embankment fill. Caltrans' March 16, 2020 transmittal to DTSC and the Regional Water Board confirmed that all Stockpile 3 BCS material had been excavated to depths where barium and lead concentrations in native soil fell below the RDIP threshold values, and that the removal and relocation of all BCS stockpile material was complete — explicitly excluding the separately regulated ADL soil along northbound SR 99. That same transmittal sought approval to place a minimum 12-inch clean soil cover and/or minimum 4-inch aggregate base layer over the BCS consolidation zones of Stockpiles 1 and 2, indicating those two stockpiles remained under the containment approach approved in the RAP while Stockpile 3 was closed out by removal and verification instead.

### Why this matters for the case file

The RAP approval is the instrument that authorized containment as the remedy and imposed the ongoing obligations — annual inspection and groundwater monitoring, land use controls — that apply to the stockpiles going forward. DTSC's March 2020 acceptance of the Stockpile 3 verification memo shows that, for at least one of the three stockpiles, the containment obligation was superseded by removal and closed out on the basis of confirmation sampling, removing that area from further RDIP monitoring. Stockpiles 1 and 2 remained subject to the cover/containment measures pending DTSC and Water Board approval of the clean cover placement described in the same correspondence.
