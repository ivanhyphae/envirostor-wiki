## Overview
The Overhead Sign #1 foundation excavation is a planned excavation within the southern portion of the Stockpile 2 BCS Containment Zone for the [[sr-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. This excavation is anticipated to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS). The management and disposal/reuse of this BCS material are addressed in the "Stockpile 2 BCS Foundation Spoil Assessment Workplan" prepared by [[geocon-consultants]].

## Site Record
The foundation excavation for Overhead Sign #1 is planned at approximate Station 1139+30. It is projected to be a 5-foot-diameter by 25-foot-deep excavation. This excavation is expected to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of [[barium-containing-soil]]. This material, along with spoils from the [[weather-station-foundation-slab-anchor]] excavation, is conservatively estimated to total 40 cubic yards.

The BCS material will be stockpiled prior to offsite landfill disposal or onsite reuse. The stockpiled material will be pre-moistened and covered with minimum 6-millimeter-thick plastic sheeting, secured with ropes and weights, and surrounded by erosion control measures. Eight discrete soil samples will be collected from the stockpile. These samples will be composited into two 4-part composite samples and analyzed for Title 22 metals.

The total [[barium]] and [[lead]] concentrations will be compared to site-specific background levels of Barium - 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and Lead - 3.8 mg/kg. If analytical data does not exceed these background concentrations, the BCS may be suitable for onsite reuse, subject to approval by Caltrans and DTSC. If onsite reuse is not approved, the BCS material will be profiled for acceptance at Forward Landfill in Manteca or another accepting landfill.

## Regulatory Status
The management of the BCS material from the Overhead Sign #1 foundation excavation is subject to Caltrans and [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) approval for onsite reuse. The material will be analyzed to determine if it is a California or Federal Resource Conservation and Recovery Act hazardous waste, based on soluble metals testing if total metal concentrations are elevated. The total barium and lead concentrations will be compared to site-specific background levels of Barium - 120 mg/kg and Lead - 3.8 mg/kg.
