The Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) is a regulatory agency that, in conjunction with the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), provides oversight for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project. The CVRWQCB's primary concern has been the protection of groundwater quality.

The CVRWQCB, along with DTSC, reviewed and approved the Feasibility Study (FS) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. They also reviewed the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and indicated that the stockpiles would require ongoing maintenance to protect groundwater quality if the SR-132 Project were not constructed. The CVRWQCB is also a reviewing agency for the Environmental Impact Report (EIR) for the SR-132 Project and has approved various technical memorandums and reports related to the remediation and containment of the soil stockpiles, including those concerning clean fill material and stormwater sampling.

The CVRWQCB, in cooperation with DTSC, is the lead regulatory agency overseeing the site mitigation under a Caltrans Interagency Agreement. Caltrans is responsible for the implementation of the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) and ongoing groundwater and stormwater monitoring activities related to the barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles, with reports submitted to both DTSC and the CVRWQCB. Annual groundwater sampling events are to continue until the CVRWQCB determines that the clean-capped BCS material no longer poses a threat to groundwater quality. Stormwater sampling events are also conducted in the vicinity of the containment zones during qualifying rain events. Caltrans will perform five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the containment measures, with monitoring and maintenance of implemented containment and potential for exposure being the focus.

The CVRWQCB has been involved in the approval of various technical memorandums related to clean fill material. For instance, the CVRWQCB, along with DTSC, reviewed and approved a Revised Technical Memorandum concerning the Basin 5 clean cap borrow area, agreeing that the composite soil sample results were consistent with naturally occurring background concentrations and suitable for capping Stockpiles 1 and 2. Similarly, the CVRWQCB, in consultation with DTSC, reviewed and approved the Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Technical Memorandum, finding the soil sample results consistent with background concentrations and meeting the Barium Containing Soil clean cap criteria. The CVRWQCB also reviewed and accepted a Technical Memorandum regarding the characterization of excavated Bent 2 soils for use as clean cap material, agreeing that the soil could be placed on top of Stockpile 2 under specific conditions to prevent jeopardizing the remedy's integrity. Furthermore, the CVRWQCB, with DTSC, reviewed and accepted a Technical Memorandum concerning the Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing excavation area, agreeing that material from 1 to 5 feet below grade could be used as clean cap soil, provided it was placed within the Stockpiles 1 and 2 fill containment zones or used as general bridge embankment fill. They also reviewed and accepted a revised Technical Memorandum for Stockpile 2 MSE wall footing excavation, agreeing that soil below 3 feet below ground surface was suitable as embankment fill or capping material.
