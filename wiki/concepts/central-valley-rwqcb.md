The Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) is the co-lead regulatory agency, alongside [[dtsc]], overseeing remediation of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles under a Caltrans-DTSC Interagency Agreement. While DTSC issues the formal correspondence and holds signature authority on most decision documents, the CVRWQCB reviews and concurs on every major technical submittal — the Feasibility Study, the Remedial Action Plan, the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) and its variances, groundwater and stormwater evaluations, and well destruction — and its particular concern throughout has been the stockpiles' potential to degrade groundwater quality, a concern distinct from DTSC's human-health focus.

## Role and distinct concern

Where DTSC's 2009 Final PEA response found the stockpiles posed no human-health risk to Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents, the CVRWQCB maintained a separate position: that the stockpiles would need to continue to be managed to protect groundwater quality if the SR-132 Project were not built. This distinction is preserved through the FS and RAP, both of which state that "the potential for the stockpiles to impact groundwater from a water quality degradation standpoint remains a concern of the CVRWQCB" even after DTSC concurred with the HHRA update. The 2014 FS and RAP name Alternative 4 (Containment) as achieving DTSC and CVRWQCB concurrence, and both agencies are recorded as approving the FS.

At the CVRWQCB's specific request, the FS also priced out complete removal and offsite disposal of Stockpile 3 with clean-fill replacement, as an alternative to consolidating it into Stockpiles 1 and 2 — a request that shaped the cost comparison in the FS and RAP even though containment was ultimately selected.

## Project organization and named representatives

Both the 2019 RDIP – Interim Phase 1 and the 2021 Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) list CVRWQCB, alongside DTSC, as the regulatory agency providing site mitigation oversight under the Caltrans-DTSC Interagency Agreement (Task Order No. 4, Amendment No. 1, dated June 17, 2016). The RDIP names Steven Meeks, P.E., Chief of Private Sites Cleanup, as CVRWQCB Project Manager; later correspondence (2021–2025) is addressed to Kyle Cockerham, PG, of the Site Cleanup Unit. DTSC's conditional approval letters for the RDIP (August 10, 2018 and October 12, 2018) each state they were prepared "in consultation with" the CVRWQCB, and both are copied to Meeks.

## Concurrence record on remedy implementation

The CVRWQCB is recorded as co-reviewer or co-approver — via DTSC correspondence stating decisions were made "in consultation with" the RWQCB — on a sequence of field decisions during Interim Phase 1 construction:

| Date | Submittal | CVRWQCB/DTSC determination |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 17, 2020 | Variance Request, Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing soil | Soil from 1–5 ft bgs (95% UCL barium 113.1 mg/kg) approved as clean cap; upper foot required placement in containment zones |
| Apr 7, 2020 | Stockpile 1 Wall Footing Sampling Tech Memo | Soil with 95% UCL above 120 mg/kg background required placement in fill containment zones or use as embankment fill, not as cap |
| Jun 22, 2020 | Stockpile 2 Wall Footing Tech Memo (revised) | Soil above 3 ft bgs required placement in containment zones; soil below 3 ft approved as embankment fill or cap, covered under the existing April 9, 2020 variance |
| Nov 6, 2020 | Bent 2 Soils Tech Memo | Soil did not meet clean-cap criteria from the April 9, 2020 variance, but was approved for placement atop Stockpile 2 at 6 inches or less if covered by base material and pavement |
| Feb 11, 2021 | Revised Soil Management Plan (retention basins) | Accepted with two field-correctable conditions (labeling of TPH-exceedance soil; escalation to CVRWQCB/DTSC if TPH impact persists at maximum basin depth) |
| Apr 22, 2021 | Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Tech Memo | Composite results found consistent with background and accepted as meeting clean-cap criteria |

In each case the acceptance is issued by DTSC's Dean Wright, but addressed to a distribution that includes CVRWQCB staff, and the March 25, 2021 email from Kyle Cockerham confirms the Water Board independently reviewed the December 17, 2020 Surface Water Report and had no comment — indicating the CVRWQCB exercised its own review rather than passively deferring to DTSC.

## Groundwater monitoring and well destruction

The CVRWQCB's groundwater-protection concern is the basis for the site's post-construction monitoring and eventual well destruction decision. The February 2024 Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data compared barium and strontium concentrations in the four remaining Caltrans wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, MW10) to FMC facility site-specific target levels approved by the CVRWQCB in FMC's own Remedial Action Plan (ERM, 2020): 6,210 µg/l for barium and 61,900 µg/l for strontium. Caltrans well concentrations were found roughly 40 times below the barium target and more than 20 times below the strontium target.

On May 3, 2024, DTSC's Dean Wright issued a letter stating that "DTSC and the RWQCB agree" the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and will not impact it in the future, and that both agencies concurred with discontinuing groundwater monitoring and decommissioning the four remaining wells. This is the CVRWQCB's culminating position on the water-quality question it had flagged as unresolved a decade earlier: the joint determination directly authorized the June 2025 destruction of MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10, closing out the monitoring obligation the RWQCB had insisted on since the 2007 HHRA era.

## Final Phase 1 acceptance

DTSC's January 18, 2023 letter accepting the Interim Phase 1 RACR as final states the review was conducted "in consultation with" the CVRWQCB and that DTSC had no comments. Final certification of the RAP itself, however, is deferred until completion of Ultimate Phase 2 (anticipated 8 to 10 years from that letter), meaning CVRWQCB's groundwater-protection oversight role remains open pending Phase 2 construction and a supplemental RDIP.
