Thallium was detected at low concentrations in stockpile soil in the June 2014 Final FS Report, well before it appeared in any water sample at the site. It had not been detected in groundwater or in surface water sampled before 2023. The December 2023 stormwater event is the first reported exceedance of the California primary MCL for thallium at the site; the preceding March 2023 stormwater round did not report thallium above MCLs. Reports covering the December 2023 result are dated February 2024 and February 2025.

The record does not give concentration values for the soil detections or for the December 2023 stormwater exceedance, and it does not record what, if anything, followed from the exceedance. What the record does establish is the sequence: a constituent present in stockpile soil since 2014 remained absent from groundwater and surface water for roughly nine years, then surfaced in stormwater at levels above the primary MCL at the end of 2023, after having been below MCLs just months earlier in March 2023.

This bears on the claim that groundwater has not been impacted: the thallium exceedance is in stormwater, not groundwater, so it does not by itself contradict that claim, but it establishes that a soil-borne constituent can mobilize into water at the site under conditions present in late 2023 that were not present in March 2023. Whether that mobilization has any bearing on groundwater conditions, and what caused the difference between the March and December 2023 sampling rounds, is not addressed in the available material.
