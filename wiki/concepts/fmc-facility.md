## Overview

The **FMC Facility** (Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation, formerly Barium Products LTD and Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation) was a chemical processing plant that operated from 1929 to approximately 1985 on property less than 500 feet north of the current Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, south of the SR‑99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The facility processed barite (barium sulfate) and celestite (strontium sulfate) into greases, lubricating oil, and pigment blanks, with sodium sulfide generated as a by‑product. From the early 1950s through the late 1970s, liquid wastes (“tailings”) were discharged to seven unlined evaporation ponds on‑site. In 1961, the State of California purchased a 4.3‑acre parcel covering a portion of those ponds for construction of SR‑99. During SR‑99 construction (circa 1963–1967), soil from and around one of the former FMC ponds was excavated and placed into three stockpiles within Caltrans right‑of‑way, forming the three stockpiles that remain today.

## Site Record

- **Operators and chronology**: Barium Products LTD and Westvaco Chlorine Products Corporation preceded FMC at the location. Chemical processing ran from 1929 to about 1985. Liquid residues were discharged to unlined ponds from the early 1950s to the 1970s. The State’s 1961 parcel acquisition included a portion of those ponds. Aerial photographs from 1963 and 1967 confirm that Stockpiles 2 and 3 had been placed by 1963, and Stockpile 1 was in its current configuration by 1967. Haul roads for all three stockpiles were within Caltrans right‑of‑way.
- **Contaminants of Potential Concern (COPCs)**: More than 16 chemicals were analyzed and detected. The primary COPCs identified in the soil stockpiles were **metals** (notably barium) and **polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**; in groundwater the primary COPCs were metals and general minerals (nitrate, total dissolved solids). Specific constituents evaluated in the human health risk assessment (HHRA) included **arsenic, barium, strontium, carcinogenic PAHs, vanadium, lead, and nickel**. Cadmium was detected above commercial/industrial CHHSLs in Stockpiles #2 and #3 during the 2004 PSI but was not considered a COPC in the HHRA because 2006 SI results were not elevated. Strontium was not considered a COPC because its maximum concentration was more than two orders of magnitude below the USEPA residential RSL of 47,000 mg/kg.
- **Concentrations and spatial distribution**: Over 440 soil samples were collected across multiple investigations (Shaw 2004, 2006, 2009; Geocon 2013). The greatest concentrations of barium are located at depths of 5 feet or greater below ground surface. Maximum surface soil concentrations of arsenic, carcinogenic PAHs, and vanadium are **within the range of local background concentrations**. Maximum surface soil concentrations of barium and nickel are **less than USEPA residential Regional Screening Levels (RSLs)** — the USEPA RSL for barium is 15,000 mg/kg (residential, 1E‑06 risk). Strontium and nitrate were detected at concentrations lower than any established safe level.
- **Groundwater**: Eight monitoring wells were installed in 2006, supplemented by two additional wells in 2012. Initial groundwater samples from June and October 2006 met primary and secondary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for all constituents analyzed. Quarterly monitoring resumed in 2013 and has continued. Groundwater flow is toward the east‑southeast at an average gradient of 0.0005 to 0.001, at depths of approximately 30–40 feet below ground surface. No cancer‑causing chemicals have been detected in groundwater; low concentrations of arsenic are believed to be naturally occurring.
- **Migration evaluation**: Surface soil at the fence line and stockpile edges was analyzed to assess wind‑blown dust and surface runoff migration; concentrations are not significantly different from those in the stockpile interiors. Leaching to groundwater was not shown to have caused off‑site impacts.

## Regulatory Status

- **Oversight agencies**: The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) provide regulatory oversight for the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and are also reviewing agencies for the CEQA EIR prepared by Caltrans for the SR‑132 West Project.
- **Risk determinations**: DTSC concluded that, as managed by Caltrans (perimeter fencing, access limited to authorized personnel, vegetative cover maintained, no soil excavation or addition, and routine groundwater/surface water monitoring), the soil stockpiles **do not pose an unacceptable risk** to Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. The Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA, 2009) and subsequent reviews support this conclusion.
- **Selected remedy**: RAP Alternative 4 (Containment) is the recommended and approved alternative. It will contain the stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the pavement of the SR‑132 West Freeway/Expressway Project; unpaved areas will receive clean fill cover. This eliminates human exposure pathways and minimizes surface water infiltration. Caltrans must enter into an **Operation and Maintenance Agreement** with DTSC and prepare an **Operation and Maintenance Plan** for DTSC review and approval. The plan will require **annual inspection** of pavement and containment features, continued **quarterly groundwater monitoring**, and DTSC **five‑year reviews** of remedy performance.

## Open Items

- **Construction‑phase dust and air monitoring**: During grading or consolidation of stockpiles (Stockpile 3 will be consolidated into the SR‑132 overcrossing abutment; excess soil placed on Stockpile 2 and covered with clean fill), DTSC will require thorough wetting of all work areas and **air monitoring** to protect workers and the public.
- **Operation and Maintenance Plan**: To be prepared and submitted to DTSC for approval prior to construction completion. It will specify annual inspection protocols and groundwater monitoring frequency.
- **Long‑term stewardship**: DTSC will evaluate the containment remedy every five years to ensure it continues to function as designed.
