## No Impact To Groundwater

### Overview

The determination that the [[caltrans-encapsulated-soil-stockpiles]] at the SR 132, Stanislaus County, California site do not impact groundwater is a key finding supporting the discontinuation of groundwater monitoring and the decommissioning of monitoring wells. This conclusion has been reviewed and approved by regulatory agencies.

### Site Record

An Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, dated February 26, 2024, was submitted by [[geocon-consultants]] on behalf of [[caltrans]]. This evaluation assessed the potential for the [[barium-and-lead-impacted-soil]] stockpiles, encapsulated beneath the State Route (SR) 132 Expressway, to impact groundwater. The evaluation noted consistency with an earlier 2014 assessment and the recent encapsulation of the soil.

Based on this evaluation, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) agreed that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and will not impact groundwater in the future. This agreement was communicated in correspondence dated May 3, 2024.

As a result of this determination, the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring and decommission the four remaining monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) was concurred with by both DTSC and the RWQCB. Six other wells (MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, and MW8) were previously destroyed in 2019 due to conflicts with highway construction.

### Regulatory Status

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) have both agreed that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater. This agreement, documented in correspondence dated May 3, 2024, supports the decision to discontinue groundwater monitoring and decommission wells.

### Open Items

The four remaining groundwater monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) are to be decommissioned as per the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan. A Well Destruction Report dated June 18, 2025, documents the destruction of four wells associated with the site.
