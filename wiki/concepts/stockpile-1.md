# Stockpile 1

Stockpile 1 was one of three barium-containing soil (BCS) piles created in the early 1960s from excavation of soil and tailings from the former FMC (Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation) evaporation ponds and adjacent native ground, placed within Caltrans right-of-way south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue interchange in anticipation of the future SR 132 interchange. Located approximately 150 feet west of Emerald Avenue, it was roughly 600 feet long east-west, 160 feet wide, with an estimated fill volume of 34,000 cubic yards. Unlike Stockpiles 2 and 3, no soil was excavated from Stockpile 1 itself for removal; instead, Stockpile 1 became the primary receiving area for barium-containing soil removed from the other two stockpiles, and its own southerly slope was excavated only to make room for construction of an MSE retaining wall.

## Role as the consolidation zone

Under the 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP), no BCS removal was planned for Stockpile 1 itself. Instead, its upper surface, southern slope area, and the adjacent vacant right-of-way extending east to the Emerald Avenue bridge abutment were designated as consolidation fill placement zones. Stockpile 1 received the entire 27,675 cubic yards (approximately 3,075 truckloads) of BCS excavated from the complete removal of Stockpile 3 between February and March 2020, transported by covered haul trucks and placed directly as compacted highway embankment fill — temporary stockpiling en route was not permitted. Stockpile 1's containment zone also received ADL (aerially deposited lead)-contaminated soil excavated from the northbound SR 99 shoulder between L Street and the Kansas Avenue off-ramp, placed in the eastern end of the containment zone in June–July 2020 under the 2016 DTSC-Caltrans Soil Management Agreement for ADL soil.

## MSE wall footing excavation and characterization

In March–April 2020, the southerly slope of Stockpile 1 was excavated (steepened) to allow clean construction of an MSE retaining wall along the southern SR 132 right-of-way boundary. The excavated slope material was removed by scrapers and placed within the Stockpile 1 containment zone. Because this excavated soil was a candidate source of clean capping material, Geocon characterized it separately from the BCS removal verification process used for Stockpiles 2 and 3.

Geocon collected soil samples at one-foot intervals to the maximum planned wall footing depth of 5 feet from three trenches (STK1-1, STK1-2, STK1-3) spaced at roughly 200-foot intervals, documented in an April 6, 2020 Technical Memorandum. Barium was detected in all 15 samples at 42 to 420 mg/kg — above the maximum site-specific background value of 120 mg/kg established in the April 9, 2020 RDIP Variance, but well below the RDIP removal threshold of 1,000 mg/kg. The calculated 95% upper confidence limit and mean of the dataset were 171.9 and 128.3 mg/kg, respectively. Lead was detected in 14 of 15 samples at 1.1 to 6.7 mg/kg, within the range of the 3.8 mg/kg site-specific background value and well below the 80 mg/kg RDIP threshold.

DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board reviewed the memo and, in an approval dated April 7, 2020, concurred with its findings except for the disposition of the material: because the 95% UCL exceeded the 120 mg/kg background threshold and the MSE wall footing area fell outside the borrow area defined in the RDIP Variance, the agencies required that this soil be placed within the Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones or used as general bridge embankment fill — it could not be used as capping material as Geocon's memo had proposed as one option. All of the excavated Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing material was accordingly placed within the containment zones.

## Containment zone extent and survey

Following consolidation, the completed Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone is located adjacent and west of Emerald Avenue between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50. The RDIP had earlier described a slightly different limit (1125+60 through 1131+95), reflecting adjustment of the containment footprint as design progressed. Dokken Engineering prepared as-built plans, cross sections, and profiles of the completed zone from contractor-provided survey data, and completed a legal description and boundary survey used by Caltrans to record a DTSC-required land use covenant/deed restriction with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder.

## Capping, erosion, and remediation

The majority of the upper surface and northern slope of the Stockpile 1 containment zone were capped with DTSC-approved clean fill material (sourced from the Carpenter Road excavation area, Basin 5, and the Carpenter Road shoofly) by fall 2020; the southern slope was capped with embankment fill and MSE wall backfill. In January 2021, a record rain event caused significant erosion of the clean cap on the exposed, unvegetated northerly slope. Plastic sheeting was installed as an interim measure; it was removed and the eroded areas regraded in September 2021 using clean fill from the Carpenter Road shoofly excavation. The slope was vegetated in October 2021 with native hydroseed/hydromulch, erosion control blanket, and fiber rolls, to be maintained by Caltrans until Ultimate Phase 2 construction completes final containment (retaining wall and pavement) along the northerly portion of Stockpile 1.

## Regulatory closure of the interim phase

Geocon submitted the Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR), covering completed containment of Stockpiles 1 and 2, to DTSC on December 23, 2021; DTSC issued an acceptance letter dated February 24, 2022. DTSC, Caltrans, the City, and other project personnel performed a joint site survey on June 28, 2022 confirming completed clean capping of the Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones and compliance with the RDIP. Groundwater and stormwater monitoring in the vicinity of Stockpile 1 continue under Caltrans and Stantec, reported jointly with Stockpile 2 monitoring data, pending a Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board determination that the clean-capped material does not pose a material threat to groundwater quality.
