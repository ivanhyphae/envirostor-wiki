Clean Fill Suitable As Cover is the compliance standard governing what soil or aggregate material could be placed over the barium-containing soil (BCS) in Stockpiles 1 and 2 to serve as the final or interim cap required by the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP). Two distinct pathways satisfied this standard on the SR 132 project: aggregate subbase material approved as an interim substitute for the RDIP's specified cover, and native soil excavated from the Carpenter Road Shoofly, characterized and approved as capping material against a barium background threshold. Whether material qualified as clean fill determined whether it could be placed directly on the stockpiles without triggering additional remedial handling.

## Aggregate subbase as interim clean cover (RDIP Variance #2)

The RDIP (Section 3.2.2) specified either a minimum 12-inch-thick clean cover fill layer over the BCS and aerially-deposited lead (ADL) consolidation zones, or, as an alternative, a minimum 4-inch-thick aggregate base (AB) layer pending later placement of structural pavement and hardscape. In RDIP Variance #2, dated September 2, 2020 and submitted to DTSC's Dean Wright, Geocon proposed substituting 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase for the AB alternative, on the basis that its gradation was primarily sand and gravel with lesser fines content similar to aggregate base. The Interim Phase I structural pavement section to be placed above it consisted of 10.2 inches of concrete pavement, 3.0 inches of hot mix asphalt, and 8.4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase, with hardscape covering areas outside the highway pavement footprint. Geocon concluded this 4-inch Class 3 aggregate subbase layer met the intent of the RDIP as adequate interim clean cover on the upper surface of Stockpiles 1 and 2 until the remaining pavement/hardscape section was placed.

## Carpenter Road Shoofly soil as clean fill (sampling plan and tech memo)

The contractor separately proposed using approximately 5,000 cubic yards of native soil excavated from the Carpenter Road Shoofly — generated once the SR 132 overcrossing structure under construction was completed — as clean capping material over portions of BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2. This required DTSC approval and characterization testing under the April 9, 2020 Variance to the RDIP.

A sampling plan for the Carpenter Shoofly area was submitted and, per DTSC's Dean Wright's email of April 7, 2021, found consistent with previous borrow area sampling efforts, with no comments; this served as DTSC's approval of the plan. Geocon then collected 24 discrete soil samples along six vertical transects (CSF1–CSF6), at 2, 5, 10, and 15 feet from the top of the slope, composited into eight 3-part composite samples and analyzed for barium and lead by EPA Method 6010B. Results were reported in the Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Characterization Testing Technical Memorandum, dated April 12, 2021:

| Analyte | Detected range | Site-specific background threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Barium | 28–96 mg/kg (10 of 10 samples) | 120 mg/kg (maximum) |
| Lead | 1.4–3.8 mg/kg (9 of 10 samples) | 3.8 mg/kg (maximum) |

All barium results fell below the 120 mg/kg background value, and all lead detections were at or below the 3.8 mg/kg background value. On this basis Geocon concluded the Carpenter Shoofly soil met the BCS clean cap criteria established in the DTSC-approved variance. DTSC's Dean Wright confirmed this in an email dated April 22, 2021, stating that DTSC and the Water Board agreed the composite results were consistent with naturally occurring background concentrations and met the Barium Containing Soil clean cap criteria — DTSC's acceptance of the Technical Memorandum. The Technical Memorandum states this characterization information would be included in the Removal Action Completion Report to be submitted following completion of the Stockpile 1 and 2 clean fill capping activities.

## Significance

Both approvals turned on the same background-threshold logic used elsewhere at the site: material is "clean" for capping purposes if its constituent concentrations fall within naturally occurring background levels rather than requiring a fixed regulatory cleanup number. For the Carpenter Shoofly soil, that threshold was explicit — 120 mg/kg barium, 3.8 mg/kg lead — and every sample cleared it, which is what allowed roughly 5,000 cubic yards of excavated highway soil to be reused on-site as stockpile cover rather than requiring disposal or import of certified clean material. For the aggregate subbase, the qualifying logic rested on material composition (gradation similar to aggregate base) rather than analytical testing, and its approval was expressly interim, pending completion of the full structural pavement/hardscape section specified in the RDIP.
