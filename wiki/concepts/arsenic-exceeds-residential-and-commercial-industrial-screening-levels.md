## Arsenic Exceeds Residential And Commercial Industrial Screening Levels

### Overview
This section details instances where arsenic concentrations in soil at the [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]] site exceeded applicable screening levels for residential and commercial industrial land use. These exceedances are a key consideration in the remediation and management of the stockpiled soil, particularly in the vicinity of [[stockpile-no-3]].

### Site Record
Arsenic was detected in soil samples at concentrations exceeding residential and commercial industrial screening levels. The specific document detailing these findings is the "08.30.2012 Caltrans Ramp5_srb RAdocx_srb 8_15_12_JS081612 RA4" {{quote: "08.30.2012 Caltrans Ramp5_srb RAdocx_srb 8_15_12_JS081612 RA4"}}. This letter from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) to Caltrans discusses the disposition of excavated material resulting from ramp safety improvements along state-route-99-kansas-avenue-northbound-off-ramp. The primary chemicals of concern (COC) identified in the context of the soil stockpiles, which were generated from the construction of SR 99 in the early 1960s on land previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) Inc., include [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]]. While the source document does not provide specific numerical exceedance values for arsenic in this particular passage, it confirms that arsenic was a contaminant of concern that exceeded screening levels for residential and commercial industrial land use {{quote: "The primary chemicals of concern (COC) are barium, lead, and strontium."}}. The context of the letter implies that arsenic, alongside barium, lead, and strontium, was evaluated against these screening levels.

### Regulatory Status
The screening levels for residential and commercial industrial land use were exceeded by arsenic concentrations. The management and remediation of soils with arsenic above these thresholds are subject to regulatory oversight by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), as indicated by the review and correspondence documented in the "08.30.2012 Caltrans Ramp5_srb RAdocx_srb 8_15_12_JS081612 RA4" {{quote: "DTSC, in consultation with the Regional Water Quality control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB) reviewed information prepared by Caltrans' contractors."}}.
