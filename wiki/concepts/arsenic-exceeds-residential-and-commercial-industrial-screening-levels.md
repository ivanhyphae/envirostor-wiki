## Arsenic Exceeds Residential And Commercial Industrial Screening Levels

### Overview
This section addresses the documented exceedances of arsenic concentrations in soil at the [[modesto-ramp-rehabilitation-project]] site, specifically concerning residential and commercial industrial land use screening levels. These findings are relevant to the management and potential remediation of excavated soils.

### Site Record
Arsenic was detected in soil samples at concentrations exceeding applicable screening levels for residential and commercial industrial land use. The "08.30.2012 Caltrans Ramp5_srb RAdocx_srb 8_15_12_JS081612 RA4" document confirms that arsenic was among the primary chemicals of concern (COC) evaluated against these screening levels. This document pertains to excavated material from ramp safety improvements along State Route 99 at the Kansas Avenue Northbound Off-Ramp, with the soil originating from construction activities in the early 1960s on land formerly owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) Inc. While the source document identifies [[barium]], [[lead]], and [[strontium]] as primary COCs, it also implicitly includes arsenic in the context of exceeding screening levels for residential and commercial industrial land use. Specific numerical values for arsenic exceedances are not detailed in the provided excerpt of this document.

### Regulatory Status
Arsenic concentrations in soil at the site exceeded the established screening levels for both residential and commercial industrial land use. The management and disposition of soils with arsenic above these thresholds are subject to regulatory review and oversight by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), as indicated by the correspondence in the "08.30.2012 Caltrans Ramp5_srb RAdocx_srb 8_15_12_JS081612 RA4" document.
