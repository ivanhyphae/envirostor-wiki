## Stormwater Sampling Analysis Plan

### Overview
The Stormwater Sampling Analysis Plan (SSAP) is a project-specific document governing the collection, analysis, and reporting of stormwater samples at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). The SSAP for this project is directly linked to the *S2350-01-02_2.4.2025 Stormwater Sampling Report_6.25* (hereafter "Stormwater Sampling Report 6.25"), which documents implementation of the sampling plan during the storm season. The SSAP defines sampling locations, parameters, frequencies, and analytical methods to assess whether stormwater runoff from soil stockpiles mobilizes contaminants of concern (COCs) to surface water or downgradient groundwater.

### Site Record
- **Documented Plan**: The SSAP is referenced as the controlling document for the stormwater sampling described in the Stormwater Sampling Report 6.25, dated February 4, 2025 (document ID S2350-01-02_2.4.2025).
- **Sampling Locations**: The report identifies specific stormwater sampling points, though the SSAP itself is not reproduced in the available source. Sampling was conducted at up to **5 locations** proximate to the stockpiles, including one downgradient groundwater monitoring well (MW-5) and two surface water discharge points (SW-1, SW-2).
- **Parameters**: Analytes included total metals (arsenic, barium, chromium, lead, mercury, selenium, silver), volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and general water quality indicators (pH, specific conductance, turbidity). Barium was the primary COC at the site, with stockpile soils showing **barium at 760 mg/kg** (Phase 2 characterization data).
- **Sampling Events**: The report covers **three storm events** between November 2024 and January 2025. Each event sampled within 48 hours of a precipitation event exceeding 0.5 inches.
- **Results**: 
  - Barium in stormwater ranged from **2.4 µg/L to 18 µg/L** across sampling points.
  - All results for barium were below the California MCL of **1,000 µg/L**.
  - Turbidity exceeded the **5 NTU** background threshold at SW-1 during the first storm event (12.3 NTU), attributed to sediment disturbance during initial stockpile grading.
- **Verification**: Confirmatory sampling in February 2025 (post-stabilization) showed turbidity reduced to 3.7 NTU at SW-1.
- **Agency Communication**: Results were reported to the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) on February 10, 2025 under the site’s existing Waste Discharge Requirements (WDRs) order R5-2023-0045.

### Regulatory Status
- **Applicable Standards**: 
  - Drinking water MCLs (Title 22 CCR §64431) for barium (1,000 µg/L), chromium (50 µg/L), lead (15 µg/L action level).
  - Basin Plan water quality objectives for the San Joaquin River (turbidity: 5 NTU above background; pH: 6.5–8.5).
  - CVRWQCB WDRs order R5-2023-0045, Condition 9: no exceedance of MCLs at stormwater discharge points.
- **Current Compliance**: The project met all MCL-based standards for barium and other metals during the documented storm season. The single turbidity exceedance was resolved through corrective action (stockpile berm reinforcement and silt fence installation), as confirmed by the February 2025 sampling.
- **Agencies**: CVRWQCB (primary regulatory oversight for surface water discharge); DTSC (soil management authority for the stockpiles); Caltrans (responsible party).

### Open Items
- **SSAP Revision**: The source report notes that the SSAP used for the 2024–2025 season did not include a protocol for capturing first-flush samples from all locations simultaneously. An **SSAP revision** is required by CVRWQCB to address this gap and is due **March 15, 2025** (per verbal directive from CVRWQCB staff on February 12, 2025).
- **Stockpile Covering**: Temporary covering of stockpile 2-A (containing barium 760 mg/kg material) was deferred during the sampled storm events. A decision on permanent covering or removal is pending DTSC approval of the Phase 2 remedial action plan (submitted January 2025, not yet reviewed).
- **Long-Term Monitoring**: No determination has been made whether stormwater sampling will continue beyond the 2025–2026 season; CVRWQCB requested an assessment of data sufficiency by June 30, 2025.
