The July 2012 Groundwater Monitoring Report, prepared by Geocon Consultants for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County), documents the results of the site's groundwater monitoring program for that sampling round. It is the vehicle through which analytical results from the site's monitoring wells were compared against California primary and secondary MCLs, and it frames the ongoing question underlying the site's remediation status: whether the stockpiled soils have impacted groundwater beneath the site.

## Scope and purpose

The report covers a single monitoring event (identified as the July 2012 round) and presents laboratory results for the site's monitoring well network. It was prepared by Geocon Consultants and submitted in the context of Caltrans' ongoing groundwater monitoring obligations for the stockpile site, addressed to reviewers including the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board.

## Results and comparison to standards

The report evaluates concentrations of metals, including chromium and manganese, against California primary and secondary MCLs. Manganese, which is subject to a secondary MCL rather than a health-based primary standard, and chromium, subject to a primary MCL, are among the constituents of concern tracked at this site given its history and the presence of FMC Corporation as an adjacent or related source of prior contamination.

## Significance

The document's core function in the case file is to serve as one data point in the longitudinal record of whether groundwater beneath the stockpile site meets applicable drinking-water standards. Because the underlying analytical values for this specific round are not present in the available material, this article cannot state which, if any, constituents exceeded primary or secondary MCLs in July 2012, or how those results compared to prior or subsequent rounds. Any claim about a trend, an exceedance, or a clean result for this round would go beyond what the source supports.
