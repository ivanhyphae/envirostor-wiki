The SR 132 Corridor refers to a segment of State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California, east of Carpenter Road. This area is designated as the proposed source for clean fill soil intended for use as cover material over the [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS) stockpiles associated with the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] remediation project. The State of California has owned the right-of-way since the 1960s, with prior land use being agricultural.

## Site record

The project plan involves excavating the top five feet of soil along the SR 132 alignment, commencing at the [[carpenter-road-area]] and extending eastward. This excavated material is intended for use as clean fill cover for the BCS stockpiles.{{quote: "The contractor plans to excavate the upper five feet of soil along the SR 132 alignment, beginning at Carpenter Road and extending eastward, for use as clean fill cover over the BCS stockpiles."}} Geotechnical test borings conducted at Carpenter Road in 2017 documented the material in this location as native soil.{{quote: "The material at this location is comprised of native soil, as documented by geotechnical test borings completed at Carpenter Road in 2017."}} A review of the Blackburn 2014 Initial Site Assessment (ISA) and the Rincon 2018 Phase II Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) by Geocon Consultants identified no documented high-risk contamination releases in the immediate vicinity of the proposed clean fill source area.{{quote: "Geocon Consultants reviewed the Blackburn 2014 ISA and Rincon 2018 Phase II ESA for information regarding potential contaminant sources and found no high-risk documented contamination releases reported in the immediate vicinity of the proposed clean fill source area."}}

On February 4, 2020, Geocon Consultants, on behalf of Caltrans, submitted a proposed sampling plan to the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) for the characterization of soil within the SR 132 Corridor source area.{{quote: "On February 4, 2020, Geocon Consultants, acting on behalf of Caltrans, submitted a proposed sampling plan to DTSC for characterization of the soil within the SR 132 Corridor source area."}} This plan, prepared by John Juhrend (PE, CEG) of Geocon, outlined 12 borings, each five feet deep, with six depth-discrete composite samples collected for analysis:

*   **0–2 feet**: Three composite samples for organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) and heavy metals.
*   **3–5 feet**: Three composite samples for heavy metals.

{{quote: "The plan, prepared by John Juhrend (PE, CEG) of Geocon, proposed 12 five-foot-deep borings with six depth-discrete four-point composite samples: 0–2 feet: organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) and heavy metals (3 composite samples); 3–5 feet: heavy metals (3 composite samples)."}}
Tentative scheduling for drilling was February 11, 2020, with a planned 48-hour analytical turnaround time if required.{{quote: "Drilling was tentatively scheduled for February 11, 2020, with a 48-hour analytical turnaround planned if necessary."}} Commencement of field work was contingent upon contract authorization (from Dokken or the City), confirmation of utility mark-out (USA notice) from Bay Cities or Geocon (with a 72-hour USA notice), and DTSC approval of the sampling plan.{{quote: "Field work was contingent upon contract authorization (Dokken or City), confirmation of utility mark-out (USA notice) from Bay Cities or Geocon mark-out with a 72-hour USA notice, and receipt of DTSC sampling plan approval."}}

Previous investigations by Rincon at residential backyards adjacent to and west of Carpenter Road, though outside the SR 132 Corridor proper, indicated pesticide and metal concentrations below applicable regulatory screening levels.{{quote: "Testing of residential backyards adjacent to and west of Carpenter Road, conducted by Rincon as part of prior investigations, indicated pesticides and metals at concentrations less than applicable regulatory screening levels. However, these results are from areas outside the SR 132 Corridor proper and are cited as context for the immediate vicinity."}}

## Regulatory status

The characterization of soil from the SR 132 Corridor is subject to review and approval by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]].{{quote: "The SR 132 Corridor soil characterization is subject to review and approval by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC)."}} Caltrans, represented by Richard C. Stewart (Engineering Geologist, PG 7926, Central Region Environmental Division), requested an expedited review from DTSC for the [[borrow-area-sampling-plan]] and acceptance of the proposed sampling approach.{{quote: "Caltrans, through Richard C. Stewart (Engineering Geologist, PG 7926, Central Region Environmental Division), requested DTSC's expedited review of the borrow-area-sampling-plan and acceptance of the proposed approach."}} As of February 4, 2020, DTSC approval was pending.{{quote: "As of February 4, 2020, DTSC approval was pending."}} Analytical results were to be submitted to DTSC after laboratory analyses were completed.{{quote: "Analytical results were to be provided to DTSC following completion of laboratory analyses."}}
