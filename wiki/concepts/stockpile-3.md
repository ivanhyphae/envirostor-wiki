# Stockpile 3

Stockpile 3 was one of three barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles placed within Caltrans right-of-way south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in the early 1960s, generated from excavation of former fmc evaporation ponds along with native soil. Located east of SR-99, it was smaller and more directly in the path of near-term construction than [[stockpile-1]] or [[stockpile-2]], and it is the only one of the three stockpiles that was fully excavated and relocated rather than capped in place. Its removal happened in two distinct episodes 8 years apart: a partial 2012 excavation tied to an SR-99 off-ramp safety project, and a complete 2020 removal as part of Interim Phase 1 of the SR-132 West Project, after which the area was verified clean and released from further monitoring under the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP).

## Site description and history

Stockpile 3 had a curvilinear shape extending northwest to southeast, roughly 1,100 feet long and 120 feet wide, with an estimated volume of approximately 24,000 cubic yards. It was bounded by SR-99 to the south and west and by commercial/light industrial development to the north and east; the Modesto Irrigation District (MID) Lateral #4 canal concrete box culvert crossed beneath its southeastern end. Like Stockpiles 1 and 2, it consisted of layered, poorly graded sand and silty sand similar to the underlying native Modesto Formation alluvium, into which pond tailings and native soil excavated during 1960s SR-99 construction had been mixed.

Site characterization dating to the 2004 Shaw Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) and the 2006 Shaw supplemental investigation identified barium as the primary chemical of potential concern (COPC) in Stockpile 3, with lesser detections of lead, arsenic, and cadmium. The 2004 PSI reported barium at Stockpile 3 up to 44,900 mg/kg; a May 2004 reanalysis of a subset of samples reported a maximum of 72,000 mg/kg, well above the residential and commercial/industrial CHHSLs of 5,200 and 63,000 mg/kg. Cadmium exceeding the commercial/industrial CHHSL was detected in 2 of 10 Stockpile 3 borings in the 2004 PSI. The 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) calculated a non-cancer hazard index of 0.02 for the current offsite resident/trespasser exposed to Stockpile 3 surface soil, well below the threshold of 1, and did not identify a cancer risk because the Stockpile 3 COPCs were not treated as carcinogens. A March 2006 stormwater sample collected from the northwestern side of Stockpile 3 (sample SW03) reported barium at 2,000 µg/L, exceeding the MCL of 1,000 µg/L; Shaw concluded this detection was isolated and that runoff remained within Caltrans right-of-way.

## 2012 partial removal (SR-99 Kansas Avenue off-ramp)

The first removal action taken at any of the three stockpiles was a partial excavation of Stockpile 3's northern end, undertaken as part of Caltrans' rehabilitation of the SR-99 northbound off-ramp to Kansas Avenue. Shoulder widening on the east side of the off-ramp required a retaining wall against the existing embankment and laying back the slope. In April 2012, Geocon completed eight direct-push borings and eleven hand-auger borings in the excavation area; barium was detected in all samples at 34 to 1,600 mg/kg (below the CHHSLs), and one sample (DP1-14.5, 1,600 mg/kg) equaled ten times the STLC for barium, the [[stlc-ttlc]] threshold above which soluble (WET) testing may be required — though calculated 95% UCLs for barium and lead were low enough that WET testing was not performed and the material did not require hazardous waste management.

Based on this data and DTSC's review, the excavated material was designated for offsite disposal as non-hazardous soil. Caltrans requested and received DTSC concurrence by letter dated May 29, 2012, and DTSC conveyed a finding on August 30, 2012 that offsite management of the Stockpile 3 soil did not pose a threat to human health or the environment. Approximately 2,800 cubic yards were excavated over ten days between September 7 and 26, 2012, loaded directly into covered trucks, and hauled under non-hazardous waste manifests to the Forward Class II landfill in Manteca (contemporary Caltrans workplan documents also reference the Fink Road landfill in Crows Landing as the intended destination). Air monitoring against a Fence Line Total Dust Action Level of 4.0 mg/m³ found airborne barium and lead well below levels of concern. The work is documented in Geocon's Stockpile 3 Excavation Summary Report dated March 15, 2013.

## 2020 complete removal (SR-132 West Project)

The 2018 Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) selected containment/encapsulation of all three stockpiles within SR-132 highway embankment fill as the site remedy, but treated Stockpile 3 differently from Stockpiles 1 and 2: rather than being capped in place, it was to be entirely relocated, with material placed in the stockpile fill consolidation zone at the eastern SR-99 bridge abutment and, secondarily, into Stockpile 2's consolidation zone. The RAP had also priced an optional alternative — full offsite removal and disposal of Stockpile 3 with import of clean replacement fill — at the request of the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board, but the adopted remedy kept the material on site as fill.

Consistent with that plan, Stockpile 3 was completely excavated between February and March 2020 and transported for placement as embankment fill within the designated Stockpile 1 containment zone. Excavation records give differing truckload volume estimates: the RACR states approximately 3,075 truckloads totaling 27,675 cubic yards (at 9 cy/truck), while the contemporaneous March 13, 2020 verification technical memorandum gives the same 3,075 truckloads as approximately 38,440 cubic yards (a preliminary estimate at 12.5 cy/truck). Transport trucks were decontaminated and tarped before leaving the exclusion zone, and a dedicated track-out pad and decontamination station were established at the south end of the stockpile.

## Verification sampling and closure of the removal area

The RDIP set BCS removal verification thresholds of 1,000 mg/kg barium (groundwater protection) and 80 mg/kg lead (DTSC residential screening level, against a residential ESL of 3,000 mg/kg for barium). Stockpile 3 was excavated to elevations between approximately 82.5 and 84 feet above MSL, exposing native soil. Twenty-four verification samples (S3 1-0 through S3 24-0) were collected between February 28 and March 12, 2020, plus two berm samples (S3 SE Berm, S3 SW Berm) from the top of the berm at the south end adjacent to the MID canal. Sample S3 16-0 was collected from storm drain trench backfill rather than native soil.

Initial results for locations S3 9-0, S3 10-0, and S3 11-0 showed barium between 2,200 and 7,000 mg/kg, exceeding the 1,000 mg/kg threshold. These three areas were deepened approximately one foot and resampled (as S3 9A-0, S3 10A-0, S3 11A-0).

| Sampling stage | Barium range (mg/kg) | Lead range (mg/kg) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial 24 base samples (incl. 3 elevated locations) | up to 7,000 | — | 3 locations exceeded 1,000 mg/kg barium threshold |
| Re-sampled after 1-ft deepening (9A, 10A, 11A) + remaining locations, final dataset | 46–970 | 1.6–40 | All below thresholds (1,000 barium / 80 lead) |

All final verification samples met both thresholds. Geocon's March 13, 2020 Technical Memorandum concluded the Stockpile 3 removal area was suitable for placement of clean embankment fill and other highway improvements without further RDIP monitoring. DTSC's Dean Wright accepted this conclusion by email on March 18, 2020, confirming no further BCS RDIP monitoring requirements applied to the former Stockpile 3 footprint. This closure is narrower than final site closure: it released the Stockpile 3 excavation area itself, not the Stockpile 1 containment zone that received the relocated material, which remained subject to ongoing RDIP construction monitoring, air monitoring, and the containment/capping requirements documented in the RACR.

Air monitoring during the February–June 2020 grading activities, which included the Stockpile 3 relocation, measured total dust against a 4.0 mg/m³ Fence Line Action Level, barium against 25 µg/m³, and lead against the CARB 30-day ambient standard of 1.5 µg/m³; results across all monitored BCS grading activities (not Stockpile 3 alone) were below these action levels.
