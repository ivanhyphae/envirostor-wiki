The [[barium-containing-soil-consolidation-and-capping]] is a remedial action undertaken as part of the [[draft-removal-action-completion-report-caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles-interim-phase-1-state-route-132-west-project]] at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (Site Code: 900259) in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. This action involved the consolidation and capping of soils containing barium.

## Site Record

Prior to remediation, the site contained three Barium Containing Soil (BCS) Stockpiles within the Caltrans State Route 132 right-of-way.
*   **Stockpile 1:** Located approximately 150 feet west of Emerald Avenue, it was about 600 feet long (east-west), 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 34,000 cubic yards (yd³).
*   **Stockpile 2:** Situated between Emerald Avenue and State Route 99, it measured approximately 1,650 feet long (east-west) and 160 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 102,000 yd³.
*   **Stockpile 3:** Formerly on the east side of State Route 99, it was approximately 1,100 feet long and 120 feet wide, with an estimated volume of 24,000 yd³.

Remedial activities, as described in the Draft Removal Action Completion Report – Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Interim Phase 1 State Route 132 West Project (Draft Phase 1 RACR), included excavating Stockpile 3, the western and eastern ends of Stockpile 2, and portions of the southern slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2 to support the construction of the new State Route 132 Expressway.

Approximately 27,675 yd³ of soil from BCS Stockpile 3 were excavated and transported as embankment fill to the BCS Stockpile 1 containment zone. Twenty-nine confirmation soil samples were collected from the bottom of the BCS Stockpile 3 excavation area and analyzed for barium and lead. Three samples exceeded the cleanup goal for barium of 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). These areas were excavated an additional foot and resampled. The final confirmation samples met the cleanup goal.

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), reviewed the Draft Phase 1 RACR submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc. (Geocon) on behalf of Caltrans. DTSC accepted the Draft Phase 1 RACR on February 24, 2022 {{quote: "ACCEPTANCE OF THE DRAFT INTERIM REMOVAL ACTION COMPLETION REPORT – CALTRANS MODESTO SOIL STOCKPILES, PHASE 1 INTERIM STATE ROUTE 132 WEST PROJECT, MODESTO, STANISLAUS COUNTY, CALIFORNIA (SITE CODE: 900259)"}}.

The Removal Action Completion Report (RACR), dated December 1, 2022, was submitted by Geocon and described the consolidation and capping activities. DTSC accepted this report as final on January 18, 2023 {{quote: "ACCEPTANCE OF THE INTERIM REMOVAL ACTION COMPLETION REPORT – CALTRANS MODESTO SOIL STOCKPILES, PHASE 1 INTERIM STATE ROUTE 132 WEST PROJECT, MODESTO, STANISLAUS COUNTY, CALIFORNIA (SITE CODE: 900259-00)"}}. This acceptance confirmed that soil removal, consolidation, and capping were completed according to the approved Remedial Action Plan (RAP), Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP), and two variance requests approved on April 17, 2020, and September 21, 2020.

## Open Items

Following the acceptance of the RACR, DTSC will prepare a draft Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement for Caltrans review and will complete a Land Use Covenant (LUC) for recording with the Stanislaus County Assessor's office {{quote: "With this acceptance DTSC will prepare a draft O&M Agreement and submit the document for Caltrans review within 30 days of this letter. A Land Use Covenant (LUC) for the remediated area will also be completed and sent for review, signature and recording with the Stanislaus County Assessor's office."}}.

Final certification of the RAP will not occur until after the Ultimate Phase 2 State Route 132 build-out effort is completed, which is anticipated in 8 to 10 years. This future phase will involve a supplemental RDIP, field oversight, an Ultimate Phase 2 RACR, and potential revisions to the O&M Plan and Agreement {{quote: "final certification of the RAP for this project will not occur until after the Ultimate Phase 2 State Route 132 build-out effort is completed (Ultimate Phase 2). The Ultimate Phase 2 work is anticipated to begin in 8 to 10 years and will include adding additional lanes and completing the final build out of State Route 132. This effort will include a supplemental RDIP for DTSCs review and approval as well as field oversight, an Ultimate Phase 2 RACR, and revisions to the O&M Plan and O&M Agreement if necessary."}}.
