## Overview
Clean fill capping is a remedial strategy implemented at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] to cover [[barium-containing-soil-aerially-deposited-lead]] (BCS/ADL) stockpiles. This method utilizes suitable excavated soil from within the [[sr-132-west-expressway-phase-1]] right-of-way to create a protective cap, preventing direct contact with and migration of the underlying BCS/ADL.

## Site record
On February 4, 2020, [[geocon-consultants]] submitted a proposed clean fill source sampling plan to [[caltrans]]. The plan identified the upper five feet of soil along the Highway 132 alignment, east of the [[carpenter-road-area]], as the intended source for clean fill material. This area has been State right-of-way since the 1960s and was previously agricultural land. Geocon's review of prior investigations, including the Blackburn 2014 ISA and Rincon 2018 Phase II ESA, indicated no documented high-risk contamination releases in the vicinity of the proposed source area. Testing of residential backyards by Rincon adjacent to and west of Carpenter Road showed pesticides and metals at concentrations below applicable regulatory screening levels.

The sampling plan proposed twelve 5-foot-deep borings. Six depth-discrete 4-point composite samples were to be analyzed:
*   **0–2 feet:** organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) and heavy metals (3 composite samples)
*   **3–5 feet:** heavy metals (3 composite samples)

Geocon tentatively scheduled drilling for February 11, 2020. Geotechnical logs of test borings completed at Carpenter Road in 2017 were appended to document that material at the location is comprised of native soil.

On April 17, 2020, the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] (Water Board) officially accepted a Variance Request (VR) to the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) dated April 9, 2020. This VR permitted the use of soil excavated from 1 to 5 feet below existing grade along the southern boundary of Stockpile 1 as clean cap soil. This material had barium concentrations with a calculated 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) of 113.1 mg/kg, which was within the range of site-specific naturally occurring background. DTSC indicated that if the UCL of similar MSE wall footing excavation soil along the southern boundary of Stockpile 2 was also under naturally occurring background concentrations, it could also be used as clean cap soil. The official approval or rejection of soil from the southern boundary of Stockpile 2 was pending data submission and review.

On September 21, 2020, DTSC and the Water Board approved a Second Variance to the RDIP (Request dated September 2, 2020). This variance allowed for the use of Class 3 subbase material as an acceptable clean cap alternative over the BCS/ADL stockpiles. A visual inspection on September 15, 2020, revealed the Class 3 subbase was primarily fine sand. Consequently, DTSC and the Water Board agreed that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches, rather than the initially proposed 4 inches, was warranted due to its fine-grained nature and the absence of pavement or hardscaping before the upcoming rainy season. DTSC and the Water Board also requested a single composite sample from the Class 3 material stockpile adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing for [[barium]] and [[lead]] analysis (EPA Test Method 6010B) to ensure it met RDIP standards. This e-mail served as DTSC's official acceptance of the request for the use of Class 3 subbase.

## Regulatory status
The sampling plan for the Highway 132 alignment soil east of Carpenter Road was submitted to DTSC for approval on February 4, 2020. As of the available records, DTSC approval or concurrence on this specific sampling plan is not documented.

DTSC and the Water Board officially accepted the use of soil from the southern boundary of Stockpile 1 (1-5 feet depth) as clean cap soil on April 17, 2020, provided barium concentrations were within naturally occurring background levels.

DTSC and the Water Board officially accepted the use of Class 3 subbase as a clean cap alternative on September 21, 2020, with the condition of a minimum 6-inch thickness and potential for increased monitoring.

## Open items
