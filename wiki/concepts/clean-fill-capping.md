## Clean Fill Capping

### Overview

Clean fill capping is a remedial action employed at the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] to cover barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially-deposited lead (ADL) consolidation zones within [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]]. This capping is intended to provide a protective layer during the SR 132 West Project.

### Site Record

The Remedial Design Implementation Plan - Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (January 30, 2019) specified a minimum 12-inch-thick clean cover fill material over BCS and ADL consolidation zones. Alternatively, a minimum 4-inch-thick aggregate base (AB) material could be placed on the upper stockpile surface, pending subsequent placement of structural pavement and hardscape.

A Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP Variance #2), dated September 2, 2020, addressed the proposed use of clean cover fill material by the highway contractor. This variance focused on the use of Class 3 aggregate subbase materials as an interim clean cover. The structural pavement section for the Interim Phase I of the SR 132 West Project includes:
*   0.85 feet (10.2 inches) concrete pavement
*   0.25 feet (3.0 inches) hot mix asphalt
*   0.70 feet (8.4 inches) Class 3 aggregate subbase

The RDIP Variance #2 concluded that the planned placement of 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase materials on the upper surface of [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]] would provide adequate interim clean cover, equivalent to aggregate base for purposes of cover material, until the full pavement section or hardscape is placed. Caltrans' gradation summaries for aggregate subbase materials indicate they are primarily comprised of sand and gravel with lesser fines content, similar to aggregate base materials.

### Regulatory Status

The use of 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase materials as clean cover fill on the upper surface of [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]] was deemed to meet the intent of the Remedial Design Implementation Plan - Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), as documented in RDIP Variance #2 (September 2, 2020).
