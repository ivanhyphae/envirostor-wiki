Clean fill capping is a remedial action employed at the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] to cover barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially-deposited lead (ADL) consolidation zones within [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]]. This capping provides a protective layer, integrating the contaminated soils into the highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Project.

## Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) Requirements

The Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, dated January 30, 2019, specified a minimum 12-inch-thick clean cover fill material over BCS and ADL consolidation zones. An alternative allowed for a minimum 4-inch-thick aggregate base (AB) material to be placed on the upper stockpile surface, provided that structural pavement and hardscape would be subsequently placed.

## Second Variance to RDIP (RDIP Variance #2)

A Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP Variance #2), dated September 2, 2020, addressed the highway contractor's proposed use of clean cover fill material. This variance focused on the use of Class 3 aggregate subbase materials as an interim clean cover. The structural pavement section for the Interim Phase I of the SR 132 West Project includes:
*   0.85 feet (10.2 inches) concrete pavement
*   0.25 feet (3.0 inches) hot mix asphalt
*   0.70 feet (8.4 inches) Class 3 aggregate subbase

RDIP Variance #2 concluded that the planned placement of 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase materials on the upper surface of [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]] would provide adequate interim clean cover, equivalent to aggregate base for purposes of cover material, until the full pavement section or hardscape is placed. Caltrans' gradation summaries for aggregate subbase materials indicate they are primarily comprised of sand and gravel with lesser fines content, similar to aggregate base materials.

## Regulatory Approval of Clean Fill Sources

Several sources of clean fill material were evaluated and approved for use:

*   **Carpenter Road Shoofly:** Characterization testing performed by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on April 12, 2021, indicated that soil material generated from the excavation of the Carpenter Road Shoofly met the BCS clean cap criteria, with barium concentrations ranging from 28 to 96 mg/kg and lead concentrations from 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg. These values were below the site-specific background threshold for barium (120 mg/kg) and the maximum site-specific background concentration for lead (3.8 mg/kg). This source was approved by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).
*   **Basin 5 Borrow Area:** A revised technical memorandum dated July 2, 2020, addressed concerns regarding chromium concentrations. DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) agreed that the composite soil sample results were consistent with naturally occurring background concentrations and suitable as clean cover material for capping Stockpiles 1 and 2. This memo was accepted by DTSC via email on July 9, 2020.
*   **SR 132 ROW Construction Excavation Area (adjacent and east of Carpenter Road):** Evaluated by Geocon in a technical memorandum dated February 27, 2020, this area was determined to generate soil material suitable for use as clean cover fill. DTSC provided approval of this source.
*   **Bent 2 Excavation Stockpiles:** Characterization testing performed by Geocon on October 30, 2020, indicated barium concentrations up to 230 mg/kg, exceeding the site-specific background value of 120 mg/kg. DTSC did not approve this source for use as clean cap material, though it was considered for partial use under specific conditions.
*   **MSE Wall Footing Excavations:**
    *   **Stockpile 1:** Geocon's technical memorandum dated April 6, 2020, found barium concentrations ranging from 42 to 420 mg/kg, exceeding the site-specific background of 120 mg/kg but below the RDIP removal threshold of 1,000 mg/kg. Lead concentrations were within background ranges. This material was deemed suitable for placement within the containment zones or as capping material pending regulatory approval.
    *   **Stockpile 2:** Geocon's technical memorandum dated June 18, 2020, found barium concentrations ranging from 53 to 930 mg/kg. Fourteen samples exceeded the 120 mg/kg background, with thirteen collected within the upper three feet. The upper three feet of excavated material required placement within the containment zones, while material excavated deeper than three feet was suitable for use as embankment fill or capping material pending approval.

## Implementation and Monitoring

The Interim Removal Action Completion Report (RACR), dated December 1, 2022, documents the completed consolidation and clean fill capping of BCS as highway embankment fill during Phase I construction. Time-lapse photography was used to document grading and clean capping operations. Inspections of the clean capped zones are conducted annually by Caltrans, with supplemental inspections after significant events, to ensure the integrity of the cap. Findings are documented in an annual report submitted to DTSC.

## Operations and Maintenance (O&M)

A Final Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement was executed between DTSC and Caltrans on June 20, 2023. This agreement outlines Caltrans' responsibility for ongoing O&M activities to ensure the integrity of the clean capped Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones. Inspections include observations of the surface cover, drainage structures, and perimeter security fencing. Any damage to the clean cap or fencing is to be documented and repaired promptly.
