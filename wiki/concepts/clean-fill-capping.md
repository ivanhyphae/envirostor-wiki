## Overview
Clean fill capping is a remedial strategy employed at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] to cover [[barium-containing-soil-aerially-deposited-lead]] (BCS/ADL) stockpiles. This involves the use of suitable excavated soil from within the [[sr-132-west-expressway-phase-1]] right-of-way as a protective cap. The objective is to prevent direct contact with and migration of the underlying BCS/ADL.

## Site record
On February 4, 2020, Geocon Consultants submitted a proposed clean fill source sampling plan to [[caltrans]] for material intended to be used as clean fill cover for BCS stockpiles. The proposed source area was the upper five feet of soil along the Highway 132 alignment, east of the [[carpenter-road-area]]. This area has been State right-of-way since the 1960s, with prior land use being agricultural. Geocon reviewed prior investigations, including the Blackburn 2014 ISA and Rincon 2018 Phase II ESA, and found no high-risk documented contamination releases in the immediate vicinity of the proposed source area. Testing of residential backyards by Rincon adjacent to and west of Carpenter Road indicated pesticides and metals at concentrations less than applicable regulatory screening levels.

The sampling plan proposed twelve 5-foot-deep borings. Six depth-discrete 4-point composite samples were to be analyzed:
*   **0–2 feet:** organochlorine pesticides (OCPs) and heavy metals (3 composite samples)
*   **3–5 feet:** heavy metals (3 composite samples)

Geocon tentatively scheduled drilling for February 11, 2020. Geotechnical logs of test borings completed at Carpenter Road in 2017 were appended to document that material at the location is comprised of native soil. [[caltrans]] transmitted this submittal to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Boards on February 4, 2020, requesting a prompt review and acceptance.

On April 17, 2020, DTSC and the Water Board officially accepted a Variance Request (VR) to the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) dated April 9, 2020. This VR pertained to the use of soil excavated from 1 to 5 feet below existing grade along the southern boundary of Stockpile 1 as clean cap soil. This material had barium concentrations with a calculated 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) of 113.1 mg/kg, which was within the range of site-specific naturally occurring background. DTSC indicated that if the UCL of similar MSE wall footing excavation soil along the southern boundary of Stockpile 2 was also under naturally occurring background concentrations, it could also be used as clean cap soil. {{quote: "This e-mail serves as DTSCs official acceptance of the VR for the soil along the southern boundary of Stockpile 1."}} The official approval or rejection of soil from the southern boundary of Stockpile 2 was pending data submission and review. {{quote: "The official approval or rejection of the soil along the southern boundary of Stockpile 2 will be provided once that data has been submitted and reviewed."}}

On September 21, 2020, DTSC and the Water Board approved a Second Variance to the RDIP (Request dated September 2, 2020). This variance allowed for the use of Class 3 subbase material as an acceptable clean cap alternative over the BCS/ADL stockpiles. {{quote: "Both DTSC and the WB agree that Class 3 subbase can be used as an acceptable clean cap alternative"}} However, a visual inspection on September 15, 2020, revealed the Class 3 subbase was primarily fine sand. Consequently, DTSC and the Water Board agreed that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches, rather than the initially proposed 4 inches, was warranted due to its fine-grained nature and the absence of pavement or hardscaping before the upcoming rainy season. {{quote: "Given its abundant fine grained nature, both agencies agree that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches rather than 4 inches is warranted and that increased monitoring may be necessary."}} DTSC and the Water Board also requested a single composite sample from the Class 3 material stockpile adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing for barium and lead analysis (EPA Test Method 6010B) to ensure it met RDIP standards. {{quote: "DTSC and the WB are requesting a single, composite sample be obtained for barium and lead analysis following Environmental Protection Agency Test Method 6010B from the stockpile of Class 3 material adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing."}} This e-mail served as DTSC's official acceptance of the request for the use of Class 3 subbase. {{quote: "This e-mail serves as DTSCs official acceptance of the Request for use of Class 3 subbase."}}

## Regulatory status
The sampling plan for the Highway 132 alignment soil east of Carpenter Road was submitted to DTSC for approval on February 4, 2020. {{quote: "Caltrans transmitted the submittal to DTSC (Dean Wright, Perry Myers) and the Water Boards (Steven Meeks) the same day, requesting quick review and acceptance."}} As of the available records, DTSC approval or concurrence on this specific sampling plan is not documented.

DTSC and the Water Board officially accepted the use of soil from the southern boundary of Stockpile 1 (1-5 feet depth) as clean cap soil on April 17, 2020, provided barium concentrations were within naturally occurring background levels. {{quote: "This e-mail serves as DTSCs official acceptance of the VR for the soil along the southern boundary of Stockpile 1."}}

DTSC and the Water Board officially accepted the use of Class 3 subbase as a clean cap alternative on September 21, 2020, with the condition of a minimum 6-inch thickness and potential for increased monitoring. {{quote: "This e-mail serves as DTSCs official acceptance of the Request for use of Class 3 subbase."}}
