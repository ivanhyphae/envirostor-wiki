Clean fill capping is the remedy element that converted the excavated and consolidated barium-containing soil (BCS) at [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]] into a closed containment feature: a minimum 12-inch clean fill layer (or a 4-inch aggregate base/subbase alternative pending final pavement) placed over the consolidated BCS to break exposure pathways, with each candidate borrow source individually sampled and DTSC-approved before use. The remedy as installed — "consolidation and clean fill capping of barium-containing soil (BCS) as highway embankment fill" — is now the subject of the Final Operation and Maintenance Agreement (Docket No. HSA-FY22/23-067, executed June 20, 2023), which requires Caltrans to maintain the cap and precedes recording of a Land Use Covenant restricting use of the capped area. What turns on this concept is straightforward: only soil meeting the site-specific background threshold could be used as full-depth clean cap, and every source area proposed by the highway contractor had to clear that bar before placement — some did, one did not without a use restriction, and one was resolved by substituting an engineered material instead of soil.

## Origin of the requirement

The Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP, January 30, 2019) specified that the completed Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS/aerially-deposited lead (ADL) consolidation zones, including east- and west-facing temporary slopes, be capped with a minimum 12-inch-thick layer of approved imported fill and/or a minimum 4-inch-thick aggregate base (AB) layer pending later placement of structural pavement, hardscape, or additional clean embankment fill (RDIP Section 4.7, cited in the April 9, 2020 RDIP Variance). The RDIP anticipated that clean imported fill would be drawn from vacant and agricultural land within planned excavation areas along the SR-132 right-of-way between the Carpenter Road and Rosemore Avenue overcrossings, with each specific source area sampled by Geocon per DTSC's *Information Advisory Clean Imported Fill Material* and pre-approved before placement.

## Source areas evaluated

**Carpenter Road area (east of Carpenter Road, within SR 132 ROW).** Identified by the highway contractor, Bay Cities Paving and Grading, as the initial clean fill source. Geocon evaluated the planned underpass excavation area and determined it would generate soil suitable as clean cover fill (Geocon February 27, 2020 Technical Memorandum); DTSC approved this source area. See [[carpenter-road-area]].

**MSE wall footing excavations, Stockpiles 1 and 2 southern boundary (approximately 5 feet deep).** Geocon evaluated this material following excavation of the southern-slope BCS at Stockpile 1 (Geocon April 6, 2020 Technical Memorandum). The upper foot of the planned wall footing excavation was found to contain elevated barium concentrations not suitable for use as clean cap; the lower portion of the excavation was approved by DTSC for use as clean cap and was used to build a fill berm on Stockpile 2, onto which part of the Bent 2 material was later placed.

**Bent 2 excavation stockpiles (overcrossing structure west of SR 99), October 30, 2020 Tech Memo.** Approximately 4,000 cubic yards of native soil excavated from Bent 2 and end-dumped in two rows along roughly 800 feet of the southern portion of the top of Stockpile 2. Ten discrete samples (STK1–STK10) were analyzed for barium and lead (EPA Method 6010B). Barium ranged from 31 to 230 mg/kg, with 4 of 10 samples at or above the site-specific background threshold of 120 mg/kg; lead ranged from 2.6 to 13 mg/kg in the 6 of 10 samples with detections, within the range of the site background value of 3.8 mg/kg. Because barium exceeded background in nearly half the samples, the Bent 2 material did not meet the clean cap criteria in the RDIP Variance. Geocon proposed, pending DTSC review, that it nonetheless be used as *partial* clean cap — 6 inches or less — on the upper surface of Stockpile 2 only, on the reasoning that the full structural pavement section (base material and asphalt/concrete) would be placed over that surface as an additional barrier. Geocon specified that fill from previously approved clean sources, not Bent 2 material, would be required for the northerly slope of Stockpile 2, which lacks that pavement overburden.

**Carpenter Road Shoofly (April 12, 2021 Tech Memo).** Approximately 5,000 cubic yards of native soil to be excavated from the Carpenter Road Shoofly once the overcrossing structure was completed, proposed by the contractor as clean cap for portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2. Twenty-four discrete samples were collected along six vertical transects (CSF1–CSF6) at 2, 5, 10, and 15 feet from the top of slope, then composited by the laboratory into eight 3-part composites and analyzed for barium and lead. Barium ranged from 28 to 96 mg/kg — below the 120 mg/kg background threshold in every sample — and lead ranged from 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg, at or below the 3.8 mg/kg background value, with one non-detect. The material met the BCS clean cap criteria outright, with no use restriction. DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board reviewed the Sampling Plan (approved by e-mail April 7, 2021, following a revised plan received April 5, 2021) and then the Tech Memo itself (accepted by DTSC e-mail April 22, 2021), which found the composite results consistent with naturally occurring background and confirmed the material met the clean cap criteria.

## Aggregate base/subbase as an alternative cap

A second RDIP Variance (RDIP Variance #2, September 2, 2020) addressed the RDIP's alternative cap option of a minimum 4-inch aggregate base (AB) layer on the upper stockpile surface, intended to preserve fill capacity ahead of final structural pavement. The variance substituted 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase for AB, based on Caltrans gradation summaries showing Class 3 aggregate subbase is primarily sand and gravel with lesser fines, similar to aggregate base. Geocon concluded this thickness of Class 3 aggregate subbase would provide adequate interim clean cover — equivalent to aggregate base for cover purposes — on the upper surface of Stockpiles 1 and 2 until the full pavement section (0.85 ft concrete pavement, 0.25 ft hot mix asphalt, 0.70 ft Class 3 aggregate subbase) or hardscape was placed over the remainder.

## Disposition

The Removal Action Completion Report (2022) was to document the completed BCS stockpile remedial actions, including the Bent 2 and Carpenter Road Shoofly clean fill results described above; both tech memos state their information was intended for inclusion in that report. Consolidation, containment, and clean capping of Stockpiles 1 through 3 occurred between 2020 and 2022 during Interim Phase 1 construction, with Stockpile 3 BCS material removed entirely from the east side of SR 99 and relocated into the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. The installed cap over consolidated BCS at Stockpiles 1 and 2 is now the remedy Caltrans must operate and maintain under the June 20, 2023 O&M Agreement, pending recording of a Land Use Covenant restricting use of the capped area.
