## Overview

Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) exceedance of the California secondary maximum contaminant level (MCL) is a documented groundwater quality issue at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County). The primary source record for this exceedance is the July 2012 Groundwater Monitoring Report (document **7453_S9525-06-44**). TDS is a secondary (aesthetic) standard; the applicable California secondary MCL is **500 mg/L**. The exceedance was identified during routine compliance monitoring at wells surrounding the stockpiled soil materials.

## Site record

The July 2012 GW Report (source file 7453_S9525-06-44_Modesto_Stockpiles_July_2012_GW_Report) presents TDS data from the site’s monitoring well network (MW‑1 through MW‑6). Specific concentrations and well locations are recorded in the report. The document shows that TDS levels in several wells exceeded the 500 mg/L secondary MCL, with the highest readings observed in wells adjacent to the stockpile staging areas. Sampling dates and depth‑specific data are included in the report tables. The exceedance was flagged for inclusion in the site’s ongoing groundwater monitoring program and for evaluation of potential remedial triggers.

## Regulatory status

The California secondary MCL for TDS (500 mg/L) is established by the State Water Resources Control Board and enforced by the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB). The July 2012 GW Report documents non‑compliance with this aesthetic standard at the time of sampling. The project’s compliance history with respect to TDS is part of the case record; no subsequent verification data approving closure of this exceedance are identified in this sole source document.

## Open items

- The July 2012 GW Report does not itself resolve the TDS exceedance; subsequent monitoring reports (not provided in this source) would be needed to determine whether TDS levels have declined or remain above the secondary MCL.
- No RWQCB approval confirmation or corrective action plan specifically addressing TDS is referenced in the 2012 report.
