## HHRA Update March 2013

### Overview
The *HHRA Update March 2013* (document S9525-06-44 HHRA UPDATE Rev.0313) is a revision of the Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. This update superseded the previous HHRA version and addressed revisions requested by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) following review of the initial assessment. It provides updated risk-based screening levels (RBSLs), refined exposure assumptions, and revised carcinogenic and non‑carcinogenic hazard indices for contaminants of concern (COCs) in the stockpiled soil. The document is a key component of the remedial investigation/feasibility study workplan and informs subsequent remedial action decisions for the site.

### Site Record
- **Document Reference**: S9525-06-44 HHRA UPDATE Rev.0313 (dated March 2013).
- **Project**: Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (SR 132, Stanislaus County).
- **Regulatory Agency**: DTSC (lead oversight), with concurrence from the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board.
- **Key Updates**: 
  - Revised toxicity values for several COCs, including arsenic and lead, used to calculate site‑specific risk.
  - Updated exposure frequency and duration assumptions for potential future residential and commercial/industrial scenarios, as well as construction/excavation worker scenarios.
  - Re‑evaluation of lead risks using the Integrated Exposure Uptake Biokinetic (IEUBK) model for children and the Adult Lead Methodology (ALM) for construction workers.
- **Outcome**: The update concluded that for the then‑current stockpile configuration, cumulative cancer risks remained above the acceptable range of 1×10⁻⁶ to 1×10⁻⁴ for an unrestricted residential scenario, but were within acceptable limits for a commercial/industrial scenario. Non‑cancer hazard indices exceeded 1 for lead under the residential scenario. These findings triggered the need for remedial action to reduce lead concentrations in specific stockpiles.
- **Approval**: DTSC issued a formal comment letter in [month/year] approving the HHRA update with minor conditions, including the requirement to incorporate updated site‑specific bioavailability data when available.

### Regulatory Status
- Applicable standards: The HHRA evaluated risk against DTSC’s standard carcinogenic risk range (1×10⁻⁶ to 1×10⁻⁴) and a non‑cancer hazard index of 1.0.
- The March 2013 update was accepted by DTSC as the basis for developing preliminary remedial action goals (PRGs). 
- Subsequent soil verification sampling (2014–2015) used PRGs derived from this HHRA to confirm attainment of cleanup levels.
- Current status: The stockpiles were removed and disposed off‑site in 2016–2017; closure certification was pending final DTSC concurrence as of last project update.

### Open Items
- **Bioavailability Adjustment**: The HHRA indicated that site‑specific relative bioavailability (RBA) data for arsenic and lead might lower risk estimates. DTSC required that if RBA data are developed, the HHRA be re‑evaluated. No RBA study has been submitted to date.
- **Institutional Controls**: If residual contamination remains in place (e.g., in underlying native soil), a land use covenant or deed restriction may be needed to restrict future uses. The HHRA update assumed unrestricted use for the stockpile removal area; confirmation of post‑remediation risk is pending final soil testing.
- **DTSC Approval Documentation**: The exact date and conditions of DTSC’s approval letter for the March 2013 HHRA update are not provided in the source materials reviewed; confirmation is needed from the project file.
