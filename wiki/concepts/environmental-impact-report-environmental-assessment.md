## Overview

The Environmental Assessment (EA) for the SR 132 Remedial Action Plan (RAP) evaluates the potential environmental consequences of remediating historical soil stockpiles at the Caltrans Modesto maintenance yard in Stanislaus County, California. Prepared as part of the project’s National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) compliance, the EA supports the Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) determination issued concurrently with approval of the RAP. The EA is incorporated by reference in the Response to Comments letter for the SR 132 RAP and is formally included as Appendix J to that letter. The document assesses direct, indirect, and cumulative effects of excavating, stockpiling, and off‑hauling contaminated soil, and addresses groundwater monitoring obligations.

## Site record

The EA documents the following site‑specific conditions and remedial actions reported in the Response to Comments letter and Appendix J:

- **Contaminants of concern**: Barium (maximum detected concentration 760 mg/kg in surface soil), arsenic (140 mg/kg), and lead (220 mg/kg) were the primary metals exceeding California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) for industrial/commercial land use.
- **Groundwater screening**: Monitoring well MW‑5 showed barium concentrations up to 1,200 µg/L, exceeding the federal MCL of 1,000 µg/L. Groundwater was not the subject of active remediation but is subject to quarterly monitoring per DTSC’s Conditional Approval.
- **Remedial phases**: 
  - Phase 1 (2019–2020): Excavation of the northern stockpile (approximately 2,300 cubic yards) north of SR 132.
  - Phase 2 (2020–2021): Excavation of the southern stockpile (approximately 3,100 cubic yards) south of SR 132.
- **Verification sampling**: Post‑excavation soil samples from sidewalls and floors of both stockpile footprints showed barium concentrations below the project’s site‑specific action level of 500 mg/kg (based on a background study). No exceedance was reported.
- **Off‑haul destination**: All excavated soil was transported to a Class I landfill in Kettleman Hills (Kings County) under a DTSC‑approved waste profile.
- **Document references**: The EA includes:
  - Air quality dispersion modeling (AERMOD) results for fugitive dust during excavation.
  - Biological resource surveys for the California tiger salamander and vernal pool fairy shrimp.
  - Cultural resources records search (no significant adverse effects identified).

## Regulatory status

- **Lead agency**: California Department of Transportation (Caltrans), with oversight by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) under the California Hazardous Substance Account.
- **NEPA determination**: Finding of No Significant Impact issued by Caltrans on March 15, 2021, based on the EA’s conclusion that all adverse effects are less‑than‑significant with implementation of mitigation measures (e.g., dust suppression, erosion control, traffic management).
- **CEQA equivalency**: The EA served as the functional equivalent of a Negative Declaration under CEQA because the project site was listed in the Caltrans NEPA‑CEQA MOU registry.
- **Applicable standards**:
  - Soils: Site‑specific action levels approved by DTSC for barium (500 mg/kg), arsenic (80 mg/kg), and lead (150 mg/kg). These values were derived from background soil data collected on‑site.
  - Groundwater: Federal MCLs (barium 1,000 µg/L, arsenic 10 µg/L, lead 15 µg/L) apply for drinking water; no groundwater use is planned at the site.
- **Compliance status**: All Phase 1 and Phase 2 excavations have been completed and verified. Groundwater monitoring at MW‑5 and two downgradient wells (MW‑6 and MW‑7) continues under DTSC‑approved quarterly monitoring plan. As of the most recent report (June 2022), no exceedances were observed in the downgradient wells.

## Open items

- **Long‑term groundwater monitoring**: DTSC’s Conditional Approval (dated April 2021) requires quarterly monitoring for at least four consecutive quarters after Phase 2 completion. The final monitoring report is pending. If the four‑quarter dataset shows stable or declining barium concentrations, a request to terminate monitoring will be submitted to DTSC.
- **Vapor intrusion assessment**: The EA identified a data gap regarding potential vapor intrusion into an on‑site storage building. A soil‑gas survey is required before occupancy of that building; the survey has not yet been completed.
- **Community air monitoring**: A post‑remediation air monitoring report was required to verify that fugitive dust control measures were effective. The commenter (Stanislaus County Air District) requested submittal of the monitoring data from the Phase 2 excavation. The report has been drafted but not yet formally accepted by the Air District.
