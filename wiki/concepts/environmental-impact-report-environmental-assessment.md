The [[california-environmental-quality-act]] (CEQA) process for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] involved the preparation of a joint Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and a Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP). Caltrans served as the lead agency under both CEQA and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA). The purpose of the EIR/EA was to evaluate the potential environmental impacts of the proposed SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, which included the remediation of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles.

## Overview

The EIR/EA process was a critical component of the project, ensuring that potential environmental consequences were identified, analyzed, and disclosed to the public. This process informed decision-making regarding the proposed freeway/expressway construction and the associated remediation of the soil stockpiles. The EIR/EA was prepared in cooperation with the Stanislaus Council of Governments (StanCOG), the City of Modesto, and Stanislaus County, with the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board acting as responsible agencies under CEQA.

## Site Record

The Draft EIR/EA was circulated for public review for 59 days between January 18, 2017, and March 17, 2017. A public hearing was held on February 22, 2017, at Mark Twain Junior High School in Modesto. A total of 305 comments were received during the public circulation period, including comments on both the Draft EIR/EA and the Draft Final RAP. The Final EIR/EA, which included responses to these comments, was certified by Caltrans on March 2, 2018. The DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, utilized this EIR/EA for its CEQA analysis of the RAP. A Notice of Determination (NOD) was filed by DTSC on March 14, 2018, indicating that the project would not have a significant effect on the environment and that mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval.

## Regulatory Status

The EIR/EA process is a regulatory requirement under CEQA. The certification of the EIR/EA and the subsequent filing of the NOD signify the completion of the environmental review process for the project. Caltrans, as the lead agency, prepared the EIR/EA, and DTSC, as a responsible agency, reviewed and commented on the hazardous substances information within the document.
