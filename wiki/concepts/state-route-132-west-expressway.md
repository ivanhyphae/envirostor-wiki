The State Route 132 (SR 132) West Expressway project in Stanislaus County, California, involves the management and remediation of soil stockpiles identified as containing barium and lead. These stockpiles are located within [[basin-5]] and have been encapsulated with clean fill.

### Soil Stockpile Management and Groundwater Evaluation

On June 11, 2020, Caltrans submitted a [[clean-fill-sampling-plan]] to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for review. This plan detailed the proposed sampling and analysis of soil intended for capping the SR 132 stockpiles, with sampling frequencies based on the location of Basin 5 adjacent to a previously tested SR 132 roadway cut section east of Carpenter Road. The DTSC, in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], approved this plan on June 12, 2020, finding it consistent with previous borrow area sampling.

An "Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California (Site Code: 900259)" was prepared by [[geocon-consultants]] and submitted by Caltrans. This report, dated February 26, 2024, evaluated the potential impact of the barium and lead-impacted soil stockpiles, encapsulated beneath the newly constructed SR 132 Expressway, on groundwater. The evaluation aligned with a 2014 assessment and the recent encapsulation efforts. On May 3, 2024, the DTSC, in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], approved this evaluation report, concurring that the encapsulated soil is not and will not impact groundwater.

### Regulatory Oversight and Cost Estimation

The DTSC oversees regulatory activities related to the SR 132 West Expressway site. An Annual Cost Estimate Letter for Fiscal Year 2025-2026, dated December 24, 2025, was sent by the DTSC to Caltrans. This letter outlines expected oversight activities and estimated costs for the period of July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026, based on DTSC's contracted estimation rates. The DTSC is authorized to require Caltrans to pay costs associated with the administration of a [[land-use-covenant]] placed as part of the cleanup. Caltrans remains liable for all response costs incurred by the DTSC.

### Open Items and Monitoring Well Decommissioning

Following the DTSC and [[central-valley-rwqcb]] approval of the groundwater evaluation report on May 3, 2024, the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring beneath the soil stockpiles was accepted. The four remaining monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) are to be decommissioned in accordance with the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan.
