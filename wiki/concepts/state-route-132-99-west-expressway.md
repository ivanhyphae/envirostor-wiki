The State Route 132 99 West Expressway project involves the remediation of soil stockpiles located near the intersection of SR 132 and SR 99 in Stanislaus County, California. These stockpiles were generated during the construction of a segment of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s. The construction traversed a parcel previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation Inc. (FMC), which had an evaporation pond. The primary chemicals of concern (COC) identified in the soil stockpiles are barium, lead, and strontium.

## Site record

The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings. The construction involved excavating a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel purchased from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation Inc. (FMC). An evaporation pond was located in the southernmost corner of this parcel. FMC and its predecessors were involved in chemical manufacturing, processing barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores and other minerals. The primary chemicals of concern (COC) are barium, lead, and strontium.

A "Final Supplemental Site Characterization Workplan, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132 and 99, Stanislaus County, California" was prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated September 2012. This workplan was developed to provide additional characterization of the soil stockpiles to evaluate and finalize a remedy for the management of hazardous substances. The additional characterization aims to assess potential lateral and vertical contaminant migration and update the Human Health Risk Assessment.

## Regulatory status

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), reviewed the "Final Supplemental Site Characterization Workplan". DTSC concurred with the Final Workplan on September 20, 2012.

## Open items

The "Final Supplemental Site Characterization Workplan" was developed to evaluate and finalize a remedy for the management of hazardous substances in the soil stockpiles.
