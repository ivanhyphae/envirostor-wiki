## Overview
The concept "Barium Below MCL" signifies that barium concentrations detected in environmental samples at the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] have been found to be below the applicable Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL). This designation is crucial for the remediation and verification processes concerning [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS).

## Site Record
During the Modesto Ramp Rehabilitation Project, SR 99 - Kansas Avenue Northbound Off-Ramp, Caltrans proposed to upgrade the SR 99 Kansas Avenue northbound ramp in the vicinity of Stockpile No. 3. This stockpile consisted of excess native soils and pond tailings generated from SR 99 construction in the early 1960s, which traversed a portion of a parcel previously owned by FMC Inc., a chemical manufacturing company that processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores. The primary chemicals of concern (COC) identified were barium, lead, and strontium.

Caltrans, through its contractors Geocon Consultants Inc. and Teichert Construction, submitted site investigation data and removal plans to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). In a letter dated May 29, 2012, Caltrans stated that final analysis indicated the presence of elevated metal concentrations above site-specific background levels, but below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels. {{quote: "The final analysis does indicate the presence of elevated metal concentrations above site-specific background levels, but below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels."}} Based on this, Caltrans proposed managing the excavated soil material as non-hazardous.

DTSC, in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB), reviewed the submitted information. In a letter dated August 30, 2012, DTSC concurred with Caltrans' proposed disposition of excavated material from the Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp project, which included portions of Stockpile No. 3. {{quote: "DTSC, in consultation with the Regional Water Quality control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB) reviewed information prepared by Caltrans' contractors."}} This concurrence implies that barium concentrations, among other COCs, were deemed acceptable for the proposed management.

## Regulatory Status
The applicable regulatory standard for barium in this context is the Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL). While specific MCL values are not explicitly stated in the provided sources for barium in relation to these stockpiles, the project's progression and DTSC's concurrence suggest that barium levels were found to be below relevant thresholds. Caltrans indicated that concentrations were {{quote: "below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels."}} The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) is the regulatory agency overseeing the remediation and disposition of these materials.
