Confirmation sampling completed after excavation of Barium Containing Soil (BCS) from Stockpile 3 provided the analytical basis for closing out that stockpile under the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP). Geocon Consultants, Inc. documented the removal and sampling results in a Technical Memorandum dated March 13, 2020, which Caltrans forwarded to DTSC on March 16, 2020, and which DTSC accepted by email on March 18, 2020 — the endpoint of the Stockpile 3 removal effort and the trigger for allowing unrestricted fill use of the area.

## What the sampling showed

The confirmation sampling was performed after BCS material was excavated from Stockpile 3 and relocated. According to the Technical Memorandum, results showed that barium and lead concentrations in the native soil remaining at the excavation depths were below the threshold values established in the RDIP. No specific concentration values are given in the available correspondence. On that basis, Geocon and Caltrans represented that all Stockpile 3 BCS material had been fully excavated and relocated.

## Regulatory review and acceptance

Richard Stewart of Caltrans transmitted the Technical Memorandum to DTSC (Dean Wright and Perry Myers) and the Regional Water Quality Control Board (Steven Meeks) on March 16, 2020, requesting approval to proceed with placing a minimum 12-inch clean soil cover and/or a minimum 4-inch aggregate base layer over the BCS consolidation zones of Stockpiles 1 and 2. That transmittal noted that the Stockpile 3 BCS removal did not include the separately regulated Aerially Deposited Lead (ADL) soil along northbound State Route 99, which remained outside the scope of this closeout.

DTSC's Dean Wright responded on March 18, 2020, stating that DTSC had reviewed the memo, agreed that the Stockpile 3 BCS had been removed and that the remaining soil met the RDIP verification thresholds, and concluded that no further BCS RDIP monitoring requirements were necessary for that area. Wright's email states this constitutes DTSC's acceptance of the memo and that the area is suitable for use as clean embankment fill.

## Significance

This acceptance closed out Stockpile 3 as a BCS management area under the RDIP: no further confirmation monitoring is required there, and the location is approved for clean embankment fill and other SR 132 highway-related construction east of SR 99. The approval is specific to Stockpile 3; it did not extend to the ADL-affected soil along northbound SR 99, and the same March 16, 2020 transmittal shows Stockpiles 1 and 2 were, at that time, still awaiting agency sign-off on cover placement rather than closed out on the same terms as Stockpile 3.
