The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) is a regulatory agency that reviews and approves key project documents and actions for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site. DTSC's approvals are required for the implementation of remedial strategies and the management of soil spoils.

### Approval of Stockpile 2 BCS Foundation Spoil Assessment Workplan

On March 4, 2022, DTSC approved the Technical Memorandum for Stockpile 2 Barium Containing Soil (BCS) Foundation Spoil Assessment Workplan, submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc. on behalf of Caltrans. This approval covers the planned sampling and management of BCS spoils generated from the installation of the Weather Station and Overhead Sign #1 on Stockpile 2. DTSC concurred with the stockpiling and sampling procedures. DTSC also agreed that if sampling results for barium and lead are below site-specific background concentrations, the material may be reused on-site with mutual agreement from DTSC and Caltrans. If results exceed background, the material is to be profiled for disposal at the Forward Landfill in Manteca or another acceptable facility.

### Approval of Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data

On May 3, 2024, DTSC, in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), approved the Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data for the Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles. This evaluation, submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc., assessed the potential impact of barium and lead-impacted soil stockpiles beneath the SR 132 Expressway on groundwater. DTSC and the RWQCB agreed that the data indicates the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and will not in the future. Consequently, DTSC and the RWQCB concurred with the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring beneath the soil stockpiles and decommission the four remaining monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) as per the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP).

### Approval of Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Sampling Plan and Technical Memorandum

On April 22, 2021, DTSC, along with the Water Board, approved the Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Technical Memorandum, dated April 12, 2021. This memorandum described the sampling and analysis completed to characterize the Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill area. The agencies agreed that the composite soil sample results were consistent with naturally occurring background concentrations and met the Barium Containing Soil clean cap criteria. This email served as DTSC's acceptance of the memorandum.

### Approval of Remedial Design Implementation Plan - Interim Phase 1

On April 3, 2019, DTSC, in consultation with the RWQCB, approved the Remedial Design Implementation Plan — Interim Phase 1 (RDIP-Interim Phase 1). This plan, submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc. for Caltrans, describes activities for the Interim Phase 1 construction project, including the removal and re-deposition of a portion of the Modesto Soil Stockpiles to support SR 132 construction. The document also detailed safety measures and sampling to protect human health and the environment. DTSC and the RWQCB had no additional comments after reviewing a revised version of the plan that addressed DTSC's comments from March 26, 2019.

### Approval of Annual Operation and Maintenance Inspection Report

On June 11, 2026, DTSC approved and accepted the Annual Operation and Maintenance Inspection Report dated January 26, 2026. This report documented an inspection at State Route 132 West Expressway at the State Route 132/99 Interchange. DTSC noted that vegetation along the road remained sparse, as observed in the previous year's report, and that erosion control materials were limiting erosion. DTSC encouraged continued vegetation along the roadway to prevent erosion. DTSC also noted the upcoming Annual Land Use Covenant Inspection Report, due by January 18, 2027.

### Variance to Remedial Design Implementation Plan

On September 2, 2020, Caltrans submitted a Second Variance to the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP Variance #2) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. This variance was prepared to address clean cover fill material proposed by the highway contractor to cap the upper surface of consolidated barium-containing soil (BCS) within Stockpiles 1 and 2 during the SR 132 West Project. The variance addressed Section 3.2.2 of the RDIP, which specified a minimum 12-inch-thick clean cover fill or a minimum 4-inch-thick aggregate base (AB) pending subsequent placement of structural pavement and hardscape. The variance proposed using a minimum of 4 inches of Class 3 aggregate subbase materials as clean cover fill, which Caltrans argued would provide adequate interim clean cover until the full pavement section/hardscape is placed.
