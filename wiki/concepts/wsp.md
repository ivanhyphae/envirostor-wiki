WSP served as construction manager (as WSP/City of Modesto) for the SR 132 project, sitting between [[geocon-consultants-inc]] — the environmental consultant that performed all barium containing soil (BCS) characterization and clean fill sampling — and both the highway contractor and [[dtsc]]. WSP staff (Rick Demi, Scott Frenette, Chad Baker, Inderjit Chadha, Ryan George, and later Michael Scott) were the addressees on Geocon's technical memoranda and the parties who routed contractor requests and sampling plans to Geocon and to Caltrans/DTSC for approval.

## Role in the clean fill approval chain

WSP's documented function was administrative and coordinating rather than technical: it received field information from the contractor (Bay Cities Paving & Grading), determined what regulatory approval was needed, and forwarded sampling plans through Geocon to DTSC. In the Carpenter Road Shoofly matter, Bay Cities' project intern Adan Corrales sent WSP (Scott Frenette) proposed clean cap test locations on February 25, 2021. Frenette responded on March 1, 2021, explaining that material from Retaining Wall No. 1110 had already been approved for use as clean cap, but that the remaining Carpenter Shoofly material required testing and DTSC approval before use, and requested from Bay Cities: (1) depth of excavation, (2) total fill volume, (3) verification of USA (utility) clearance or identification of conflicts, and (4) the contractor's planned excavation schedule. Geocon's John Juhrend then compiled the sampling plan "per details provided by WSP" and transmitted it to DTSC's Richard Stewart on April 2, 2021, with Frenette copied, for forwarding to DTSC for approval.

This sequence — contractor to WSP to Geocon to DTSC — was the operative approval pathway for using excavated native soil as clean cap over barium containing soil (BCS) in [[stockpile-1]] and [[stockpile-2]]. WSP's role was to identify which material still required testing (as distinct from the already-approved Wall 1110 material) and to supply the logistical details (excavation depth, volume, utility clearance, schedule) that Geocon needed to write the sampling plan.

## Recipient of Geocon's characterization findings

WSP construction managers were the addressees of record for the series of Geocon technical memoranda documenting BCS removal verification and clean fill/clean cap characterization across the site, including:

- Stockpile 1 MSE Wall Footing Excavation Characterization Testing (April 6, 2020)
- Stockpile 2 MSE Wall Footing Excavation Characterization Testing (June 18, 2020, addressed to Michael Scott)
- Stockpile 2 and Stockpile 3 BCS Removal Verification Testing (March 9 and March 13, 2020)
- BCS Stockpiles Clean Cap Borrow Area Characterization Testing (February 27, 2020) and the Basin 5 Clean Cap Borrow Area memo (revised July 2, 2020)
- Bent 2 Excavation Soil Stockpiles Characterization Testing (October 30, 2020)
- Carpenter Road Shoofly Clean Fill Characterization Testing (April 12, 2021)

Each memo states that its findings "will be included in the Removal Action Completion Report to be submitted following completion of the Stockpile 1 and 2 clean fill capping activities," making WSP the recipient of the record on which that completion report was to be built, but the sources here do not show WSP taking further action on the individual findings beyond receipt.

## Significance for the case file

WSP's correspondence is significant chiefly as the documentary link showing how field decisions (what material to excavate, where to test it, when to place it) were translated into the DTSC approval record. The Carpenter Shoofly exchange in particular shows WSP distinguishing between already-approved material (Wall 1110) and material still requiring testing, which is the operative distinction DTSC and Geocon relied on before that shoofly soil could be certified as suitable [[clean-soil-cover]] over BCS. No source in this set shows WSP itself performing sampling, analysis, or regulatory determinations — those functions were performed by Geocon and DTSC respectively.
