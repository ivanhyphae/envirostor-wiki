# SR 132 West Expressway Phase 1

The SR 132 West Expressway Phase 1 project involved the remediation of the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] through containment and integration into the highway embankment. This project was overseen by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] (RWQCB). The remediation strategy, as detailed in the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP), involved consolidating barium-containing soil (BCS) and capping it with clean fill material.

## Site Record

The remediation of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was implemented as part of the SR 132 West Expressway Phase 1 project. The remedy involved the consolidation and clean fill capping of barium-containing soil (BCS) as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway. {{quote: "The remedy consists of consolidation and clean fill capping of barium-containing soil (BCS) as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway."}} This project was located adjacent and west of Emerald Avenue, encompassing two main stockpile areas: Stockpile 1 between SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and Stockpile 2 between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, from SR 132 Stations 1135+35 to 1148+30. {{quote: "The California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the California Department of Transportation (Proponent) enter into this Operation and Maintenance Agreement (Agreement) for the site located adjacent and west of Emerald Avenue between approximate State Route (SR) 132 Stations 1125+50 (Stockpile 1) and between Emerald Avenue and SR 99 between approximate SR 132 Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30 (Stockpile 2) in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. Stockpiles 1 and 2 collectively constitute the Site."}}

The project was divided into phases. Phase 1, the initial construction phase, included building retaining walls and bridge abutments, and paving the southern half of the site, with the northern half covered by a graded engineered soil layer. {{quote: "The initial construction phase (see Phase 1: Typical Soil Stockpile Cross-Section) will build retaining walls and bridge abutments, and pave the southern half of the site. The northern half will be covered with a graded engineered soil layer."}} This phase was planned to begin in 2018 and end in 2020. {{quote: "The opening phase would begin in 2018 and end in 2020."}}

A [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and approved by the DTSC on April 3, 2019. {{quote: "The RDIP was submitted to the Department of Toxic Substances and subsequently approved on April 3, 2019."}} An Errata to the January 30, 2019 RDIP was issued post-construction contract award to update roles and responsibilities. {{quote: "This Post Construction Contract Award Errata updates the pertinent sections of the RDIP to reflect changes to previously identified roles and responsibilities, namely striking Geocon as the consultant employed by the state."}}

The project involved various regulatory approvals and agreements. A Final Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement was enclosed by DTSC on June 20, 2023. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) has enclosed a final copy of the Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Phase 1 Interim State Route 132 West Project, Modesto, Stanislaus County, California (Site)."}} The next step was recording the Land Use Covenant (LUC). {{quote: "The next project task will be recording the Land Use Covenant (LUC) on the remediated area to restrict its use."}}

Variances to the RDIP were requested and approved. For example, a second variance request accepted on September 21, 2020, allowed for the use of Class 3 aggregate subbase material as a clean cap alternative, with a revised minimum thickness of 6 inches due to its fine-grained nature. {{quote: "Both DTSC and the WB agree that Class 3 subbase can be used as an acceptable clean cap alternative however, a visual inspection of this stockpiled material conducted on September 15, 2020 revealed that it is primarily a fine sand with most of the grains likely small enough to pass through a No. 4 sieve. Given its abundant fine grained nature, both agencies agree that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches rather than 4 inches is warranted and that increased monitoring may be necessary."}} Another variance request, accepted on April 17, 2020, allowed for the use of excavated MSE wall footing soil from Stockpile 1 as clean cap soil, provided its barium concentrations were within naturally occurring background levels. {{quote: "Both agencies agree that this material can therefore be used as clean cap soil on the project."}}

## Regulatory Status

The remediation of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was conducted under the oversight of the DTSC and the RWQCB. The containment alternative, Alternative 4, was recommended by Caltrans and concurred with by DTSC and RWQCB. {{quote: "Based on an evaluation of the remedial actions, Caltrans recommends Alternative 4, containment by construction of the State Route 132 West project. DTSC and RWQCB concur with this recommendation."}} This alternative was chosen to provide long-term protection of human health and the environment while balancing contaminant isolation with project needs. {{quote: "This alternative offers long-term protection of human health and the environment, while balancing contaminant isolation with removal/disposal costs and the need for elevated fill material necessary to construct the State Route 132 West project between Needham Street and Carpenter Avenue."}}

An Operation and Maintenance Agreement was finalized, and an Operations and Maintenance Plan (O&M Plan) was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and submitted in December 2022. {{quote: "This O&M Plan has been prepared for barium-containing soil stockpiles that were consolidated, capped with clean fill material within designated Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones, and utilized as highway embankment fill between State Route 99 and west of Emerald Avenue during construction of Interim Phase I of the SR 132 West Project."}} Comments on the Draft O&M Plan were provided by DTSC on August 1, 2022. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), has reviewed the Draft Operation and Maintenance Plan (Draft O&M Plan) noted above which will be included in the Remedial Action Completion Report as Appendix G."}}

## Open Items

As of June 20, 2023, the recording of the Land Use Covenant (LUC) on the remediated area was the next project task. {{quote: "The next project task will be recording the Land Use Covenant (LUC) on the remediated area to restrict its use."}} The DTSC indicated they would send a draft of the LUC for review and signature within the following weeks. {{quote: "DTSC will send a draft of the LUC for your review and signature within the next several weeks."}}
