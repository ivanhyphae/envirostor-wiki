# Groundwater Monitoring Discontinuation

## Overview

Groundwater Monitoring Discontinuation refers to the regulatory closure of long-term groundwater surveillance beneath the former barium- and lead-impacted soil stockpiles at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (Site Code 900259) on State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California. Following a formal statistical evaluation of groundwater data, the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) jointly approved cessation of monitoring and decommissioning of the remaining four monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10). This decision was communicated in DTSC’s approval letter dated May 3, 2024, and marks a key step toward site closure under the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan.

## Site Record

- **Evaluation Report**: *Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California*, dated February 26, 2024, prepared by Geocon Consultants Inc. on behalf of Caltrans.
- **Regulatory Review**: DTSC and RWQCB reviewed the Evaluation Report. Both agencies concluded that “the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and that it will not impact groundwater in the future.”
- **Supporting Basis**: The conclusion was supported by:
  - Consistency with an earlier 2014 evaluation.
  - The recent encapsulation of the impacted soil beneath the newly constructed SR‑132 Express Way.
- **Approved Action**: DTSC and RWQCB concurred with the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring and decommission the four remaining wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) in accordance with the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan.
- **Agency Sign-off**: The approval letter was signed by Dean Wright, PG, Project Manager, DTSC Site Mitigation and Restoration Program, on May 3, 2024.

[Note: The source document does not provide specific chemical concentrations, statistical methods, or threshold values. Those details are contained in the February 26, 2024 Evaluation Report, which is referenced but not included in the provided source material.]

## Regulatory Status

The discontinuation decision was issued as an explicit approval by DTSC, in consultation with RWQCB. As of May 3, 2024, the groundwater monitoring obligation is considered fulfilled. No ongoing compliance monitoring is required, provided that the four wells are decommissioned per the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan. The site is currently in compliance with regulatory requirements as determined by the two oversight agencies.

## Open Items

- The four monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) must be decommissioned as per the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan. A decommissioning completion report may be required but is not specified in the source.
- No additional groundwater monitoring is required; however, DTSC’s letter does not explicitly waive future monitoring if site conditions change (e.g., if the encapsulation is disturbed). This is not documented as an open item but could be inferred from the closure nature of the decision.
