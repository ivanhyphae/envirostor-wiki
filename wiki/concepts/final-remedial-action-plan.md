## Overview

The Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles outlines the strategy for addressing soil contamination at the site located south of the State Route (SR) 99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The selected remedy, Alternative 4 (Containment), integrates the stockpiled soil into the construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. This involves using the soil as embankment fill, backfill for retaining walls, and beneath bridge abutments and roadway pavement. The plan was developed by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] on behalf of [[caltrans]] and reviewed by the [[dtsc]] and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). Public comments were solicited and addressed prior to the finalization of the RAP.

## Site Record

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles consist of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of material, primarily originating from the former Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility. This material was placed in three distinct stockpiles within the Caltrans right-of-way in the early 1960s during the construction of SR 99.

Site characterization studies, including an Initial Site Assessment (ISA) in 2003, a Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) in 2004, and subsequent site investigations (SI) in 2006 and 2012, identified barium as the primary contaminant of concern (COPC) in the soil stockpiles, with concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs). Cadmium was initially identified in Stockpiles #2 and #3 in the 2004 PSI but was later deemed not a COPC due to irreproducible results in subsequent investigations. Other detected COPCs in soil include metals such as lead, strontium, and arsenic, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Groundwater monitoring has consistently shown concentrations below California Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) for drinking water.

Alternative 4, Containment, involves incorporating the stockpiles into the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. During the interim progress phase (planned for completion by 2018, with construction ongoing), northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 not covered by roadways will be graded for drainage and capped with a minimum 6- to 12-inch-thick vegetated clean soil cap. The ultimate build-out phase (planned for completion by 2028) will fully contain the stockpiles beneath roadway pavements, behind retaining walls, and within bridge abutments. Stockpile 3 will be largely consolidated within the SR 132 Overcrossing abutment and excess soil placed on top of Stockpile 2, then covered with clean soil. Excavation will be limited to reconfiguring stockpiles to meet project design criteria, not for removal.

A Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) for Interim Phase 1 was prepared by Geocon in January 2019, detailing the technical approach for containment during construction.

## Regulatory Status

The [[dtsc]] and CVRWQCB are the oversight agencies for the RAP. The Final Remedial Action Plan was approved by the [[dtsc]] on March 14, 2018. The selected remedy, Alternative 4 (Containment), is considered protective of human health and the environment by eliminating exposure pathways and minimizing surface water infiltration. An Operation and Maintenance Agreement with DTSC and an Operation and Maintenance Plan will be required, including annual inspections and ongoing groundwater monitoring. DTSC will re-evaluate the containment remedy every 5 years.

## Open Items

The Final RAP was not revised as a result of public comments received during the 59-day public review period that began on January 18, 2017. A separate RDIP will be prepared for the Ultimate Phase 2 portion of the project.
