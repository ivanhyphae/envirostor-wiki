The Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles details the strategy for managing contaminated soil as part of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. It outlines the selected remedy of containment, integrating the stockpiled soil into the project's construction. The plan was developed by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] for [[caltrans]] and reviewed by the [[dtsc]] and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

## Regulatory Approval and Public Comment

The [[dtsc]] approved the Final Remedial Action Plan on March 14, 2018. Prior to this approval, a 59-day public comment period was held from January 18, 2017, to March 17, 2017, for the Draft EIR/EA and Draft Final RAP. Public notices were published in local newspapers, and a public hearing was held on February 22, 2017. A total of 305 comments were received, with 62 specifically addressing the Draft Final RAP. The [[dtsc]], in consultation with the CVRWQCB, provided responses to comments pertaining to the soil stockpiles, which were incorporated into Appendix J of the Final EIR/EA. The Final RAP was not revised as a result of the public comments received.

## Remedial Action: Containment

The selected remedy, Alternative 4 (Containment), involves incorporating the approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpiled soil into the construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. This includes using the soil as embankment fill, backfill for retaining walls, and beneath bridge abutments and roadway pavement.

The project will be constructed in two phases:
*   **Interim Progress Phase (completion by 2018, construction ongoing):** The northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2, which will not be covered by roadways, will be graded for drainage and capped with a minimum 6- to 12-inch-thick vegetated clean soil cap.
*   **Ultimate Build-Out Phase (completion by 2028):** The stockpiles will be fully contained beneath roadway pavements, behind retaining walls, and within bridge abutments. Stockpile 3 will be largely consolidated within the SR 132 Overcrossing abutment, with excess soil placed on top of Stockpile 2 and then covered with clean soil.

Excavation will be limited to reconfiguring stockpiles to meet project design criteria, not for removal. A Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) for Interim Phase 1 was prepared by Geocon in January 2019.

## Site Characterization and Contaminants

Site characterization studies, including an Initial Site Assessment (ISA) in 2003, a Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) in 2004, and subsequent site investigations (SI) in 2006 and 2012, identified barium as the primary contaminant of concern (COPC) in the soil stockpiles, with concentrations exceeding commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs). Cadmium was initially identified but later deemed not a COPC. Other detected COPCs include metals such as lead, strontium, and arsenic, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Groundwater monitoring has consistently shown concentrations below California Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs).

## Other Alternatives Considered

The Feasibility Study (FS) evaluated several remedial alternatives:
*   **No Action:** This alternative would provide the lowest level of protection and was deemed to have low regulatory and community acceptance.
*   **Institutional Controls:** This involved site management activities such as limiting access, maintaining fences, prohibiting soil disturbance, and continuing groundwater monitoring. It offered a higher level of protection than no action but might not be acceptable to the community.
*   **Removal and Offsite Disposal:** This alternative would provide the greatest protection and community acceptance but would have the highest short-term impacts (air quality, traffic) and the highest cost, with no identified funding. It was also deemed less implementable due to the need for clean fill to replace the removed soil for project construction.
