Sam Haack, P.E., signed the May 29, 2012 letter to [[dtsc]] as Project Manager for [[caltrans]] District 10, requesting review and concurrence on the disposition of material excavated from [[stockpile-3]] for the SR 99 Kansas Avenue off-ramp safety improvements. The letter is the vehicle by which Caltrans sought DTSC sign-off that excavated stockpile material could be managed as non-hazardous soil rather than proceeding through a formal Interim Remedial Action Workplan.

In the letter, Haack laid out the project context and the basis for the request: an estimated 6,000 cubic yards of material would be excavated for the widened roadbed, drainage, and retaining wall, intersecting Stockpile #3 of the soil stockpiles placed for the future State Route 132 Expressway project. Haack reported that [[geocon-consultants-inc]] had been contracted to perform soil analysis under DTSC's direction, and that the analysis showed elevated metal concentrations above [[site-specific-background-levels]] but below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels. On that basis, Haack stated the material was suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as non-hazardous soil, and that the contractor would haul it to the Stanislaus County Fink Road Landfill. Haack noted the contract was on hold pending DTSC's concurrence that the Stockpile #3 material could be managed as non-hazardous.

The letter was copied to a distribution list spanning Caltrans District 10 leadership (Carrie Bowen, Dinah Bortner), Central Region Environmental staff (Margaret Lawrence, Gail Miller, Juergen Vespermann, Richard C. Stewart), District 10 project managers (Christina Hibbard, Mark McAvoy), Central Region Construction (Cliff Adams), Public and Legislative Affairs (Chantel Miller), and the contractor Raul Ortiz of Teichert Construction — establishing Haack's letter as the formal internal and external record of the request across the project's construction, environmental, and management functions.

The follow-up letter of August 24, 2012, addressed to both DTSC (Steven Becker, Randy Adams) and the Regional Water Quality Control Board (Steven W. Meeks), provided the further site investigation data and concluded that an Interim Removal Action Workplan was not needed for the ramp project. That later letter is not signed by Haack in the material provided.
