Caltrans District 6 is the contracting entity and project owner for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles investigation and remediation, associated with the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Stanislaus County, California. The District 6 Hazardous Waste Branch managed the supplemental site investigation (SSI) under Contract No. 06A1580, Task Order No. 44, EA No. 10-403500. This investigation was conducted to address environmental data gaps identified by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), as required for regulatory agency approval of the SR 132 project.

Caltrans District 6 retained Geocon Consultants, Inc. to perform the SSI at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles, located south of the State Route 99 and Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto. The SSI involved advancing 68 borings for soil sampling and analysis. DTSC directed the work, with CVRWQCB consultation. Geocon's correspondence was addressed to Mr. Richard Stewart, PG, of Caltrans District 6. The SSI report, originally dated December 14, 2012, was revised on March 1, 2013, to incorporate DTSC's feedback from a February 15, 2013, review letter. Caltrans staff, including Sam Haack, were on the distribution list for the SSI submittal.

A Human Health Risk Assessment Update, also prepared for Caltrans District 6 by Geocon Consultants, was reviewed by DTSC's Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) on January 9, 2013. HERO's review memorandum identified the project as the "CalTrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles." Previous project documents under Caltrans District 6 include the May 2007 Site Investigation Report, "Characterization of Soil Stockpiles, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles," and the May 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment, both reviewed by HERO in a July 17, 2007, memorandum.

Caltrans District 6 operates under DTSC oversight, with CVRWQCB consultation, for the characterization and remediation of the Modesto Soil Stockpiles. The SSI report includes a disclaimer stating that its contents do not necessarily reflect the official views or policies of the State of California or the Federal Highway Administration and do not constitute a standard, specification, or regulation. The project is subject to DTSC review and approval, as evidenced by the February 15, 2013, DTSC review letter that led to revisions in the SSI report.

The SSI report was completed to address data gaps necessary for regulatory agency approval of the State Route 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. The provided sources do not specify whether final approval has been granted or if further submittals are pending.
