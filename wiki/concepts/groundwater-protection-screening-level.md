## Overview
The **Groundwater Protection Screening Level (GPSL)** is a project-specific, risk-based threshold developed for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA) to evaluate the potential for contaminants in soil stockpiles to impact underlying groundwater, particularly the shallow aquifer. It was established as a key decision criterion in the Final Soil Management Plan (SMP) Approval E-mail (dated August 24, 2020, from DTSC Project Manager D. Dinu to Caltrans) to determine whether stockpiled soil could be left in place or required excavation and off-site disposal. The GPSL is distinct from California Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) or federal screening levels; it is derived using site-specific parameters including soil type, depth to groundwater, and contaminant mobility.

## Site record
- **Document source**: The GPSL is defined in the Final SMP Approval E-mail (August 24, 2020), which approved the SMP for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. The E-mail explicitly states: "The GPSL for each COPC was calculated using the SESOIL transport model with site-specific input parameters including soil bulk density, organic matter content, and depth to groundwater (approximately 15 feet bgs)."
- **Contaminants of Potential Concern (COPCs)**: The GPSL was applied to arsenic, barium, lead, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) identified in stockpile soils from the site (primarily from SR 132 roadway maintenance and reconstruction materials).
- **Specific GPSL values** (from the E-mail, units in mg/kg soil):
  - Arsenic: 0.9 mg/kg
  - Barium: 180 mg/kg
  - Lead: 12 mg/kg
  - Benzo(a)pyrene: 0.15 mg/kg
  - Naphthalene: 0.5 mg/kg
- **Verification results**: Confirmatory soil samples collected from three stockpiles (SP-1, SP-2, SP-3) in September 2020 showed that barium concentrations in SP-2 (220 mg/kg) and benzo(a)pyrene in SP-1 (0.22 mg/kg) exceeded their respective GPSLs. Arsenic and lead were below GPSLs in all stockpiles. Exceedances triggered a Corrective Action Notice (CAN-2020-01, October 2020) requiring removal of affected soil to a Class II landfill, which was completed by December 2020 (7,500 tons of soil removed).
- **Spatial distribution**: GPSL exceedances were localized to the upper 2 feet of SP-2 (barium) and the southern quadrant of SP-1 (benzo(a)pyrene), as per post-excavation confirmation sampling (January 2021 report).

## Regulatory status
- **Applicable standards**: The GPSL is not a general California or federal standard; it is site-specific and authorized by DTSC under the SMP for this project. It is more stringent than California MCLs for groundwater (e.g., MCL for barium is 1,000 µg/L, while the GPSL of 180 mg/kg soil corresponds to a modeled leachate concentration of 15 µg/L at the water table).
- **Agency enforcement**: DTSC (Project Manager D. Dinu) approved the GPSL framework as part of the SMP. Compliance is assessed against the GPSL, not MCLs, for soil stockpile management.
- **Current status**: As of the latest DTSC site status memo (March 2021), all stockpiles that exceeded GPSL thresholds have been remediated (soil removal completed December 2020). The remaining stockpiles (SP-3 fully, SP-1 partially) are in compliance with GPSL criteria and were left in place for future use as roadway fill under the SMP conditions.

## Open items
- **Long-term monitoring**: No groundwater monitoring is required because GPSL exceedances were addressed via soil removal, and modeling indicated no groundwater degradation. However, DTSC may request a re-evaluation of GPSL values if new stockpiles (e.g., from future SR 132 work) are added, as noted in the SMP Approval E-mail: "If new stockpiles are generated, propose GPSLs to DTSC for review within 30 days."
- **Unresolved data gap**: The GPSL for lead (12 mg/kg) is notably low relative to California background soil lead (15–40 mg/kg in Stanislaus County). This discrepancy is flagged in the response to comments document (August 2020) as an unresolved technical issue; Caltrans argued that background exceedances might occur but DTSC maintained the GPSL pending field verification. No further action has been taken to revise it.
