The Operation and Maintenance Plan governs long-term care of the clean-fill cap over barium-containing soil (BCS) Stockpiles 1 and 2 at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, which were consolidated and buried as highway embankment fill beneath SR 132 during Interim Phase 1 construction. Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared the plan for Caltrans; DTSC, in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board, reviewed and commented on the draft before Caltrans and DTSC executed it as a formal Operation and Maintenance Agreement under Health and Safety Code section 25355.5(a)(1)(C) and/or section 25187. The plan's requirements — advance notification before any cap disturbance, inspection triggers, and annual reporting — are the mechanism by which the buried BCS remedy is monitored now that the soil is no longer physically accessible for verification.

## Draft review and revisions

DTSC issued written comments on the Draft O&M Plan (dated March 2022) on August 1, 2022, directed to Geocon's John E. Juhrend. The comments required several substantive changes before the plan could be finalized:

- Section 2.1 was to specify a minimum advance-notice period for any BCS disturbance; DTSC recommended 10 working days so that prior written DTSC authorization could be obtained before work began.
- Section 2.4 was to reference Health and Safety Code section 25123.3(b)(4)(A) explicitly, since the section's stated minimum cap-liner thickness (6 millimeters) satisfies that provision but the statute is expressed in mils, creating potential ambiguity; DTSC also flagged a misspelling of "millimeter."
- Section 3.1 was to add inspection triggers beyond the annual inspection: fires, substantial rainfall (1 inch in 24 hours), significant seismic events, and traffic accidents capable of damaging the cap — of particular concern on the north-side vegetated slope, which was to remain unpaved until Ultimate Phase 2 construction.
- Section 2.7's periodic patrols were to also document unauthorized access, including homeless encampments, and any resulting impact to the cap, again with emphasis on the north vegetated slope.
- The O&M Site Inspection Form was to be expanded (the comment is truncated in the record but calls for additional detail in the form's fields).
- A misspelling of "Maintenance" in the draft's subject line was noted.

The final O&M Plan incorporated the 10-working-day notification requirement in Section 2.1, confirming that DTSC's comment was adopted. The final plan also expanded its scope from two stockpiles named in the draft to three: it describes BCS Stockpiles 1 through 3, consolidated and contained within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones, whereas the underlying O&M Agreement and cover correspondence refer only to Stockpiles 1 and 2 as constituting the Site.

## Final Agreement and operative terms

DTSC transmitted the final O&M Agreement to Caltrans (Richard Stewart) on June 20, 2023, under Docket No. HSA-FY22/23-067, describing the installed remedy as consolidation and clean-fill capping of BCS as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway. The cover letter states that recording of a Land Use Covenant over the remediated area was the next project task, to be sent to Caltrans for signature. The Agreement makes Caltrans liable for all DTSC oversight costs, invoiced quarterly, and requires Caltrans to retain records for a minimum of six years after termination of the Agreement. It designates Richard Stewart (Caltrans) and Dean Wright (DTSC) as the points of contact for notification and reporting under the plan, with any change of contact to be handled by addendum.

The plan's substantive control is Section 2.1: no disturbance of BCS material within the Stockpile 1 and 2 Containment Zones is permitted without prior written DTSC authorization, following at least 10 working days' advance notice from Caltrans. Where the O&M Plan's requirements conflict with standard Caltrans highway maintenance procedures, the O&M Plan controls.

## Annual inspections under the plan

The plan's reporting obligation has continued into operation. Caltrans (Adam Inman) submitted the Annual Operation and Maintenance Inspection Report dated January 26, 2026, covering an inspection at the SR 132/SR 99 interchange; the submission noted no observed issues and documented maintenance-crew debris removal on the north slope of Stockpile 2. DTSC (Arielle McLeskey) approved and accepted the report as final on June 11, 2026, while noting that vegetation along the roadway remained sparse — a condition also flagged in the prior year's report — and that leftover erosion control materials appeared to be limiting erosion in the interim. DTSC's response identifies the next required deliverable as the Annual Land Use Covenant Inspection Report, due no later than January 18, 2027, indicating that the O&M Plan's annual inspection cycle runs alongside a separate, parallel reporting obligation tied to the Land Use Covenant.
