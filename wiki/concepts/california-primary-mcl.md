The California primary MCL is the recurring benchmark against which Geocon's Title 22 dissolved metals and general minerals results were compared in every groundwater monitoring report for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles, from the March/May 2012 baseline through at least the May 2016 event. Across roughly a dozen sampling rounds, the primary MCL was exceeded only by nitrate as nitrogen — repeatedly, including in the upgradient well — while the metals designated as the site's chemicals of concern (barium, lead, strontium) never approached their respective primary MCLs.

## Nitrate: the recurring primary MCL exceedance

Nitrate as nitrogen, evaluated against a primary MCL of 10 mg/l, was the only general mineral or dissolved metal to exceed a primary MCL in any sampling round reviewed. The exceedance was not confined to wells near the stockpiles: it also occurred at MW-1, the site's upgradient well, in nearly every round, which each report interpreted as possibly reflecting natural background nitrate rather than a stockpile-related impact.

MW-1's own nitrate concentration (ranging from about 9.2 to 17 mg/l across rounds, and above 10 mg/l in most of them) was repeatedly flagged by Geocon as likely representing natural background rather than a site-related effect, since MW-1 sits upgradient of the stockpiles. By May 2016, nitrate at both remaining wells (MW-1 and MW-7) had dropped below the primary MCL for the first time in the series reviewed, consistent with the general downward drift in the later rounds.

## Metals and other general minerals: no primary MCL exceedances

For every sampling round from March 2012 through May 2016, Geocon's reports state that dissolved metals — including barium, strontium, and lead (the three chemicals of concern identified by DTSC), as well as arsenic, chromium, vanadium, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, copper, cobalt, antimony, and zinc — were reported at concentrations that did not exceed their respective primary MCLs (or other applicable numeric water quality thresholds) in any well, in any round. Manganese exceedances recurring across several rounds (MW-4 in March/May 2012, MW-6 in January and November 2012, MW-8 in November 2012, MW-3 and MW-9 in September 2012, MW-10 in February 2014) were each measured against the secondary MCL of 50 µg/l for taste and odor, not a primary MCL. Total dissolved solids (TDS) exceedances, recurring in most rounds at MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10, were likewise assessed against the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l.

## Significance for the site narrative

Because barium, lead, and strontium — the metals DTSC identified as the primary chemicals of concern tied to the former FMC evaporation ponds and the stockpiled soil — never exceeded primary MCLs in any monitoring round, the groundwater monitoring record does not establish a drinking-water-standard exceedance attributable to the stockpiles' metals content. The only primary MCL exceedances recorded across the monitoring history are for nitrate, a general mineral parameter that the same reports repeatedly attribute to background conditions evidenced by the upgradient well rather than to the stockpiles, and that exceedance had resolved below the primary MCL by the May 2016 event, the last round detailed in the material reviewed. The February 2024 updated statistical evaluation subsequently recommended decommissioning the remaining four monitoring wells and discontinuing groundwater monitoring.
