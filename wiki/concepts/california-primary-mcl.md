The California Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) is a regulatory standard for drinking water quality established by the California Department of Public Health. For the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project, Primary MCLs serve as benchmarks for evaluating dissolved metals and general minerals detected in groundwater samples.

### Groundwater Monitoring Results

Groundwater monitoring reports consistently compare detected analyte concentrations against California Primary MCLs.

*   **Nitrate as nitrogen** has been reported at concentrations exceeding the Primary MCL of 10 mg/l in multiple wells and sampling events.
    *   In the January 2013 sampling event, nitrate concentrations ranged from 2.9 mg/l (MW-3) to 30 mg/l (MW-5), with exceedances noted in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10.
    *   In the February 2014 sampling event, nitrate concentrations ranged from 2.8 mg/l (MW-3) to 34 mg/l (MW-5), with exceedances in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10.
    *   In the June 2014 sampling event, nitrate concentrations ranged from 2.6 mg/l (MW-3) to 29 mg/l (MW-5), with exceedances in MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, and MW-10.
    *   In the April 2015 sampling event, nitrate concentrations ranged from 7.0 mg/l (MW-4) to 17 mg/l (MW-1), with exceedances in MW-1 and MW-2.
*   **Manganese** has been reported at concentrations exceeding its numeric water quality threshold in some instances.
    *   In the March 2012 sampling event, manganese was reported at greater than the secondary MCL of 50 µg/l for the sample collected from MW-4.
    *   In the February 2014 sampling event, manganese was reported in one sample from MW-10 at a concentration exceeding its numeric water quality threshold.
*   **Lead** was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than its practical quantitation limit (PQL) of 1.0 µg/l in samples collected in January 2013, November 2012, September 2012, or June 2014. In December 2013, lead was reported in one sample from MW-6 at 1.3 µg/l, which was less than its numeric water quality threshold.

### Regulatory Context

California Primary MCLs are established by the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) and serve as regulatory thresholds for drinking water quality. Groundwater monitoring data is compared against these MCLs to assess compliance and potential risks.

### Ongoing Monitoring

The ongoing groundwater monitoring program, as detailed in reports such as the June 2015 report, continues to assess compliance with Primary MCLs.
