## Overview

**Paul Ruffin** is identified in the Interagency Agreement Task Order Contract 43A0404-04 as the project manager or lead representative for the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) District 10, responsible for oversight of the Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation at the State Route 132 site in Stanislaus County, California. His role involves coordination between Caltrans, regulatory agencies, and contractors to manage the excavation, testing, and disposal of contaminated soil stockpiles associated with historical highway construction activities. Ruffin’s name appears in the contract documentation as a point of contact for project execution and compliance.

## Site Record

In the **Interagency Agreement Task Order Contract 43A0404-04** (executed between Caltrans and the California Department of Toxic Substances Control, or DTSC), Paul Ruffin is listed as the Caltrans District 10 project manager. The contract, dated approximately 2019, defines the scope of work for remediation of soil stockpiles located along SR 132 near Modesto. These stockpiles were generated during prior highway improvement projects and contain elevated concentrations of arsenic, barium, and other heavy metals. Specific documented concentrations include arsenic up to 120 mg/kg (exceeding DTSC’s California Human Health Screening Level of 0.07 mg/kg for residential use) and barium at 760 mg/kg (exceeding the DTSC residential screening level of 750 mg/kg). The stockpiles, totaling approximately 15,000 cubic yards, were staged at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site (identified as Site ID 1002-0024). Ruffin’s responsibilities include overseeing Phase 1 (2019–2020) mobilization, stockpile characterization sampling, and coordination with DTSC for approval of the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) submitted in March 2020.

## Regulatory Status

The project is governed by the **Interagency Agreement** between Caltrans and DTSC under the California Health and Safety Code, with DTSC as the lead regulatory agency. Applicable standards include DTSC’s California Human Health Screening Levels (for residential and industrial land use), the Medical Marijuana Waste Program (relevant to historical site use), and **Regional Water Quality Control Board** Basin Plan objectives for groundwater protection. As of the last documented status in the Task Order (October 2020), the site was in compliance with interim measures, including soil stockpile cover and erosion controls, pending final remediation approval. The arsenic exceedance relative to the DTSC residential screening level (0.07 mg/kg) and the barium exceedance (760 mg/kg vs. 750 mg/kg) were noted as action triggers. No further regulatory actions had been reported at that time.

## Open Items

- **Final Remedial Action Plan Approval**: The RAP submitted March 2020 remained pending DTSC approval as of the last document update. Approval conditions may require additional verification sampling.
- **Verification Sampling Results**: Post-excavation verification sampling data (e.g., soil confirmation samples at 3-foot intervals) were not yet submitted to DTSC for review as of the contract’s active period.
- **Disposal Documentation**: Confirmation of off-site disposal at a Class I or II landfill, with manifests, is outstanding in the project records available.
- **Groundwater Monitoring**: No groundwater monitoring wells were installed under the current contract; residual contamination could require future downgradient monitoring if stockpile removal is incomplete.
