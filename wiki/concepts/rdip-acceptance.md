## Overview

Rdip Acceptance is a regulatory decision point in the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA) concerning the acceptance criteria and formal concurrence by the overseeing regulatory agency — the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) — that soil stockpiles meet established cleanup standards and are suitable for reuse, relocation, or closure. The term appears in project correspondence and remedial action documentation as a milestone requiring DTSC sign-off before stockpiled materials can be removed from the site or repurposed.

## Site record

The Rdip Acceptance determination applies to soil stockpiles generated during Phase 1 construction activities (2019–2020) along the State Route 132 corridor in Stanislaus County. Stockpile characterization sampling was conducted to evaluate concentrations of metals, petroleum hydrocarbons, and volatile organic compounds against project-specific cleanup goals.

Analytical results for stockpile material were compared to the screening levels established in the project's approved Remedial Action Plan (RAP). DTSC correspondence dated March 15, 2021, references Rdip Acceptance as a required confirmation step following completion of stockpile sampling and data validation. The letter specifies that acceptance is contingent upon demonstration that all stockpile analytical results fall below the residential and commercial screening levels adopted in the RAP for the site.

Specific concentration thresholds tied to the Rdip Acceptance determination include:
- Arsenic below 12 mg/kg (residential screening level)
- Lead below 80 mg/kg (residential screening level)
- Total petroleum hydrocarbons as diesel (TPH-d) below 100 mg/kg
- Total petroleum hydrocarbons as motor oil (TPH-mo) below 500 mg/kg

Stockpile SP-3, located in the northern staging area, required additional confirmation sampling after initial results showed TPH-d at 110 mg/kg in one composite sample collected February 2021. Follow-up discrete sampling in April 2021 returned TPH-d concentrations of 45 mg/kg, 62 mg/kg, and 78 mg/kg, all below the 100 mg/kg threshold.

The Phase 1 Remedial Action Completion Report (dated June 2021) documents that all 14 stockpiles undergoing Rdip Acceptance review achieved concentrations below applicable thresholds. Stockpiles SP-1 through SP-14 are listed with final maximum detected concentrations for each analyte of concern, all falling within the acceptance limits.

## Regulatory status

DTSC provided formal Rdip Acceptance via letter dated July 22, 2021 (DTSC Reference No. SR132-MOD-2021-07), confirming that stockpiles SP-1 through SP-14 meet the cleanup standards specified in the RAP. The acceptance is conditioned on the following:
- Stockpile material designated for off-site reuse must be transported to a facility permitted to accept the material type under its waste discharge requirements
- Stockpiles relocated within the project right-of-way must be placed in areas characterized with comparable or lower background concentrations
- Transport manifests must be retained in the project file for a minimum of five years

No further DTSC review of Phase 1 stockpile disposition is required, except in the event of material relocation to an area not previously characterized under the RAP.
