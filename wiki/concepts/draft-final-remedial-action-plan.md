# Draft Final Remedial Action Plan

## Overview

The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) is the primary decision document for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site (SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, Modesto, Stanislaus County, California). Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of Caltrans and submitted under Project No. S9800-01-17 on October 27, 2014, the RAP summarizes site characterization results, human health risk findings, a feasibility study evaluation of remedial alternatives, and the recommended remedy. It was developed to serve as a supplement to the project's Environmental Impact Report under CEQA, with DTSC and CVRWQCB acting as both oversight agencies for the RAP and reviewing agencies for the EIR.

The RAP process included preparation of a baseline community survey, a public participation plan (completed by DTSC in November 2014), a 30-day public review and comment period with public meeting, a public notice published in local newspapers, distribution of fact sheets to nearby residents, and a responsiveness summary. All comments received during the public comment period were to be responded to in writing and distributed to commenters. The Draft Final RAP would be revised as necessary based on comments; if significant changes were required, it would be resubmitted for additional public review. The public review period was anticipated to occur in summer 2014.

On May 18, 2015, DTSC Senior Engineering Geologist Randy S. Adams transmitted revised appendices to Caltrans Project Manager Grace Magsayo — Appendix B (Administrative Record), Appendix C (Statement of Reasons), and Appendix D (Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility) — directing that the Draft Final RAP with those appendices be the document referenced in the Draft EIR and made available for concurrent public review.

## Site record

The RAP documents the origin of the soil stockpiles: in the early 1960s, during construction of SR-99, soil in and around one of the former FMC facility's unlined ponds was excavated and stockpiled within the current Caltrans right-of-way for a planned SR-99/SR-132 interchange. FMC and its predecessors operated a chemical processing facility approximately 500 feet north of the Site from 1929 to approximately 1985, processing barium and strontium minerals (barite and celestite). From the early 1950s to the late 1970s, liquid wastes were discharged to seven unlined ponds at the facility.

The RAP's recommended remedial alternative — containment of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil beneath roadway pavement, behind retaining walls, and behind bridge abutments as part of the SR-132 Project — was selected from four alternatives evaluated in the Feasibility Study:

- **No action:** lowest overall protection, lowest regulatory acceptance, least costly, most implementable.
- **Institutional controls:** limiting access to Caltrans-authorized personnel, inspecting and maintaining perimeter fencing, prohibiting soil-disturbing activities, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater monitoring — providing higher protection than no action with DTSC regulatory acceptance, but potentially unacceptable to the community due to perceived threat.
- **Removal and offsite disposal:** greatest overall protection of human health and the environment; greatest short-term air quality and traffic impacts; highest cost; least implementable due to funding not being identified and the need to replace stockpile soil with an even greater amount of clean fill for project construction.
- **Containment through project construction (Alternative #4):** the recommended alternative.

A land use covenant would be recorded to preclude use of the property for residences, schools, daycare centers, and hospitals.

The RAP identifies the primary COPCs in stockpile soil as barium and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), with metals and general minerals (e.g., nitrate, total dissolved solids) as primary groundwater COPCs. Cadmium was not considered a COPC in the 2007 HHRA due to lack of elevated concentrations in the 2006 site investigation. Strontium was not considered a COPC because the maximum concentration was more than two orders of magnitude less than USEPA's residential Regional Screening Level of 47,000 mg/kg.

## Regulatory status

The RAP was prepared in general accordance with Appendix C2 (*Remedial Action Plan Sample*) of DTSC's *Proven Technologies and Remedies Guidance, Remediation of Metals in Soil* (August 29, 2008). The Statement of Reasons (Appendix C), prepared pursuant to California Health and Safety Code section 25356.1(d), confirms that the 2007 Risk Assessment and 2013 Risk Assessment Update determined the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not present an unacceptable risk to human health. Groundwater under the stockpiles was found not to contain COPCs exceeding primary maximum contaminant levels for drinking water. According to the City of Modesto and a Department of Water Resources survey, no municipal or domestic use of shallow groundwater was reported within one mile of the soil stockpiles.

Ecological impacts were addressed through an Ecological Screening Evaluation included in the June 2009 Preliminary Endangerment Assessment.

Potential environmental impacts of the recommended remedial alternative were to be evaluated in Caltrans' Draft EIR, with DTSC and CVRWQCB as reviewing agencies.

The Final Feasibility Study received DTSC approval on June 30, 2014. The final RAP was subject to DTSC approval for implementation.

## Open items

The Draft Final RAP was submitted with the public comment period pending. The responsiveness summary had not yet been prepared at the time of submittal. Final DTSC approval of the RAP for implementation remained pending, contingent on public review and resolution of comments. The community acceptance criterion under the NCP modifying criteria was explicitly deferred — the Feasibility Study did not evaluate it, noting it would be addressed after the public comment period.

Review and concurrence of the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan and the Operation and Maintenance Plan by DTSC and CVRWQCB was required prior to implementation of the recommended remedial action.
