## Draft Final Remedial Action Plan

### Overview

The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans). It was submitted to the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) in a revised version dated October 27, 2014 (Project No. S9800-01-17). The RAP documented the characterization of contaminant impacts, risk evaluations, and the selection of a preferred remedial alternative for three soil stockpiles (Stockpiles 1–3) located south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, Stanislaus County. The stockpiles were created in the early 1960s when soil from an FMC chemical processing facility—including pond tailings from unlined evaporation ponds (1950s–1970s)—was excavated and placed within the Caltrans right-of-way for a planned interchange. The primary contaminant of potential concern (COPC) is barium, with maximum detected concentrations up to 130,000 mg/kg in deeper stockpile zones.

The RAP recommended **Alternative 4 – Containment** as the preferred remedy: use of the stockpile soil as embankment fill for the SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project, covered by roadway pavement, retaining walls, and bridge abutments, with a vegetated clean soil cap (6–12 inches thick) on temporarily exposed areas during the interim phase. This plan was incorporated as a supplement to the Draft Environmental Impact Report (EIR) for the SR-132 Project and made available for public review from January 18 to March 17, 2017.

### Site Record

- **Origin**: Stockpiles formed from FMC tailings and native soil during SR-99 construction in the early 1960s. Aerial photos from 1963 show Stockpiles 2 and 3 in place; by 1967 all three stockpiles existed in their current configuration.
- **Characterization**: 
  - 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation (Shaw): barium exceeded commercial/industrial CHHSL (63,000 mg/kg) in multiple samples; cadmium exceedances noted but later determined to be false positives (Geocon 2012 SSI found cadmium <1.0 mg/kg in all 348 samples).
  - 2006 Site Investigation (Shaw): installed 8 monitoring wells; groundwater met all primary and secondary MCLs.
  - 2012 Supplemental Site Investigation (Geocon): additional fenceline/perimeter borings; no lateral migration of barium above residential screening levels; cadmium ruled out as COPC.
- **Volume**: Approximately 160,000 cubic yards of stockpile soil.
- **Risk Assessment**: 
  - 2007 HHRA (Shaw): concluded stockpiles do not pose unacceptable risk to offsite residents, trespassers, construction workers, or hypothetical shallow groundwater users.
  - 2013 HHRA Update (Geocon): confirmed earlier conclusions; barium, strontium, lead, and PAHs evaluated; maximum surface concentrations of barium and nickel below USEPA residential RSLs.
  - DTSC concurred in a December 17, 2009 letter that current management (fencing, vegetative cover, access control, groundwater monitoring) is protective.
- **Removal Action**: September 2012 – approximately 2,800 yd³ of Stockpile 3 excavated for ramp rehabilitation; transported as non-hazardous to Forward Class II landfill in Manteca. Air monitoring showed airborne barium and lead below levels of concern.
- **Stormwater Monitoring**: March 2006 and February 2014 events; one sample from Stockpile 3 showed barium at 2,000 µg/L (MCL = 1,000 µg/L), but runoff was confined to Caltrans ROW. DTSC concluded stormwater is not a chronic exposure issue.
- **Groundwater Monitoring**: Quarterly monitoring since June 2013; no COPCs above primary MCLs. No municipal or domestic use of shallow groundwater within one mile per City of Modesto and DWR survey.

### Regulatory Status

- **Lead Oversight Agency**: DTSC (under Interagency Agreement June 22, 2012); Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) as co‑regulator.
- **Applicable Standards (ARARs)**: Table 1 of the RAP lists federal and state ARARs including:
  - CHHSLs for barium: residential = 5,200 mg/kg, commercial/industrial = 63,000 mg/kg.
  - Primary MCLs for groundwater (e.g., barium MCL = 1,000 µg/L).
  - USEPA Regional Screening Levels (RSLs) for residential soil.
- **Plan Status**: Draft Final RAP approved by DTSC for public noticing on May 18, 2015 (letter from Randy S. Adams). Public comment period held January 18 – March 17, 2017; DTSC issued responsiveness summary. Final RAP approval pending certification of the Final EIR/EA by Caltrans.
- **Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility (NBAR)**: Issued by DTSC as Appendix D of the RAP (revised May 18, 2015; further revised November 14, 2017).
- **CEQA**: The RAP is incorporated into the SR-132 West Freeway/Expressway Project EIR/EA (lead agency: Caltrans; DTSC and CVRWQCB are reviewing agencies).

### Open Items

- **Final Approval**: DTSC will issue a final decision on the Draft Final RAP after Caltrans certifies the Final EIR/EA (anticipated 2018 per revised schedule, but actual date not documented in provided sources).
- **Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP)**: To be prepared by Caltrans after RAP approval; details construction methods, dust control, and air monitoring.
- **Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement**: Required to ensure long‑term integrity of the cap and containment; includes annual inspections and five‑year reviews by DTSC.
- **Land Use Covenant**: Must be recorded to preclude residential, school, daycare, and hospital uses on the property.
- **Phased Construction**: Interim progress phase (two‑lane expressway) originally scheduled for completion by 2018, later revised to 2020; ultimate build‑out (four lanes) by 2028. Remedial Action Completion Reports required after each phase.
- **Community Health Concerns**: Commenters alleged health impacts from stockpiles; DTSC responded that county health department (209‑558‑7000) has resources to assess whether reported illnesses exceed expected rates, but DTSC lacks epidemiology expertise. No further action documented in sources.
