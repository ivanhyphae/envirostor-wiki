## Overview

The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) is a critical document for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site, a component of the [[sr-132]] West Freeway/Expressway Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for [[caltrans]], the RAP details the site characterization, human health risk assessments, and evaluates remedial alternatives, ultimately recommending containment as the preferred remedy. This plan supplements the project's [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) and is subject to oversight by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). The RAP development process included public notices, meetings, and comment periods, with revisions incorporated based on feedback.

## Site record

The soil stockpiles, estimated at approximately 160,000 cubic yards, originated from soil excavated in the early 1960s at the former FMC facility, which processed [[barium]] and [[strontium]] minerals from 1929 to approximately 1985. The primary contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) identified in the stockpile soil were [[barium]] and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Metals and general minerals were identified as primary groundwater COPCs.

The RAP recommended Alternative #4, which involves the containment of the stockpile soil beneath roadway pavement, behind retaining walls, and behind bridge abutments as part of the SR 132 Project. This was one of four alternatives evaluated in the Feasibility Study (FS):

*   **No action:** Described as offering the lowest overall protection and regulatory acceptance, being the least costly, and most implementable.
*   **Institutional controls:** This included limiting access, inspecting and maintaining fencing, prohibiting soil-disturbing activities, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater monitoring. It provided higher protection than no action and had DTSC regulatory acceptance but might be unacceptable to the community.
*   **Removal and offsite disposal:** This alternative provided the greatest overall protection but had the greatest short-term air quality and traffic impacts, the highest cost, and was least implementable due to funding needs and the requirement to replace excavated soil with clean fill for project construction.
*   **Containment through project construction (Alternative #4):** This was the recommended alternative.

A land use covenant would be recorded to restrict the property's use to non-residential purposes.

The Draft Final RAP was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and submitted on October 27, 2014. The document was revised in response to DTSC comments dated September 18, 2014.

The Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) with the attached Draft Final RAP was circulated for public review from January 18, 2017, to March 17, 2017. A public hearing was held on February 22, 2017.

The Final EIR/EA, dated March 12, 2018, included responses to comments received during the public review period.

## Regulatory status

The RAP was prepared in general accordance with DTSC's *Proven Technologies and Remedies Guidance, Remediation of Metals in Soil* (August 29, 2008). The 2007 and 2013 Risk Assessment Updates determined that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not present an unacceptable risk to human health. Groundwater beneath the stockpiles did not contain COPCs exceeding primary maximum contaminant levels (MCLs). Surveys by the City of Modesto and the Department of Water Resources reported no municipal or domestic use of shallow groundwater within one mile of the stockpiles.

The Final Feasibility Study received DTSC approval on June 30, 2014. The final RAP was subject to DTSC approval for implementation.

## Open items

At the time of submittal, the Draft Final RAP was pending public comment, and the responsiveness summary had not yet been prepared. Final DTSC approval for implementation was contingent on public review and resolution of comments. The community acceptance criterion, a modifying criterion under the National Contingency Plan (NCP), was deferred for evaluation after the public comment period. Review and concurrence of the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan and the Operation and Maintenance Plan by DTSC and CVRWQCB were required prior to implementing the recommended remedial action.
