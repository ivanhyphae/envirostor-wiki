## Overview

The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) is a key deliverable for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] site, which is part of the [[sr-132]] West Freeway/Expressway Project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. Prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. for [[caltrans]], the RAP outlines the site characterization, human health risk assessments, and evaluates remedial alternatives, ultimately recommending containment as the preferred remedy. This document supplements the project's [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) and is subject to oversight by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB). The RAP process involved public notices, meetings, and comment periods, with revisions made based on feedback received.

## Site record

The soil stockpiles, estimated at approximately 160,000 cubic yards, originated in the early 1960s from soil excavated at the former FMC facility. This facility processed barium and strontium minerals from 1929 to approximately 1985. The primary contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) identified in the stockpile soil were [[barium]] and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). Metals and general minerals were identified as primary groundwater COPCs.

The RAP recommended Alternative #4, which involves the containment of the stockpile soil beneath roadway pavement, behind retaining walls, and behind bridge abutments as part of the SR 132 Project. This was one of four alternatives evaluated in the Feasibility Study (FS):

*   **No action:** Described as offering the lowest overall protection and regulatory acceptance, being the least costly, and most implementable. {{quote: "No action: Offered the lowest overall protection, lowest regulatory acceptance, was least costly, and most implementable."}}
*   **Institutional controls:** This included limiting access, inspecting and maintaining fencing, prohibiting soil-disturbing activities, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater monitoring. It provided higher protection than no action and had DTSC regulatory acceptance but might be unacceptable to the community. {{quote: "Institutional controls: Included limiting access, inspecting and maintaining fencing, prohibiting soil-disturbing activities, maintaining vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater monitoring. This provided higher protection than no action and had DTSC regulatory acceptance but might be unacceptable to the community."}}
*   **Removal and offsite disposal:** This alternative provided the greatest overall protection but had the greatest short-term air quality and traffic impacts, the highest cost, and was least implementable due to funding needs and the requirement to replace excavated soil with clean fill for project construction. {{quote: "Removal and offsite disposal: Provided the greatest overall protection but had the greatest short-term air quality and traffic impacts, the highest cost, and was least implementable due to funding needs and the requirement to replace excavated soil with clean fill for project construction."}}
*   **Containment through project construction (Alternative #4):** This was the recommended alternative. {{quote: "Containment through project construction (Alternative #4): The recommended alternative."}}

A land use covenant would be recorded to restrict the property's use to non-residential purposes. {{quote: "A land use covenant would be recorded to restrict the property's use to non-residential purposes (e.g., precluding residences, schools, daycare centers, and hospitals)."}}

The Draft Final RAP was prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. and submitted on October 27, 2014. {{quote: "REVISED DRAFT FINAL REMEDIAL ACTION PLAN CALTRANS MODESTO SOIL STOCKPILES STATE ROUTE 132 WEST FREEWAY/EXPRESSWAY PROJECT MODESTO, STANISLAUS COUNTY, CALIFORNIA"}} The document was revised in response to DTSC comments dated September 18, 2014. {{quote: "This Draft Final RAP includes revisions made in response to comments on the Draft RAP provided by the DTSC in their letter to Caltrans dated September 18, 2014."}}

The Draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) with the attached Draft Final RAP was circulated for public review from January 18, 2017, to March 17, 2017. {{quote: "The Draft EIR/EA with attached Draft Final RAP was circulated for public review from January 18, 2017 to March 17, 2017."}} A public hearing was held on February 22, 2017. {{quote: "The Public Hearing on the Draft EIR/EA and attached Draft Final RAP was held on February 22, 2017."}}

The Final EIR/EA, dated March 12, 2018, included responses to comments received during the public review period. {{quote: "The Draft EIR/EA with attached Draft Final RAP was circulated for public review from January 18, 2017 through March 17, 2017. Responses to comments received are included in Appendix J – Comments and Responses."}}

## Regulatory status

The RAP was prepared in general accordance with DTSC's *Proven Technologies and Remedies Guidance, Remediation of Metals in Soil* (August 29, 2008). {{quote: "The RAP was prepared in general accordance with DTSC's *Proven Technologies and Remedies Guidance, Remediation of Metals in Soil* (August 29, 2008)."}} The 2007 and 2013 Risk Assessment Updates determined that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not present an unacceptable risk to human health. {{quote: "Based on stockpiles soil testing, the 2007 Risk Assessment and the 2013 Risk Assessment Update determined that the stockpiles, and collectively, as currently managed, do not present an unacceptable risk to human health."}} Groundwater beneath the stockpiles did not contain COPCs exceeding primary maximum contaminant levels (MCLs). {{quote: "Groundwater analysis resulted in the same conclusion."}} Surveys by the City of Modesto and the Department of Water Resources reported no municipal or domestic use of shallow groundwater within one mile of the stockpiles. {{quote: "According to the City of Modesto and a Department of Water Resources survey, there is no reported municipal or domestic use of shallow groundwater within one mile of the soil stockpiles."}}

The Final Feasibility Study received DTSC approval on June 30, 2014. {{quote: "Final Feasibility Study, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County, California (DTSC, June 30, 2014)."}} The final RAP was subject to DTSC approval for implementation. {{quote: "The final RAP was subject to DTSC approval for implementation."}}

## Open items

At the time of submittal, the Draft Final RAP was pending public comment, and the responsiveness summary had not yet been prepared. Final DTSC approval for implementation was contingent on public review and resolution of comments. The community acceptance criterion, a modifying criterion under the National Contingency Plan (NCP), was deferred for evaluation after the public comment period. {{quote: "The community acceptance criterion, a modifying criterion under the National Contingency Plan (NCP), was deferred as it was not evaluated in the Feasibility Study and was to be addressed after the public comment period."}} Review and concurrence of the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan and the Operation and Maintenance Plan by DTSC and CVRWQCB were required prior to implementing the recommended remedial action. {{quote: "Review and concurrence of the Remedial Design and Implementation Plan and the Operation and Maintenance Plan by DTSC and CVRWQCB were required prior to implementing the recommended remedial action."}}
