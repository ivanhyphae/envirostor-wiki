## Appendix J Comments and Responses

Appendix J of the Final [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) for the [[sr-132]] West Freeway/Expressway Project contains the compiled comments received during the public circulation period for the Draft EIR/EA and Draft Final [[remedial-action-plan]] (RAP). The public comment period for these documents ran from January 18, 2017, to March 17, 2017. A joint public hearing was held on February 22, 2017.

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), responded directly to comments pertaining to the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. Caltrans, as the Lead Agency for CEQA, agreed with DTSC's responses and incorporated them into Appendix J. This appendix also serves as DTSC's formal response to comments on the Draft Final RAP. A total of 305 comments were received, with 62 specifically addressing the Draft Final RAP. These comments were categorized as State Agency Comments, Local and County Agency Comments, Individual Comments (letters and emails), Public Hearing Comments (comment cards), and Public Hearing Transcript Comments. Eleven master responses were prepared to address similar comments submitted by multiple parties.

A compact disk (CD) containing all comments and responses, including those specific to the Draft Final RAP, was enclosed with the Response to Comments letter dated March 13, 2018. Comments and responses are also available online via Caltrans at <http://www.dot.ca.gov/d10/x-project-sr132west.html> and on the DTSC EnviroStor website.

### Site Record

During the public comment period, concerns were raised regarding the toxic waste soil stockpiles located within the Freeway right of way. One commenter specifically identified the stockpiles between Kansas and Elm as consisting of "toxic sludge drain off from 60 years of the FMC chemical plant" and listed contaminants including barium, arsenic, and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), along with petroleum hydrocarbons. The commenter provided details on the potential hazards associated with these substances, citing information from Wikipedia and Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS). For example, regarding barium, it was noted that "Soluble barium components are poisonous. Affects nervous system causing cardiac irregularities, tremors, weakness, anxiety, dyspnea (shortness of breath) and paralysis." {{quote: "Soluble barium components are poisonous. Affects nervous system causing cardiac irregularities, tremors, weakness, anxiety, dyspnea (shortness of breath) and paralysis."}} For arsenic, it was stated that "Arsenic and many of its compounds are especially potent poisons." {{quote: "Arsenic and many of its compounds are especially potent poisons."}} PAHs were described as "Highly carcinogenic" with potential impacts on child development and increased cancer risk. {{quote: "Highly carcinogenic. High prenatal exposure to PAH is associated with lower IQ and childhood asthma. PAH pollution during pregnancy – low birth rate, premature delivery, and heart malformations. Cord blood of exposed babies shows DNA damage linked to cancer. Increased behaviorial problems at ages six and eight."}}

Historical information from the BUREAU OF MINES 1961 YEAR, VOLUME 1 (1962) indicated that FMC Corp. began producing barium hydroxide monohydrate at its Modesto, California plant. {{quote: "FMC Corp. began producing barium hydroxide monohydrate at its Modesto, Calif. plant."}} Barium hydroxide was described as "corrosive and toxic" and capable of causing damage to organs including blood, kidneys, lungs, the nervous system, liver, and mucous membranes. {{quote: "It is corrosive and toxic... Causes damage to the following organs: blood, kidneys, lungs, the nervous system, liver, and mucous membranes. DANGER! Corrosive"}}

Comments also addressed the proposed remedial action alternatives. One comment noted that "levees would not achieve the same level of protection that Draft Final RAP Alternative 4, Containment provides." {{quote: "levees would not achieve the same level of protection that Draft Final RAP Alternative 4, Containment provides."}}

### Regulatory Status

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) acted as a responsible agency under CEQA, responding to comments pertaining to the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. Caltrans, as the lead agency, incorporated DTSC's responses into its own responses within Appendix J.

### Open Items

During the public comment period, questions were raised regarding changes to the original design of the project since 2014 and whether toxic waste soil stockpiles would be removed. {{quote: "Do you plan to remove the toxic waste soil stockpiles currently in the Freeway right of way?"}} One commenter requested that all their previous comments from the 2014 design consideration be brought forward and inserted into the record for the current environmental impact statement, asserting that their objections remained relevant absent any changes from the previous plan. {{quote: "I request that all of my comments from the previous design be brought forward and inserted into the record of comments regarding the current environmental impact statement. Absent any changes from the previous plan, my objections remain relevant. Please confirm that this request is being honored."}}
