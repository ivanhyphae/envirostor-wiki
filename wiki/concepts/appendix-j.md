## Overview
[[appendix-j]] is a component of the Final [[environmental-impact-report-environmental-assessment]] (EIR/EA) for the [[state-route-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. It compiles public comments received on the Draft EIR/EA and the Draft Final [[remedial-action-plan]] (RAP), along with the corresponding responses. The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), provided responses to comments specifically concerning the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. [[caltrans]], as the Lead Agency for CEQA, concurred with DTSC's responses and incorporated them into Appendix J.

## Site Record
Appendix J contains comments and responses related to the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] located within the freeway right-of-way. One commenter identified stockpiles between Kansas and Elm as consisting of "toxic sludge drain off from 60 years of the FMC chemical plant" and listed contaminants including [[barium]], arsenic, and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), along with petroleum hydrocarbons. {{quote: "Soil contaminated with barium, arsenic, and polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons and soil containing petroleum hydrocarbons."}}

Specific concerns were raised regarding the toxicity of these substances:
*   **Barium:** Described as having "Soluble barium components are poisonous. Affects nervous system causing cardiac irregularities, tremors, weakness, anxiety, dyspnea (shortness of breath) and paralysis." {{quote: "Soluble barium components are poisonous. Affects nervous system causing cardiac irregularities, tremors, weakness, anxiety, dyspnea (shortness of breath) and paralysis."}} Historical information from the BUREAU OF MINES 1961 YEAR, VOLUME 1 (1962) indicated that FMC Corp. began producing barium hydroxide monohydrate at its Modesto, California plant. {{quote: "FMC Corp. began producing barium hydroxide monohydrate at its Modesto, Calif. plant."}} Barium hydroxide was described as "corrosive and toxic" and capable of causing damage to organs including blood, kidneys, lungs, the nervous system, liver, and mucous membranes. {{quote: "It is corrosive and toxic... Causes damage to the following organs: blood, kidneys, lungs, the nervous system, liver, and mucous membranes. DANGER! Corrosive"}}
*   **Arsenic:** Stated as "Arsenic and many of its compounds are especially potent poisons." {{quote: "Arsenic and many of its compounds are especially potent poisons."}}
*   **Polynuclear Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs):** Described as "Highly carcinogenic. High prenatal exposure to PAH is associated with lower IQ and childhood asthma. PAH pollution during pregnancy – low birth rate, premature delivery, and heart malformations. Cord blood of exposed babies shows DNA damage linked to cancer. Increased behaviorial problems at ages six and eight." {{quote: "Highly carcinogenic. High prenatal exposure to PAH is associated with lower IQ and childhood asthma. PAH pollution during pregnancy – low birth rate, premature delivery, and heart malformations. Cord blood of exposed babies shows DNA damage linked to cancer. Increased behaviorial problems at ages six and eight."}} MSDS information also noted that "This product contains polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons some of which have produced cancer in laboratory animals and humans."
*   **Petroleum Hydrocarbons:** Also known as Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH), some compounds can affect the central nervous system, causing headaches and dizziness at high levels, or a nerve disorder called "peripheral neuropathy." Other TPH compounds can affect the blood, immune system, lungs, skin, and eyes.

Comments also addressed proposed remedial action alternatives, with one noting that "levees would not achieve the same level of protection that Draft Final RAP Alternative 4, Containment provides." {{quote: "levees would not achieve the same level of protection that Draft Final RAP Alternative 4, Containment provides."}}

The public comment period for the Draft EIR/EA and Draft Final RAP was from January 18, 2017, to March 17, 2017. A joint public hearing was held on February 22, 2017. A total of 305 comments were received, with 62 specifically addressing the Draft Final RAP. Comments were categorized as State Agency Comments, Local and County Agency Comments, Individual Comments (letters and emails), Public Hearing Comments (comment cards), and Public Hearing Transcript Comments. Eleven master responses were prepared to address similar comments. A compact disk (CD) containing all comments and responses was enclosed with the Response to Comments letter dated March 13, 2018.

## Regulatory Status
The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) acted as a responsible agency under CEQA, responding to comments pertaining to the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. [[caltrans]], as the lead agency, incorporated DTSC's responses into its own responses within Appendix J.

## Open Items
During the public comment period, questions were raised regarding changes to the original project design since 2014 and whether toxic waste soil stockpiles would be removed. {{quote: "Do you plan to remove the toxic waste soil stockpiles currently in the Freeway right of way?"}} One commenter requested that all previous comments from the 2014 design consideration be brought forward and inserted into the record for the current environmental impact statement, asserting that their objections remained relevant absent any changes from the previous plan. {{quote: "I request that all of my comments from the previous design be brought forward and inserted into the record of comments regarding the current environmental impact statement. Absent any changes from the previous plan, my objections remain relevant. Please confirm that this request is being honored."}}
