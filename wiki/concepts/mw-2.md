MW-2 is one of the eight original monitoring wells installed in 2006 within Stockpile 1/Stockpile 2 area and has been sampled at nearly every monitoring event from 2006 through at least April 2019. Across every round with a reported geochemical type, MW-2 has been calcium-dominant bicarbonate water, consistent with groundwater beneath the stockpiles rather than the sodium-dominant water associated with locations downgradient of the former FMC facility. This consistency is one of the geochemical lines of evidence used to argue that the stockpiles are not altering local groundwater chemistry.

## Geochemistry and Stiff diagrams

Stiff diagrams built from general minerals data (calcium, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate alkalinity, sulfate) were used at each event to classify each well's water type. MW-2 was calcium-dominant in October 2006, and remained calcium-dominant in every subsequent event reported in the record: March 2012, May 2012, September 2012, November 2012, January 2013, March 2013, June 2013, September 2013, December 2013, February 2014, June 2014, April 2015, April 2017, and April 2019. The June 2012 report notes that in 2006 the sample from MW-2 (along with MW-3 and MW-4) showed "a similar balance of calcium and sodium, suggesting a mix of these water types," but that by March 2012 MW-2 had become clearly calcium-dominant. Successive reports (e.g., the April 2015 and April 2017 conclusions) describe MW-2, together with MW-1, MW-4, and MW-7, as "consistently" calcium-dominant, in contrast to MW-3, MW-6, and MW-8, which were consistently sodium-dominant, and MW-5, which shifted between the two types over time. MW-2's stability distinguishes it from wells such as MW-5, MW-9, and MW-10, whose Stiff-diagram classification changed between sampling rounds.

## Nitrate and TDS

Nitrate as nitrogen and total dissolved solids (TDS) were reported for MW-2 at multiple events, but the available source excerpts more often give the range across all wells than the MW-2-specific value. Where MW-2 is separately identifiable:

- May 2012: general minerals summarized on Table 3; MW-2 was among wells with calcium-dominant water and was not named among the wells exceeding the nitrate primary MCL of 10 mg/L (MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, and MW-6 were named as exceeding or meeting it).
- Site-wide nitrate ranges are reported for most rounds (e.g., 2.6–24 mg/L in April 2017, "three of which" exceeding the MCL, among MW-1 through MW-4 and MW-7), but the individual MW-2 value is not distinguished from the range in the excerpted text for most events.
- April 2017: MW-1, MW-2, and MW-4 are named together as the wells whose nitrate and TDS exceeded, respectively, the primary MCL (10 mg/L) and secondary MCL (500 mg/L), among the wells sampled that round (MW-1 through MW-4, MW-7).
- April 2019: MW-1, MW-2, MW-5, and MW-6 are named as exceeding the nitrate primary MCL, and MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-5, and MW-6 as equaling or exceeding the TDS secondary MCL (500 mg/L).

Individual numeric nitrate/TDS values specific to MW-2 are not given in the excerpted material; only its inclusion in the list of exceeding wells is established for the April 2017 and April 2019 events.

## Dissolved metals

MW-2 was sampled for Title 22 dissolved metals (including thallium, manganese, barium, lead, strontium) at each event alongside the other wells. The March 2012 report notes that CVRWQCB split samples showed higher thallium concentrations at MW-2 (along with MW-4, MW-5, and MW-6) compared with the site's own samples, though at the same order of magnitude, and that thallium results were otherwise consistent between the 2006 and 2012 sampling despite different laboratories. Manganese exceedances were reported at other wells (e.g., MW-1 in April 2017, MW-5 in April 2019, MW-10 in February 2014) but MW-2 is not named among wells with manganese or other dissolved-metals exceedances in any of the excerpted conclusions sections. Barium, lead, and strontium were consistently reported at concentrations similar to historical levels and below water quality thresholds across the site, including at MW-2.

## Depth to groundwater and well status

MW-2 was purged and sampled using disposable bailers at multiple events (e.g., April 2015), with groundwater monitored in the field for pH, electrical conductivity, and temperature. By April 2015, groundwater flow at the site was toward the south at an average gradient of 0.004. As the water table declined at the site over time, MW-2 went dry at some later events: it is not listed among the wells sampled in October 2015 or May 2016 (when only MW-1 and MW-7, and in October 2015 also MW-2, had measurable water — MW-2 was specifically measured at 38.72 feet below TOC in October 2015, but by May 2016 MW-2 was among the wells reported dry or with insufficient water to sample). In October 2016, MW-2 was among the wells with measurable depth to groundwater (range 38.77 to 47.10 feet below TOC across MW-1, MW-2, MW-5, MW-6, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10), while MW-3, MW-4, and MW-8 were dry that round.

## Later monitoring and well destruction

MW-2 continued to be sampled through at least April 2019, consistently returning calcium-dominant water. A 2025 DTSC letter states that six of the original ten wells were decommissioned in 2019 following years of data supporting that no metals were leaching into groundwater from the stockpiles, and that a Well Destruction Report addressed the remaining four wells. The letter does not identify by well number which six were decommissioned in 2019 or which four remained, so it is not established in the record whether MW-2 was among the wells destroyed in 2019 or among the four addressed by the later Well Destruction Report.
