The Stockpile 2 BCS Foundation Spoil Workplan, a Technical Memorandum dated February 28, 2022, prepared by [[geocon-consultants-inc]] for WSP/City of Modesto, details the proposed management, disposal, and reuse of barium-containing soil (BCS) generated from foundation excavations for the State Route 132 Project. This BCS material originated from the southern portion of the [[stockpile-2-bcs-containment-zone]].

## Site Record and Proposed Management

BCS material was generated from two foundation excavations: the Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor (approximate Station 1138+60) and Overhead Sign #1 (approximate Station 1139+30). The former, excavated to approximately 4.5 feet deep in July/August 2021, yielded about 2 cubic yards of BCS material that was spread on adjacent ground. The latter is a planned 5-foot-diameter by 25-foot-deep excavation expected to generate approximately 18.2 cubic yards of BCS material. A conservative estimate of 40 cubic yards of BCS material from both foundations will be stockpiled prior to offsite landfill disposal or onsite reuse. The workplan proposes that the pre-moistened BCS material will be placed on and covered with minimum 6-millimeter-thick plastic sheeting, surrounded by erosion control measures, with the stockpile cover secured by ropes and weights.

## Sampling and Analysis

Eight discrete soil samples will be collected from the stockpile on a grid pattern at various depths. These samples will be composited into two 4-part composite samples for analysis of Title 22 metals using EPA Test Methods 6010B and 7471A (mercury), with a 48-hour turn-around time. Soluble metals testing may be required if total metal concentrations are elevated to determine if the BCS material is a California or Federal RCRA hazardous waste. The total [[barium]] and lead concentrations will be compared to site-specific background levels: barium at 120 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) and lead at 3.8 mg/kg.

## Regulatory Approval and Reuse Potential

If total barium and lead concentrations do not exceed these background levels, the stockpiled BCS may be suitable for onsite reuse, subject to approval by Caltrans and DTSC. If onsite reuse is not approved, the BCS material will be profiled for acceptance at Forward Landfill in Manteca or another accepting landfill. The management and disposal of BCS material are subject to Caltrans and DTSC approval.
