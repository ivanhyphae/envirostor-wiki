Title 22 MCLs — California Maximum Contaminant Levels defined under Title 22 of the California Code of Regulations — are the standard against which every groundwater and surface water sampling round at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles has been evaluated since regular monitoring resumed in March 2012. Across roughly ten years of quarterly and later annual groundwater reports, and separate surface water sampling events beginning in 2016, dissolved metals results were consistently compared to California primary MCLs, while total dissolved solids (TDS) was compared to the secondary MCL of 500 mg/l. The record shows that dissolved metals results at the monitoring wells almost never exceeded their numeric water quality thresholds, while TDS repeatedly exceeded the secondary MCL and nitrate repeatedly exceeded its primary MCL in several wells — establishing a consistent pattern in which the metals of greatest concern at the site (barium, lead, strontium) stayed well below drinking-water thresholds even as general minerals parameters did not.

## Dissolved metals results against Title 22 primary MCLs

Groundwater samples were analyzed for Title 22 dissolved metals (including strontium) by EPA Test Methods 6020/7470 at each monitoring event. The recurring finding, stated in nearly identical language in report after report, was that barium, lead, and strontium — the site's three chemicals of concern — were reported at concentrations "similar to historical levels" and "significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds":

| Monitoring event | Metals exceeding Title 22 MCLs |
|---|---|
| March–May 2012 | None |
| June 2012 | Manganese (well MW-10) |
| September 2012 | Manganese (wells MW-3 and MW-9) |
| November 2012 | Manganese (well MW-8) |
| January 2013 | Manganese (well MW-6) |
| December 2013 | Manganese (well MW-6) |
| February 2014 | Manganese (well MW-10) |
| June 2014 | None |
| April 2015 | None |
| May 2016 | None |

The only metal that recurred as an exceedance across multiple rounds was manganese, and it moved between different wells (MW-10, MW-3/MW-9, MW-8, MW-6) rather than persisting at any one location. No exceedance of lead, barium, or strontium against their Title 22 MCLs is recorded in any of these reports. Lead was frequently reported as non-detect or below the practical quantitation limit (e.g., not reported at or above the 1.0 µg/l PQL in the November 2012 and January 2013 rounds), and where detected (1.3 µg/l at MW-6 in December 2013) remained below its threshold.

## General minerals: TDS and nitrate against MCLs

Unlike the Title 22 metals, general minerals parameters showed a persistent, multi-well, multi-year pattern of secondary MCL exceedance:

| Monitoring event | TDS exceeding secondary MCL (500 mg/l) | Nitrate exceeding primary MCL |
|---|---|---|
| May 2012 | MW-4, MW-5, MW-6 | — |
| June 2012 | MW-9, MW-10 | MW-9 |
| September 2012 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | (exception noted, wells not listed) |
| January 2013 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | (exception noted) |
| December 2013 | MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10 | MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10 |
| February 2014 | MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 |
| June 2014 | MW-1, MW-4, MW-5, MW-6, MW-10 | MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10 |
| April 2015 | MW-1, MW-4 | MW-1, MW-2 |
| May 2016 | MW-7 (510 mg/l) | None |

TDS exceedances against the secondary MCL were reported in nearly every monitoring round from 2012 through 2016, most persistently at wells MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10, and nitrate exceedances against the primary MCL recurred at the same subset of wells (MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, MW-9, MW-10) through 2014. The pattern narrowed by 2015–2016: April 2015 showed nitrate exceedances only at MW-1 and MW-2, and May 2016 showed only a single, marginal TDS exceedance at MW-7 (510 mg/l, just above the 500 mg/l threshold) with no nitrate exceedance at all. None of the groundwater reports describe a regulatory consequence tied specifically to these TDS or nitrate exceedances; the reports consistently frame them as general minerals results distinguished from the Title 22 metals findings.

## Surface water sampling against Title 22 MCLs

Surface water sampling events (October and December 2016) applied the same Title 22 dissolved metals framework to stormwater runoff from the stockpiles. The reports for both events conclude that stormwater falling on the stockpiles was retained within the site/right-of-way boundary and did not migrate offsite, based on site reconnaissance rather than exceedance findings; the specific analytical values for these events are not given in the available material.

## Soil: STLC thresholds distinct from MCLs

Distinct from the drinking-water MCLs applied to groundwater and surface water, Title 22 CCR also establishes the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) used to evaluate soil for hazardous waste characterization. Of 33 stockpile soil samples analyzed for WET soluble barium, concentrations ranged from 39 to 2,300 mg/l, with 28 samples exceeding the STLC of 100 mg/l; DI-WET soluble barium ranged from 1.8 to 220 mg/l, with 9 samples exceeding the STLC. The 2007 Shaw evaluation and the Final FS Report both note that because the barium at the site is present primarily as barite (barium sulfate) rather than more soluble non-barite compounds, the Title 22 STLC evaluation criteria "are not strictly applicable to the Site" — meaning the STLC exceedance count does not, by the source documents' own framing, directly establish the soil as hazardous waste under the barium-specific standard. Separately, only two stockpile soil samples exceeded the 50 mg/kg total lead threshold that triggers WET soluble testing (at 150 and 1,500 mg/kg); WET soluble lead in those two samples was 2.9 and 5.7 mg/l, and DI-WET soluble lead 0.07 and 0.1 mg/l — in each case below levels that would drive hazardous characterization.

## Significance for site closure and land use

The consistent finding that dissolved barium, lead, and strontium in groundwater remained below their Title 22 primary MCLs across all monitoring events through 2016 has been the basis for the recurring conclusion, in each Geocon groundwater report, that these chemicals of concern do not exceed numeric water quality thresholds at the site. This distinguishes the site's groundwater condition from its soil condition: the stockpiled soil itself required a land use covenant (LUC) under Title 22, Division 4.5, Chapter 39, Section 67391.1 CCR because soil contaminant concentrations in Stockpiles 2 and 3 exceeded residential screening levels, restricting the site to transportation use and barring unrestricted uses such as residential, school, or hospital development — a soil-based, risk-screening-level restriction that is separate from, and not resolved by, the groundwater MCL compliance record described above.
