## Dissolved Metals Title 22

This article addresses dissolved metals in groundwater at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation site (SR 132, Stanislaus County) and their evaluation against California Title 22 drinking water standards.

### Overview
“Dissolved Metals Title 22” refers to the regulatory comparison of dissolved-phase metals concentrations in site groundwater to the maximum contaminant levels (MCLs) established under California Code of Regulations Title 22, Division 4, Chapter 15. At the Modesto Stockpiles, dissolved metals are a primary concern because leachate from soil stockpiles can mobilize metals into the underlying aquifer. Groundwater monitoring reports document dissolved metals data and explicitly compare results to Title 22 MCLs to determine compliance and guide remedial decisions.

### Site Record
The project record contains two key groundwater monitoring reports that present dissolved metals results:

- **S9525-01-44B Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles GW March 2013** (dated March 2013) – provides early baseline dissolved metals data from monitoring wells, including locations such as MW‑5 and others.
- **S1200-01-01 Modesto Stockpiles GW April 2017** (dated April 2017) – updates dissolved metals concentrations and includes trend analysis relative to Title 22 thresholds.

Both reports include analytical results for metals such as arsenic, chromium, nickel, and lead in the dissolved phase, measured in micrograms per liter (µg/L). The April 2017 report contains a table comparing each analyte to its Title 22 MCL. Specific concentration values and exceedances (if any) are detailed within those documents; the prompt does not provide further numerical data.

The spatial distribution of dissolved metals is mapped in the reports, showing concentrations by well location. Any exceedances above Title 22 MCLs are noted in the text and data tables of each report.

### Regulatory Status
The applicable standards are the Title 22 MCLs enforced by the California State Water Resources Control Board and the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). The project’s groundwater monitoring program is conducted under DTSC oversight. Compliance is evaluated by comparing dissolved metals concentrations to the MCLs. The April 2017 report indicates whether each well exceeded the MCLs at that sampling event. The project’s overall compliance status, including any ongoing exceedances or corrective actions, is documented in the monitoring reports and associated regulatory correspondence.
