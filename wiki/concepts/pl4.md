Pl4 is one of five stormwater sample locations (PL1 through PL5) established at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, sampled on an on-call basis during qualifying rain events from April 2013 onward. It sits at the drainage basin outfall adjacent to Stockpile 3, northeast of the SR 99 off-ramp to Kansas Avenue, at the point where stormwater enters the basin; Caltrans reported that water in the basin originates exclusively from drainage inlets in the depressed section of SR 99 north and south of Kansas Avenue, meaning PL4 receives highway runoff rather than water that has necessarily contacted the stockpiled soil directly. PL4 is distinct from [[pl5]], located within three feet of the basin's gate valve to the SR 99 storm drain system, which Caltrans reported has not been opened in the recent past.

## Sampling history and field conditions

PL4 was sampled in every event reviewed for this article: October 2016, December 2016, March 2017, January 2018, and March 2018. Unlike PL5 and the background locations BG1/BG2, which were repeatedly reported as dry or containing insufficient water to sample (e.g., PL5 in December 2016, March 2018, and January 2018; BG2 in March 2017 and March 2018), PL4 consistently held enough water to sample across all five events, reflecting its position as a basin low point receiving highway drainage.

## Manganese exceedances

PL4 is the location with the most consistent record of manganese exceeding the secondary MCL of 50 µg/l across the reviewed events:

| Date | Manganese exceedance at PL4 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 2016 | not reported as exceeding | dilution performed for other locations/analytes |
| Dec 2016 | exceeded secondary MCL (50 µg/l) | PL3 also exceeded; PL1/PL2/BG2 did not |
| Mar 2017 | exceeded secondary MCL | reported for PL2 and PL4 only |
| Jan 2018 | not specified as exceeding in text | — |
| Mar 2018 | exceeded secondary MCL, range 54–370 µg/l across PL2–PL4 and BG1 | PL4 among the exceeding locations |

Manganese exceedances at PL4 recurred across at least three of the five reviewed events (December 2016, March 2017, and March 2018), making it, alongside PL3, one of the two most consistently affected locations for this analyte. Because manganese also exceeded the secondary MCL in background location BG1 in December 2016 and March 2018, Geocon's reporting did not treat the PL4 manganese exceedances as evidence of stockpile-related contamination; the secondary MCL is a taste-and-odor/welfare-based standard rather than a health-based primary standard.

## Other metals and dissolved metals detections

Across the sampling events, PL4 consistently returned detections of barium, calcium, chromium, copper, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, vanadium, and zinc, along with intermittent detections of antimony, arsenic, cadmium (March 2017), and lead (March 2018, at 46 µg/l at PL3 rather than PL4). None of these other reported concentrations at PL4 were described as exceeding primary or secondary MCLs in the reviewed reports. Beryllium, mercury, selenium, silver, and thallium were consistently not detected above method detection limits at PL4 across the events reviewed.

Laboratory QA/QC notes across multiple events record that PL4 samples required dilution for several analytes because of high target-analyte concentrations, failing internal standards, or matrix interference — for example, dilution for lead and thallium in March 2017, and for metals generally in March 2018 — but these are laboratory processing notes rather than reported exceedances.

## Significance for the site's runoff-containment narrative

Across all five reviewed events, Geocon's surface water management sections state that observations during sampling and site reconnaissance did not identify stormwater migrating offsite from the Caltrans right-of-way, and that runoff appeared to remain contained. PL4's location — receiving SR 99 highway drainage into the basin adjacent to Stockpile 3, rather than direct pile runoff — combined with the absence of primary MCL exceedances there, supported this containment conclusion in each report. The one primary MCL exceedance recorded in the March 2018 event (lead at 46 µg/l) occurred at PL3, outside the right-of-way, not at PL4, and Geocon's report characterized the March 2018 results overall as otherwise consistent with prior events. The DTSC's 2026 letter approving the FY24/25 Storm Water and Surface Water Monitoring Report states that results were all within reporting limits, consistent with PL4's individual record of no primary MCL exceedances across the reviewed sampling rounds.
