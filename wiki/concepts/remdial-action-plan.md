## Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles

### Overview

The Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, associated with the State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Modesto, California, outlines the selected cleanup strategy for soil containing metals. The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) approved this RAP. The primary objective of the RAP is to ensure long-term protection of human health and the environment by preventing exposure to contaminants and minimizing surface water infiltration into groundwater beneath the stockpiles.

### Site Record

The RAP addresses three soil stockpiles totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards. These stockpiles are located within the state right-of-way south of Kansans Avenue, specifically between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99. The soil contains metals, primarily [[barium]], [[strontium]], and [[lead]].

The selected cleanup alternative detailed in the RAP is containment of the stockpiles during the construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. This containment will be achieved through the construction of retaining walls, bridge abutments, and by placing the stockpiles beneath the roadway. Unpaved areas will be covered with clean fill.

The RAP mandates long-term management and monitoring. This includes annual inspections of the containment features (pavement and other elements) and annual groundwater monitoring to ensure the integrity of the remedy. Land use controls will also be implemented.

Caltrans, as the lead agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) for the project. DTSC reviewed this documentation and conducted an independent analysis. DTSC determined that the containment remedy, with the implementation of project controls (mitigation measures identified in the Final EIR/EA), will not have a significant adverse effect on the environment. DTSC prepared a Statement of Findings and a Notice of Determination (NOD) in compliance with CEQA.

### Regulatory Status

The DTSC has approved the RAP, signifying their concurrence with the selected remedy. This approval is contingent upon the implementation of the containment measures and the specified long-term management and monitoring activities.

### Open Items

The RAP requires ongoing implementation of containment, land use controls, annual inspections, and annual groundwater monitoring to ensure the long-term effectiveness of the remedy.
