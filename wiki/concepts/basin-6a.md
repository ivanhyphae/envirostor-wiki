## Overview

**Basin 6a** is one of the engineered stormwater retention/infiltration basins at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation site (State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California). It was constructed as part of the site’s stormwater management system to contain and treat runoff from the remediated soil stockpile areas. Basin 6a is specifically referenced in the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) Final Soil Management Plan (SMP) Approval E-mail as a location requiring adherence to post-remediation maintenance and monitoring conditions.

Per the *Final SMP Approval E-mail* (dated August 27, 2021, from DTSC to Caltrans), Basin 6a is subject to ongoing inspection and maintenance obligations to ensure it does not become a conduit for contaminated sediment or stormwater migration. The basin’s role is to capture surface water runoff from the northern portion of the site, which historically held stockpiled soil containing arsenic, lead, and other metals above background levels.

## Site Record

- **Document Source:** DTSC’s *Final SMP Approval E-mail*, dated August 27, 2021 (subject: “RE: Final Remedial Action Completion Report and Soil Management Plan, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 132”).
- **Key References in E-mail:** The e-mail states: “Basin 6a shall be maintained in accordance with the SMP’s stormwater control measures, including annual sediment depth inspections and removal if sediment accumulation exceeds 6 inches in depth.”
- **Construction Details:** Basin 6a was constructed with an impermeable liner (0.5 mm HDPE) over a compacted clay subgrade, as specified in the site’s 2018 *Final Remedial Design Report*. The basin’s design capacity is 0.85 acre-feet, with a maximum water depth of 3 feet.
- **Verification Data:**
  - Post-construction soil sampling at Basin 6a’s base (January 2020) showed arsenic at 4.2 mg/kg, lead at 12.5 mg/kg, and barium at 95 mg/kg — all below agricultural screening levels.
  - Basin sidewall soil samples collected adjacent to the basin (November 2020) detected arsenic at 6.8 mg/kg and lead at 18.7 mg/kg, within the site’s established background range (arsenic 2.1–7.9 mg/kg, lead 11–34 mg/kg).
- **Current Status (per SMP):** Basin 6a is listed as “Inspection-Only,” meaning no active remediation is required unless maintenance thresholds are exceeded. The basin is subject to quarterly visual inspections and annual sediment depth surveys.

## Regulatory Status

- **Applicable Standards:**
  - Sediment depth limit: 6 inches (established by DTSC in the *Final SMP Approval E-mail*).
  - Soil screening levels for the site: DTSC’s *Human and Ecological Risk Assessment* (2017) background thresholds for arsenic (≤7.9 mg/kg) and lead (≤34 mg/kg).
  - Stormwater discharge: The basin’s overflow is subject to Basin Plan water quality standards per the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB).
- **Agency Oversight:** DTSC is the lead regulatory agency for soil contamination issues; RWQCB has concurrent authority over potential stormwater discharges to surface water (e.g., adjacent agricultural drains).
- **Compliance:** Basin 6a is currently in compliance with its SMP conditions. No exceedances of sediment depth or contamination thresholds have been reported since the final inspection of October 2021. The basin remains in the site’s ongoing operations and maintenance (O&M) program.

## Open Items

- **Annual Sediment Depth Survey:** The next mandatory survey is due by November 2024. Results must be submitted to DTSC within 30 days of completion per the SMP.
- **Vegetation Management:** The *Final SMP Approval E-mail* notes that Basin 6a’s sidewalls must be kept free of woody vegetation that could compromise the liner; no inspection report since February 2023 has documented compliance with this condition. DTSC may request photographic evidence during next review.
- **Liner Integrity Testing:** While not explicitly required for Basin 6a, adjacent basins (6b and 6c) are scheduled for 5-year liner integrity testing in 2026. The SMP does not mandate analogous testing for Basin 6a, but DTSC has discretion to require it based on inspection findings.
