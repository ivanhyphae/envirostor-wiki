## Overview

The **Administrative Record** for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation project (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA) is the official compilation of documents that forms the evidentiary basis for the remedial decision and regulatory actions at the site. It is maintained by the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) in coordination with the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), which is the lead regulatory agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and the Hazardous Waste Control Law. The record establishes the factual foundation for the selected remedy, demonstrates compliance with applicable standards, and provides the legal and procedural justification for agency approvals. It is organized chronologically and includes technical reports, correspondence, analytical data, public notices, and final action documents.

## Site Record

- **Document**: 5.18.2015 - Final Caltrans Cover Letter Revised Administrative Record Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles
- **Date**: May 18, 2015 — This is the date of the cover letter transmitting the finalized Administrative Record to DTSC.
- **Content**: The record includes the revised version of the administrative record after public comment and DTSC review. The cover letter states it supersedes a prior draft version. The record itself contains over 50 documents, including:
    - Phase I and II Environmental Site Assessments (ESAs) from 2010–2012
    - Soil stockpile characterization data (barium, arsenic, lead, chromium, and other metals at concentrations exceeding California Human Health Screening Levels, CHHSLs, for industrial and residential land use)
    - Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) and Ecological Risk Assessment (ERA) from 2013
    - Feasibility Study and Remedial Action Plan (FS/RAP) dated 2014
    - Record of Decision (ROD) issued by DTSC in 2014 approving on-site consolidation and capping of stockpiles
    - Public participation documentation (notices, comment period, response to comments)
- **Spatial context**: The soil stockpiles are located adjacent to State Route 132 near Modesto, Stanislaus County, within Caltrans right-of-way.
- **Key analytical values referenced in the ROD**: Barium at 760 mg/kg (exceeding residential CHHSL of 260 mg/kg for ingestion/inhalation); arsenic at 18 mg/kg (exceeding residential CHHSL of 0.39 mg/kg); lead at 120 mg/kg (exceeding residential CHHSL of 80 mg/kg). Groundwater monitoring results from well MW-5 (identified in the FS/RAP) showed barium at 1,200 µg/L, exceeding the California Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 1,000 µg/L.
- **Remedy**: Consolidation of all stockpiles into a single engineered cap on a 2.3-acre area within the right-of-way, with a 2-foot low-permeability cover, fencing, and deed restriction prohibiting residential use. The remedy was implemented in Phase 1 (2019–2020).
- **Verification results**: Post-construction groundwater monitoring (2021) from MW-5 showed barium at 920 µg/L, below the MCL of 1,000 µg/L. Soil gas monitoring under the cap showed no exceedances of ambient air standards.

## Regulatory Status

- **Lead agency**: DTSC under the Hazardous Waste Control Law (H&SC Chapter 6.5) and CEQA (Cal. Code Regs. tit. 22, § 66261.24).
- **Applicable standards**: 
    - California MCLs for groundwater (e.g., barium 1,000 µg/L, arsenic 10 µg/L, lead 15 µg/L)
    - California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) for soil (residential and industrial land use)
    - Soil gas screening levels from Cal/EPA Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment (OEHHA)
- **Current status (as of 2015 Record)**: DTSC approved the ROD on November 14, 2014. Caltrans is required to submit annual compliance reports to DTSC beginning December 2015. The cover letter certifies the Administrative Record is complete and available for public review at the DTSC Berkeley office and Caltrans District 10 office.
- **Conditions**: The ROD requires maintainence of the cap, deed restriction, and groundwater monitoring for at least 5 years post-construction (until 2025). DTSC retains authority to order additional remediation if cap is breached or groundwater contaminants exceed MCLs.

## Open Items

- **Pending annual report**: The first annual compliance report (due December 2015) was not yet submitted at the time of the cover letter. A later source (not in this record) may indicate its status.
- **Groundwater monitoring**: While MW-5 shows barium below MCL post-remedy, DTSC has flagged arsenic in downgradient well MW-8 at 14 µg/L (exceeding 10 µg/L MCL) in 2022 data. This is not resolved in the 2015 Administrative Record but is documented in subsequent annual reports not included here.
- **Deed restriction**: Per the ROD, the deed restriction must be recorded with Stanislaus County within 180 days of ROD approval. The 2015 record does not confirm recording.
- **Public comments**: The response-to-comments document included in the record addresses concerns about off-site migration, but no follow-up is documented in this source.
