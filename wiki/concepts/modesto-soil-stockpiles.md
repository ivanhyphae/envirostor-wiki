The Modesto Soil Stockpiles are approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil generated during the construction of the State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Modesto, California. These stockpiles, containing metals primarily barium, strontium, and lead, were managed through a containment remedy approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). The containment involved placing the soil behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway, with unpaved areas covered by clean fill to prevent exposure and minimize surface water infiltration.

### Site Description and Containment

The stockpiles were located south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange in Stanislaus County, occupying three areas within the state right-of-way: between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99. The containment remedy includes long-term land use controls and annual inspections of containment features, along with groundwater monitoring.

### Regulatory Approvals and Deliverables

The California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and a Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the project. The EIR/EA was certified on March 2, 2018. DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, approved the project on March 14, 2018, determining it would not have a significant environmental effect, with mitigation measures as a condition of approval. The project is documented under State Clearinghouse Number 2010012010.

Geocon Consultants, Inc. prepared a Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) Workplan in September 2012, addressing comments from DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB). This workplan outlined procedures for a supplemental site investigation, with results to be presented in separate reports on contamination extent and human health risk assessment. The finalized SSI Report was to be uploaded to the EnviroStor and Caltrans project websites.

DTSC accepted the Draft Interim Removal Action Completion Report (Draft Phase 1 RACR) on February 24, 2022. This report described consolidation and capping activities for barium-containing soils (BCS) as part of the Interim Phase 1 Construction effort for the SR 132 West project. Stockpile 1 had an estimated volume of 34,000 cubic yards (yd3), Stockpile 2 had an estimated volume of 102,000 yd3, and Stockpile 3 had an estimated volume of 24,000 yd3. Excavation of Stockpile 3 and portions of Stockpile 2 involved approximately 27,675 yd3 of soil transported and placed within the BCS Stockpile 1 containment zone. Confirmation samples from the Stockpile 3 excavation area indicated barium concentrations above the cleanup goal of 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg) in three areas, which were subsequently deepened and resampled.

DTSC accepted the Removal Action Completion Report (RACR) for the Interim Phase 1 effort on January 18, 2023. This acceptance followed the approval of the RAP, Remedial Design and Implementation Plan (RDIP), and two variance requests to the RDIP. The RACR included As-Built Plans, cross-section figures, final survey data, and the final Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Plan. DTSC indicated that a draft O&M Agreement and a Land Use Covenant (LUC) for the remediated area would be prepared for Caltrans' review. Final certification of the RAP will occur after the Ultimate Phase 2 SR 132 build-out.

### Site Inspections and Monitoring

Caltrans maintains an O&M Site Inspection Form for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles. Annual inspections assess containment features such as retaining walls, pavement, drainage structures, and slopes of the stockpiles. Photographs document conditions, including the north slope of Stockpile #2, drainage structures on the north slope of Stockpile #2, retaining walls for Stockpile #2, pave surface conditions for Stockpile #1, and the north slope of Stockpile #1.
