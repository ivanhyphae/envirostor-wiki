## Modesto Soil Stockpiles

### Overview
The Modesto Soil Stockpiles refer to approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, primarily barium, strontium, and lead, generated during the construction of the State Route (SR) 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project in Modesto, California. These stockpiles were managed through a containment remedy approved by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). The containment strategy involved placing the stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway, with unpaved portions covered by clean fill. This approach was designed to prevent exposure and minimize surface water infiltration into groundwater.

### Site Record
The stockpiles are located south of the SR 99/Kansas Avenue Interchange in Stanislaus County. Specifically, they occupied three areas within the state right-of-way: between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99, and east of SR 99. The containment remedy includes long-term land use controls and annual inspections of the pavement and other containment features, along with groundwater monitoring, to ensure the remedy's integrity.

### Regulatory Status
The California Department of Transportation (Caltrans), acting as the Lead Agency under the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA), prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and a Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the project. Caltrans certified the EIR/EA on March 2, 2018. The DTSC, as a CEQA Responsible Agency, utilized the EIR/EA for the RAP's CEQA analysis. On March 14, 2018, DTSC approved the project, determining that it would not have a significant effect on the environment. Mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval. A Statement of Overriding Considerations was not adopted for this project by DTSC.

The project is documented in a Notice of Determination filed with the Office of Planning and Research, State Clearinghouse Number 2010012010. Information regarding the project is available through the DTSC's EnviroStor database, with associated global IDs 60001626 and 50280024.
