# MW-10

## Overview
MW-10 is a groundwater monitoring well located south of Kansas Avenue and east of State Route 99, within the Modesto Soil Stockpiles project area in Stanislaus County, California. The well was installed as part of an expanded monitoring network to characterize groundwater conditions associated with the soil stockpiles that Caltrans generated during construction of a segment of SR 99 in the early 1960s. The stockpile materials include excess native soils and pond tailings from a historic evaporation pond previously owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC).

## Site record
MW-10 was installed in June 2012 alongside well mw-9|MW-9, and its installation is documented in the "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California," prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012. MW-9 was located south of Kansas Avenue and west of SR 99, while MW-10 was placed south of Kansas Avenue and east of SR 99.

The primary chemicals of concern for the site are barium, lead, and strontium, stemming from FMC's historical chemical manufacturing operations, which involved processing barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores and other minerals. The source documents confirm that the June 2012 report discusses the installation of both MW-9 and MW-10, along with groundwater monitoring results for May, June, and July 2012. However, the available record does not provide well-specific construction details (depth, screened interval, diameter), lithology encountered, or individual analytical results for MW-10. The November 29, 2012, DTSC letter acknowledges receipt and review of the three groundwater monitoring reports but does not break out data by well.

## Regulatory status
The dtsc|Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region, reviewed the three 2012 groundwater monitoring reports and, by letter dated November 29, 2012, concurred with the reports as submitted. The concurrence was signed by randy-s-adams|Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist in the Brownfields and Environmental Restoration Program. DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as data becomes available.

No regulatory thresholds, exceedances, or compliance determinations specific to MW-10 are documented in the available source material.

## Open items
- Future groundwater monitoring reports are to be submitted as data becomes available, per DTSC's November 29, 2012, concurrence letter. The source record does not indicate whether subsequent monitoring reports containing MW-10 data have been submitted or reviewed.
