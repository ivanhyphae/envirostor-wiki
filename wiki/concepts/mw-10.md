MW-10 is one of two monitoring wells (with [[mw-9]]) installed in June 2012 north of the three FMC-derived soil stockpiles, adjacent to SR 99, to establish upgradient groundwater conditions at the site. Its record over more than a decade of monitoring shows a well that never exceeded a dissolved-metals drinking water threshold except for manganese on a few occasions, but that consistently ran higher than most other site wells in nitrate and total dissolved solids (TDS), and whose water type shifted between sodium- and calcium-dominant character in its first months before settling calcium-dominant. MW-10 stopped producing water by 2019 and was ultimately decommissioned as part of Caltrans's stockpile encapsulation construction.

## Installation and construction

MW-10 was installed by Geocon on May 29–30, 2012, using hollow-stem auger drilling, at a location north of the stockpiles and adjacent to SR 99's east side (MW-9 was installed at the same time on the west side). The well was constructed to approximately 40 feet with 2-inch Schedule 40 PVC casing, a 10-foot 0.010-inch slotted screen from about 29.5 to 39.5 feet, and a #2/12 Monterey sand filter pack from 27.5 feet to the boring bottom. Soil at the MW-10 location was interbedded silt and sand of the Modesto Formation, with some clay noted at 30 feet; groundwater was first encountered at 35.4 feet. The well was developed on June 18, 2012, and first sampled June 20, 2012.

## Dissolved metals

DTSC identified barium, lead, and strontium as the site's primary chemicals of concern. Across the monitoring history, MW-10's results for these three analytes were consistently below their numeric drinking water thresholds. Barium and strontium tracked at levels similar to other wells and well below the EPA Drinking Water Health Advisory levels shown on the site's time-series plots (1,000 µg/L for barium; 4,000 µg/L for strontium). Lead was frequently not detected above the practical quantitation limit; where reported, values were low (for example, MW-10 was not among the wells reporting lead above 1.0 µg/L in most rounds).

Manganese was the one dissolved metal that repeatedly triggered exceedances at MW-10. It was flagged as an exceedance of its numeric threshold (taste-and-odor secondary MCL) in the June 2012 report (the only such occurrence among all site wells that round), again in the September 2012 round (along with MW-3 and MW-9), and again in February 2014. Other minor detections at MW-10 not exceeding thresholds included cobalt, copper, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, and zinc at various rounds.

## Nitrate and TDS

MW-10 was one of the wells most frequently flagged for nitrate (as nitrogen) and TDS exceedances of their respective primary and secondary MCLs (10 mg/L and 500 mg/L), a pattern shared with MW-5, MW-6, and (for nitrate) MW-1, the site's upgradient well used as a natural-background reference.

This is a materially different profile from MW-9, which was not consistently named among the nitrate/TDS-exceeding wells despite being installed under the same program and screened in the same shallow aquifer zone. The persistent TDS and nitrate exceedances at MW-10, sustained across roughly two years of bi-monthly and then quarterly sampling, distinguish it from most of the other wells and indicate a location within a zone of naturally higher mineralization rather than an isolated event.

## Geochemical character (Stiff diagrams)

MW-10's general mineral chemistry showed more variability early in the monitoring program than most other wells. In June 2012, MW-10 was reported as sodium-dominant, in contrast to MW-9 (calcium-dominant) sampled the same day. By July and September 2012 the well had shifted to calcium-dominant, and it remained calcium-dominant through November 2012, January 2013, March 2013, June 2013, September 2013, December 2013, February 2014, and June 2014. Site reports repeatedly noted this June-2012-to-July-2012 shift as one of the few instances of a well changing dominant water type during the monitoring program (the other being MW-5, which shifted from calcium- to sodium-dominant in 2013).

## Later monitoring and decommissioning

By October 2018, MW-10 could not be located beneath brush and weeds during the site visit. By April 2019 the well was dry, along with MW-9, and no sample could be collected. The April 2019 report's Groundwater Elevation and Ionic Composition Map records MW-10 as "NA" (not analyzed due to insufficient water) for that round, consistent with the broader trend of decreasing water availability across the shallow wells as the encapsulation-related regrading proceeded.

DTSC's January 2026 letter response confirms that six of the ten original monitoring wells were decommissioned in 2019 to allow continued construction of the Caltrans highway, and that a 2025 Well Destruction Report addressed destruction of the remaining four wells. The source material does not specify by name which four wells remained active through 2025 versus which six were decommissioned in 2019; MW-10's status within that later well-destruction sequence is not stated in the available material.
