# MW-10

## Overview
MW-10 is a groundwater monitoring well located within the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] area in Stanislaus County, California. It was installed as part of an expanded monitoring network to characterize groundwater conditions associated with soil stockpiles generated by [[caltrans]] during the construction of SR 99. These stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings from a historic evaporation pond formerly owned by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC).

## Site record
MW-10 was installed in June 2012, south of Kansas Avenue and east of State Route 99, alongside well [[mw-9]]. Its installation is documented in the "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California," prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012.

The primary chemicals of concern at the site are [[barium]], lead, and strontium, originating from FMC's historical chemical manufacturing operations.

In June 2014, groundwater samples were collected from MW-10. The Stiff diagram for MW-10 indicated a calcium-dominant composition. Nitrate as nitrogen was reported at a concentration of 29 mg/l, which equals or exceeds the primary MCL for nitrate of 10 mg/l. Total dissolved solids (TDS) were reported at a concentration of 680 mg/l, exceeding the secondary MCL for TDS of 500 mg/l. Sulfide was reported at a concentration of 0.023 mg/l.

## Regulatory status
The dtsc|Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region, reviewed the groundwater monitoring reports for May, June, and July 2012. By letter dated November 29, 2012, DTSC concurred with these reports. DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as data becomes available.

The June 2014 groundwater sampling event indicated that nitrate concentrations in MW-10 equaled or exceeded the primary MCL for nitrate (10 mg/l), and TDS concentrations exceeded the secondary MCL for TDS (500 mg/l). Barium and strontium concentrations in MW-10 were reported at concentrations similar to historical levels and remained significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds.

## Open items
Future groundwater monitoring reports are to be submitted as data becomes available, as per DTSC's November 29, 2012, concurrence letter. The available source material does not indicate whether subsequent monitoring reports containing MW-10 data have been submitted or reviewed.
