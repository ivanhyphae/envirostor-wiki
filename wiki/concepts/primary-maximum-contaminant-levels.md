The primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) is a regulatory standard for drinking water quality. In the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project, primary MCLs are used as benchmarks to evaluate the safety of surface water samples.

## Site record

In December 2015, stormwater sampling was conducted at locations PL1 through PL4. Analytical results for dissolved metals were reported, including antimony, barium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, manganese, magnesium, molybdenum, potassium, sodium, strontium, vanadium, and zinc. Arsenic was reported for sample PL3, and lead was reported for sample PL4. None of the reported concentrations for these analytes exceeded their respective primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs). Manganese concentrations in samples PL2, PL3, and PL4 exceeded the secondary MCLs, which are based on taste and odor and welfare considerations. Beryllium, cadmium, mercury, selenium, silver, and thallium were not detected at concentrations equal to or greater than their practical quantitation limits (PQLs) in samples PL1 through PL4. The analytical results were consistent with previous findings at each sampling location.

## Regulatory status

Primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) are established as regulatory standards for drinking water quality. The project record indicates that concentrations of various dissolved metals, including antimony, barium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, manganese, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, vanadium, zinc, arsenic, and lead, were evaluated against their respective primary MCLs. The findings from the December 11, 2015, stormwater sampling report indicated that none of the reported concentrations exceeded these primary MCLs.
