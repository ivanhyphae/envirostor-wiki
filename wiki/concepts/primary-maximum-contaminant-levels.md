The primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) is a regulatory standard for drinking water quality. In the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project, primary MCLs are used as benchmarks to evaluate the safety of surface water and groundwater samples.

## Site record

In the May 2012 groundwater monitoring report, primary MCLs were used as a threshold for comparison with analytical results. For the samples collected in March 2012, concentrations of arsenic, barium, chromium, vanadium, and strontium were reported, but none exceeded their respective primary MCLs. Other dissolved metals, including antimony, cobalt, copper, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, and selenium, were also reported, and their concentrations did not exceed their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water.

In the November 2012 groundwater monitoring report, analytical results for dissolved metals were compared to primary MCLs. Concentrations of arsenic, barium, chromium, strontium, and vanadium were reported, but none exceeded their respective primary MCLs. Similarly, concentrations of cobalt, copper, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, and selenium were reported, and none exceeded their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water, with the exception of manganese in the sample from MW-8.

In the January 2013 groundwater monitoring report, dissolved metals concentrations were compared to primary MCLs. Concentrations of arsenic, barium, chromium, strontium, and vanadium were reported, but none exceeded their respective primary MCLs. Cobalt, copper, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, and zinc were also reported, and their concentrations did not exceed their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water, with the exception of manganese in the sample from MW-6.

In the January 6, 2016, stormwater sampling report, analytical results for dissolved metals were compared to primary MCLs. For samples PL1 through PL5, barium, calcium, chromium, copper, magnesium, potassium, sodium, and strontium were reported, and none of these concentrations exceeded their respective primary MCLs. Lead was also reported in sample PL3, and its concentration did not exceed its primary MCL. Arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, cobalt, mercury, selenium, silver, and thallium were not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than their practical quantitation limits (PQLs). For background samples BG1 and BG2, barium, calcium, chromium, copper, magnesium, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, and zinc were reported, and none of these concentrations exceeded their respective primary MCLs. Antimony was reported in sample BG2, and vanadium in sample BG1, with neither exceeding their primary MCLs. Arsenic, beryllium, cadmium, cobalt, lead, mercury, selenium, silver, and thallium were not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than their respective PQLs for samples BG1 and BG2.

## Regulatory status

Primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) are established by the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) and are used as regulatory standards for drinking water quality. The project record indicates that concentrations of various dissolved metals in groundwater and surface water samples have been evaluated against these primary MCLs. In multiple sampling events, including May 2012, November 2012, January 2013, and January 2016, reported concentrations for most analyzed metals have not exceeded their respective primary MCLs.
