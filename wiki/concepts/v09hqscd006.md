##Overview

V09HQSCD006 is the identifier for a hazardous waste variance issued to the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). The variance, granted under California Health and Safety Code § 25143, waives certain hazardous waste management requirements—including permitting, transportation, manifesting, storage, and land disposal—for aerially deposited lead (ADL)-contaminated soil that Caltrans excavates, stockpiles, and reuses within highway right-of-way projects. It was the regulatory framework governing ADL soil management for Caltrans projects from 2009 until it was superseded by the 2016 Soil Management Agreement for ADL-Contaminated Soils. Projects that completed Project Approval and Environmental Document (PA&ED) before July 1, 2016, continue operating under the variance’s terms (as modified by the transition plan) rather than the new Agreement.

### Waived Requirements

The variance waives Caltrans’ obligation to obtain a permit for a disposal facility and any other generator requirements concerning transportation, manifesting, storage, and land disposal of hazardous waste, provided the following conditions are met.

### Soil Concentration Thresholds (from Section 2 of the Variance, as referenced in the Transition Plan)

| Condition | Extractable Lead (mg/L, modified WET using DI water) | Total Lead (mg/kg) | Use Restriction |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| 1 | ≤ 1.5 | ≤ 1,411 | Use as fill: placed ≥5 ft above max historical water table; covered with ≥1 ft of nonhazardous soil maintained to prevent erosion. |
| 2 | ≤ 150 | ≤ 3,397 | Use as fill: placed ≥5 ft above max historical water table; protected from infiltration by a pavement structure maintained by Caltrans. |

### pH Restrictions
- Soil with pH ≥ 5.5: eligible for the above categories.
- Soil with pH 5.0 to < 5.5: may only be used as fill under paved roadway (subject to the pavement condition above).
- Soil with pH ≤ 5.0: must be managed as hazardous waste.

### Applicability
The variance applies only to soil that is not RCRA hazardous waste and is hazardous primarily due to aerially deposited lead from motor vehicle exhaust. It does not apply to any other hazardous waste.

### Transition to 2016 Agreement
Projects that completed PA&ED before July 1, 2016, continue using the variance’s terms (including the concentration thresholds and pH restrictions above) with additional reporting requirements for Caltrans: written notification to the appropriate RWQCB at least 30 days prior to advertisement for bids, and compliance with all applicable water quality regulations (SWRCB/RWQCB, storm water permits, etc.).

## Regulatory status

- **Status:** Expired (as of July 1, 2014).  
- **Current Applicability:** The variance is no longer in effect for new projects. Caltrans and DTSC entered into the 2016 Soil Management Agreement for ADL-Contaminated Soils, which supersedes the variance for projects that have not completed PA&ED before July 1, 2016.  
- **Ongoing Obligations:** Projects that were subject to the variance prior to the transition (i.e., those that completed PA&ED before July 1, 2016) remain bound by the variance’s substantive conditions (thresholds, pH, placement requirements) as modified by the Transition Plan. Additional reporting to DTSC, RWQCB, AQMD/APCD, and local CUPAs is required per the Transition Plan (Sections 4.10–4.11 of the Agreement’s transition provisions).  
- **Compliance:** The variance documentation itself does not indicate any findings of noncompliance; the Transition Plan notes that projects under the variance must continue to meet all state water quality laws and obtain necessary permits.

## Open items

- **Project Notifications:** For projects transitioning under the variance terms, Caltrans must provide written notification to DTSC and other agencies within 10 days of determining that the Agreement applies, including project number, description, limits, laboratory results, and construction specifications (Section 4.10 of the Agreement).  
- **Start-of-Construction Notification:** At least five days before construction begins, Caltrans must provide a written notification to DTSC with project number and other information (Section 4.11).  
- **Document Posting:** Within 30 days of the Agreement’s effective date (July 1, 2016), Caltrans was required to post on its public website PDFs of the Agreement, the DTSC fact sheet, and a list of active projects using the Agreement. DTSC was also required to post information regarding reuse of excavated soils and land use specifics for lead.  
- **Transition Plan Deliverables:** The Transition Plan (dated January 19, 2017) outlines steps for projects to shift from the variance to the Agreement. Implementation dates and completion of that transition are not documented in the provided source excerpts.
