Richard Stewart, P.G. (PG 7926), served as the Caltrans Task Manager for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation, the position through which nearly all technical submittals to [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] passed for review during characterization and reuse decisions for soil from [[stockpile-1]], [[stockpile-2]], and related excavations. He held this role continuously across the project's task orders, from Task Order 6 (Agreement 43A0260/DTSC #08-T3616, executed June 22, 2012, term through May 31, 2013) through Task Order 4 (Agreement 43A0310/DTSC #12-T0106, executed June 1, 2013, term through May 31, 2016), and remained in the role as of the 2020–2021 correspondence on Bent 2 soils, the MSE wall footing sampling, and the Carpenter Shoofly clean fill area. His listed office moved from 855 M Street, Fresno during the task-order period to 2015 Shields Ave., Ste. 100, Fresno by 2021, while his role as the point of transmittal between the project's consultant, [[geocon-consultants-inc]], and the regulatory agencies remained the same.

## Role in the review chain

Stewart's function in the record is consistently that of conduit and certifying signatory rather than technical author. Geocon's John Juhrend would send sampling plans or technical memoranda to Stewart with an explicit request to forward them to DTSC, and Stewart would transmit them to DTSC's project manager — Randy Adams under Task Order 6, later Steven Becker and then [[dean-wright]] — and to the Water Board, typically copying Caltrans project staff (Bhullar, Vespermann, Lugo, Leongson, Pavel, and others) and WSP personnel. This pattern recurs across:

- The Stockpile 1 and 2 MSE Wall Sampling Plan (forwarded March 31, 2020), proposing barium and lead sampling at ten locations along the wall footing excavations at approximately 200-foot intervals, screened against a maximum site-specific barium background of 120 mg/kg and a lead background of 3.8 mg/kg.
- The Stockpile 1 Wall Footing Sampling Tech Memo (transmitted by Stewart April 6, 2020; accepted by Dean Wright April 7, 2020), where DTSC and the Water Board required that the sampled soil — found above the 120 mg/kg background at the 95% Upper Confidence Limit — be placed within the Stockpiles 1 and 2 fill containment zones or used as general bridge embankment fill, rather than as capping material, because the footing area fell outside the borrow area designated in the Remedial Design Implementation Plan.
- The Stockpile 2 Wall Footing Tech Memo (revised version sent by Stewart June 18, 2020, correcting a mislabeled 120 mg/kg value from lead to barium; accepted June 22, 2020), where the agencies agreed soil above 3 feet bgs must go to containment zones while material below 3 feet was usable as embankment fill or capping material under the existing April 9, 2020 Variance Request, without need for a further variance.
- The Bent 2 Soils Tech Memo (transmitted by Stewart November 2, 2020; accepted November 6, 2020), reporting that excavated Bent 2 soils exceeded background criteria for clean cap use; DTSC and the Water Board nonetheless permitted its placement atop Stockpile 2 at 6 inches or less, capped with clean base material and roadway pavement, conditioned on adherence to the rainy-season surface water sampling and erosion controls in the Revised SWPPP Amendment dated October 22, 2020.
- The Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Sampling Plan and Tech Memo, where Stewart forwarded Juhrend's April 2, 2021 sampling-location proposal to Dean Wright, and later (April 20, 2021) forwarded the resulting test results after acknowledging he had initially missed them in his inbox; DTSC's April 22, 2021 acceptance found the composite sample results consistent with naturally occurring background and compliant with the Barium Containing Soil clean cap criteria.

## Significance

As the named Caltrans Task Manager on both governing interagency task orders, Stewart is the certifying signatory of record for the work performed under them, having signed Task Order 6 and Task Order 4 attesting that the described services were within project scope and necessary for completion. Because DTSC's and the Water Board's conditional approvals throughout 2020–2021 were addressed and issued specifically to him, the case file's record of what was proposed, what was accepted, and under what placement conditions runs through his correspondence rather than through direct agency-to-consultant contact.
