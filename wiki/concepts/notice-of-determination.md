This Notice of Determination, filed by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) with the Governor's Office of Planning and Research State Clearinghouse (SCH No. 2010012010), records DTSC's March 14, 2018 approval of the containment remedy for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles]] and closes out the CEQA process on which that approval depended. It documents that DTSC acted as a CEQA Responsible Agency relying on an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) that [[caltrans]], as Lead Agency, had certified twelve days earlier, on March 2, 2018. For reviewers, the filing is the formal, dated record that DTSC's approval of the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and Caltrans's CEQA certification of the project are two distinct actions tied together by this document, and that a Statement of Overriding Considerations adopted by Caltrans does not carry over to DTSC's action.

## What was determined

The notice states five determinations DTSC made in approving the project on March 14, 2018:

- The project will not have a significant effect on the environment.
- An EIR that included this project was prepared and certified by Caltrans under [[ceqa]].
- Mitigation measures were made a condition of project approval.
- A Statement of Overriding Considerations was not adopted for this project (as distinct from Caltrans's project-level statement, which the notice specifies "is not applicable to DTSC's project").
- Findings were made pursuant to CEQA.

This distinction matters for liability allocation: Caltrans's overriding-considerations finding covers unavoidable significant impacts of the highway project generally, while DTSC's own CEQA findings for the RAP rest instead on a determination of no significant effect, with mitigation measures conditioning approval rather than being overridden.

## Project description as filed

The notice describes the remedy DTSC approved: containment of three soil stockpiles totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards, containing metals — primarily barium, strontium, and lead — located in the state right-of-way south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, in three areas (between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue; between Emerald Avenue and SR 99; and east of SR 99). The stockpiles are to be contained behind retaining walls and bridge abutments and beneath the roadway, with clean fill cover over unpaved portions. The notice states this containment approach is intended to provide long-term protection by preventing exposure and minimizing infiltration of surface water into groundwater beneath the stockpiles, and that land use controls, inspection of pavement and other containment features, and groundwater monitoring will be conducted annually to verify the remedy's integrity — establishing the annual monitoring and inspection obligation as a stated condition of the approved remedy rather than a discretionary practice.

## Public record locations

The notice designates the locations where the final environmental document, comments and responses, and the record of project approval are made available: Caltrans District 10 (1976 Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. Blvd., Stockton), the DTSC file room (8800 Cal Center Drive, Sacramento), a Caltrans project webpage, and two DTSC EnviroStor profile pages, identified by global ID numbers 60001626 and 50280024. These EnviroStor identifiers are the traceable record locators for the site going forward.
