The groundwater at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along State Route (SR) 132 in Stanislaus County, California, has been determined to be not impacted by the encapsulated soil stockpiles. This conclusion is supported by evaluations conducted by Geocon Consultants, Inc. on behalf of Caltrans, in consultation with the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB). The encapsulation of the soil beneath the SR 132 Expressway further supports this determination.

### Regulatory Approval of Groundwater Data

On May 3, 2024, the DTSC, in consultation with the Central Valley RWQCB, approved the "Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles" report, dated February 26, 2024. This report, prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., evaluated the potential impact of barium and lead in the soil stockpiles on groundwater. DTSC and the RWQCB agreed that the data indicates the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and will not impact it in the future. This conclusion is consistent with an earlier 2014 evaluation. As a result of this determination, DTSC and the RWQCB concurred with the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring and decommission the four remaining monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) as per the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan.

### Groundwater Monitoring History and Well Decommissioning

Groundwater monitoring was initiated in 2012 with the installation of ten monitoring wells to assess potential contamination migration from the soil stockpiles. The Central Valley RWQCB and DTSC reviewed the groundwater monitoring reports, which, over years of data, supported the conclusion that no metals were leaching into the groundwater. Consequently, the agencies agreed to the decommissioning of these wells. Six of the ten wells were decommissioned in 2019 to facilitate highway construction. A Well Destruction Report, dated June 18, 2025, and prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc., outlines the procedures for destroying the remaining four wells. DTSC conditionally approved this report on January 30, 2026.
