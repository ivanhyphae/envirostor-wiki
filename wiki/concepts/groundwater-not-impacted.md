# Groundwater Not Impacted

The determination that the barium- and lead-contaminated soil stockpiles have not affected, and will not affect, groundwater beneath the site is the finding that closed out groundwater monitoring at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles and cleared the way to decommission the monitoring well network. It rests on a decade-plus of quarterly monitoring data reviewed by [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], culminating in a 2024 comparative evaluation that both agencies accepted, and it is the technical basis on which four remaining wells were approved for destruction ahead of Phase II construction of SR 132.

## History of the finding

Ten groundwater monitoring wells were installed in 2006 around the three soil stockpiles (excess native soil and pond tailings from 1960s-era SR 99 construction, containing barium, lead, and strontium originating from the former Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation parcel) to track whether these metals were leaching to groundwater while an encapsulation remedy was developed. DTSC's June 27, 2012 letter, reviewing the Groundwater Monitoring Report covering the June 14, 2006, October 5, 2006, and March 12, 2012 sampling events (with RWQCB split samples collected in March 2012), concurred with the report's findings and directed that monitoring continue on at least a quarterly schedule, with future reports to include trend comparisons across sampling events and legended stiff diagrams.

A 2014 comparative evaluation of groundwater data was prepared as part of the remedial planning process; it is referenced repeatedly in later documents as the baseline finding that the stockpiles were not affecting groundwater. That evaluation was updated in an Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data dated February 26, 2024, prepared by Geocon Consultants on behalf of Caltrans, which DTSC (in consultation with the RWQCB) approved by letter dated May 3, 2024. The 2024 approval letter states that the data indicate the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and will not impact groundwater in the future, and that this conclusion is supported by both the consistency of the updated data with the 2014 evaluation and the recent physical encapsulation of the soil beneath the newly constructed SR 132 Express Way.

## Consequences for the monitoring network

The no-impact finding was the stated basis for discontinuing groundwater monitoring at the site. Six of the original ten wells (MW1, MW2, MW3, MW5, MW7, and MW8) were destroyed in 2019 due to conflicts with highway construction activity. In the May 3, 2024 approval, DTSC and the RWQCB concurred with Geocon's recommendation that monitoring beneath the soil stockpiles be discontinued entirely and that the four remaining wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) be decommissioned under the approved Remedial Design and Implementation Plan. Geocon's June 18, 2025 Well Destruction Report cites the declining water table and the final encapsulation of the remaining stockpiles during Phase II construction, alongside the consistency of recent data with the 2014 evaluation, as the grounds for recommending decommissioning, and confirms that the four wells were subsequently destroyed. DTSC's January 30, 2026 letter recounts that after years of data supporting no metals leaching into groundwater from the stockpiles, the RWQCB and DTSC agreed to decommissioning, and it conditionally approves the resulting Well Destruction Completion Report subject to a Geological Services Branch comment to be incorporated into the final version.

## Significance

This finding is the technical predicate for retiring the site's groundwater monitoring obligation altogether: with no impact demonstrated across the 2006–2024 dataset and no offsite migration recorded, DTSC and the RWQCB treated further monitoring wells as no longer capable of producing useful data once Phase II encapsulation is complete. It also underlies the storm water monitoring that continued in place of groundwater sampling — the January 30, 2026 letter notes that surface water run-off is separately collected to confirm the stockpile metals are not displaced into surface water, and that the FY24/25 Storm Water Sampling Report results were all within reporting limits. The conditional approval of the Well Destruction Completion Report leaves one GSB comment outstanding as of that letter; the record does not show that the final, corrected version has yet been submitted or accepted.
