# Aerially Deposited Lead

## Overview

Aerially deposited lead (ADL) at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site refers to lead-contaminated soil generated from the unpaved shoulder of northbound State Route 99 that contains lead concentrations exceeding California and federal hazardous waste criteria. This material is distinct from the barium-containing soil that constitutes the primary stockpile mass. ADL soil was encountered during Interim Phase 1 of the SR 132 West Project and was consolidated within the designated Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone as part of the site's interim encapsulation remedy.

The regulatory framework governing ADL management at this site is the 2016 *Soil Management Agreement for Aerially Deposited Lead-Contaminated Soils* between DTSC and Caltrans, referenced in both the [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] and the [[interim-removal-action-completion-report]].

## Site Record

### Location and Source

ADL-contaminated soil was removed from the northbound unpaved shoulder area of SR 99, specifically between L Street and the Kansas Avenue off-ramp (Section 3.5.4 of both RACR versions). This area lies adjacent to the former location of stockpile-1|Stockpile 3 (east of SR 99) and is depicted on multiple project figures as the "ADL Removal Area." The project location map (Figure 1, December 2022) marks this area with purple shading, distinct from the Barium-Containing Soil Stockpile areas shown in yellow.

### Regulatory Definition and Criteria

Under the 2016 DTSC-Caltrans Agreement, "ADL-contaminated soil" is defined as excavated soil that, based on a 95% upper confidence limit, contains:

- Total lead concentrations greater than 80 mg/kg, and/or
- Soluble lead (Waste Extraction Test) greater than or equal to 5.0 milligrams per liter

The sources do not provide the specific total or soluble lead concentrations measured in the ADL soil removed from the SR 99 shoulder at this site. The record states only that the material "contains lead concentrations in excess of California and Federal hazardous waste criteria" (Section 3.5.4).

### Volume and Placement

ADL-contaminated soil was excavated and placed in the eastern end of the Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone during June and July of 2020 (Section 3.5.4). The Stockpile 3 BCS Removal Site Plan (Figure 3) includes a "BARIUM AND ADL CONTAINING SOIL RELOCATION" table that documents earthwork volumes:

| Material | Calculated (CY) | Adjusted (CY) |
|----------|-----------------|---------------|
| BCS      | 29,100          | 3,000         |
| ADL      | 1,300           | 200           |

### Placement Requirements

The 2016 DTSC Agreement imposes specific placement restrictions on ADL-contaminated soil reused as fill (Section 3.5.4):

- Must always be at least five feet above the highest groundwater elevation
- Depending on lead concentrations, may need to be covered with at least one foot of clean soil or a pavement structure
- May not be placed in areas where it might contact groundwater or surface water (such as streams and rivers)
- Must be buried in locations protected from erosion that may result from stormwater runoff

At this site, ADL soil was consolidated within the Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone alongside barium-containing soil, and capped with DTSC-approved clean fill materials as part of the overall containment system. The as-built plans (Figures 3-1 and 3-2, December 2022) depict the combined "Barium-Containing Soil (BCS) Stockpile and BCS/Aerially Deposited Lead (ADL) Consolidation Zones" as yellow-shaded areas along the SR 132 alignment.

### Post-Remediation Status

The Draft Interim RACR transmittal memorandum (December 23, 2021) confirmed: "The BCS Stockpiles and aerially deposited lead impacted soil removed from the unpaved shoulder of northbound State Route 99 have been consolidated and capped with DTSC-approved clean fill materials."

### Preconstruction Coordination

ADL management procedures were reviewed during the January 29, 2020 RDIP preconstruction meeting, attended by representatives of Caltrans, the City of Modesto, DTSC, WSP, BCPG, and Geocon. Discussion items included "BCS and ADL excavation/placement/containment" and "tarping of BCS/ADL transport trucks" (Section 2.2).

## Regulatory Status

The 2016 *Soil Management Agreement for Aerially Deposited Lead-Contaminated Soils* (DTSC and Caltrans) governs the reuse of ADL-contaminated soil from Caltrans rights-of-way. The agreement's placement restrictions—minimum five-foot separation from highest groundwater, clean cover requirements, and erosion protection—apply in perpetuity to the ADL material now encapsulated within the Stockpile 1 Containment Zone.

The overall containment remedy remains under DTSC oversight, with Caltrans responsible for five-year reviews that will assess "potential for exposure to contained and capped BCS/ADL soil materials" (Section 5.3 / Section 4.4 of the OMP). DTSC will provide project certification following completion of final containment measures during Ultimate Phase 2.

## Open Items

- **Five-Year Reviews**: Caltrans is required to perform periodic five-year reviews assessing the effectiveness of interim containment measures, including evaluation of potential exposure to contained ADL soil materials.
- **Ultimate Phase 2**: A supplemental RDIP will be prepared for the Ultimate Phase 2 portion of the SR 132 West Project, which may involve additional ADL soil management if further ADL-impacted areas are encountered.
- **Project Certification**: DTSC certification will not occur until all final containment measures—including those for ADL soil—are completed in Ultimate Phase 2.
