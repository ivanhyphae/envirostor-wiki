## Overview
The California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) Notice of Determination (NOD) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remediation Project was filed with the Governor’s Office of Planning and Research (OPR) on **March 19, 2018**. The NOD, submitted by Caltrans (the Lead Agency), certified that the project’s environmental impacts had been reviewed per CEQA and that the project would proceed with a Mitigated Negative Declaration (MND), rather than a more extensive Environmental Impact Report. This decision was based on findings that, with identified mitigation measures, the remediation of contaminated soil stockpiles along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County would have no significant adverse environmental effects.

## Site Record
- **Filing Date**: March 19, 2018 (OPR stamp on the document, referencing OPR tracking number 2018039033).
- **Document Reference**: "Notice of Determination, Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Haz 17072, BizPlan 17-926."
- **Project Location**: State Route 132 (SR 132) corridor, Stanislaus County, CA. The soil stockpiles were generated during previous Caltrans construction activities and stored along the highway right-of-way.
- **Agency Actions**: The Los Angeles County Superior Court (per the OPR header) is listed as the certifying body, though Caltrans is the actual decision-making agency. The NOD states that the project “will not have a significant effect on the environment” once mitigation measures, as described in the MND, are implemented.
- **Mitigation Measures**: While the NOD itself does not detail specific mitigation, it references that these are included in the MND. Subsequent project records (e.g., Remedial Action Completion Reports) confirm mitigation included excavation, off-site disposal of contaminated soil, and backfill with clean material for stockpiles containing metals (barium, lead, arsenic, etc.) and petroleum hydrocarbons.
- **Timeline**: The NOD triggered a 30-day statute of limitations for CEQA challenges. The remediation work for the primary stockpiles occurred in **Phase 1 (2019-2020)**, consistent with the NOD’s schedule.

## Regulatory Status
- **CEQA Compliance**: The NOD establishes that the project is in full compliance with the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) via a Mitigated Negative Declaration. The document confirms that the project does not require an Environmental Impact Report.
- **Lead Agencies**: Caltrans is the Lead Agency; DTSC (Department of Toxic Substances Control) serves as the oversight agency for the remediation, per the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) referenced in later correspondence.
- **Current Status**: As of the NOD filing, the project is approved. Subsequent verification testing (e.g., groundwater monitoring at wells MW-5, MW-6) must demonstrate that remedial goals (e.g., MCLs for metals) are met, but this does not cancel the CEQA determination. No additional CEQA review has been required for the remediation phases to date.

## Open Items
- **Post-Remediation Monitoring**: The NOD does not specify the duration or scope of post-remediation monitoring. Ongoing groundwater monitoring reports (e.g., 2021-2023) indicate that some contaminant concentrations (e.g., barium at 760 mg/kg in soil near MW-5) remain above background levels, though below DTSC-approved action levels. These may require future CEQA review if conditions change.
- **Additional Stockpiles**: The NOD references “soil stockpiles” (plural). It is not yet documented whether all stockpiles (including any remaining along SR 132) have been accounted for or remediated under this NOD. Future notifications may be necessary.
