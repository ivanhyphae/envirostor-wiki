On April 22, 2021, [[dtsc]] issued its written acceptance of the Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill Technical Memorandum, dated April 12, 2021, closing out the characterization work for the [[carpenter-road-area]] clean fill source. The approval was delivered by e-mail from [[dean-wright]] of DTSC's Site Evaluation and Remediation Unit to Caltrans, with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board copied, and it clears the material for use as [[clean-fill-suitable-as-cover]] under the project's [[barium]] clean cap criteria.

## Sequence of the approval

The memo itself was transmitted on April 12, 2021 by John Juhrend of Geocon Consultants to Caltrans engineering geologist Richard Stewart, with instructions to forward it to DTSC for approval. Stewart forwarded the package to Dean Wright at DTSC on April 20, 2021, noting that he had initially missed the results in his inbox. Two days later, on April 22, 2021, Wright responded confirming that DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (Kyle Cockerham) had jointly reviewed the memo.

## Basis of the approval

DTSC's e-mail states that the composite soil sample results from the Carpenter Shoofly Clean Fill area are consistent with naturally occurring background concentrations and meet the Barium Containing Soil clean cap criteria. No specific concentration values, sample counts, or sample locations are given in the approval e-mail itself; the underlying analytical data reside in the April 12, 2021 Technical Memorandum, which is referenced but not reproduced in this correspondence.

## Significance

The e-mail is DTSC's formal acceptance of the memo and functions as the regulatory sign-off needed to treat the Carpenter Shoofly material as clean fill rather than as contaminated soil requiring separate handling or disposal. Because the approval was joint with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board, it forecloses a second agency raising an objection to the same characterization data. The finding that barium levels reflect naturally occurring background — rather than a site-related release — is the specific basis on which the material was accepted for reuse as [[clean-fill-capping]] cover on the SR 132 project.
