The Soil Management Plan (SMP) for the State Route 132 (SR 132) Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case file details the procedures for managing soil impacted by Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH) during the construction of two proposed retention basins. The plan was developed by the City of Modesto and reviewed by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB).

## Site record

The revised Soil Management Plan (SMP) submitted on February 8, 2021, by the City of Modesto, outlined procedures for segregating and covering soil with Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH) above screening levels. The screening level for diesel range total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH-d) was adjusted to 500 mg/kg to align with the DTSC screening level for commercial/industrial soil {{quote: "the screening level for diesel range total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH‐d) has been lowered to 500 mg/kg to reflect the DTSC screening level for commercial/industrial soil"}}.

The SMP proposed two excavation activities:
*   Targeted excavation of TPH-d impacted soils at borings RB-10 and RB-11 within Basin 6A, followed by confirmation soil sampling {{quote: "Targeted excavation of TPH‐d impacted soils at borings RB‐10 and RB‐11 within Basin 6A and confirmation soil sampling"}}.
*   Excavation of approximately 500 cubic yards from south of 611 Franklin within Basin 6B, where no known TPH-d impacted soils were present {{quote: "Excavation of 500 cubic yards +/‐ from just south of 611 Franklin within Basin 6B where there are no known TPH‐ d impacted soils"}}.

## Regulatory status

The revised SMP, dated February 8, 2021, was officially accepted by the Water Board (WB) and DTSC via email on February 11, 2021 {{quote: "This e‐mail serves as the WB/DTSC official acceptance of the SMP"}}. This acceptance followed the incorporation of comments provided by DTSC and the WB on January 31, 2021 {{quote: "The revised SMP addresses comments provided by DTSC and the WB provided on January 31, 2021"}}.

Two minor comments were provided by DTSC and the WB on February 11, 2021, which could be addressed in the field without further revision of the SMP:
*   Soil with TPH above screening levels should be labeled until transported offsite {{quote: "The SMP indicates that soil with Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH) above screening levels will be segregated and covered. In addition, it is requested that this material be labeled until it is transported offsite."}}.
*   A process for handling the scenario where TPH-impacted soil remains at the maximum excavation depth needs to be established, involving notification and consultation with the WB and DTSC {{quote: "The SMP does not discuss the actions that will be taken should TPH impacted soil still be present when the maximum depth of the basins is achieved. Should this scenario occur, Caltrans should be alerted and consultation with the WB and DTSC should take place to determine appropriate next steps."}}.

As of February 8, 2021, the Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) had not yet responded to requests for a meeting to discuss the appropriate screening level for the protection of groundwater {{quote: "As you are aware, the Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) has not responded to requests for a meeting to discuss the appropriate screening level for protection of groundwater"}}. The outcome of this discussion would apply to soils left onsite or reused onsite at finished grade {{quote: "however, the outcome of that discussion will apply to soils left onsite or reused onsite at finished grade."}}.

## Open items

As of February 11, 2021, the SMP did not explicitly detail the actions to be taken if TPH-impacted soil is still present when the maximum depth of the basins is achieved. The plan requires that Caltrans be alerted and consultation with the WB and DTSC occur to determine appropriate next steps in such a scenario {{quote: "The SMP does not discuss the actions that will be taken should TPH impacted soil still be present when the maximum depth of the basins is achieved. Should this scenario occur, Caltrans should be alerted and consultation with the WB and DTSC should take place to determine appropriate next steps."}}.
