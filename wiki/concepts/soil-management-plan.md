## Soil Management Plan (SMP)

### Overview
The Soil Management Plan (SMP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation (State Route 132, Stanislaus County, CA) is the site‑specific document governing the handling, stockpiling, characterization, and off‑site disposal or beneficial reuse of contaminated soil generated during remedial activities. The SMP was developed by Caltrans and approved by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) as the lead regulatory agency. Approval was conveyed via the Final SMP Approval E‑mail, which establishes the SMP as a binding component of the project’s remedial action.

### Site Record
- **Document Status**: Final SMP Approval E‑mail (dated [exact date not provided in the source – mark as “not documented in provided source”]), issued by DTSC.
- **Applicable Area**: Soil stockpiles along SR 132, Stanislaus County, California – the specific stockpile identifiers (e.g., SP-1, SP-2) and their respective locations are defined within the SMP.
- **Key Conditions Cited in the Approval E‑mail**:
  - Stockpile segregation by contaminant type and concentration level (e.g., metals, pesticides, VOCs).
  - Sampling frequency: minimum one composite sample per 500 cubic yards of stockpiled soil.
  - Analytical parameters: barium, lead, arsenic, DDT/DDE/DDD, and other constituents of concern per the site Remedial Investigation Report.
  - Disposal criteria: soil exceeding DTSC’s maximum allowable concentrations for off‑site landfill disposal must be transported to a Class I facility; soil meeting residential or industrial screening levels may be considered for beneficial reuse under a separate DTSC-approved reuse plan.
  - Verification sampling: confirmation samples from excavated areas prior to backfill, with results submitted to DTSC within 30 days.
- **Volumes/Phases**: The SMP covers an estimated 12,000 cubic yards of stockpiled soil across three phases (Phase 1: 2019–2020, Phase 2: 2021–2022, Phase 3: 2023–2024). These figures are stated in the approval e‑mail as subject to adjustment based on field conditions.

### Regulatory Status
- **Applicable Standards**: DTSC’s California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) for residential land use, and the Title 22 California Code of Regulations hazardous waste criteria (total for metals, EPA Method 1311 TCLP for leaching potential).
- **Enforcement**: DTSC’s Project Manager (name not documented in source) retains authority to modify SMP requirements via e‑mail approval or formal amendment; DTSC reserves the right to halt soil movement if the SMP is not followed.
- **Compliance**: Current as of the approval date; ongoing compliance will be tracked through quarterly SMP implementation reports due to DTSC.

### Open Items
- **Pending Deliverable**: Final SMP implementation report for Phase 3, due 60 days after stockpile removal completion.
- **Contested Issue**: The approval e‑mail notes a pending DTSC review of the proposed beneficial reuse criteria for soil with concentrations between residential and industrial screening levels. Caltrans must submit additional leachability data for two stockpiles (SP-4 and SP-7) before reuse can proceed.
- **Future Action**: A SMP amendment is required if any new stockpile area exceeding 2,000 cubic yards is discovered.
