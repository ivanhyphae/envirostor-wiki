# Soil Management Plan (SR 132 Modesto Retention Basins)

The Soil Management Plan governs excavation, confirmation sampling, handling, and disposal of impacted soil for two retention basins (Basin 6A and Basin 6B) built as part of new construction along SR 132 in Modesto. A revised version dated February 8, 2021, submitted by the City of Modesto's consultant, was accepted by [[dtsc]] and [[central-valley-rwqcb]] on February 11, 2021, subject to two minor field-implementable conditions. The plan's screening approach — and an unresolved question about the appropriate groundwater-protective threshold — governed how excavation proceeded at each basin.

## Development and submittal

The City of Modesto, through consultant Rincon Consultants, submitted a revised SMP to Caltrans on February 8, 2021, addressing comments DTSC and the Regional Water Board had provided on January 31, 2021. The revision lowered the screening level for diesel-range total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH-d) to 500 mg/kg, matching DTSC's screening level for commercial/industrial soil. The submittal noted that the Regional Water Quality Control Board had not responded to requests for a meeting to discuss the appropriate TPH-d screening level for protection of groundwater, and that the outcome of that separate discussion would apply specifically to soils left onsite or reused onsite at finished grade — not to the two excavation actions described in the SMP.

Those two excavation actions, proposed to proceed under the SMP as submitted, were:

1. Targeted excavation of TPH-d impacted soil at borings RB-10 and RB-11 within Basin 6A, with confirmation soil sampling.
2. Excavation of approximately 500 cubic yards from just south of 611 Franklin within Basin 6B, an area with no known TPH-d impacted soil.

Caltrans (Richard Stewart) forwarded the submittal to the Water Board on February 9, 2021, flagging that the City "may be preparing to move forward with excavation... without acceptance of the SMP by DTSC and the Regional Board," and stating that Caltrans had told the City the SMP required regulatory acceptance first.

## Regulatory acceptance and conditions

DTSC (Dean Wright) issued joint DTSC/Water Board acceptance of the SMP on February 11, 2021, addressed to Caltrans. The acceptance stated that the revised SMP adequately addressed the January 31, 2021 comments, subject to two remaining minor comments:

- TPH-impacted soil segregated and covered under the plan should also be labeled until it is transported offsite.
- The SMP did not address what should happen if TPH-impacted soil remained present once the basins reached maximum excavation depth; should that occur, Caltrans was to be alerted and was to consult with the Water Board and DTSC to determine next steps.

DTSC's email specified that these two comments could be incorporated in the field, so the SMP itself did not require further revision, and the email served as the official acceptance. Because the second condition is contingent — triggered only if TPH-impacted soil remained at maximum basin depth — the available record does not show whether that scenario occurred at either basin or whether the described consultation with Caltrans, the Water Board, and DTSC took place.
