## Overview
Alternative 4, "Containment," is the selected remedy for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] as detailed in the Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP). This alternative integrates the soil stockpiles into the construction of the [[state-route-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. Containment is achieved through the construction of retaining walls, [[bridge-abutments]], and by placing roadway pavement over the stockpiles. Unpaved areas will be covered with clean fill. This approach aims to protect human health and the environment by eliminating exposure pathways and minimizing surface water infiltration into groundwater. It is considered cost-effective, technically feasible, and compliant with Applicable or Relevant and Appropriate Requirements (ARARs).

## Site Record
Under Alternative 4, the soil stockpiles are to be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway pavement of the [[state-route-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. Unpaved portions will have clean fill cover.

Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2 are expected to remain in their current locations, with potential height increases using clean soil to meet the design grade of the elevated section of [[state-route-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]]. A significant portion of Stockpile 3 will be consolidated within the [[state-route-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]] Overcrossing abutment at Needham Avenue. Excess soil from this consolidation will be placed atop Stockpile 2 and covered with clean soil.

During construction, to minimize dust and ensure public safety, all stockpile areas will be thoroughly wetted down before and during work. Air monitoring will also be required.

The [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) has stated that the stockpiles, as currently managed, do not pose an unacceptable risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. Current management includes maintaining perimeter fencing, limiting access to authorized workers, maintaining vegetative cover, conducting surface and groundwater monitoring, and prohibiting soil placement or removal.

The maximum surface soil concentrations of arsenic, carcinogenic polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), and vanadium are within local soil background ranges. [[barium]] and nickel concentrations are below U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (U.S. EPA) safe levels. Strontium and nitrate are also detected at concentrations lower than safe levels. Vibrations from traffic are not expected to cause contaminant migration from the stockpiles into groundwater.

## Regulatory Status
Alternative 4 requires Caltrans to enter into an Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement with the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and prepare an O&M Plan for DTSC's review and approval. The O&M Plan will mandate annual inspections of the pavement and other containment features. Groundwater monitoring will continue, and DTSC will conduct evaluations of the containment remedy every five years to ensure it is functioning as designed.

## Open Items
Caltrans concurs with the DTSC's responses regarding Alternative 4.
