The [[dtsc]] conditionally approved the Storm Water and Surface Water Monitoring Report (FY24/25) for the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site on January 30, 2026. This approval is part of the regulatory oversight for the State Route 132 West Expressway project in Stanislaus County.

### DTSC Approval of Storm Water and Surface Water Monitoring Report

On January 30, 2026, the [[dtsc]] issued a letter to Adam Inman of [[caltrans]] conditionally approving the Storm Water Sampling Report (FY24/25), prepared by Geocon Consultants, Inc. The report details the results of storm water sampling conducted to evaluate the integrity of the stockpile containment system during qualifying rain events. The DTSC's Geological Services Branch (GSB) and Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) reviewed the report. The DTSC found that the results of the Storm Water Sampling Report were all within reporting limits.

### Site Context

The Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles site, also known as the State Route 132 West Expressway, is located south of the intersection of State Route 99 and Kansas Avenue in Modesto, Stanislaus County. The site contains three stockpiles with soil contaminated with barium since the 1960s. Phase I of the Remedial Action Plan involved moving soil from Stockpile 3 to Stockpiles 1 and 2. Phase II will utilize the remaining stockpiles for the construction of the 4-lane highway. Ten groundwater monitoring wells were installed in 2012 to monitor for potential contamination migration to groundwater. Six of these wells were decommissioned in 2019 to facilitate highway construction. The DTSC, in coordination with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board, reviewed years of data indicating no metals were leaching into the groundwater, leading to the agreement for well decommissioning.
