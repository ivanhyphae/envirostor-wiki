## SR 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles Remediation

### Overview
The SR 132 Modesto project involves the remediation of soil impacted by Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons (TPH) in connection with the construction of two retention basins along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. The remediation efforts are guided by a [[soil-management-plan]] (SMP) that outlines excavation, sampling, handling, and disposal procedures for impacted soil. Key regulatory agencies involved include the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]].

### Site Record
A revised [[soil-management-plan]] dated February 8, 2021, was submitted by [[rincon-consultants]] on behalf of the City of Modesto. This plan addressed comments from the DTSC and the Water Board. The screening level for diesel-range TPH (TPH-d) was lowered to 500 mg/kg, reflecting the DTSC screening level for commercial/industrial soil.

The SMP proposed two excavation activities:
*   Targeted excavation of TPH-d impacted soils at borings RB-10 and RB-11 within Basin 6A, followed by confirmation soil sampling.
*   Excavation of approximately 500 cubic yards of soil from an area south of 611 Franklin within Basin 6B, where no known TPH-d impacted soils were present.

### Regulatory Status
On February 11, 2021, the DTSC and the Water Board officially accepted the revised SMP via email. However, two minor comments were provided:
*   Soil with TPH above screening levels should be segregated, covered, and labeled until transported offsite.
*   If TPH-impacted soil remains at the maximum depth of the basins, Caltrans should be alerted, and consultation with the Water Board and DTSC should occur to determine next steps.

The [[central-valley-rwqcb]] had not yet responded to requests for a meeting to discuss the appropriate screening level for the protection of groundwater, which would apply to soils left onsite or reused onsite at finished grade.

### Open Items
*   The DTSC and Water Board requested that soil with TPH above screening levels be labeled until transported offsite.
*   A contingency plan is required should TPH-impacted soil be present at the maximum depth of the basins, necessitating consultation with the Water Board and DTSC.
*   The appropriate screening level for the protection of groundwater for soils left onsite or reused onsite at finished grade was pending discussion with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]].
