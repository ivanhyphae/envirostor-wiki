Lead is a metal identified as a contaminant of potential concern (COPC) in soil and groundwater at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. Its presence has been evaluated in relation to human health risk assessments and regulatory thresholds.

## Site Record

### Soil

*   **Stockpile 1:** Maximum detected concentrations (MDCs) for lead in surface soil samples from September 2012 were 17 mg/kg, compared to the Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) Exposure Point Concentration (EPC) of 12 mg/kg. The *LeadSpread* model, using the MDC for lead in Stockpile 1 surface soil, did not indicate that any residential receptor would have a blood lead concentration greater than 10 μg/dL.
*   **Stockpile 2:** The 95% Upper Confidence Limit (UCL) for lead in Stockpile 2 surface soil was estimated to be 30 mg/kg. The *LeadSpread* model results indicated that all percentiles of adults and children would have blood lead concentrations less than 10 μg/dL.
*   **Stockpile 3:** Lead was detected at 34 mg/kg in surface soil samples from September 2012, compared to the HHRA EPC of 12 mg/kg. The 95th UCL for lead in Stockpile 3 surface soil was estimated to be 6.7 mg/kg. The *LeadSpread* model did not indicate that offsite residents or trespassers would have a blood lead concentration greater than 10 μg/dL.
*   **Future Construction Zone Soil (0 to 20 feet bgs):** The 95th UCL for lead in this zone was estimated to be 54 mg/kg. The *LeadSpread* model results indicated that blood lead concentrations would be less than 10 μg/dL for a pica child.
*   **Bent 2 Excavation Soil Stockpiles:** Lead was detected in 6 of 10 soil samples at concentrations ranging from 2.6 to 13 mg/kg, within the range of the maximum site-specific background concentration for lead of 3.8 mg/kg. Final excavation soil samples contained lead concentrations ranging from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg, below the Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) verification thresholds.
*   **Carpenter Road Shoofly:** Lead was detected in 9 of 10 soil samples at concentrations ranging from 1.4 to 3.8 mg/kg, less than or equal to the maximum site-specific background concentration for lead of 3.8 mg/kg.
*   **Total Metals Analysis Results (Shaw 2006 SI):** Lead was detected in stockpile soil samples at concentrations considerably higher than background values.
*   **Stockpile 3 Excavation (June 2012):** Airborne levels of lead were well below levels of concern during excavation activities.

### Groundwater

*   **June and October 2006:** The maximum detected concentration (MDC) for lead in groundwater samples was 3.4 µg/L, below the federal action level of 15 µg/L.
*   **January 2013:** Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the practical quantitation limit (PQL) of 1.0 µg/L.
*   **February 2014:** Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective PQL.
*   **April 2015:** Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective PQL.
*   **May 2016:** Lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective PQL.
*   **October 2016:** Lead was reported in sample PL3.
*   **January 2018:** Lead was detected in sample PL3 at a concentration of 46 µg/L, exceeding its primary maximum contaminant level (MCL) for drinking water of 15 µg/L.
*   **February 2020 (BCS Removal Verification):** Lead concentrations ranged from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg.

### Surface Water Runoff

*   **March 2018:** Lead was detected in sample PL3 at a concentration of 46 µg/L, exceeding its primary MCL for drinking water of 15 µg/L.
*   **February 2024:** Lead was not detected at concentrations equal to or greater than the reporting limit of 0.010 mg/L.

## Regulatory Status

*   **Drinking Water Thresholds:** The primary MCL for lead in drinking water is 15 µg/L.
*   **Stockpile 1:** The *LeadSpread* model did not indicate an unacceptable hazard to current residents/trespassers.
*   **Stockpile 2:** The *LeadSpread* model indicated blood lead concentrations would be less than 10 μg/dL for adults and children.
*   **Stockpile 3:** The *LeadSpread* model did not indicate an unacceptable hazard to current residents/trespassers.
*   **Future Construction Zone Soil:** The *LeadSpread* model indicated blood lead concentrations would be less than 10 μg/dL for a pica child.
*   **Groundwater:** The maximum lead detection in groundwater was 3.4 µg/L (June 2012), less than the federal action level of 15 µg/L. Lead concentrations in groundwater have generally remained below the PQL of 1.0 µg/L.
*   **Surface Water:** Lead exceeded the primary MCL of 15 µg/L in sample PL3 (46 µg/L) in March 2018. However, in February 2024, lead was not detected above the reporting limit.

## Open Items

*   The exceedance of the primary MCL for lead in surface water sample PL3 in March 2018 requires attention.
