Lead is the second contaminant of concern at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, present at lower magnitude and lower risk than [[barium]] but tracked throughout site investigation, risk assessment, and remediation because it co-occurs with barium in the stockpile soils and separately as aerially deposited lead (ADL) along the SR 99 shoulder. Lead governed specific verification thresholds during Interim Phase 1 construction and determined how excavated soil from different areas could be reused, but the record consistently shows lead concentrations at the stockpiles remaining below regulatory screening levels and close to site background, in contrast to barium's much larger exceedances.

## Site history and characterization

Lead in stockpile soil originates from the same source as barium: soil and pond tailings from the former Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) facility, relocated to form Stockpiles 1 through 3 during 1960s-era construction of SR 99. Shaw's 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation and 2006 Site Investigation identified lead, along with arsenic, as detected in all three stockpiles at concentrations exceeding background values, though at levels far below those of barium, which was the primary chemical of potential concern (COPC). Of 278 total soil samples analyzed in the 2006 investigation, only two contained total lead concentrations exceeding 50 mg/kg (the threshold triggering hazardous waste soluble testing), at 150 and 1,500 mg/kg; Waste Extraction Test soluble lead for those two samples was 2.9 and 5.7 mg/l, both below hazardous waste soluble thresholds.

## Human health risk assessment

The 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) and the Geocon 2013 HHRA Update evaluated lead separately from other metals using DTSC's LeadSpread model, run for each stockpile and for the future construction scenario. In every scenario, modeled blood lead concentrations for adults, children, and the conservative "pica child" receptor remained below the 10 µg/dL level of concern:

- Stockpile 1 surface soil: maximum detected concentration (MDC) of lead used as the exposure-point concentration (EPC); no receptor exceeded 10 µg/dL.
- Stockpile 2 surface soil: 95th percentile UCL of 30 mg/kg used; all percentiles below 10 µg/dL.
- Stockpile 3 surface soil: 95th UCL of 6.7 mg/kg used; no exceedance.
- Future construction zone soil (0–20 feet bgs): 95th UCL of 54 mg/kg; pica child blood lead remained below 10 µg/dL, and since a construction worker's exposure is less conservative than a pica child's, no unacceptable hazard was concluded for construction workers. Only 1.2% of 165 soil samples analyzed for lead exceeded 20 mg/kg (one detection of 150 mg/kg, one of 1,500 mg/kg).
- Future offsite resident (exposed only to construction dust): calculated respirable dust lead concentration of 0.15 µg/m³ using the maximum 1,500 mg/kg soil concentration, well below the model's default of 1.5 µg/m³.
- Hypothetical shallow groundwater user: maximum lead detected in groundwater was 3.4 µg/L, below the federal action level of 15 µg/L.

The 2013 HHRA Update compared 2012 Fenceline and Perimeter Boring data against the 2007 EPCs and found lead concentrations at similar orders of magnitude — for example, Stockpile 1 surface soil lead of 17 mg/kg (2012) versus 12 mg/kg (HHRA EPC), and Stockpile 3 lead of 34 mg/kg (2012) versus 12 mg/kg (HHRA EPC) — concluding the original 2007 risk and hazard calculations remained valid. This distinguishes lead's trajectory from barium's: lead never approached a residential or industrial screening level triggering a standalone response, so it rode alongside barium into the same containment remedy rather than driving separate remedial decisions.

## Removal verification thresholds during construction

The 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) set a lead verification threshold of 80 mg/kg, based on the DTSC residential screening level, for soil excavated from the BCS removal areas (Stockpile 3, and the western and eastern ends of Stockpile 2) during Interim Phase 1 construction. This threshold worked alongside the barium threshold of 1,000 mg/kg (groundwater protection); a sample had to satisfy both before an excavation area was accepted as clean.

In practice, lead was never the limiting parameter — every removal area that failed verification did so on barium, and lead consistently cleared 80 mg/kg by a wide margin:

| Removal area | Lead range (mg/kg) | Barium range (mg/kg) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stockpile 3 (29 verification samples) | 1.6–40 | 46–970 | Met both thresholds; no further excavation required |
| Stockpile 2 west end (10 samples) | 1.5–35 | 54–760 | Met both thresholds |
| Stockpile 2 east end (14 samples) | 1.5–35 | 54–760 | Met both thresholds |

Three Stockpile 3 sample locations and four Stockpile 2 locations initially exceeded the barium threshold (ranging 1,100–7,000 mg/kg) and required deepening by approximately one foot and resampling; lead was not cited as a reason for any resampling.

## Site-specific background and clean fill screening

A DTSC-approved Variance to the RDIP (April 9, 2020) established site-specific upperbound background concentrations of 120 mg/kg for barium and 3.8 mg/kg for lead, drawn from a 2007 Shaw background data summary, for screening proposed clean fill borrow sources. This lead background value became the practical screen for whether excavated native soil could be reused as cap material without being diverted into the BCS containment zones:

| Source area | Lead range (mg/kg) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Carpenter Road borrow area (Feb 2020) | 1.7–7.0 (background 2.0) | Approved as clean fill |
| Basin 5 borrow area (Jun 2020) | 5.8–11 (background 2.0) | Approved as clean fill |
| Stockpile 1 MSE wall footing (Apr 2020) | 1.1–6.7 | Within background range; suitable for reuse, but diverted to containment zones because of elevated barium |
| Stockpile 2 MSE wall footing (Jun 2020) | 3.5–22 | Same order of magnitude as background; diverted with barium-affected material |
| Bent 2 excavation end dumps (Oct 2020) | 2.6–13 (6 of 10 samples) | Failed on barium (4 of 10 samples ≥120 mg/kg); considered for partial clean cap only |
| Carpenter Road Shoofly (Apr 2021) | 1.4–3.8 | Met clean cap criteria for both barium and lead |

In every case where material was rejected as clean cap, the disqualifying parameter was barium, not lead — lead concentrations in these borrow and excavation areas consistently sat at or near the 3.8 mg/kg background value.

## Aerially deposited lead (ADL)

Separately from the barium-driven BCS program, shallow soil excavated from the unpaved northbound SR 99 shoulder between L Street and the Kansas Avenue off-ramp contained lead at hazardous waste concentrations under the 2016 DTSC–Caltrans *Soil Management Agreement for Aerially Deposited Lead-Contaminated Soils*. Under that agreement, "ADL-contaminated soil" is defined as soil containing total lead greater than 80 mg/kg and/or soluble lead (Waste Extraction Test) at or above 5.0 mg/L, based on a 95% upper confidence limit. This ADL soil was placed within the eastern end of the Stockpile 1 BCS Containment Zone in June–July 2020, subject to the agreement's requirements that ADL material remain at least five feet above the highest groundwater elevation, be covered with clean soil or pavement depending on lead level, and be sited away from potential contact with groundwater or surface water and protected from erosion.

## Air monitoring during grading

Airborne lead was monitored alongside barium during BCS grading activities between February and June 2020, using personal and perimeter sampling analyzed by NIOSH Method 7300 (modified). Results were compared to an action level of 1.5 µg/m³, based on the California Air Resources Board's 30-day average ambient lead public health standard. Reported airborne lead concentrations throughout grading, excavation, fill placement, and trenching activities remained below this action level, supporting the conclusion that contractor dust control measures were effective.

## Significance

Lead has not been an independent driver of remedial decisions at this site: it never exceeded its 80 mg/kg verification threshold in any BCS removal area, never pushed a clean fill source out of compliance on its own, and modeled blood lead levels never approached the 10 µg/dL threshold of concern under any exposure scenario evaluated. Its practical role has been as a secondary check riding alongside barium — every documented exceedance and resulting excavation deepening in the construction record was attributable to barium — except for the ADL soil, which is governed by its own separate agreement and placement conditions rather than the RDIP's stockpile verification thresholds.
