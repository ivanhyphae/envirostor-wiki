# Lead

## Overview

Lead (Pb) is identified as a contaminant of concern at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site, located along [[sr-132]] (SR 132) in Stanislaus County, California. Its presence has been documented in soil stockpiles and, to a lesser extent, in groundwater. Remedial actions and monitoring efforts have addressed lead concentrations in relation to site-specific cleanup goals and regulatory standards.

## Site record

Lead has been detected in soil samples from the Modesto Soil Stockpiles. For instance, in Stockpile No. 3, one soil sample had a lead concentration at or higher than 10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC), though the calculated 95% Upper Confidence Level for lead was below 10 times the STLC. {{quote: "One soil sample has a barium concentration higher than 10 times the Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration (STLC) and five soil samples have lead concentrations at or higher than 10 times the STLC. However, the calculated 95% Upper Confidence Level for barium and lead is well below 10 times the respective STLC."}} Further analysis using the Waste Extraction Test (WET) indicated that these samples fell below the STLC, classifying the material as non-hazardous. {{quote: "The results of these samples fell below the STLC and thus the material is not classified as a hazardous waste."}}

During the excavation and removal of Stockpile 3 in 2012, air monitoring indicated that airborne levels of lead were well below regulatory thresholds. {{quote: "The results of air monitoring completed during the BCS Stockpile 3 remedial excavation activities indicated that airborne levels of barium and lead were well below regulatory thresholds."}}

In the context of the SR 132 West Project, lead was a COPC (Chemical of Potential Concern) in the soil stockpiles. The Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP) provided a soil excavation verification threshold for lead of 80 mg/kg, based on the DTSC residential screening level. {{quote: "Lead - 80 mg/kg based on DTSC residential screening level"}}

During the removal and placement of Stockpile 3 BCS material between February and March 2020, verification soil samples showed lead concentrations ranging from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg, which were below the RDIP verification threshold of 80 mg/kg. {{quote: "Barium concentrations ranged from 46 to 970 mg/kg, and lead concentrations from 1.6 to 40 mg/kg."}}

Similarly, for the western and eastern ends of Stockpile 2 BCS removal, final verification soil samples contained lead concentrations ranging from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg, also below the 80 mg/kg threshold. {{quote: "Barium concentrations ranged from 54 to 760 mg/kg, and lead concentrations from 1.5 to 35 mg/kg."}}

Excavation of BCS from the southerly slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2 for MSE wall footing areas also showed lead concentrations generally within the range of the site-specific background concentration of 3.8 mg/kg and less than the RDIP removal threshold of 80 mg/kg. {{quote: "Lead concentrations were generally within range of the site-specific background concentration of 3.8 mg/kg, and less than the RDIP removal threshold of 80 mg/kg."}}

Aerially deposited lead (ADL) contaminated soil from the northbound SR 99 shoulder was placed in the eastern end of the BCS Stockpile 1 Containment Zone. This ADL soil contained lead concentrations in excess of California and Federal hazardous waste criteria. {{quote: "The ADL soil contains lead concentrations in excess of California and Federal hazardous waste criteria and was placed in accordance with the 2016 Soil Management Agreement for Aerially Deposited Lead-Contaminated Soils between DTSC and Caltrans (DTSC, 2016)."}}

Air monitoring during BCS grading activities between February 2020 and June 2020 included analysis for lead. Results indicated airborne levels of lead were below designated project action and risk levels. {{quote: "The results of air monitoring performed during BCS grading activities for the Project indicate airborne levels of total dust, barium, and lead below designated project action and risk levels, confirming effective dust c"}}

In the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective practical quantitation limit (PQL) in any of the ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10). {{quote: "During the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective practical quantitation limit (PQL) in samples from any of the ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10)."}} Historical lead concentrations in groundwater, as plotted in Figure 5 of the June 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Report, were generally below 4 micrograms per liter (µg/L). {{quote: "The figure shows concentrations generally below 4 micrograms per liter (µg/L) across all monitoring wells over this historical period."}}

## Regulatory status

The applicable numeric water quality threshold for lead is the California Department of Public Health Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for drinking water, set at 15 µg/L. {{quote: "The applicable numeric water quality threshold for lead is the California Department of Public Health Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for drinking water, which is set at 15 µg/L."}} As of the June 2014 sampling event, lead concentrations in all site monitoring wells remained below this 15 µg/L threshold. {{quote: "As of the June 2014 sampling event, lead concentrations in all site monitoring wells remained below this 15 µg/L threshold."}}

The DTSC residential screening level for lead was 80 mg/kg, used as a verification threshold in the RDIP. {{quote: "Lead - 80 mg/kg (residential screening level)"}}

## Open items

Quarterly groundwater monitoring is ongoing, which includes monitoring for lead. No specific open items related to lead are identified beyond routine ongoing monitoring.
