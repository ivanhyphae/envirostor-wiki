# Lead

## Overview

Lead is identified as one of the primary chemicals of concern in groundwater at the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA) by the dtsc|California Department of Toxic Substances Control. Its concentrations are monitored quarterly across the site's groundwater monitoring well network as part of the dissolved metals sampling program. This program is conducted by geocon-consultants|Geocon Consultants, Inc. under Task Order No. 17 (Project No. S9800-01-17).

## Site record

During the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective practical quantitation limit (PQL) in samples from any of the ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10). This indicates lead was not detected above the PQL in any well during this sampling round.

Figure 5 of the [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]] plots lead concentrations versus time from May 27, 2006, through the June 2014 event. The figure shows concentrations generally below 4 micrograms per liter (µg/L) across all monitoring wells over this historical period. A note on the figure specifies that values of one-half the PQL were assumed for concentrations reported below their PQL.

The report concludes that dissolved metals concentrations, including lead, for the June 2014 groundwater samples were reported at levels similar to historical concentrations. None of the reported dissolved metals concentrations exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values.

## Regulatory status

The applicable numeric water quality threshold for lead is the California Department of Public Health Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for drinking water, which is set at 15 µg/L. This threshold is displayed on Figure 5 of the [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]].

As of the June 2014 sampling event, lead concentrations in all site monitoring wells remained below this 15 µg/L threshold. No exceedance of the lead MCL has been documented in the monitoring record through June 2014.

The [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]] was submitted to [[caltrans]] (Sam Haack), dtsc|California Department of Toxic Substances Control (Randy Adams), and the CVRWQCB|Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (Steve Meeks).

## Open items

Quarterly groundwater monitoring is ongoing. The next sampling event was scheduled for September 2014. Continued monitoring of lead as a primary chemical of concern is required per the existing monitoring program. No specific open items related to lead are identified beyond routine ongoing monitoring.
