# Lead

## Overview

Lead is one of three primary chemicals of concern in groundwater identified by the California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). It is monitored quarterly across the site's groundwater monitoring well network as part of the dissolved metals sampling program conducted by geocon-consultants|Geocon Consultants, Inc. under Task Order No. 17 (Project No. S9800-01-17).

## Site record

During the June 2014 groundwater monitoring event, lead was not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the respective practical quantitation limit (PQL) in samples from any of the ten monitoring wells (MW-1 through MW-10). This represents a consistent absence of detectable lead across the entire well network for this sampling round.

Lead concentrations versus time are plotted in Figure 5 of the [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]], covering the period from May 27, 2006, through the June 2014 event. The figure shows concentrations generally below 4 micrograms per liter (µg/L) across all monitoring wells over the full historical record. A note on the figure indicates that values of one-half the PQL were assumed for concentrations reported below their PQL.

The report concludes that dissolved metals concentrations, including lead, for the June 2014 groundwater samples were reported at concentrations similar to historical levels and that none of the reported dissolved metals concentrations exceeded their respective numeric water quality threshold values.

## Regulatory status

The applicable numeric water quality threshold for lead is the California Department of Public Health Primary Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) for drinking water, set at 15 µg/L, as displayed on Figure 5.

As of the June 2014 sampling event, lead concentrations in all site monitoring wells remained below this threshold. No exceedance of the lead MCL has been documented in the monitoring record through June 2014.

The [[june-2014-groundwater-monitoring-report]] was submitted to caltrans|Caltrans (Sam Haack), DTSC (Randy Adams), and the CVRWQCB|Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (Steve Meeks).

## Open items

Quarterly groundwater monitoring is ongoing, with the next sampling event scheduled for September 2014. Continued monitoring of lead as a primary chemical of concern will be required per the existing monitoring program. No specific open items related to lead are identified beyond routine ongoing monitoring.
