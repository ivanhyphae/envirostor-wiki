## Overview

Kansas Avenue is a key geographic reference point within the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project area. It is used to delineate the location of former Stockpile 3 and associated [[barium-containing-soil]] (BCS). This area is situated east of [[sr-99]] and between Kansas Avenue and the Modesto Irrigation District (MID) water canal. The [[remedial-action-plan]] identified three stockpiles, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals, located south of Kansas Avenue.

## Site record

Former Stockpile 3, located east of [[sr-99]] between Kansas Avenue and the MID water canal, was excavated. A total of 3,075 truck-loads of BCS material, estimated at approximately 38,440 cubic yards, was transported from this location for placement within the Stockpile 1 fill containment zone. The excavation and removal of BCS from Stockpile 3 were performed in general accordance with the January 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP). The RDIP specified verification testing thresholds and excavation targets for Stockpile 3.

The [[remedial-action-plan]] identified three stockpiles of soil containing metals (primarily barium, strontium, and lead) occupying areas within the state right-of-way south of Kansas Avenue. These areas were located between Carpenter Avenue and East Emerald Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and [[sr-99]], and east of [[sr-99]].

Groundwater monitoring wells MW-9 and MW-10 were installed south of Kansas Avenue, with MW-9 located west of [[sr-99]] and MW-10 located east of [[sr-99]].

The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings generated during the construction of [[sr-99]] north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s.

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are located adjacent to State Route 99 (east and west) and south of Kansas Avenue in Modesto.

## Regulatory status

The [[remedial-action-plan]] for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] was approved by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) on March 14, 2018. The approved cleanup alternative involves the [[containment-of-stockpiles]] during construction of the [[sr-132]] West Freeway/Expressway Project. Containment is to be achieved through retaining walls, [[bridge-abutments]], and placement beneath the roadway. Unpaved portions will be covered with clean fill. Long-term protection is to be ensured through land use controls, annual inspections of containment features, and groundwater monitoring.

The DTSC letter dated December 7, 2012, to Caltrans discussed the need to address the use of soil stockpile material in the State Route 132 West Expressway project and/or its final disposition as it relates to human health and water quality prior to design and construction of the State Route 132 West Expressway project.
