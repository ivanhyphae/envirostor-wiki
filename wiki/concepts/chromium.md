Chromium is a metal that has been analyzed in soil and groundwater samples collected as part of the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case file. Its presence and concentration have been evaluated in relation to potential human health risks and regulatory thresholds.

## Soil Stockpile Investigations

In the Shaw 2006 investigation, chromium was among the metals analyzed in soil samples. In Stockpile #2, the Maximum Detected Concentration (MDC) for total chromium was selected as a chemical of potential concern (COPC). The MDC for total chromium was divided by seven, with one part assumed to be chromium VI and six parts assumed to be chromium III, based on the assumption that chromium VI was not used at the site and would have oxidized to chromium III over time. This assumption aligns with EPA's Integrated Risk Information System (IRIS) database methodology. In Stockpile #3, chromium was detected in surface soil samples at concentrations equal to or less than its respective background MDC and was therefore not selected as a COPC in the 2007 HHRA.

In the Technical Memorandum dated February 27, 2020, regarding the BCS Stockpiles Clean Cap Borrow Area Characterization Testing, chromium concentrations in soil ranged from 7.5 to 11 mg/kg, with a site-specific background concentration of 7.1 mg/kg. In the Technical Memorandum dated July 2, 2020, concerning Basin 5 Clean Cap Borrow Area Characterization Testing, chromium concentrations ranged from 8.5 to 11 mg/kg, with a site-specific background concentration of 8.6 mg/kg (based on 5-foot deep soil sample data).

## Groundwater Monitoring

Groundwater samples collected in June and October 2006 had chromium as one of the twelve metals selected as COPCs for evaluation of a hypothetical shallow groundwater user.

In subsequent groundwater sampling events, chromium concentrations were reported as follows:

*   **May 2012:** Range of <0.50 to 2.5 µg/l.
*   **September 2012:** Range of 1.0 to 1.3 µg/l.
*   **November 2012:** Range of 1.0 to 1.3 µg/l.
*   **December 2013:** Reported for nine of ten samples with ranges of <0.50 to 3.9 µg/l.
*   **February 2014:** Reported for all ten samples with ranges of <0.50 to 4.2 µg/l.
*   **June 2014:** Reported for all ten samples with ranges of <0.50 to 2.5 µg/l.
*   **April 2015:** Reported for all four samples with ranges of <0.50 to 2.5 µg/l.
*   **May 2016:** Reported for two samples with concentrations of 1.0 and 1.2 µg/l.
*   **October 2016:** Beryllium, chromium, mercury, silver, thallium, and vanadium were not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than their respective method detection limits (MDLs) for samples PL1 through PL5. For samples BG1 and BG2, cadmium, chromium, manganese, and nickel were reported, with no concentrations exceeding primary or secondary MCLs.
*   **March 2017:** Antimony, barium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, and vanadium were reported for samples PL1 through PL5.
*   **January 2018:** Antimony, arsenic, barium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, lead, manganese, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, vanadium, and zinc were reported for some or all of the samples.
*   **March 2018:** Chromium was reported for some or all samples.

An equipment blank collected in January 2013 had a chromium concentration of 1.2 µg/l. The report noted that this did not appear to significantly influence sample results as chromium concentrations in samples were similar to historical data.

## Regulatory Considerations

The calculated chromium VI concentration in the soil stockpiles was greater than its risk-based US EPA RSL but less than its CHHSL. The Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) considered it unlikely that chromium VI was present in the stockpiles.

None of the reported dissolved chromium concentrations in groundwater samples collected between May 2012 and March 2018 exceeded their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water.
