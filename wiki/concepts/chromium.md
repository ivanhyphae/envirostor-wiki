Chromium at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles has consistently registered as a secondary concern relative to barium, lead, and strontium: it appears throughout the stockpile soil, groundwater, and stormwater datasets at concentrations near or slightly above local background, but no measured exceedance of a soil, groundwater, or drinking-water threshold for total chromium is recorded in the case file. The one departure from that pattern is not a measured value but a modeling assumption — a calculated hexavalent chromium (chromium VI) fraction used in the 2007 and 2013 human health risk assessments — which the DTSC toxicologist reviewing the work considered unlikely to reflect actual site conditions.

## Soil: background-level detections, no COPC status

In the Shaw 2004 Preliminary Site Investigation, chromium was analyzed in all 194 stockpile soil samples and 49 native soil samples alongside antimony, arsenic, barium, iron, and strontium, but Shaw identified barium as the only metal present at concentrations of concern; chromium was not carried forward as a primary contaminant of potential concern (COPC) for the site's remedial investigations. Across the larger 278-sample stockpile soil dataset summarized in the Final FS Report and Draft Final RAP, chromium (along with arsenic, cobalt, and copper) was detected at concentrations only "slightly exceeding background concentrations," in contrast to barium, lead, nickel, vanadium, and zinc, which were found "considerably higher than background values."

In the 2013 clean-fill borrow area characterization for the BCS (barium-containing soil) stockpile cap, chromium concentrations in composite samples ranged from 7.5 to 11 mg/kg against a site-specific 2007 Shaw background value of 7.1 mg/kg, and in the Basin 5 borrow area testing (memo dated July 2, 2020), chromium ranged 8.5 to 11 mg/kg against a background of 8.6 mg/kg (based on 5-foot-deep sample data). Both borrow areas were found suitable to generate clean cover fill for capping Stockpiles 1 and 2 on the basis of these results.

## Human health risk assessment: total chromium and the chromium VI assumption

Chromium was selected as a COPC for the current-exposure evaluations of Stockpile 1 (with beryllium, cobalt, copper, lead, mercury, nickel, and barium) and Stockpile 2 (added to the seven metals evaluated via 95% UCL), and for the combined Stockpiles 1–3 future construction worker and offsite resident scenario. Because chromium VI was not used at the FMC facility associated with the site's contamination history, the 2013 HHRA Update reasoned that any chromium VI historically present would likely have oxidized to chromium III over the roughly 40 years the piles had been dormant. On that basis, the maximum detected concentration (MDC) for total chromium was apportioned as one part chromium VI to six parts chromium III — an assumption the assessment attributes to EPA's IRIS database methodology — and this split, rather than a directly measured chromium VI concentration, was used to derive exposure point concentrations for Stockpile 2 and for the Stockpiles 1–3 combined assessment.

The DTSC Human and Ecological Risk Office's review of Table 2 (chemicals found in the stockpiles) flagged this explicitly: the chromium VI concentration used in the risk calculations "is a calculated, rather than a measured, value," and while it exceeded the risk-based U.S. EPA Regional Screening Level, it remained below the California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL). The reviewing toxicologist noted there was no history of chromium VI use at the FMC facility or in the vicinity of the stockpiles and concluded it was "unlikely that chromium VI is present in the stockpiles." No independent chromium VI analytical method was applied to confirm or refute the assumption in the reviewed material.

For all four exposure scenarios in which chromium was a COPC — Stockpile 1 current exposure, Stockpile 2 current exposure, and the combined Stockpiles 1–3 future construction worker/offsite resident scenario — both the 2007 HHRA and the 2013 Update concluded that calculated cancer risks were orders of magnitude below the 1E-6 criterion and hazard quotients were below 1, and that the 2012 data confirmed rather than undermined those 2007 conclusions.

## Groundwater: repeated detection, no exceedance

Total or dissolved chromium was tested in nearly every semiannual groundwater monitoring round from 2006 through at least 2016, generally alongside arsenic and vanadium as metals "reported for each of the samples collected." In no round did chromium concentrations exceed a numeric drinking-water threshold, and this conclusion is repeated consistently across the May 2012, September 2012, November 2012, January 2013, June 2014, and other monitoring reports: "concentrations of arsenic, barium, chromium, strontium and vanadium were reported for the samples collected from each well [but] none of the reported concentrations exceed their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water."

One data-quality issue is documented: in the January 2013 groundwater QA/QC review, the equipment blank reported chromium at 1.2 µg/l, a low-level detection in a supposedly clean blank sample. Geocon's assessment was that the chromium concentrations reported for the actual well samples "appeared similar to those previously reported for each of the wells," so the blank contamination was judged not to have significantly influenced the results, and no data qualification was applied.

Chromium was not selected among the twelve metals used to evaluate the hypothetical shallow groundwater user pathway in the 2007 HHRA's onsite shallow groundwater COPC list in the version used for the noncancer hazard calculation (HI = 0.9); that list comprised barium, cobalt, copper, lead, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, selenium, silver, vanadium, and zinc, with chromium appearing instead in the parallel soil-metals lists.

## Stormwater: detected intermittently, not flagged against MCLs

In stormwater/surface water sampling rounds (October 2016, March 2017, January 2018, March 2018), chromium was among the dissolved metals reported for some or all of the perimeter and background samples (PL and BG series), grouped with barium, calcium, cobalt, copper, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, and vanadium. In the October 2016 round, chromium was specifically noted as *not* detected above method detection limits in the primary perimeter samples (PL1–PL5), while it was reported in background sample BG2. In the March 2017, January 2018, and March 2018 rounds, chromium was reported among the routinely detected dissolved metals without being singled out as exceeding a primary or secondary MCL; those exceedance flags in the same reports were reserved for lead (March 2018, sample PL3) and manganese (multiple rounds). The January 2018 lab QA/QC noted that several samples, including chromium, required dilution due to matrix interference or failing internal standards, but this was treated as a laboratory handling note rather than a data quality exclusion.

## What the record does and does not establish

Across soil, groundwater, and stormwater media, chromium is a persistently detected but consistently sub-threshold constituent at this site, and it was never named — unlike barium, strontium, and lead — as a primary chemical of concern by DTSC. Its most consequential appearance in the file is not an analytical result but a risk-assessment modeling choice: the assumed 1:6 chromium VI-to-chromium III split, used because chromium VI was not documented as an FMC-related contaminant and was assumed to have oxidized over decades of dormancy. That assumption produced the only reported exceedance associated with chromium at the site — of the RSL, not the CHHSL — and DTSC's own reviewer treated the underlying premise (that chromium VI is actually present) as unlikely rather than as a finding requiring further confirmation. The file does not record any follow-up chromium VI-specific testing or any regulatory requirement arising from that calculated value.
