## Overview

Chromium is a metal that has been analyzed in soil and groundwater samples collected as part of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case file. Its presence and concentration have been evaluated in relation to potential human health risks and regulatory thresholds.

## Site Record

In the Shaw 2006 investigation, chromium was among the metals analyzed in soil samples. In Stockpile #2, the MDC for total chromium was selected as a chemical of potential concern (COPC). The MDC for total chromium was divided by seven, with one part assumed to be chromium VI and six parts assumed to be chromium III, based on the assumption that chromium VI was not used at the site and would have oxidized to chromium III over time. This assumption aligns with EPA's Integrated Risk Information System (IRIS) database methodology. In Stockpile #3, chromium was detected in surface soil samples at concentrations equal to or less than its respective background MDC and was therefore not selected as a COPC in the 2007 HHRA.

Groundwater samples collected in June and October 2006 had chromium as one of the twelve metals selected as COPCs for evaluation of a hypothetical shallow groundwater user. In 2012 groundwater samples, chromium was reported with concentrations that were not exceeding numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water. For instance, in May 2012, chromium was reported with a range of <0.50 to 2.5 µg/l. In September 2012, chromium was reported with ranges of 1.0 to 1.3 µg/l. In November 2012, chromium was reported with ranges of 1.0 to 1.3 µg/l. In December 2013, chromium was reported for nine of ten samples with ranges of <0.50 to 3.9 µg/l. In February 2014, chromium was reported for all ten samples with ranges of <0.50 to 4.2 µg/l. In June 2014, chromium was reported for all ten samples with ranges of <0.50 to 2.5 µg/l. In April 2015, chromium was reported for all four samples with ranges of <0.50 to 2.5 µg/l. In May 2016, chromium was reported for two samples with concentrations of 1.0 and 1.2 µg/l. In March 2017, chromium was reported for samples PL1 through PL5. In January 2018, chromium was reported for some or all samples. In March 2018, chromium was reported for some or all samples.

An equipment blank collected in January 2013 had a chromium concentration of 1.2 µg/l. The report noted that this did not appear to significantly influence sample results as chromium concentrations in samples were similar to historical data.

## Regulatory Status

The calculated chromium VI concentration in the soil stockpiles was greater than its risk-based US EPA RSL but less than its CHHSL. The Human and Ecological Risk Office (HERO) considered it unlikely that chromium VI was present in the stockpiles.

None of the reported dissolved chromium concentrations in groundwater samples collected between May 2012 and March 2018 exceeded their respective numeric water quality thresholds for drinking water.
