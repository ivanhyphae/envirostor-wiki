STLC (Soluble Threshold Limit Concentration) and TTLC (Total Threshold Limit Concentration) are the California hazardous waste criteria under Title 22 used throughout the Modesto Soil Stockpiles project to determine whether excavated soil and stockpile material must be managed and disposed of as California-regulated hazardous waste. Both terms appear repeatedly across the Supplemental Site Investigation, Feasibility Study, and Remedial Action Plan documents as the regulatory basis for waste classification decisions, distinct from the CHHSLs and MCLs used for human-health and groundwater risk evaluation. The site's central chemicals of concern — barium, lead, and strontium, arising from FMC's historical processing of barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores — are the analytes for which STLC/TTLC comparisons drive whether stockpiled soil can remain in place, be reused, or must be manifested as hazardous waste for offsite disposal.

## Role in the project's regulatory framework

STLC and TTLC are identified in the acronym lists of the Final Feasibility Study (S9800-01-17, June 2014) and the Supplemental Site Investigation Report (S9525-06-44, Rev. 0313) as the applicable Title 22 hazardous waste thresholds, distinguished from the CHHSLs and MCLs applied to the same soil and groundwater data for risk-based decisions. TTLC establishes the total concentration of a constituent above which a waste is classified as hazardous; STLC establishes the concentration in a waste extraction test (WET) leachate above which a waste is similarly classified, and is used together with the "10x rule" relationship between total and leachable concentrations that is standard under the Title 22 framework referenced in these reports.

## Function in the stockpile characterization and remedial planning

Across the SSI, FS, and RAP/Draft Final RAP documents, STLC/TTLC comparisons for barium, lead, and strontium in the three soil stockpiles (and in soils excavated during ramp rehabilitation work at Stockpile 3, State Route 99 Kansas Avenue northbound off-ramp) determine the waste characterization pathway for material that cannot remain in place. This classification is what separates soil that can be handled as non-hazardous fill or cover from soil that must be manifested and transported to a permitted hazardous waste facility, directly bearing on remedial cost and disposal logistics for any excavation alternative evaluated in the Feasibility Study.

## Limits of the available record

The specific numeric STLC and TTLC threshold values, and the constituent-by-constituent comparison results (i.e., which stockpile samples, if any, exceeded STLC or TTLC for barium, lead, or strontium), are not given in the material excerpted here beyond their identification as defined acronyms. The source documents that would contain the detailed data tables and waste classification determinations — the SSI Report, the Final FS Report, and the RAP/Draft Final RAP — are listed among the project record but their body text and tables applying STLC/TTLC to specific sample results are not reproduced in the passages available.
