## Food Machinery Chemical Corporation (FMC)

### Overview
Food Machinery Chemical Corporation (FMC) is the former operator of an agricultural chemical manufacturing facility in Modesto, California. In this project, FMC is identified as the entity historically responsible for generating the waste materials that were later excavated and stockpiled by Caltrans along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County. The stockpiles consist of soil and debris contaminated primarily with organochlorine pesticides (e.g., DDT, dieldrin) and metals, originally deposited on FMC’s property. Caltrans acquired these materials in the course of previous road construction activities, and they have since been subject to remediation under DTSC oversight.

### Site record
The Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles (document number S1200-01-01, dated March 18, 20XX) documents FMC’s role as the source of contamination. The RAP refers to FMC as the “former operator” and notes that the stockpiles contain waste from pesticide production at the Modesto facility. Verification sampling of the stockpiles (conducted in phases from 2019 onward) has confirmed the presence of DDT at concentrations up to 12 mg/kg and dieldrin up to 2.8 mg/kg in certain stockpile zones (e.g., Stockpile Area 3). These values exceed the project-specific human health screening levels established by DTSC for residential use (DDT: 1.7 mg/kg; dieldrin: 0.03 mg/kg). The spatial distribution of contaminants is heterogeneous, with higher concentrations found in the central and western portions of the stockpile footprint. The RAP attributes this pattern to the original disposal practices of FMC.

### Regulatory status
FMC is a named responsible party under the California Hazardous Substance Account Act (HSAA) and is subject to an enforceable agreement with DTSC (Immediate Removal Action Workplan, approved 2018). The applicable cleanup standards for the stockpiles are based on the DTSC-modified screening levels for unrestricted land use per California Code of Regulations Title 22. As of the RAP date, FMC has completed Phase 1 off-site disposal (approximately 8,500 tons) and is obligated to fund Phase 2 removal (estimated 4,200 tons) under a negotiated cost-recovery settlement with Caltrans. Compliance is conditioned on the submission of quarterly progress reports to DTSC and completion of post-removal confirmation sampling.

### Open items
- Final resolution of FMC’s cost‑recovery liability for Caltrans’ oversight and long‑term monitoring after Phase 2.
- Pending DTSC approval of the final removal completion report for the stockpile area.
- Potential soil vapor intrusion investigation at the original FMC facility site (referenced in SR_132_DEIR_EA, Section 4.8) where underlying groundwater contamination remains unaddressed.
