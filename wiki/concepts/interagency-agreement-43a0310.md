## Overview

Interagency Agreement **43a0310** is a legally binding instrument governing the remediation of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site along State Route 132 in Stanislaus County, California. The source document on file is **43A0310-04 A01 Executed**, which constitutes the fully executed version of the agreement. This agreement establishes the roles, responsibilities, financial arrangements, and technical scope between the participating agencies—principally the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) and the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), consistent with California’s hazardous substance cleanup framework. The agreement delineates the terms under which the soil stockpiles are characterized, removed, treated, and disposed, and it sets the schedule and verification requirements for remedial actions.

## Agreement details

The executed document (43A0310-04 A01) specifies the following elements from the project record:

- **Parties**: Caltrans (lead) and DTSC (oversight). Other agencies (e.g., Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board) may be referenced in later amendments.
- **Effective date**: The date of execution shown on the document signature block (exact date not provided in the available source excerpt).
- **Scope**: Investigation and cleanup of contaminated soil stockpiles at multiple locations adjacent to SR 132 (milepost ranges as defined in the agreement). Contaminants of concern include metals (arsenic, lead, barium) and organic compounds (TPH, PAHs) at concentrations above residential and/or industrial screening levels.
- **Deliverables**: Work plans, monitoring reports, health and safety plans, and a final remedial action completion report. Specific deliverables are enumerated in the agreement’s scope of work exhibit.
- **Funding**: Cost-sharing formula and maximum obligation amounts (not shown in the available excerpt).
- **Duration**: The agreement remains in effect until all remedial actions are certified complete and the case is closed (no sunset date provided).

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable authority**: The agreement is executed under the authority of the California Health and Safety Code (Chapter 6.8, Hazardous Substance Account Act) and DTSC’s oversight regulations (Cal. Code Regs. tit. 22, div. 4.5). 
- **Compliance**: As of the date of execution, the agreement establishes the baseline for compliance. No violations or non-compliance findings are documented in the agreement itself.
- **Certification**: The final completion report must demonstrate attainment of cleanup goals defined in the agreement (typically background levels for metals, MCLs for groundwater, and residential screening levels for soil). The site remains under DTSC oversight until certification is granted.

The agreement itself contains no open items; it is an executed, actionable document. However, the following deliverables and milestones are required under the agreement and remain pending until completed:
