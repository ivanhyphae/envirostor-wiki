## Overview

The Virginia Corridor Trailway is a proposed multi-use trail facility identified within the California Environmental Impact Report (DEIR/EA) for the State Route 132 (SR 132) corridor improvement project in Stanislaus County, California. Its role within the project is as a potential mitigation or community amenity element, integrated into the larger highway widening and realignment work. The trailway is referenced in the SR 132 DEIR/EA Appendices A–F as part of the project’s environmental documentation, but specific design details, exact alignment, and construction phases are not fully characterized in these source documents.

## Site record

The Virginia Corridor Trailway is documented in the *SR 132 DEIR/EA Appendices A–F*, specifically within the context of project alternatives and environmental impact analysis. According to these appendices, the trailway is intended to connect existing and planned regional trail networks, running parallel to portions of SR 132. The documented facts from the record include:

- **Location**: The trailway is planned within the SR 132 project footprint, primarily between the communities of Modesto and the Virginia Corridor area, adjacent to the highway right-of-way. Exact milepost or stationing references are not provided in the reviewed appendices.
- **Design parameters**: The DEIR/EA appendices describe the trailway as 10–12 feet wide, paved, with shoulders and vegetative buffers. No specific lengths or square footage are given.
- **Construction timeline**: The trailway is linked to Phase 2 of the overall SR 132 project (2019–2020 timeline per the document), but no separate schedule is noted for the trailway itself.
- **Environmental context**: The appendices note that the trailway alignment passes through areas with documented soil contamination from historical agricultural operations, including elevated arsenic and lead levels near SR 132. However, specific soil sampling results for the trailway corridor itself are not included in these sources.

## Regulatory status

The Virginia Corridor Trailway is subject to the same environmental and regulatory framework as the larger SR 132 project. Key regulatory actions documented in the sources include:

- **California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA)**: The trailway is analyzed in the DEIR/EA prepared by Caltrans (Lead Agency). The document is pending certification; no final decision is recorded in the appendices.
- **National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA)**: The project’s Environmental Assessment (EA) is bundled with the DEIR; the trailway is included in the cumulative impact analysis.
- **Surface water and drainage**: The appendices indicate the trailway must comply with National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permits for stormwater runoff during construction and operation.
- **Hazardous materials**: Because the trailway crosses areas of known soil contamination (arsenic, lead, pesticides), Caltrans has committed to a Remedial Action Plan (RAP) under DTSC oversight before trail construction proceeds. Compliance status is pending as of the document’s date.

## Open items

- **Detailed design**: The DEIR/EA appendices do not provide final engineering for the trailway alignment, including specific cross-sections, fencing, lighting, or intersection treatments.
- **Soil remediation verification**: The record states that if trailway construction occurs within identified soil contamination zones, additional confirmatory sampling and a site-specific Health and Safety Plan will be required. This is not yet documented.
- **Mitigation monitoring**: A Mitigation Monitoring and Reporting Program (MMRP) is referenced in the appendices, but specific trailway-related monitoring commitments (e.g., dust control, noise barriers) are not quantified.
- **Community input**: The appendices note public comments regarding trail connectivity and safety; no formal response or design modification is recorded.
- **Funding**: The DEIR/EA does not specify a dedicated funding source for the trailway; it is listed as a “potential enhancement” but not a committed project component.
