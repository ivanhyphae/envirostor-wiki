The Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the [[caltrans]] Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site details the strategy for managing contaminated soil stockpiles generated during the construction of State Route 99 in the 1960s. This plan, approved by the [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], proposes containing the stockpiles within the structural elements of the future State Route 132 West Expressway/Freeway project.

### Site Description and Contamination

The site consists of three soil stockpiles, totaling approximately 160,000 cubic yards, situated south of Kansas Avenue, east and west of the State Route 99/Kansas Avenue interchange. These stockpiles, averaging 20 feet in height, originated from the excavation of an industrial waste pond on Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) property during early 1960s State Route 99 construction. The FMC facility had processed materials including [[barium]] and [[strontium]], with residual liquids disposed of in unlined ponds.

A 2007 Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) determined that, under current management practices—which include perimeter fencing, restricted access for Caltrans personnel, vegetative cover, a prohibition on soil removal, and groundwater monitoring—the soil and groundwater do not present an unacceptable human health risk. Further soil sampling in 2012 corroborated these findings.

### Proposed Remedial Action

Caltrans' selected remedial action, designated Alternative 4, is containment through the integration of the stockpiles into the State Route 132 West project. This approach received concurrence from the [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. The containment strategy involves placing the stockpiles behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath the roadway pavement of the State Route 132 project, specifically between Needham Street and Carpenter Avenue.

The State Route 132 West project is planned in two phases:
*   **Initial Construction Phase:** Scheduled from 2018 to 2020, this phase includes the construction of retaining walls and bridge abutments, along with paving the southern portion of the site. The northern section will be covered with a graded engineered soil layer.
*   **Ultimate Build-Out Phase:** This phase is slated to commence in 2028.

### Regulatory Approval and Next Steps

The draft final RAP underwent review and was approved by the [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] for public notice and comment. Following the public comment period, the [[dtsc]] will review all submitted comments and prepare a Responsiveness Summary. Subsequently, the [[dtsc]] may issue its approval of the draft final RAP, potentially incorporating modifications based on public feedback received on both the RAP and the draft Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA).

Upon approval of the RAP by the [[dtsc]], a Remedial Design Implementation Plan will be developed under the oversight of the [[dtsc]] and [[central-valley-rwqcb]]. This plan will provide detailed specifications for the containment of the stockpiles as part of the State Route 132 project.
