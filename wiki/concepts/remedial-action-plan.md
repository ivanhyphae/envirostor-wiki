## Remedial Action Plan

The Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles governs the final disposition of approximately 160,000 cubic yards of barium-impacted soil stockpiled since the early 1960s within Caltrans right-of-way south of Kansas Avenue. It was developed by Caltrans under the oversight of [[dtsc]], in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], and was formally required as a precondition for regulatory approval of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. DTSC approved the RAP on March 14, 2018, selecting containment of the stockpiles within the highway structure itself as the final remedy — an outcome that ties the fate of the contaminated soil directly to construction of the SR 132 West project and imposes long-term operation, maintenance, and monitoring obligations on Caltrans.

### Origins and regulatory requirement

The stockpiles originated when Caltrans purchased a 4.3-acre parcel from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) in 1961 and excavated pond tailings and native soil — containing elevated barium and PAHs from FMC's former evaporation ponds — to build embankment fill for the SR99/132 interchange. DTSC, via Interagency Agreements with Caltrans dating to January 2005, reviewed successive rounds of site characterization and determined that before the SR 132/99 West Expressway could be designed and constructed, Caltrans and StanCOG would need to obtain DTSC and RWQCB concurrence on the stockpiles' use in construction or their final disposition, through either a Removal Action Workplan or a RAP depending on cost. DTSC's December 17, 2009 letter conditioned continued no-action status on Caltrans maintaining the perimeter fence, limiting access to authorized personnel, prohibiting excavation or off-site soil removal, maintaining vegetative cover and grade, and maintaining groundwater monitoring — conditions that remained the operative interim management regime while the RAP was developed.

### Data gaps and the 2012–2013 Supplemental Site Investigation

A July 26, 2012 meeting among Caltrans, DTSC, RWQCB, and [[geocon-consultants-inc]] identified three data gaps that had to be closed before a remedy could be finalized: fenceline sampling to assess offsite/vertical migration, perimeter sampling to define lateral stockpile limits for future consolidation, and additional sampling to address elevated cadmium detected in Stockpiles 2 and 3 during a 2004 investigation. Geocon's resulting Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI), revised March 2013 in response to DTSC's February 15, 2013 comment letter, found that fenceline and perimeter boring results did not indicate lateral or vertical migration of metals — notably [[barium]] — beyond state or federal residential screening levels (with an isolated arsenic exception attributed to background), and that cadmium was confirmed not to exceed residential screening levels, eliminating it as a chemical of potential concern. This closed the identified data gaps and cleared the path for the HHRA update and RAP development to proceed.

### Alternatives evaluated and the selected remedy

The Draft Final RAP evaluated remedial alternatives including no action, removal and off-site disposal, and containment through SR 132 West construction. Alternative 3 (Removal) — excavating and transporting all 160,000 cubic yards to an off-site disposal facility — was found technically feasible and ARAR-compliant but was rejected as the recommended remedy because it caused the greatest short-term air quality impacts and was less cost-effective than containment. Caltrans recommended, and DTSC and RWQCB concurred in, **Alternative 4 (Containment)**: consolidating the stockpiles behind retaining walls, within bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement of the SR 132 West project, with clean fill cover over unpaved portions. This alternative was selected because it achieves long-term protection of human health and the environment by eliminating the human exposure pathway and minimizing stormwater infiltration to groundwater beneath the stockpiles, while balancing contaminant isolation against removal/disposal cost and the project's need for elevated fill material between Needham Street and Carpenter Avenue.

### Public review and approval

DTSC and RWQCB reviewed and approved the Draft Final RAP for public noticing prior to circulation. Caltrans released the Draft EIR/EA with the attached Draft Final RAP for public review from January 18 to March 17, 2017 (extended from an original March 3 close), and a joint Caltrans/DTSC/RWQCB public hearing was held February 22, 2017 at Mark Twain Junior High School in Modesto (moved from the Red Event Center due to a scheduling conflict). Of 300 total comments received on the combined EIR/EA and RAP, 62 specifically addressed the Draft Final RAP; DTSC, in consultation with RWQCB, prepared responses to RAP-specific comments, which Caltrans incorporated into its own Appendix J responses as the CEQA lead agency. Public comments raised concerns about the durability and maintenance of a concrete cap, the adequacy of past site monitoring, and unquantified claims of elevated illness among nearby residents; DTSC's responses reiterated that current stockpile management (fencing, access restriction, vegetative cover, groundwater/surface water monitoring) was protective of human health and that Alternative 4 would achieve long-term protection once implemented. DTSC approved the RAP on March 14, 2018, following completion of this comment-and-response process.

### Post-approval obligations

Approval of Alternative 4 requires Caltrans to enter into an Operation and Maintenance Agreement with DTSC and to prepare an Operation and Maintenance Plan subject to DTSC approval, including annual inspection of the pavement and other containment features. Groundwater monitoring continues, and DTSC will evaluate the containment remedy every five years to confirm it is performing as designed. Construction is planned in two phases: Phase 1 (targeted 2018–2020) builds retaining walls and bridge abutments and paves the southern half of the site, covering the northern half with an engineered soil layer; Phase 2 (targeted to begin 2028) completes the four-lane build-out. DTSC and RWQCB oversight of a subsequent Remedial Design Implementation Plan governs the engineering details of how the stockpiles are physically contained during construction.
