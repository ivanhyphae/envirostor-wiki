## Overview

The **Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan** is a project-specific quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) document governing the verification, characterization, and acceptance of imported soil used as structural clean fill in the remediation of Basin 5 at the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA). The plan establishes the sampling protocols, analytical parameters, acceptance criteria, and documentation requirements for ensuring that imported fill material meets regulatory and project specifications. Its role is to prevent recontamination of the remediated basin and to maintain compliance with DTSC-approved remedial action objectives.

## Site record

The Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan (document #: Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan.md) was developed by Caltrans and its environmental contractors in coordination with the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). Key documented details include:

- **Regulatory oversight**: The plan was incorporated by reference into the DTSC-approved Remedial Action Plan for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site.
- **Acceptance criteria**: Imported fill must meet California Title 22 hazardous waste thresholds, with specific action levels for constituents of concern identified in the Basin 5 source media (including arsenic, barium, chromium, lead, and total petroleum hydrocarbons). Maximum contaminant levels for groundwater protection (MCLs) were applied as screening benchmarks where applicable.
- **Sampling protocol**: A minimum of one composite sample per 500 cubic yards of imported fill, with discrete confirmatory samples for every 2,000 cubic yards. Sampling includes field duplicates, trip blanks, and equipment blanks at a rate of 1 per 20 samples.
- **Analytical parameters**: Full Title 22 metals by EPA Method 6010B/7471A, total petroleum hydrocarbons by EPA Method 8015M, volatile organic compounds by EPA Method 8260B, and pH. Polychlorinated biphenyls and pesticides were included on an as-needed basis based on source history.
- **Verification results**: As of the most recent project reporting (Phase 2, 2022), approximately 14,500 cubic yards of imported clean fill were placed in Basin 5. All incoming loads received pre-acceptance analytical clearance. No exceedances of action levels were documented in the verification sampling for the placed fill.
- **Documentation**: Each load was accompanied by a source material certification attesting to non-hazardous status, and chain-of-custody records for all off-site soil source origins were maintained.

## Regulatory status

- **Applicable standards**: The plan operates under California Code of Regulations Title 22 (hazardous waste classification) and DTSC’s Remedial Action Plan for the Modesto Soil Stockpiles Site (approved May 2019). Specific acceptance thresholds were developed by Caltrans and concurred with by DTSC in a technical memorandum dated August 15, 2019.
- **Compliance**: The current status is **in compliance** with the approved plan. All placed fill has met acceptance criteria. Monthly reporting to DTSC includes a summary of verification results and a cumulative fill volume log.
- **Agency roles**: DTSC retains enforcement authority. The Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board has been notified of the fill placement but has not asserted independent jurisdiction over this activity.

## Open items

- **Pending certification**: A final certification report documenting the total volume placed, all analytical results, and closure conditions for Basin 5 fill placement has not been submitted to DTSC. The report is due 60 days after completion of the final lift.
- **Contingency plan**: In the event that future imported fill fails acceptance criteria (i.e., exceeds action levels), the plan requires immediate cessation, segregation, and DTSC consultation. No such events have occurred, but the contingency protocol remains untested.
- **Long-term institutional controls**: The placement of clean fill in Basin 5 does not remove the need for any deed restriction or land use covenant that may be required for the remediated basin as a whole. This issue remains under review by DTSC.
