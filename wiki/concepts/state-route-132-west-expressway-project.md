The State Route 132 West Expressway Project is the transportation improvement — a new limited-access expressway connecting SR 99 to SR 120/SR 108 in Stanislaus County — whose right-of-way and construction footprint intersect the stockpiled soils that are the subject of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles case file. The expressway project is the reason the stockpiles were characterized, remediated, and closed out under a Remedial Action Plan (RAP): the highway could not be built through or near the affected parcels until the soil contamination question was resolved, and the project's Final Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (FEIR/EA), dated March 2, 2018, and the RAP approval of the same period are the documents that tie the environmental cleanup to the construction schedule.

## Relationship between the expressway project and the stockpile remediation

The stockpiles addressed in this case file sit within or adjacent to the SR 132 West Expressway alignment. The environmental review for the highway — captured in the Final EIR/EA main document and its appendices (Appendices A–G and H–K), both dated March 2, 2018 — incorporates the soil contamination findings and remedial planning developed separately under the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles investigation. The Remedial Action Plan for the stockpiles was approved on March 14, 2018, two days before the FEIR/EA date, and the Caltrans Modesto Statement of Findings (SOF) is dated March 15, 2018 — placing the RAP approval and the SOF immediately ahead of the final EIR/EA, consistent with the cleanup determination being a precondition for finalizing the highway's environmental documentation.

## Interagency and consultant framework

The site characterization and remedial work that the expressway project depended on were carried out under a series of interagency agreements and task orders between Caltrans and the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), including agreement 43A0310-04 (with Amendment 01 and Task Order Amendment 02) and agreements 43A0404-04 and 43A0487-04, all executed to fund and direct DTSC's oversight of the Modesto Stockpiles work. Geocon Consultants, Inc. performed the site characterization and remedial planning under these agreements, producing the Supplemental Site Characterization Workplan (final, September 20, 2012) and follow-up remedial action reporting, including the March 1, 2012 SR 132 West Expressway follow-up report addressed to Steven R. Becker, that fed into the RAP ultimately approved in March 2018.

## Significance for the expressway project

Because the RAP approval (March 14, 2018) and the Modesto SOF (March 15, 2018) predate the Final EIR/EA by only days, the environmental clearance for the SR 132 West Expressway construction was completed against a soil cleanup determination that had just been finalized rather than one resolved years in advance. This sequencing means the expressway's environmental record and the stockpile remediation record are administratively contemporaneous, and a reviewer examining the FEIR/EA's treatment of hazardous materials or soil conditions along the alignment should read it alongside the RAP approval letter and SOF as the operative closure documents for the contamination issue the highway project needed resolved.
