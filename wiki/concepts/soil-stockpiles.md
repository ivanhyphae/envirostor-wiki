The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are a remediation site located in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, associated with the [[sr-132-west-expressway-project]]. These stockpiles contain barium-containing soil (BCS) and aerially deposited lead (ADL) that were generated from excavations related to the former FMC Corporation evaporation ponds and placed in lifts for future highway alignment. The implemented remedy involves the consolidation and containment of these soils by incorporating them as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway, capped with clean fill and structural pavement. The [[dtsc]] is the lead regulatory agency overseeing the investigation and remediation, with [[caltrans]] as the proponent and responsible party. An [[operation-and-maintenance-plan]] and a [[land-use-covenant]] are in place to manage the site post-remediation.

## Site Description and History

The soil stockpiles are situated adjacent to and west of Emerald Avenue, within two designated containment zones that were consolidated and capped with clean fill material as highway embankment fill during Interim Phase 1 construction of the SR 132 West Project. These zones correspond to Stockpile 1, located between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50, and Stockpile 2, located between approximate SR 132 Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30. The remedy consists of consolidation and clean fill capping of barium-containing soil (BCS). Typical cross-sections illustrate the BCS and ADL consolidation zone beneath roadway structural pavement sections, cover fill, and imported retaining wall backfill material. For Stockpile 1, this extends from STA 1125+50 to 1132+00. For Stockpile 2, this extends from STA 1135+35 to 1141+71 and STA 1141+71 to 1148+30.

The stockpiles originated from material excavated in the early 1960s for the future SR 132 highway alignment. This material was partially generated from excavations in evaporation ponds that contained elevated heavy metals, notably barium, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). These ponds were used from the 1950s to the 1970s by the Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC Corporation) for the discharge of liquid residue from their barium processing operations. In 1961, a 4.3-acre parcel at the southwest corner of the FMC facility, including a portion of the ponds, was purchased by the State for the construction of SR 99. Native soil and pond tailings were removed and placed in lifts to form bridge abutments and embankment fills for the future SR 99/132 Interchange south of FMC. The soil was stockpiled in three distinct locations within Caltrans right-of-way: Stockpile 1 south of Kansas Avenue and west of Emerald Avenue; Stockpile 2 south of Kansas Avenue, between Emerald Avenue and SR 99; and Stockpile 3 south of Kansas Avenue and east of SR 99. Stockpiles 1 and 2 are bordered by residential areas to the south.

Previous environmental investigations, including an Initial Site Assessment (ISA) in 2003 and a Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) in 2004, identified potential residual chemicals from the FMC impoundments. The PSI detected elevated barium concentrations in stockpile samples above the commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL). Elevated cadmium was also detected in some samples from Stockpile 2 and Stockpile 3. Further investigation in 2006 characterized the soil stockpiles and assessed groundwater quality. Groundwater sampling indicated that the groundwater met drinking water standards for the analyzed constituents. A Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) in 2007 identified metals (notably barium) and PAHs as primary COPCs in the soil stockpiles, but concluded that none posed potential health risks to current or future construction workers, offsite residents, or trespassers. DTSC, in a letter dated December 17, 2009, concurred that the stockpiles, as managed on Caltrans ROW, did not pose a risk to human health and directed Caltrans to continue managing them until the SR 99/Highway 132 Interchange was constructed, while maintaining the existing groundwater monitoring system.

A Supplemental Site Investigation (SSI) Workplan was developed in September 2012 to address data gaps identified by DTSC and RWQCB, including perimeter ROW fenceline stockpile soil sampling, perimeter stockpile soil sampling to define lateral limits, and additional sampling in areas of elevated cadmium. The purpose of this SSI was to gather data for re-evaluation of adjacent resident exposure and potential vertical contaminant migration, to be incorporated into the Remedial Action Plan (RAP).

## Regulatory Oversight and Maintenance

The [[dtsc]] oversees the remediation and maintenance of the soil stockpiles under an Operation and Maintenance Agreement. A [[land-use-covenant]] is recorded on the remediated area to restrict its use. DTSC provides annual cost estimates for oversight activities, including review of the O&M Plan, cost estimates, remedial action completion reports, O&M Agreements, and Land Use Covenants.

For Fiscal Year 2022-2023, DTSC oversight costs were estimated for O&M Plan review, fiscal year cost estimate, review and approval of the Interim Phase 1 Remedial Action Completion Report, completion of the O&M Agreement, completion of the Land Use Covenant, and project management. For Fiscal Year 2023-2024, DTSC oversight costs were estimated for fiscal year cost estimate, completion of the Land Use Covenant, review and approval of the Annual Inspection (due by January 31, 2024), and project management. For Fiscal Year 2024-2025, DTSC oversight costs were estimated for fiscal year cost estimate, review and approval of the Annual Inspection (due by January 18, 2025), amendment/review of the Interagency Agreement (set to expire on 6/30/2025), and project management.

Caltrans is responsible for performing five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the containment measures, focusing on monitoring and maintenance of implemented containment and potential for exposure. The Annual Operation and Maintenance Inspection Report dated January 26, 2026, was approved by DTSC on June 11, 2026. This report noted that vegetation along the roadway was sparse, but erosion control materials were limiting erosion. Encouraging vegetation along the roadway is recommended to prevent erosion.
