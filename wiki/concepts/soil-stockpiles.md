## Overview

The Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles are a remediation site located in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California, associated with the [[sr-132-west-expressway-project]]. The stockpiles contain [[barium]]-containing soil (BCS) and aerially deposited lead (ADL). The implemented remedy involves consolidation and containment of these soils by incorporating them as highway embankment fill for the SR 132 West Expressway, capped with clean fill and structural pavement. The [[dtsc]] is the lead regulatory agency overseeing the investigation and remediation, with [[caltrans]] as the proponent and responsible party. An [[operation-and-maintenance-plan]] and a [[land-use-covenant]] are in place to manage the site post-remediation.

## Site record

The soil stockpiles are located adjacent and west of Emerald Avenue, specifically within two designated containment zones: Stockpile 1 between approximate SR 132 Stations 1125+50 and 1133+50, and Stockpile 2 between approximate SR 132 Stations 1135+35 and 1148+30. These areas were consolidated and capped with clean fill material as highway embankment fill during Interim Phase 1 construction of the SR 132 West Project. The remedy consists of consolidation and clean fill capping of barium-containing soil (BCS).

Typical cross-sections show the BCS and ADL consolidation zone beneath roadway structural pavement sections, cover fill, and imported retaining wall backfill material. For Stockpile 1, this extends from STA 1125+50 to 1132+00. For Stockpile 2, this extends from STA 1135+35 to 1141+71 and STA 1141+71 to 1148+30.

Caltrans is responsible for performing five-year reviews to assess the effectiveness of the containment measures. These reviews will focus on monitoring and maintenance of the implemented containment and potential for exposure.

## Regulatory status

The [[dtsc]] oversees the remediation and maintenance of the soil stockpiles under an Operation and Maintenance Agreement. A [[land-use-covenant]] is recorded on the remediated area to restrict its use. The [[dtsc]] also provides annual cost estimates for oversight activities, including review of the O&M Plan, cost estimates, remedial action completion reports, O&M Agreements, and Land Use Covenants.

For Fiscal Year 2022-2023, DTSC oversight costs were estimated for O&M Plan review, fiscal year cost estimate, review and approval of the Interim Phase 1 Remedial Action Completion Report, completion of the O&M Agreement, completion of the Land Use Covenant, and project management.

For Fiscal Year 2023-2024, DTSC oversight costs were estimated for fiscal year cost estimate, completion of the Land Use Covenant, review and approval of the Annual Inspection (due by January 31, 2024), and project management.

For Fiscal Year 2024-2025, DTSC oversight costs were estimated for fiscal year cost estimate, review and approval of the Annual Inspection (due by January 18, 2025), amendment/review of the Interagency Agreement (set to expire on 6/30/2025), and project management.

The Annual Operation and Maintenance Inspection Report dated January 26, 2026, was approved by DTSC on June 11, 2026. This report noted that vegetation along the roadway was still sparse, but erosion control materials were limiting erosion. Vegetation along the roadway is encouraged to prevent erosion.

## Open items
