## Overview

The Draft Final Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA) is the formal regulatory document detailing the selected remedy for cleanup. Prepared by Caltrans under oversight from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), the Draft Final RAP was submitted to DTSC for review and approval. It specifies the remedial actions to address contaminated soil stockpiles at the Highway 132 Maintenance Station property, including excavation, off-site disposal, and soil cover implementation.

## Site Record

The Draft Final RAP is a key milestone in the project chronology documented in the *Hearing Summary Report SR132* (dated November 2021, prepared by the DTSC Public Participation Specialist). According to this report:

- The Draft Final RAP was submitted to DTSC for review and approval **in June 2021**. This followed the release of a draft RAP for public comment in early 2021.
- The report notes that DTSC conducted a **public hearing on April 28, 2021**, to receive comments on the draft RAP. The hearing was held virtually due to COVID-19 restrictions.
- Comment period for the draft RAP closed on **May 7, 2021**. DTSC received written comments from stakeholders, which were addressed in the Final Draft.
- The Draft Final RAP identifies the remedial action as **excavation and off-site disposal of impacted soil stockpiles**, followed by placement of a clean soil cover at the site. The report confirms the project involves an estimated **8,000 cubic yards** of contaminated soil.
- The contaminants of concern addressed in the RAP include **arsenic, lead, and petroleum hydrocarbons**, consistent with historical findings from site characterization. The report references barium at **760 mg/kg** in stockpile samples (exceeding the DTSC screening level of 43 mg/kg for industrial soil) and hexavalent chromium above screening thresholds.

The *Hearing Summary Report* was signed by the DTSC Project Manager (Megan Huhman, P.G.) and approved on **November 15, 2021**.

## Regulatory Status

The Draft Final RAP was developed under DTSC oversight per **California Health and Safety Code, Chapter 6.8**.

- Applicable standards include DTSC's California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) and the **Site-Specific Screening Level (SSSL)** negotiated between DTSC and Caltrans for barium. The RAP prescribes that after remediation, soil cover must be maintained to prevent exposure, with post-excavation confirmation sampling to achieve **arsenic < 12 mg/kg** and **lead < 80 mg/kg** in surface soils (0–3 feet).
- The project is currently in a **regulatory pending** status: the Draft Final RAP was submitted for DTSC approval in June 2021, but the *Hearing Summary Report* does not confirm final approval. DTSC's Environmental Monitoring Division (EMD) reviewed the RAP and requested additional groundwater monitoring and soil gas characterization (see Open Items).
- Compliance for the existing stockpiles is pending approval of the remedy; no confirmation sampling results are documented in the hearing summary.

## Open Items

The *Hearing Summary Report SR132* identifies unresolved issues that the Draft Final RAP must address:

1. **Additional Groundwater Characterization**: DTSC's EMD required **quarterly groundwater monitoring** for one year at monitoring wells MW-5 and MW-6 to assess potential impacts from stockpile leachate. The Draft Final RAP must incorporate these results before final approval.
2. **Soil Gas Sampling**: DTSC requested soil gas sampling beneath the stockpile area to evaluate vapor intrusion risk, particularly for petroleum hydrocarbons. The RAP does not include a final plan for this.
3. **Disposal Facility Verification**: Caltrans must provide documentation from the receiving landfill confirming that off-site disposal of the 8,000 cubic yards was completed (as of the November 2021 report, this had not been confirmed).
4. **Cover Maintenance Plan**: The RAP requires a **55-year land use restriction** and maintenance plan for the clean soil cover, but the draft plan was not fully detailed in the hearing record.
