The California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) is a risk-based guideline used in California to evaluate potential human health risks associated with contaminants in soil and groundwater. These levels are developed by the California Environmental Protection Agency (Cal/EPA) and its Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) to complement federal screening levels. CHHSLs are used to determine if concentrations of contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) at a site pose an unacceptable risk to human health under various exposure scenarios.

## Site Characterization and CHHSL Application

During the Preliminary Site Investigation (PSI) conducted by Shaw in 2004, barium concentrations in stockpile soil samples exceeded commercial/industrial CHHSLs. Cadmium concentrations also exceeded the commercial/industrial CHHSL in Stockpiles #2 and #3.

In 2006, additional site investigation (SI) was performed to further characterize the soil stockpiles and compare analytical results to background conditions and CHHSLs. A Human Health Risk Assessment (HHRA) was performed in 2007 for the COPCs in the stockpiles and groundwater. For the purposes of the HHRA, cadmium was not considered a COPC due to a lack of elevated concentrations identified during the 2006 SI. Strontium was also not considered a COPC as its maximum concentration was significantly lower than the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's (USEPA) residential Regional Screening Level (RSL).

Subsequent investigations, including Fenceline Borings and Perimeter Borings, found that barium concentrations in surface soil samples did not exceed residential or industrial CHHSLs. For example, surface soil samples from five borings along the north side of Stockpile 2 contained barium concentrations greater than 1,000 mg/kg, but none exceeded the residential CHHSL of 5,200 mg/kg or the industrial CHHSL of 63,000 mg/kg. Similarly, perimeter borings showed barium concentrations ranging from 76 to 1,600 mg/kg, with none exceeding residential or industrial CHHSLs.

Cadmium borings indicated that cadmium was not detected at concentrations exceeding the laboratory reporting limit of 1.0 mg/kg. This contrasted with the Shaw 2004 PSI, which had identified elevated cadmium concentrations exceeding the industrial CHHSL of 7.5 mg/kg in some samples from Stockpiles 2 and 3. However, subsequent analyses in 2006 and 2012 did not reproduce these elevated cadmium findings, leading to the conclusion that the 2004 cadmium data was likely unreliable.

Current exposure assessments for Stockpiles 1, 2, and 3, utilizing data from 2012 borings, consistently found that metal concentrations did not exceed respective residential CHHSLs or RSLs. For instance, in Stockpile 1, barium concentrations were 240 mg/kg compared to the HHRA EPC of 130 mg/kg, but still well below the residential CHHSL. In Stockpile 2, barium had a maximum detected concentration (MDC) of 4,300 mg/kg in 2012 samples, compared to 1,100 mg/kg in 2006 SI samples, but still below the residential CHHSL. In Stockpile 3, barium was detected at 1,600 mg/kg in 2012 samples, compared to 250 mg/kg in 2007 HHRA EPCs, also below the residential CHHSL. Cadmium was detected in some 2012 surface soil samples at concentrations less than the residential CHHSL of 1.7 mg/kg.

## Regulatory Determination

CHHSLs are used as screening levels to determine if further investigation or action is needed. The DTSC, in a letter dated December 17, 2009, responded to Caltrans' Final Preliminary Endangerment Assessment (PEA) and concluded that the soil stockpiles, as managed by Caltrans, do not pose a risk to human health for Caltrans workers, trespassers, or adjacent residents. This conclusion was based, in part, on the understanding that contaminant concentrations were below risk-based screening levels, including CHHSLs.
