Clean Fill Source Testing is the process of sampling and analyzing soil to ensure it meets environmental criteria for use as clean fill on the [[sr-132-west-expressway-project]]. This testing is essential for verifying that the soil is free from contaminants that could pose risks to human health or the environment.

### Proposed Clean Fill Source Testing Plans

Two distinct plans for clean fill source testing have been documented for the project.

One plan, outlined in the "Hwy 132 Clean Fill Source Testing Exhibit," identified a proposed clean fill source within the Route 132 Right-of-Way (ROW) at and east of Carpenter Road. This plan specified a maximum excavation depth of 5 feet, with 12 borings to be advanced to this depth. From these borings, three 4-point composite samples were to be created. Analytical testing was to include pesticides and metals for the 0-2 foot interval and metals for the 2-5 foot interval. This testing was detailed on Sheet L-13, an engineering layout plan for the intersection of Route 132 and N. Carpenter Road.

A second plan, detailed in the "Basin 5 Clean Fill Sampling Plan," focused on a basin area within the SR 132 Basin 5 Proposed Clean Fill Source. This plan indicated a 10-foot cut depth, with an estimated volume of 20,000 cubic yards. Six test pit locations were designated, and the sampling protocol involved two depth-discrete 3-part composite samples, resulting in six total composite soil samples. Testing parameters were specified for three depth intervals: 0-2 feet (pesticides and metals using EPA methods 8081A/6010B/7471A), 2-5 feet (metals using EPA methods 6010B/7471A), and 5-10 feet (metals using EPA methods 6010B/7471A). A 48-hour laboratory turnaround time was required. This plan is associated with engineering layout plans, including sheet L-15, which depicts highway improvements for Route 132 in Modesto.
