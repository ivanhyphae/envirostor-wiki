The Weather Station Foundation Slab/Anchor, located at approximate Station 1138+60 within the southern portion of the [[stockpile-2-bcs-containment-zone]], was one of two structure foundation excavations addressed in [[geocon-consultants-inc]]'s February 28, 2022 Technical Memorandum on Stockpile 2 BCS foundation spoil disposal. Its foundation excavation, 4.5 feet deep, was completed in July/August 2021, before any workplan for managing the resulting spoils existed — the excavated barium-containing soil (BCS) was spread on the adjacent ground surface rather than contained, and the memorandum was written retroactively to address that material along with spoil anticipated from a nearby planned excavation (Overhead Sign #1, Station 1139+30).

The weather station excavation generated approximately 2 cubic yards of BCS material. Combined with the 18.2 cubic yards projected from the Overhead Sign #1 excavation, the two sources were expected to yield a conservative total of 40 cubic yards of BCS spoil, to be consolidated into a single stockpile rather than managed separately by origin.

## Disposition of the spread material

Because the weather station spoil had already been spread on the ground surface rather than stockpiled at the time the memorandum was written, the workplan called for it to be gathered along with the Overhead Sign #1 spoil, pre-moistened, and placed in a stockpile covered with minimum 6-millimeter plastic sheeting, secured with ropes and weights and surrounded by erosion control measures as necessary. The combined stockpile was to be sampled with eight discrete grid-pattern samples composited into two 4-part composites, analyzed for Title 22 metals (EPA Methods 6010B and 7471A for mercury) on an expedited 48-hour turnaround, with soluble metals testing (WET/TCLP) available if total metal results were elevated.

Resulting total barium and lead concentrations were to be compared against site-specific background levels of 120 mg/kg (barium) and 3.8 mg/kg (lead). If neither constituent exceeded background, the material could be considered for onsite reuse subject to Caltrans and DTSC approval of the reuse location; if either agency withheld approval, the material was to be profiled for offsite disposal at Forward Landfill in Manteca or another accepting facility. Because the weather station spoil was folded into the combined 40-cubic-yard stockpile rather than sampled separately, no constituent-specific results attributable to the weather station excavation alone are given in the source material, and the memorandum does not report which disposition — onsite reuse or landfill disposal — was ultimately selected.

## Significance

The weather station excavation is notable in the record chiefly for what it reveals about handling practice rather than for its own analytical results: BCS-bearing spoil from a completed foundation excavation was left spread on open ground for roughly six to seven months before a containment and sampling plan was drafted to address it, and the eventual sampling plan addressed it only as part of a pooled stockpile with the still-unexcavated Overhead Sign #1 material rather than as a discrete, traceable source.
