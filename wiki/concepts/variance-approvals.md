The variance approvals concept anchors the Bent 2 excavation soil stockpile testing to a prior DTSC decision that redefined what counted as acceptable clean cap material for Stockpile 2: the *Variance to Remedial Design Implementation Plan*, dated April 9, 2020, established an upperbound background threshold for barium of 120 mg/kg, and this threshold became the standard against which [[geocon-consultants]] evaluated the Bent 2 stockpile soil in the October 30, 2020 technical memorandum. Because four of ten samples exceeded that threshold, the Bent 2 material failed to meet the variance's clean cap criteria outright, forcing Geocon to propose a narrower, conditional use of the soil rather than its unrestricted placement as cap.

## What the variance established

The April 9, 2020 variance document, approved by DTSC, set the barium background value of 120 mg/kg as the criterion for soil to qualify as clean cap on [[bent-2-area]] Stockpile 2. It also underpinned an earlier, separate approval: a fill berm generated from the lower portion of the Stockpile 2 mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall footing excavation had already been approved by DTSC for use as clean cap, and a portion of the Bent 2 end dumps were placed directly on top of that pre-approved berm. The variance thus governed two distinct batches of excavated soil at the same location — the MSE footing material, which met the criterion and was approved, and the Bent 2 excavation material, which did not.

## Testing against the variance threshold

Ten discrete samples (STK1–STK10) from the Bent 2 end dump piles were analyzed for barium and lead by California Laboratory Services under EPA Method 6010B. Barium was detected in all ten samples, ranging from 31 to 230 mg/kg; four of the ten equaled or exceeded the 120 mg/kg threshold. Lead was detected in six of ten samples, from 2.6 to 13 mg/kg, staying within the range of the site-specific lead background of 3.8 mg/kg and raising no comparable issue.

The barium exceedances meant the Bent 2 stockpile soil, as a whole, did not satisfy the clean cap criteria the variance had established — [[clean-soil-cover-suitable]] status could not be claimed for this material under the existing approval.

## The proposed conditional use

Rather than treating the failed criterion as disqualifying the material outright, Geocon's memorandum proposed a fallback use: the soil could serve as partial clean cap, limited to six inches or less, on the upper surface of Stockpile 2, on the reasoning that the full [[clean-fill-capping]] system for that surface would also include a structural pavement section (base material and asphalt/concrete) placed over the entire top of the stockpile — an additional barrier layer that would overlie the marginal soil. This proposal was explicitly conditioned on further DTSC review and approval; the memorandum does not record that DTSC had granted this expanded variance at the time of writing. For the northerly slope of Stockpile 2, where no comparable pavement cap was planned, the memorandum stated that fill material from previously approved clean fill sources would be required instead, effectively excluding the Bent 2 material from that portion of the cap.

## Status and significance

The memorandum states this information would be included in the Removal Action Completion Report following completion of the Stockpile 1 and 2 clean fill capping activities, but the source material does not show DTSC's decision on the six-inch partial cap proposal. The variance approval process here matters because it determined, sample by sample, which excavated soil could be reused on site as cap versus which required substitution with previously approved clean fill — a distinction with direct cost and disposal implications for approximately 4,000 cubic yards of Bent 2 material, and one left partially unresolved in this record.
