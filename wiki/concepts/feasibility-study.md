## Feasibility Study

### Overview
The Feasibility Study (FS) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case file is the planning document that identifies, screens, and evaluates remedial alternatives for addressing contaminated soil stockpiles at the SR 132 project site in Stanislaus County, California. Developed under Caltrans SR 132-99 Task Order 4, the FS provides the technical basis for selecting a remedy. Its role in this project is to translate site characterization data (from the Remedial Investigation/RI) into actionable cleanup options, considering media (soil and groundwater), contaminants of concern (COCs—primarily barium and arsenic), and regulatory thresholds. The FS was finalized in September 2021.

### Site Record
- **Document reference**: *Feasibility Study Report, SR 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles*, prepared under Caltrans SR 132-99 Task Order 4, dated September 2021.
- **Project context**: The FS follows the RI (completed May 2020) and precedes the Proposed Remedial Action Plan (PRAP) (submitted August 2022). It addresses three stockpile locations: Stockpile A (east end, ~15,000 cubic yards), Stockpile B (west end, ~8,000 cubic yards), and Stockpile C (central, ~4,000 cubic yards), all composed of native soil excavated during highway construction.
- **COCs and concentrations**: The FS cites maximum concentrations from the RI:
  - Barium: 760 mg/kg in Stockpile A (exceeding Caltrans action level of 500 mg/kg).
  - Arsenic: 45 mg/kg in Stockpile B (exceeding background threshold of 12 mg/kg).
  - Groundwater beneath Stockpile C: barium at 1,400 µg/L (exceeding MCL of 1,000 µg/L).
- **Screening criteria applied**: EPA Region 9 Preliminary Remediation Goals (PRGs), California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs), and DTSC modified screening levels. The FS uses a tiered screening approach: first to human health (residential and industrial scenarios), then to ecological (Hazard Index = 1.0), and finally to cumulative risk (Target Cancer Risk = 1E-06).
- **Alternatives evaluated**:
  - Alternative 1: No Action (baseline).
  - Alternative 2: On-site consolidation with low-permeability cover (geomembrane cap) and institutional controls (deed restrictions).
  - Alternative 3: Off-site disposal at a Class II landfill (non-hazardous waste).
  - Alternative 4: On-site treatment via soil mixing with Portland cement (solidification/stabilization) and placement in a capped area.
- **Cost estimates**: Alternative 2 estimated at $2.5 million; Alternative 3 at $4.8 million; Alternative 4 at $3.2 million (2021 dollars, includes monitoring).
- **Effectiveness analysis**: The FS rates each alternative on implementability, short-term and long-term effectiveness, and cost. Alternative 4 scored highest due to permanent contaminant immobilization and reduced long-term maintenance.

### Regulatory Status
- **Applicable standards**: 
  - Barium: MCL in groundwater = 1,000 µg/L (California Title 22); Caltrans action level in soil = 500 mg/kg (based on background).
  - Arsenic: Caltrans background threshold in soil = 12 mg/kg (Stanislaus County alluvial deposits); no MCL for soil, but DTSC uses 0.4 mg/kg for residential screening (not applied here because site is industrial highway right-of-way).
- **Agencies**: California Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) oversees the Voluntary Cleanup Agreement; California Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) Central Valley Region has oversight for groundwater impacts under Resolution No. R5-2019-0073.
- **Compliance status**: The FS was approved by DTSC via letter dated December 17, 2021, with conditions requiring: (1) confirmation of groundwater monitoring well network around Stockpile C; (2) quarterly monitoring for barium until MCL attainment; and (3) a pilot test of solidification/stabilization before full-scale implementation of Alternative 4.

### Open Items
- **Pilot test pending**: As of last project status update (March 2023), a 500-cubic-yard pilot test of cement mixing for Stockpile A was planned but not yet initiated. Results will determine whether alternative 4 proceeds or requires modification.
- **Groundwater monitoring data**: Quarterly sampling from wells MW-1 and MW-2 (installed June 2022) had two exceedances of barium MCL in Q4 2022 (1,100 µg/L and 1,290 µg/L), but the trend is undocumented beyond initial reporting. DTSC requested a trend analysis in the next quarterly report (due April 2023).
- **Deed restriction negotiation**: For Alternative 2 (cover), Caltrans and DTSC disagree on the duration of institutional controls (perpetual vs. 30-year term). No resolution documented.
