# Feasibility Study

The Feasibility Study (FS) is the CERCLA-derived analysis that identified, screened, and evaluated remedial alternatives for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles and recommended the containment remedy ultimately adopted in the [[remedial-action-plan]]. Prepared by Geocon Consultants under the June 22, 2012 Interagency Agreement between [[dtsc]], the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), and [[caltrans]], the FS moved through Draft and Final versions before DTSC approval, with the Final FS submitted June 24, 2014 and the Draft Final FS having been the subject of DTSC comments dated February 11, 2014 that were incorporated into the Final version.

## Purpose and method

The FS followed USEPA's CERCLA *Guidance for Conducting Remedial Investigations and Feasibility Studies* (1988). It first identified general response actions (GRAs) — no action, institutional controls, removal, containment, treatment, and reuse/recycle/reclaim — then screened remedial technologies and process options within each GRA against effectiveness, implementability, and cost. Technologies surviving that screen were carried into a detailed comparative evaluation against the nine National Contingency Plan (NCP) criteria: two threshold criteria (overall protection of human health and the environment; compliance with ARARs), five balancing criteria (long-term effectiveness and permanence; reduction of toxicity, mobility, and volume; short-term effectiveness; implementability; cost), and two modifying criteria (regulatory acceptance; community acceptance) — the latter two addressed only after the draft FS was finalized and the public comment period on the RAP had occurred, not within the FS itself.

## Screening outcome

Treatment and reuse/recycle/reclaim were eliminated at the screening stage on implementability and cost grounds, consistent with DTSC's *Proven Technologies and Remedies Guidance, Remediation of Metals in Soil* (2008), which found that among 188 metals-contaminated sites it reviewed, "containment by capping" and "excavation and offsite disposal" were the most frequently implemented remedies. The FS cited site-specific factors favoring a streamlined screening: the primary COPC (barium) is present as barite, a low-solubility form; contamination is confined above natural grade to depths less than 15 feet; groundwater COPC concentrations are below MCLs; and no ecological habitat or sensitive receptors are affected. Four alternatives were retained for full evaluation:

- **Alternative 1 – No action**
- **Alternative 2 – Institutional controls**
- **Alternative 3 – Removal** (excavation and offsite disposal)
- **Alternative 4 – Containment**

Institutional controls were retained even though they largely describe the site's existing managed condition, because they remained a fallback if the SR-132 Project were not built.

## Comparative evaluation

No numerical cleanup goal was set. Because the human health risk assessment (HHRA) and its 2013 update found risks and hazard indices below regulatory thresholds under current management, the FS adopted a performance-based cleanup goal: eliminate exposure routes to COPCs and reduce the potential threat to groundwater, with DTSC and CVRWQCB retaining authority to determine when the remedy is complete.

| Alternative | Protectiveness | Cost basis | Implementability |
|---|---|---|---|
| No action | Lowest of the four; site controls and monitoring would lapse | Least costly | Most implementable |
| Institutional controls | Higher than no action; DTSC-accepted as protective under current conditions | Second lowest | Second most implementable |
| Removal | Greatest theoretical protectiveness; short-term air quality and traffic impacts from ~175 truckloads/day over ~30 days for removal, plus a similar volume and duration to import replacement fill | Approximately $21.5 million (removal of all stockpiles) or $2.65 million (Stockpile 3 only); no funding source identified | Least implementable of the four — technically feasible but constrained by cost and by the need to replace removed fill with an even larger imported volume to build the project |
| Containment | Effective long-term; stockpiles serve directly as project embankment fill | Funded through the SR-132 Project construction budget | Most implementable given available funding and the coincidence of remedy and project need |

The removal alternative's greenhouse gas emissions from heavy equipment and trucking were calculated at 529,200 pounds of CO2. Removal disposal costs assumed a portion of stockpile soil (primarily Stockpile 1) would go to a Class II (non-hazardous) facility and a portion (primarily Stockpile 2) to a Class I (California hazardous) facility. At CVRWQCB's request, the FS separately costed complete removal and offsite disposal of Stockpile 3 alone with clean-fill replacement, rather than evaluating removal of all three stockpiles as the only removal scenario.

## Recommended alternative

The FS recommended **Alternative 4 – Containment**, achieved by incorporating the stockpiles as embankment fill, retaining-wall backfill, and bridge-abutment fill for the SR-132/SR-99 interchange — the same purpose for which the stockpiles were placed on the site in the early 1960s. The FS reasoned that removal and replacement costs were "not warranted for the degree of protection of human health and the environment achieved," given that the HHRA and its update already found no unacceptable risk under current conditions, and that construction funding for containment was already available through the SR-132 Project while no funding source existed for removal. The Statement of Reasons accompanying the Draft Final RAP later quantified this cost differential: the containment remedy, including purchase of clean replacement soil where needed, was calculated as approximately 20 times less costly than excavation and offsite disposal.

## Regulatory disposition

The Draft Final FS was submitted to DTSC and CVRWQCB for review; DTSC's comment letter is dated February 11, 2014. The Final FS was submitted June 24, 2014, addressed to DTSC's Randy Adams, CEG, and is dated by DTSC's records as approved June 30, 2014. The RAP subsequently summarized the FS results and stated that the FS "has been reviewed and approved by the DTSC and CVRWQCB." The FS's recommended alternative was carried forward into the RAP's proposed remedy without a stated change, including the same two-phase construction schedule (interim progress phase by 2018; ultimate build-out by 2028) and the same treatment of Stockpile 3 as fully contained within the interim phase.
