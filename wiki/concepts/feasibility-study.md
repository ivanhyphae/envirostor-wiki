The Feasibility Study (FS) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles project evaluated potential remedial actions to address contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) identified in the stockpiles. The FS process, conducted by Geocon Consultants Inc., involved identifying General Response Actions (GRAs), screening remedial technologies, and evaluating retained alternatives against nine criteria established by the National Contingency Plan (NCP). The primary goal was to support the selection of an appropriate remedy for the stockpiles, which were intended for use as embankment fill in the SR 132 Project. The FS was approved by the DTSC and Central Valley-RWQCB on June 30, 2014.

## Site Record

The FS evaluated four alternatives: No Action, Institutional Controls, Removal, and Containment.

*   **Alternative 1: No Action** was deemed to provide the lowest level of protection and regulatory acceptance, despite being the least costly and most implementable. It would not reduce the toxicity, mobility, or volume of COPCs.
*   **Alternative 2: Institutional Controls** involved continued management of the stockpiles, including limiting access, maintaining fencing and vegetative cover, and continuing groundwater monitoring. This alternative was considered protective of human health and the environment under current conditions, with regulatory acceptance from the DTSC. It would not reduce the toxicity, mobility, or volume of COPCs but would maintain acceptable risk levels for trespassers and offsite residents.
*   **Alternative 3: Removal** involved excavating and disposing of stockpile soil offsite. This alternative was considered to provide the greatest protection but also presented the highest short-term impacts (air quality, traffic), the highest cost (estimated at \$21.5 million for full removal and replacement), and was deemed the least implementable due to the need to import significant amounts of clean fill for the SR-132 Project. It would provide good overall protection by eliminating potential exposure to COPCs.
*   **Alternative 4: Containment** was identified as the recommended alternative. This involved incorporating the stockpiles as fill material in the construction of the SR-132/SR-99 interchange. The stockpiled soil would be contained behind retaining walls and bridge abutments and beneath roadway pavement. An interim progress phase (by 2018) would involve constructing a two-lane roadway over southern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2, with northern portions graded for drainage and capped with a 6- to 12-inch clean soil cap. Stockpile 3 was planned for complete containment within the interim progress phase. The ultimate build-out (by 2028) would ensure complete containment. This alternative was favored for its effectiveness, technical feasibility, cost-effectiveness (approximately 20 times less than removal), and ability to minimize potential contaminant migration.

The [[human-health-risk-assessment]] (HHRA) conducted in 2007 and updated in 2012 indicated that the soil stockpiles, as managed, do not pose an unacceptable risk to human health for current or future offsite residents, trespassers, or construction workers. The HHRA identified metals, notably [[barium]], and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) as primary COPCs in soil. Cadmium was not considered a COPC in the HHRA due to lack of elevated concentrations in the 2006 SI. Strontium was also not considered a COPC as its maximum concentration was significantly below the USEPA residential Regional Screening Level (RSL).

The cleanup goal for the project was determined to be performance-based, ensuring no route of exposure to COPCs and reducing potential threats to groundwater, rather than achieving a numerical cleanup goal, as the HHRA demonstrated acceptable health risks under current management.

## Regulatory Status

The DTSC and Central Valley-RWQCB reviewed and approved the FS. The DTSC concurred with the HHRA update findings in a letter dated April 4, 2013. The recommended remedial alternative (Containment) is to be implemented under the oversight of the DTSC and Central Valley-RWQCB, who will make the final determination of completion. A land use covenant will be recorded to preclude certain sensitive land uses.

## Remedial Design

Following the FS, details of the project construction were to be presented in a Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP).
