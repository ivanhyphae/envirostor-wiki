## Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility (NBAR)

### Overview

The Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility (NBAR) for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] project was prepared by the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) as required by California Health and Safety Code (HSC) section 25356.1(e). The original intent of this requirement was to identify potentially responsible parties (PRPs) who would have an aggregate allocation exceeding 50%, thereby enabling them to convene arbitration. However, the statutory provisions for arbitration were repealed by Senate Bill 1018, effective June 27, 2012. Consequently, the NBAR's sole remaining purpose is to promote settlement and reduce transaction costs. DTSC determined that in this case, with a relatively small number of PRPs and Caltrans bearing the costs for remediation and future operation and maintenance, a specific allocation of liability percentages would not expedite settlement or reduce transaction costs.

### Site Record

DTSC determined that Caltrans assumes full responsibility for remediation and operation and maintenance costs associated with the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]], State Route 132, West Freeway/Expressway Project, Stanislaus County, California. This includes costs for the recommended remedial alternative of containment and associated monitoring programs administered to evaluate the effectiveness of the remedial alternative.

### Regulatory Status

The requirement for a Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility stemmed from HSC section 25356.1(e). However, the arbitration provisions associated with this requirement were repealed by Senate Bill 1018 (Stats 2012, Chap 39), effective June 27, 2012. Accordingly, all statutory provisions and procedures associated with the arbitration proceeding were repealed.
