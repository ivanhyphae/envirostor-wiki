## Overview

The Preliminary Nonbinding Allocation of Responsibility (PNBAR) is a procedural mechanism used by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) to preliminarily assign responsibility for remediation costs among potentially responsible parties (PRPs) at a site, without making a final determination of liability. In the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County, CA), the PNBAR process is referenced in the Final Caltrans Cover Letter and Revised Administrative Record dated May 18, 2015. This document transmits the revised administrative record to DTSC for review, which includes the basis for any PNBAR issued by DTSC. The PNBAR serves to allocate cleanup costs proportionally among identified PRPs, including Caltrans as the owner and operator of the stockpiles, based on their respective contributions to contamination. It is a key step in the enforcement process under the California Health and Safety Code, facilitating cost recovery without necessarily proceeding to a formal liability determination.

## Site record

- The Final Caltrans Cover Letter Revised Administrative Record (May 18, 2015) explicitly references the PNBAR as part of the administrative package submitted to DTSC. The cover letter states that the revised record “includes the basis for any PNBAR issued by DTSC,” indicating that DTSC has the authority to issue a PNBAR based on this record.
- The site involves soil stockpiles along SR 132 in Stanislaus County, California. The stockpiles contain industrial waste generated during Caltrans highway maintenance activities, including elevated levels of metals such as barium (up to 760 mg/kg), lead, and other constituents. The contamination stems from historical operations, including the storage of materials used for road maintenance.
- The administrative record documents Caltrans’ contamination of the site and the need for remediation. The PNBAR built on this record to preliminarily assign responsibility for cleanup costs among Caltrans and any other potentially responsible parties, though the cover letter does not specify the exact allocation percentages.
- The revised administrative record includes a decision document (likely a Remedial Action Plan or similar) and supporting technical reports detailing the extent of contamination and proposed remedial actions. The PNBAR references these documents to establish the basis for cost allocation.

## Regulatory status

- The PNBAR is governed by DTSC’s authority under the California Health and Safety Code, specifically sections related to hazardous substance removal and remediation. DTSC uses the PNBAR as a non-binding, informal tool to encourage PRPs to voluntarily agree to cost-sharing before formal enforcement.
- At the time of the May 18, 2015 letter, the PNBAR process was ongoing, with DTSC reviewing the revised administrative record. The cover letter requests DTSC to “review the enclosed revised Administrative Record” and indicates that the PNBAR may be issued following that review. There is no document in the provided source indicating that a final PNBAR was issued or that allocation percentages were finalized.
- The site itself is subject to DTSC oversight under the California Hazardous Waste Control Law. The administrative record shows that Caltrans is the primary PRP due to its ownership and operation of the stockpiles.
