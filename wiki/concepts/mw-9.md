## Overview

MW-9 is a groundwater monitoring well installed as part of the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. It is located south of Kansas Avenue and west of SR 99. Its installation and initial sampling were documented in the June 2012 groundwater monitoring report.

## Site record

MW-9 was installed in June 2012, along with MW-10, as an addition to the existing monitoring well network at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. MW-9 is situated on the west side of SR 99, south of Kansas Avenue, while MW-10 is on the east side of SR 99 at the same location. The installation and sampling of MW-9 were detailed in the *"Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California,"* prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012. This report was part of a submission to the [[dtsc]] that also included the May 2012 and July 2012 monitoring reports. MW-9 is part of the overall monitoring well network designated as MWs 1 through 10 at the site.

In September 2013, the depth to groundwater at MW-9 was measured at 33.74 feet below the top of the well casing (TOC). The groundwater flow direction was determined to be toward the southeast with an average gradient of 0.0007. During the September 2013 sampling event, MW-9 was found to have a calcium-dominant composition, as indicated by its Stiff diagram.

In June 2014, the depth to groundwater at MW-9 was measured at 35.09 feet below TOC. The groundwater flow direction was toward the south-southeast with an average gradient of 0.0008. The Stiff diagram for MW-9 in June 2014 also indicated a calcium-dominant composition.

In October 2016, MW-9 was noted to have sufficient groundwater for measurement, with a depth to groundwater of 45.19 feet below TOC. The groundwater flow direction was toward the south at an average gradient of 0.007.

In April 2017, MW-9 was dry.

In October 2018, MW-9 was dry.

In April 2019, MW-9 was dry.

The source material does not provide specific concentration data, screening level comparisons, or exceedance determinations for MW-9.

## Regulatory status

On November 29, 2012, Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist at the [[dtsc]], issued a letter to Richard Stewart, P.G., of Caltrans. In this letter, DTSC concurred with the groundwater monitoring reports submitted, including the June 2012 report that documented the installation and sampling of MW-9. DTSC's review was conducted in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region. DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available. The primary chemicals of concern at the site are barium, lead, and strontium, which originate from historical operations by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation. These operations involved processing barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores, and the soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings from Caltrans' construction activities in the early 1960s.
