MW-9 is one of two monitoring wells (with [[mw-10]]) installed in June 2012 upgradient of and adjacent to the three Modesto soil stockpiles, extending the original eight-well network (2006, [[mw-1]] through [[mw-7]] and MW-8) so that background groundwater quality could be characterized alongside the stockpile-adjacent wells. Across roughly twelve years of quarterly and later annual sampling, MW-9 was the well most consistently associated with nitrate and manganese exceedances at the site, and it also showed one of the two instances (with MW-5) of a well's water type shifting mid-monitoring from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant, an anomaly Geocon repeatedly flagged as possible mixing of water types rather than attributing it to the stockpiles.

## Well construction and setting

MW-9 was installed on May 29–30, 2012, by Gregg using a hollow-stem auger rig, together with MW-10, at a location north of the stockpiles and adjacent to SR 99 on the west side (MW-10 is on the east side). The well was constructed to approximately 40 feet using 2-inch Schedule 40 PVC casing with a 10-foot, 0.010-inch-slotted screen set from about 29.5 to 39.5 feet, a #2/12 Monterey sand filter pack from 27.5 to 40 feet, a two-foot bentonite seal, and Portland cement grout to surface. The boring encountered interbedded silt and sand of the Modesto Formation to the full 40-foot depth, consistent with prior site descriptions. Groundwater was first encountered at 33.5 feet. The well was developed on June 18, 2012, with roughly ten well volumes (8.5–10 gallons) removed by hand-bailing and submersible pump.

## Water type and general minerals

In the initial June 2012 sampling, MW-9 was calcium-dominant (MW-10 was sodium-dominant at that time). MW-9 remained calcium-dominant through most of the monitoring history — September and November 2012, January, March, June, and September 2013, and February and June 2014 reports all classify it as calcium-dominant, consistent with wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, and MW-10. However, the December 2013 sampling round reported MW-9 as sodium-dominant, a reversal Geocon's report for that round described as potentially indicating "a mixing of different water types" at the well, paralleling a similar, earlier shift already observed at MW-5. By April 2019, Geocon's summary of the full 2012–2019 record again characterized MW-9 as consistently calcium-dominant, treating the December 2013 result as an isolated deviation rather than a new pattern.

Nitrate as nitrogen was reported above the California primary MCL of 10 mg/L at MW-9 repeatedly, beginning with the first post-installation sampling:

The June 2012 well-installation report is the only source among those reviewed that gives an isolated MW-9 nitrate value (13 mg/L) and flags it explicitly as exceeding the primary MCL; later monthly/quarterly reports group MW-9 with other exceeding wells (commonly MW-1, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10) without always breaking out its individual concentration in the retrieved text. TDS at MW-9 was reported at 510 mg/l in June 2012 and 970 mg/l in September 2012, both above the 500 mg/l secondary MCL; by September 2012 MW-9 was grouped with MW-5, MW-6, and MW-10 as wells with TDS above threshold.

## Dissolved metals

MW-9 is one of the wells at which manganese was reported above its water-quality threshold on multiple sampling rounds — September 2012 (with MW-3), March 2013 (with MW-1 and MW-7 in that round's report), and February 2014 — making it, along with MW-3 and MW-10, one of the more frequently cited wells for manganese exceedances in the monitoring history. Barium, lead, and strontium — the site's three primary chemicals of concern per DTSC — were reported at MW-9 at concentrations Geocon's reports repeatedly described as "similar to historical levels" and "significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds" in every report reviewed; lead was frequently not detected above the 1.0 µg/l PQL. Nickel, molybdenum, and manganese were among the metals reported for the MW-9 sample in September 2012, with manganese exceeding threshold that round.

## Depth to water and well status

Depth-to-groundwater measurements at MW-9 declined into dryness later in the monitoring program. In October 2018, water levels in MW-1 through MW-7 and MW-9 ranged from 36.36 to 46.99 feet below the top of casing, indicating MW-9 was still productive. By April 2019, however, both MW-9 and MW-10 were reported dry, and the April 2019 field visit also noted MW-9's well box was missing its plug or lid. The April 2015 report similarly lists MW-9 among wells (with MW-3, MW-5, MW-8, and MW-10) that were dry during that round. This pattern of intermittent dryness at MW-9 — productive in some rounds, dry in others — recurs through the later years of monitoring, consistent with the general decline in shallow groundwater levels described for the north/upgradient wells.

## Later status

By 2025, DTSC's site correspondence records that six of the original ten wells were decommissioned in 2019 to allow continued highway construction, and that a Well Destruction Report dated June 18, 2025 addressed destruction of the remaining four wells. The available material does not identify by name which four wells were destroyed in 2025 or confirm whether MW-9 was among the six decommissioned in 2019 or among the four addressed in the 2025 report.
