# MW-9

## Overview

MW-9 is a groundwater monitoring well installed as part of the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]] in Stanislaus County, California. The well is located south of kansas-avenue-off-ramp|Kansas Avenue and west of sr-99|SR 99. Its installation and initial sampling were documented in the June 2012 groundwater monitoring report, one of three reports submitted by Geocon Consultants and reviewed by dtsc|DTSC in November 2012.

## Site record

MW-9 was installed alongside mw-10|MW-10 as an addition to the existing monitoring well network at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. The two wells were placed on opposite sides of SR 99 south of Kansas Avenue: MW-9 on the west side, MW-10 on the east side.

The well installation and groundwater sampling results for MW-9 were reported in *"Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California,"* prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012. This report was submitted to DTSC as part of a package that also included the May 2012 and July 2012 monitoring reports.

MW-9 is part of the broader monitoring well network designated as mws-1-10|MWs 1 through 10 at the site.

## Regulatory status

On November 29, 2012, randy-s-adams|Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist at DTSC, issued a letter to Richard Stewart, P.G., of Caltrans concurring with the subject groundwater monitoring reports, including the June 2012 report that documented MW-9's installation and sampling. DTSC reviewed these reports in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region.

DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available. The primary chemicals of concern at the site are barium, lead, and strontium, stemming from historical operations by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation, which processed barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores on a 4.3-acre parcel that included an evaporation pond. The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings from Caltrans' construction of an SR 99 segment north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960s.

No site-specific concentration data, screening level comparisons, or exceedance determinations for MW-9 are documented in the available source material.
