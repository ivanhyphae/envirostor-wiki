## Overview

MW-9 is a groundwater monitoring well installed as part of the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. It is located south of Kansas Avenue and west of SR 99. Its installation and initial sampling were documented in the June 2012 groundwater monitoring report.

## Site record

MW-9 was installed in June 2012, along with MW-10, as an addition to the existing monitoring well network at the Modesto Soil Stockpiles site. MW-9 is situated on the west side of SR 99, south of Kansas Avenue, while MW-10 is on the east side of SR 99 at the same location. The installation and sampling of MW-9 were detailed in the *"Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012, Modesto Soil Stockpiles, SR 99 and 132, Stanislaus County, California,"* prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012. {{quote: "The June 2012 report also discusses the installation of monitoring wells identified as MW-9, located south of Kansas Avenue and west of SR 99; and MW-10, located south of Kansas Avenue and east of SR 99."}} This report was part of a submission to the [[dtsc]] that also included the May 2012 and July 2012 monitoring reports. MW-9 is part of the overall monitoring well network designated as MWs 1 through 10 at the site.

In September 2013, the depth to groundwater at MW-9 was measured at 33.74 feet below the top of the well casing (TOC). {{quote: "In September 2013, depth to groundwater at the Site ranged from 33.74 (MW-1) to 42.05 (MW-5) feet below TOC."}} The groundwater flow direction was determined to be toward the southeast with an average gradient of 0.0007. {{quote: "Based on the groundwater elevation data, the groundwater flow is toward the southeast at an average gradient of 0.0007, which is generally consistent with historical flow."}} During the September 2013 sampling event, MW-9 was found to have a calcium-dominant composition, as indicated by its Stiff diagram. {{quote: "In September 2013, the samples from wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9 and MW-10 had a calcium-dominant composition while the samples from wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6 and MW-8 were sodium-dominant."}}

In June 2014, the depth to groundwater at MW-9 was measured at 35.09 feet below TOC. {{quote: "In June 2014, depth to groundwater at the Site ranged from 35.09 (MW-1) to 42.86 (MW-5) feet below TOC."}} The groundwater flow direction was toward the south-southeast with an average gradient of 0.0008. {{quote: "Based on the groundwater elevation data, the groundwater flow is toward the south-southeast at an average gradient of 0.0008, which is generally consistent with historical flow."}} The Stiff diagram for MW-9 in June 2014 also indicated a calcium-dominant composition. {{quote: "In June 2014, the samples from wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-4, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10 had a calcium-dominant composition while the samples from wells MW-3, MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8 were sodium-dominant."}}

In October 2016, MW-9 was noted to have sufficient groundwater for measurement, with a depth to groundwater of 45.19 feet below TOC. {{quote: "In October 2016, depth to groundwater measured in wells MW-1, MW-2, MW-5, MW-6, MW-7, MW-9, and MW-10 ranged from 38.77 to 47.10 feet below TOC."}} The groundwater flow direction was toward the south at an average gradient of 0.007. {{quote: "Based on the groundwater elevation data, the groundwater flow was toward the south at an average gradient of 0.007, which is generally consistent with historical flow which has been toward the south to southeast."}}

In April 2017, MW-9 was dry. {{quote: "Monitoring wells MW-5, MW-6, and MW-8 through MW-10 were dry or had insufficient water to purge and sample."}}

In October 2018, MW-9 was dry. {{quote: "Well MW-10 could not be located beneath brush and weeds. We measured the depth to groundwater in each well using a battery-operated water level meter. ... Monitoring wells MW-3, MW-4, and MW-8 were dry."}}

In April 2019, MW-9 was dry. {{quote: "Wells MW-9 and MW-10 were dry."}}

The source material does not provide specific concentration data, screening level comparisons, or exceedance determinations for MW-9.

## Regulatory status

On November 29, 2012, Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist at the [[dtsc]], issued a letter to Richard Stewart, P.G., of Caltrans. In this letter, DTSC concurred with the groundwater monitoring reports submitted, including the June 2012 report that documented the installation and sampling of MW-9. {{quote: "DTSC concurs with the subject groundwater monitoring reports..."}} DTSC's review was conducted in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region. {{quote: "The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region (RWQCB) has reviewed:..."}} DTSC requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available. {{quote: "DTSC concurs with the subject groundwater monitoring reports and requests that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available."}} The primary chemicals of concern at the site are barium, lead, and strontium, which originate from historical operations by Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation. These operations involved processing barium sulfate and strontium sulfate ores, and the soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings from Caltrans' construction activities in the early 1960s. {{quote: "The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings that were generated when Caltrans constructed a segment of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960's. Excavating the segment traversed a portion of a 4.3-acre parcel purchased from Food Machinery and Chemical Corporation (FMC) that contained an evaporation pond. ... The primary chemicals of concern (COC) are barium, lead, and strontium."}}

The soil stockpiles consist of excess native soils and pond tailings that were generated when Caltrans constructed a segment of SR 99 north of Kansas Avenue in the early 1960's.
