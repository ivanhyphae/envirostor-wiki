# Pl5

Pl5 is a surface water sampling location within the onsite drainage basin adjacent to and south of Stockpile 3, sited within three feet of a gate valve that would release stormwater from the basin to the SR 99 collection system conveying flow to the Tuolumne River. Because the gate valve controls whether water in the basin ever leaves the site, Pl5 functions as the point where a release pathway to the river would be evaluated if it existed. Across the sampling record from October 2016 through March 2018, the location was frequently dry, and the gate valve was never reported open, so the pathway it monitors was never activated during any recorded event.

## Location and purpose

Pl5 and PL4 are both located at the drainage basin next to Stockpile 3: PL4 is where stormwater enters the basin, and Pl5 is at the gate valve end. Per information Caltrans provided to Geocon, stormwater in the basin originates exclusively from drainage inlets in the depressed section of SR 99 north and south of Kansas Avenue, and the gate valve is opened only if the basin nears a capacity that could jeopardize the northbound lanes of SR 99. Caltrans reported no occurrence of the gate valve being opened in the recent past, and none of the surface water reports reviewed record it being opened during a sampling event. The sample locations, including Pl5, were approved by DTSC and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB).

## Sampling history

Pl5 was sampled inconsistently because the location was frequently dry or held insufficient water:

|---|---|---|
| Oct 28, 2016 | Yes | Collected within 3 feet of gate valve; valve not opened |
| Dec 15, 2016 | No | Location dry |

| Jan 8, 2018 | No | Insufficient water |

## Analytical results

In the October 28, 2016 event, dissolved arsenic, cadmium, cobalt, lead, and selenium were reported for Pl5 (selenium reported only at Pl5 among the PL locations that round), along with antimony, barium, calcium, copper, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, and zinc. None of the reported concentrations exceeded their respective primary or secondary MCLs. Beryllium, chromium, mercury, silver, thallium, and vanadium were not detected above method detection limits. Laboratory notes indicate the sample required dilution for nitrate, sulfide, chloride, and for barium, beryllium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, lead, magnesium, manganese, nickel, potassium, sodium, thallium, and vanadium due to matrix interference or failing internal standard.

In the March 24, 2017 event, dissolved antimony, barium, cadmium, calcium, chromium, cobalt, copper, magnesium, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, sodium, strontium, vanadium, and zinc were reported for Pl5, along with lead. Manganese was not reported for Pl5 that round (it was reported for PL2 and PL4 instead). None of the reported concentrations for Pl5 exceeded primary or secondary MCLs. Beryllium, mercury, silver, and thallium were not detected above method detection limits. The laboratory diluted the Pl5 sample for barium, calcium, magnesium, manganese, potassium, and sodium due to high target or non-target analyte concentrations, and separately diluted it for lead and thallium due to a failing internal standard in the original run.

Across both sampled rounds, Pl5 did not produce an MCL exceedance for any analyte, and the site-wide reports for these events describe results as consistent with the sampling series beginning in 2013 and consistent with stormwater remaining within the Caltrans right-of-way. The March 2018 report notes an elevated lead concentration that round, but attributes it to sample location PL3, collected outside the right-of-way — not to Pl5, which was not sampled that round due to insufficient water.

## Significance

Pl5's evidentiary role is narrower than the other PL locations: because it sits at the controlled outlet rather than an open runoff path, its primary significance is confirming that the gate valve has not been opened and that the basin has not discharged to the SR 99 storm system during monitored events, rather than characterizing ongoing runoff chemistry. The frequent dry/insufficient-water status recorded at Pl5 (three of five reviewed events) itself supports the conclusion, stated in each report's surface water management section, that stormwater falling on the stockpiles is retained within the Caltrans right-of-way rather than migrating offsite through this basin.
