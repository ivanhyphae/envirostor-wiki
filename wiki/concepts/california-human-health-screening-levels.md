## California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs)

### Overview
California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) are risk-based concentrations developed by the California Environmental Protection Agency (CalEPA) for evaluating potential human health risks from chemical contaminants in soil and soil gas. In the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles remediation case, CHHSLs serve as the primary human health toxicity screening thresholds against which site-specific metal concentrations in Stockpile #3 were compared to determine appropriate reuse and disposal options.

### Site Record
In the May 29, 2012 letter from Caltrans District 10 Project Manager Sam Haack to DTSC Chief Steven Becker, the analytical results for soil excavated from Stockpile #3 during the SR 99 at Kansas Avenue ramp safety improvement project were reported. The final analysis indicated that elevated metal concentrations were present above site-specific background levels but **below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels** (no specific chemical or concentration values provided in the source document). The approximately 6,000 cubic yards of material excavated for roadbed widening, drainage, and retaining wall construction was thus classified as non-hazardous based on CHHSL comparison.

The CHHSL-based determination supported DTSC concurrence for off-site disposal of the material at the Stanislaus County Fink Road Landfill as non-hazardous soil, subject to disclosure and review of site characterization data.

### Regulatory Status
CHHSLs are not enforceable cleanup standards but are screening levels used by DTSC, Regional Water Quality Control Boards, and other agencies to:
- Identify whether contaminants pose a potential risk under residential or commercial/industrial exposure scenarios
- Determine if further site investigation or remediation is warranted
- Support decisions on soil reuse and disposal classification

For the Stockpile #3 material, the demonstrated compliance with both residential and commercial/industrial CHHSLs supported a non-hazardous classification for off-site disposal. No exceedances of CHHSLs were documented in the source material.
