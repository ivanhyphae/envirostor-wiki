[[california-human-health-screening-levels]] (CHHSLs) are benchmarks used by Caltrans to evaluate the potential health risks associated with contaminants in soil. In the context of the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]] remediation case file, CHHSLs were referenced to determine the suitability of excavated material for reuse.

## Overview

California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs) were used by Caltrans to assess the safety of excavated soil from [[stockpile-3-excavation]]. This material was intersected by ramp improvements at State Route (SR) 99 at Kansas Avenue. Soil analysis indicated elevated metal concentrations, but these were found to be below the applicable CHHSLs for residential and commercial/industrial scenarios. This finding supported the conclusion that the soil was suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite disposal as non-hazardous material.

## Site Record

During the ramp safety improvement project at SR 99 and Kansas Avenue, approximately 6000 cubic yards of material were excavated. This excavation intersected [[stockpile-3-excavation]]. Soil analysis conducted by [[geocon-consultants]] indicated the presence of elevated metal concentrations. Specifically, regarding [[barium]], one sample (B17-4.5) had a concentration that {{quote: "does not exceed the commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Level (CHHSL) for barium of 63,000 mg/kg."}} Other analyses of Stockpile 3 samples showed barium levels ranging from 31 to 44,300 mg/kg, 64 to 230 mg/kg, 38 to 93 mg/kg, and 35 to 178 mg/kg. These concentrations were reported as being {{quote: "below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels."}}

## Regulatory Status

The soil material excavated from [[stockpile-3-excavation]] was determined to be suitable for reuse because its contaminant concentrations were below the California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSLs). Specifically, the concentrations were {{quote: "below residential and commercial/industrial California Human Health Screening Levels."}} This determination was made by Caltrans, with the expectation of concurrence from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC). Based on this assessment, the material was deemed suitable for reuse as structural backfill or for offsite reuse/disposal as a non-hazardous soil.

## Open Items

Caltrans requested review and concurrence from the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) regarding the disposition of excavated material from [[stockpile-3-excavation]]. The ramp project was on hold pending DTSC's concurrence that the material could be managed as non-hazardous. This request was made via a letter dated May 29, 2012.
