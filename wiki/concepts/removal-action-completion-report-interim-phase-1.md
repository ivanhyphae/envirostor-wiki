The Removal Action Completion Report – Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Interim Phase 1 State Route 132 West Project, dated December 1, 2022, documents the consolidation and capping activities for barium-containing soils (BCS) as part of the Interim Phase 1 construction at the State Route 132 West project in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), accepted this report as final on January 18, 2023, signifying the conclusion of the Interim Phase 1 remediation efforts.

### Site Remediation Activities

In accordance with the approved Remedial Action Plan (RAP) and the approved Interim Phase 1 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP), the report details the excavation of BCS Stockpile 3, the western and eastern ends of BCS Stockpile 2, and soil removal from the southern slopes of BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2. These actions supported the construction of the new State Route 132 Expressway.

*   **BCS Stockpile 3:** Approximately 27,675 cubic yards (yd3) of soil were excavated and transported to the BCS Stockpile 1 containment zone. Twenty-nine confirmation soil samples were collected from the excavation area. Three samples initially showed barium concentrations above the cleanup goal of 1,000 milligrams per kilogram (mg/kg). These areas were deepened by approximately one foot and resampled, with final confirmation samples indicating barium and lead concentrations below the cleanup goals.
*   **BCS Stockpile 2 (Western and Eastern Ends):** Soil was removed using scrapers and placed within the BCS Stockpile 2 containment zone. Verification samples were collected from eight locations on the western end and twelve on the eastern end. Initial confirmation samples indicated four areas above the 1,000 mg/kg barium cleanup goal. These areas were deepened by an additional foot, and subsequent samples confirmed that all remaining soil met the verification thresholds for barium (1,000 mg/kg) and lead (80 mg/kg).
*   **BCS Stockpiles 1 and 2 (Southern Slopes):** Soil was removed from the southern slopes to allow for the construction of a mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall. Prior to removal, soil samples were collected at 200-foot intervals down to a maximum of five feet below the surface. While barium and lead concentrations were below cleanup goals, barium was found above the site-specific background concentration of 120 mg/kg. Therefore, all material removed was placed in the BCS Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones as embankment fill.
*   **Aerially Deposited Lead:** Soil with aerially deposited lead was removed from the unpaved shoulder of a portion of State Route 99 adjacent to BCS Stockpile 3, in accordance with the 'Soil Management Agreement for Aerially Deposited Lead-Contaminated Soils' between DTSC and Caltrans (2016). This material was placed in the containment zone in the eastern portion of BCS Stockpile 1.

### Regulatory Acceptance and Future Actions

The Draft Phase 1 RACR indicated that the completed work adhered to the approved RAP or the two variance requests to the RDIP, approved on April 17, 2020, and September 21, 2020. DTSC accepted the document as a technical status report with no comments.

The DTSC accepted the Removal Action Completion Report – Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles, Interim Phase 1 State Route 132 West Project on January 18, 2023. This acceptance signifies the completion of the Interim Phase 1 remediation efforts.

The DTSC will prepare a draft Operation and Maintenance (O&M) Agreement for Caltrans review within 30 days of the acceptance letter. A Land Use Covenant (LUC) for the remediated area will also be completed and sent for review, signature, and recording with the Stanislaus County Assessor's office.

Final certification of the RAP will not occur until after the Ultimate Phase 2 State Route 132 build-out effort is completed, anticipated in 8 to 10 years. This future phase will involve adding additional lanes and completing the final build-out of State Route 132. It will require a supplemental RDIP for DTSC review and approval, field oversight, an Ultimate Phase 2 RACR, and potential revisions to the O&M Plan and O&M Agreement. The Ultimate Phase 2 project will be conducted under a separate agreement between DTSC and Caltrans.
