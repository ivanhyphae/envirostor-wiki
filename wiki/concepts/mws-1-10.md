# Mws 1 10

## Overview
Monitoring wells MW-1 through MW-10 comprise the groundwater monitoring network for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. These ten wells are installed in the shallow zone of the upper aquifer within the modesto-formation, completed in unconsolidated sand, silty sand, and silt layers. The network monitors groundwater potentially affected by soil stockpiles generated during [[caltrans]] construction. The primary chemicals of concern (COCs) for the site are [[barium]], lead, and strontium.

## Site record

### Well installation and network configuration
Monitoring wells MW-1 through MW-10 were installed into the unconsolidated sand, silty sand, and silt layers within the modesto-formation underlying the site. All ten wells were completed within the shallow zone of the upper aquifer, where first-encountered groundwater is at approximately 30 feet below ground surface (bgs) under confined to semi-confined conditions.

MW-9 and MW-10 were installed as additions to the network, as documented in the "Additional Well Installation and Groundwater Monitoring Report - June 2012" prepared by Geocon Consultants and dated November 2012. MW-9 is located south of Kansas Avenue and west of SR 99; MW-10 is located south of Kansas Avenue and east of SR 99.

### June 2014 monitoring event
On June 4, 2014, Geocon Consultants observed each of the ten well boxes for signs of tampering or required maintenance; none were observed and no maintenance was required. Depth to groundwater, dissolved oxygen (DO), and oxygen-reduction potential (ORP) were measured in all ten wells.

Depth to groundwater measurements from the top of well casings (TOC) ranged from 35.09 feet (MW-1) to 42.86 feet (MW-5) below TOC. The recorded groundwater elevations for each well on June 4, 2014 were:

*   MW-1: 45.30 feet
*   MW-2: 45.19 feet
*   MW-3: 45.07 feet
*   MW-4: 45.05 feet
*   MW-5: 44.92 feet
*   MW-6: 45.44 feet
*   MW-7: 44.65 feet
*   MW-8: 44.44 feet
*   MW-9: 45.19 feet
*   MW-10: 45.21 feet

Based on these elevations, groundwater flow direction was toward the south-southeast at an average gradient of 0.0008, consistent with historical flow patterns. Historical depth-to-water measurements from the site indicate groundwater flow direction in the shallow upper aquifer is generally toward the southeast with hydraulic gradients varying from 0.0006 to 0.001.

### Sampling and purging procedures
On June 4 and 5, 2014, Geocon purged approximately one to three well volumes of water (0.5 to 4.5 gallons) from MW-1 through MW-10 using a submersible pump or disposable bailer. During purging, groundwater was monitored for pH, electrical conductivity, temperature, and turbidity. Groundwater samples were collected using disposable bailers and decanted through slow emptying devices. Samples for dissolved metals analysis were filtered using a hand-pressure pump through a 0.45-micron filter. Purged groundwater was placed into one Department of Transportation-approved 17-H, 55-gallon drum and transported to Geocon's Rancho Cordova office pending analytical results, with disposal scheduled at Inviro-Tec Disposal in Lincoln, California, in August 2014.

### Analytical results (June 2014)
Time-series plots in the June 2014 Groundwater Monitoring Report (Geocon Project No. S9800-01-17, Task Order No. 17, August 2014) show data for MW-1 through MW-10:

*   **Barium**: Concentrations for all ten wells plotted well below the water quality threshold of 1,000 micrograms per liter (µg/L), with most readings between 0 and 400 µg/L over the monitoring period from May 2006 through August 2014.
*   **Lead**: Concentrations for MW-1 through MW-10 were generally below 4 µg/L, all below the water quality threshold of 15 µg/L.
*   **Strontium**: Data for MW-1 through MW-10 were plotted from February 2012 through June 2014 against a water quality threshold of 4,000 µg/L.

## Regulatory status

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Regional Water Quality Control Board, Central Valley Region, reviewed the May, June, and July 2012 groundwater monitoring reports prepared by Geocon Consultants. In a letter dated November 29, 2012, from Randy S. Adams, C.E.G., Senior Engineering Geologist, DTSC concurred with the groundwater monitoring reports and requested that future groundwater monitoring reports be completed as groundwater data becomes available.

The primary chemicals of concern for the site are barium, lead, and strontium. The sources document water quality thresholds for the monitoring wells: 1,000 µg/L for barium, 15 µg/L for lead, and 4,000 µg/L for strontium. All MW-1 through MW-10 results for barium and lead plotted below these thresholds in the June 2014 monitoring event.

## Open items
None documented in the available sources. The June 2014 monitoring report notes that purge water was to be disposed of in August 2014, but no subsequent confirmation or unresolved issues are recorded in the source materials provided.
