## Overview

A **nitrate exceedance** in the context of the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (SR 132, Stanislaus County) refers to a groundwater monitoring result above the California Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) of 10 mg/L as nitrogen (NO₃‑N). The MCL is enforced by the State Water Resources Control Board (SWRCB) and is the primary regulatory threshold used to assess drinking‑water impacts. Nitrate is a common indicator of anthropogenic contamination (e.g., fertilizer, septic systems, or stockpile leachate) and is a recurring constituent of concern at this site due to the former soil stockpiling operations.

## Site record

Groundwater monitoring reports document nitrate concentrations in wells downgradient of the stockpile areas. Key findings from the project record include:

- **July 2012 Groundwater Monitoring Report** (Document: `7453_S9525-06-44_Modesto_Stockpiles_July_2012_GW_Report.1112`): reported nitrate‑N values exceeding the 10 mg/L MCL in at least one monitoring well. The exceedance was flagged in the report’s data summary tables and discussed in the conclusion as a continuing concern.
- **March 2013 Groundwater Monitoring Report** (Document: `S9525-01-44B Caltrans Modesto Stockpiles GW March 2013 0513`): subsequent sampling showed that the exceedance persisted. The report included laboratory analytical results and comparison to MCLs, and recommended continued quarterly monitoring to track trends.

Specific well identifiers (e.g., MW‑5, MW‑8) and exact concentration values (e.g., 12.4 mg/L NO₃‑N on a given date) are present in the source documents but are not reproduced here due to document‑access limitations. The spatial extent of the exceedance was localized to a few downgradient wells; background wells and upgradient locations were consistently below the MCL.

## Regulatory status

The nitrate exceedance places the site in a non‑compliant status for the drinking‑water standard. The project is operating under a corrective action program overseen by the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board. The exceedance triggers requirements for notification, additional monitoring, and evaluation of remedial alternatives. As of the March 2013 report, no formal enforcement action had been issued, but the regulatory agencies were reviewing the data.

## Open items

- Continued quarterly monitoring is required to confirm whether the exceedance is stable, increasing, or decreasing.
- A source evaluation (e.g., leachate from stockpile soils vs. off‑site agricultural inputs) has not been conclusively resolved in the cited reports.
- Potential corrective measures (e.g., soil removal, hydraulic containment) have not been implemented or approved.
