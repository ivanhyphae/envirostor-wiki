## Overview

The Draft Operation and Maintenance Plan 2022 (Draft O&M Plan) is a project document submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc. on behalf of the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles site (Site Code 900259), specifically addressing the Interim Phase 1 State Route 132 West Project in Stanislaus County, California. The plan outlines inspection, maintenance, and repair activities for the portion of State Route 132 that serves as the clean cap cover over two barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles. The plan was prepared for inclusion as Appendix G of the Remedial Action Completion Report. The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC), in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB), reviewed the Draft O&M Plan and issued formal comments on August 1, 2022.

## Site Record

The Draft O&M Plan was dated March 2022 and submitted by Mr. John E. Juhrend, P.E., CEG, Project Manager at Geocon Consultants, Inc., headquartered at 3160 Gold Valley Drive, Suite 800, Rancho Cordova, California 95742. The document included a blank O&M Site Inspection Form intended for use by field crews to document the condition of the cap and SR 132 freeway.

The plan addressed the following specific components:
- Two barium-containing soil (BCS) stockpiles located beneath the SR 132 freeway alignment
- The clean cap cover consisting of highway pavement and associated infrastructure
- A vegetated slope on the north side of the freeway that will remain until the final phase of the freeway is complete
- Mechanically Stabilized Earth (MSE) walls

DTSC's review resulted in six specific comments requiring revisions:

1. **Typographical error**: The word "Maintenance" was misspelled in the Subject line of the Draft O&M Plan.

2. **Section 2.1 – Advance notification**: DTSC recommended adding a minimum 10 working days advance notice to DTSC prior to any disturbance of BCS, ensuring prior written authorization is obtained before planned disturbance begins.

3. **Section 2.4 – Health and Safety Code compliance**: DTSC recommended including text stating that Health and Safety Code (HSC) 25123.3(b)(4)(A) requirements will be met. The plan specified minimum thickness of 6 millimeters, but DTSC noted HSC uses mils (not millimeters). The word "millimeter" was also misspelled and required correction.

4. **Section 3.1 – Inspection triggers**: DTSC recommended adding that in addition to annual inspections, inspections should occur after events such as fires, substantial rainfall (1 inch in 24 hours), significant seismic events, and traffic accidents that may cause damage to the cap—particularly important for the north vegetated slope.

5. **Section 2.7 – Periodic patrols**: DTSC recommended noting that periodic patrols should also identify unauthorized access such as homeless encampments and assess any resulting impact to the cap, especially on the north vegetated slope.

6. **O&M Site Inspection Form**: DTSC recommended expanding line items under each stockpile with subheadings listing specific elements, including:
   - MSE Wall – Bulging areas, out of plane
   - Highway Pavement – Cracks, potholes
   - Median and Shoulders – Cracks, potholes
   - North Slope – Vegetation, Rills, Slumping
   - Storm Drains – Debris/obstructions, erosion around inlets/outlets

DTSC requested these revisions be incorporated before the plan was submitted as Appendix G of the Remedial Action Completion Report.

## Regulatory Status

The Draft O&M Plan is a regulatory deliverable subject to DTSC and RWQCB review and approval. The project is currently in a pre-approval phase; DTSC's August 1, 2022, letter conditioned final acceptance of the plan on incorporation of the specified revisions. The plan must also comply with Health and Safety Code Section 25123.3(b)(4)(A) requirements regarding minimum liner thickness.

## Open Items

- The Draft O&M Plan required revisions to address six comments from DTSC before inclusion as Appendix G of the Remedial Action Completion Report
- Typographical errors (misspelling of "Maintenance" and "millimeter") required correction
- The O&M Site Inspection Form required expansion with specific subheadings for inspection elements
- The minimum thickness specification (6 millimeters) required clarification to ensure consistency with HSC's use of mils
- The advance notification period for BCS disturbance required formalization at 10 working days

The project is pending submission of the revised plan incorporating DTSC's comments. No documentation has been located in the source material indicating whether these revisions have been completed and approved.
