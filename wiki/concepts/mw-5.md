MW-5 is one of the ten Caltrans monitoring wells installed adjacent to the Modesto Soil Stockpiles and, across every sampling round from 2012 through the mid-2010s, was the site's most anomalous well: consistently the deepest to groundwater, chronically prone to purging dry, and the location with the highest recurring nitrate and TDS values of any well on the property. Its ionic character also shifted over time, switching from calcium-dominant to sodium-dominant water beginning in 2013, a change Geocon flagged as possible mixing of different water types at that location. Despite these anomalies, dissolved metals results from MW-5 — including barium, lead, and strontium, DTSC's three primary chemicals of concern — never exceeded numeric water quality thresholds in any reported round.

## Well conditions and sampling difficulty

MW-5 was the shallowest-yielding well at the site and went dry during purging in nearly every monitoring event for which records are available: in March and May 2012 (dry after ~1 gallon), September and November 2012 (dry after 0.65–0.75 gallon), and January, March, and June 2013 (dry after 1–1.5 gallons). In June 2013, Geocon redeveloped the well using a surge block between purging attempts specifically to address this problem, reasoning in the March 2013 report that elevated turbidity and sediment near the screen might be obstructing inflow and compromising sample representativeness. The well continued to require multiple recovery cycles afterward, and by the April 2015, May 2016, and April 2017 monitoring events MW-5 was reported dry or with insufficient water to sample at all — a persistent-yield decline consistent with the broader depth-to-water increases recorded across the site over the same years.

Depth to groundwater at MW-5 was the greatest of any well at the site in every round where all wells were measured, ranging from about 38 feet below TOC in 2012 to over 42 feet below TOC by late 2013:

## General minerals: nitrate and TDS

MW-5 repeatedly returned the highest nitrate-as-nitrogen and TDS concentrations of any monitoring well at the site, consistently exceeding the primary MCL for nitrate (10 mg/L) and the secondary MCL for TDS (500 mg/L):

Unlike the upgradient well MW-1, whose elevated nitrate and TDS were repeatedly characterized in these reports as possible natural background, MW-5 sits adjacent to the stockpiles, and its readings were the highest recorded anywhere at the site in nearly every round — yet Geocon's conclusions sections never attributed this pattern to a specific source, and no threshold-driven regulatory response tied to MW-5 nitrate or TDS appears in the record.

## Ionic composition: the calcium-to-sodium shift

Stiff diagram analysis classified MW-5 as calcium-dominant water in every round from March 2012 through June 2013, grouped with the wells interpreted as representing water beneath Stockpile 1 and Stockpile 2. Beginning with the September 2013 report, MW-5 was reported as sodium-dominant, a change Geocon described as "consistently calcium-dominant during previous sampling events," noting the shift "may be indicative of a mixing of different water types at this location." The well remained sodium-dominant through December 2013, February 2014, and June 2014. By April 2019, MW-5 had reverted to calcium-dominant, one of the wells "consistently" reported that way across the 2012–2019 record according to the April 2019 report's summary — indicating the sodium-dominant interval at MW-5 was a multi-year but not permanent departure from its baseline chemistry.

## Metals: no exceedances despite anomalous general chemistry

Notwithstanding its distinctive nitrate, TDS, and ionic behavior, MW-5 dissolved metals results tracked the rest of the site: barium, lead, and strontium were reported at concentrations "similar to historical levels" and "significantly less than their numeric water quality thresholds" in report after report. Isolated, non-recurring detections were noted at MW-5 specifically — antimony in May 2012 and again at 0.72 µg/l in March 2013 (Lab ID 1300826-10) — but none triggered an exceedance finding. The April 2019 report identified manganese in the MW-5 sample at 59 µg/l, exceeding its secondary (taste-and-odor) MCL; manganese exceedances at MW-5 or nearby wells had also been noted in some earlier rounds (e.g., March 2013, when manganese was detected at MW-1 and MW-7 rather than MW-5). No exceedance of barium, lead, or strontium — the site's three primary chemicals of concern — was ever reported for MW-5 in the available record.

## Later monitoring and decommissioning context

By the mid-2010s MW-5 was among the wells increasingly dry or unsamplable, consistent with a declining water table reflected across the site (by October 2016, MW-3, MW-4, and MW-8 were also dry, and by April 2017 MW-5 itself was dry along with MW-6 and MW-8 through MW-10). DTSC's January 2026 letter states that six of the ten original monitoring wells were decommissioned in 2019 and that the remaining four were the subject of a 2025 Well Destruction Report; the letter does not identify by name which wells were decommissioned in 2019 or which four remained, so it cannot be confirmed from this record whether MW-5 was among the wells destroyed in 2019 or among the final four addressed in 2025.
