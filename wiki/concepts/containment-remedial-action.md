## Overview
Containment is a General Response Action (GRA) considered for the Caltrans Modesto Soil Stockpiles. This GRA aims to restrict the mobility of contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) through technologies such as stormwater controls and capping, thereby reducing or eliminating potential exposure pathways and minimizing water infiltration into the stockpiles. Containment can be achieved either through integration with the planned SR-132 Project or by placing a clean soil cap over the stockpiles.

## Site record
Containment is evaluated as Alternative 4 in the Final Feasibility Study Report. This alternative can be implemented in two ways:

*   **Containment by Capping with Planned Construction of the SR-132 Project:** This approach involves using the stockpiled soil as fill within the SR-132 Project. Specifically, the soil will be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement. During an interim progress phase (to be completed by 2018), portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 not covered by permanent structures will be graded for drainage and covered with a vegetated, clean soil cap (minimum 6 to 12 inches thick). For the ultimate build-out (to be completed by 2028), all stockpiles will be fully encapsulated. Median areas will be covered by pavement or a synthetic liner and clean soil layer. Stockpile 3 will be largely placed in the stockpile fill consolidation zone within the eastern abutment for the SR-132 bridge over SR-99 during the interim progress phase, with any remainder placed in the Stockpile 2 fill consolidation zone.
    *   The capital costs for this option are considered moderate to high, with ongoing monitoring and maintenance costs being moderate. {{quote: "The cost of containment by capping beneath the planned SR-132 project, including excavation of portions of the stockpiles and consolidation behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath a vegetated clean soil cap and roadway pavement, is considered to be moderate to high for capital costs and moderate in terms of ongoing monitoring and maintenance (Table 5)."}}
    *   The volume of soil requiring excavation from Stockpiles 1 and 2 for consolidation is not significant. {{quote: "The volume of soil requiring excavation from Stockpiles 1 and 2 for consolidation behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath a vegetated clean soil cap and roadway pavement, is considered to be moderate to high for capital costs and moderate in terms of ongoing monitoring and maintenance (Table 5). The bulk of the capital cost of this alternative will be in grading of the soil for the interim progress phase of the project, placement of the clean soil cap over the northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2, and placement of paving or a synthetic liner and clean soil cap over median areas for the ultimate build-out of the SR-132 Project."}}
*   **Containment by Capping with Clean Soil Layer:** If the SR-132 Project were not constructed, this alternative would involve placing a clean soil cap over the stockpiles. This cap would provide a similar level of protection as capping integrated with the SR-132 project.
    *   The capital costs for this option are also considered moderate to high, with moderate ongoing monitoring and maintenance costs. {{quote: "The cost of containment by capping beneath a vegetated clean soil layer if the SR-132 project were not constructed is considered to be moderate to high for capital costs and moderate in terms of ongoing monitoring and maintenance (Table 6). The bulk of the capital cost of this alternative will be in grading of the stockpiles for drainage, placement of a one-foot-thick layer of clean soil over the stockpiles, and revegetation."}}
    *   This option would likely require greater long-term monitoring and maintenance to ensure the cap and vegetative cover remain effective. {{quote: "A vegetated clean soil layer cap would likely require a greater degree of long-term monitoring and maintenance to ensure that the cap and vegetative cover remain viable and effective."}}

Both forms of containment are expected to reduce the potential mobility of COPCs by precluding infiltration but will not affect the toxicity or volume of the COPCs. {{quote: "This alternative by either form of cap will further reduce the potential mobility of the COPCs in the stockpiles via an impermeable surface that would preclude infiltration, but will have no effect on toxicity (low) or volume."}}

Monitoring of groundwater and stormwater is expected to continue at current levels until ultimate build-out is complete. {{quote: "Monitoring costs for groundwater and stormwater monitoring will likely continue at levels similar to current costs until the ultimate build-out is complete."}}

## Regulatory status
Containment is anticipated to receive regulatory acceptance from the DTSC and CVRWQCB because it further eliminates exposure pathways and reduces COPC mobility. {{quote: "This alternative is anticipated to receive regulatory acceptance by further eliminating exposure pathways to COPCs in the soil and reducing their mobility through encapsulation either within the planned SR-132 project or beneath a vegetated clean soil cap if the SR-132 project is not constructed."}} Both forms of capping are expected to comply with State and Federal regulations and be protective of human health and the environment. {{quote: "This alternative by either type of cap (the planned construction of the SR-132 Project or placement of a vegetated clean soil layer) would comply with State and Federal regulations in that either form of cap would be protective of human health and the environment (groundwater)."}}

## Open items
The Final Feasibility Study Report recommends Alternative 4 – Containment as the preferred alternative. {{quote: "Based on the screening of alternatives and comparative analysis, **Alternative 4 – Containment** is the recommended alternative."}} Details of construction will be presented in a Remedial Design Implementation Plan after approval of the Remedial Action Plan (RAP).
