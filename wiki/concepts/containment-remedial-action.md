## Overview
Containment is a General Response Action (GRA) considered for the [[caltrans-modesto-soil-stockpiles]]. This GRA aims to restrict the mobility of contaminants of potential concern (COPCs) through technologies such as stormwater controls and capping, thereby reducing or eliminating potential exposure pathways and minimizing water infiltration into the stockpiles. Containment can be achieved either through integration with the planned [[state-route-132-west-freeway-expressway-project]] or by placing a clean soil cap over the stockpiles.

## Site record
Containment is evaluated as [[alternative-4]] in the Final Feasibility Study Report (FS Report, June 2014). This alternative can be implemented in two ways:

*   **Containment by Capping with Planned Construction of the SR-132 Project:** This approach involves using the stockpiled soil as fill within the SR-132 Project. Specifically, the soil will be contained behind retaining walls, bridge abutments, and beneath roadway pavement. During an interim progress phase (to be completed by 2018), portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2 not covered by permanent structures will be graded for drainage and covered with a vegetated, clean soil cap (minimum 6 to 12 inches thick). For the ultimate build-out (to be completed by 2028), all stockpiles will be fully encapsulated. Median areas will be covered by pavement or a synthetic liner and clean soil layer. Stockpile 3 will be largely placed in the stockpile fill consolidation zone within the eastern abutment for the SR-132 bridge over SR-99 during the interim progress phase, with any remainder placed in the Stockpile 2 fill consolidation zone.
    *   The capital costs for this option are considered moderate to high, with ongoing monitoring and maintenance costs being moderate (FS Report, June 2014, Table 5).
    *   The volume of soil requiring excavation from Stockpiles 1 and 2 for consolidation is not significant. The bulk of the capital cost of this alternative will be in grading of the soil for the interim progress phase of the project, placement of the clean soil cap over the northern portions of Stockpiles 1 and 2, and placement of paving or a synthetic liner and clean soil cap over median areas for the ultimate build-out of the SR-132 Project (FS Report, June 2014).
*   **Containment by Capping with Clean Soil Layer:** If the SR-132 Project were not constructed, this alternative would involve placing a clean soil cap over the stockpiles. This cap would provide a similar level of protection as capping integrated with the SR-132 project.
    *   The capital costs for this option are also considered moderate to high, with moderate ongoing monitoring and maintenance costs (FS Report, June 2014, Table 6). The bulk of the capital cost of this alternative will be in grading of the stockpiles for drainage, placement of a one-foot-thick layer of clean soil over the stockpiles, and revegetation.
    *   This option would likely require greater long-term monitoring and maintenance to ensure the cap and vegetative cover remain effective (FS Report, June 2014).

Both forms of containment are expected to reduce the potential mobility of COPCs by precluding infiltration but will not affect the toxicity or volume of the COPCs (FS Report, June 2014).

Monitoring of groundwater and stormwater is expected to continue at current levels until ultimate build-out is complete (FS Report, June 2014).

## Regulatory status
Containment is anticipated to receive regulatory acceptance from the [[department-of-toxic-substances-control]] (DTSC) and the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB) because it further eliminates exposure pathways and reduces COPC mobility (Draft Final Remedial Action Plan, October 2014). Both forms of capping are expected to comply with State and Federal regulations and be protective of human health and the environment (Draft Final Remedial Action Plan, October 2014).

## Open items
The Final Feasibility Study Report recommends Alternative 4 – Containment as the preferred alternative (FS Report, June 2014). Details of construction will be presented in a Remedial Design Implementation Plan after approval of the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) (Draft Final Remedial Action Plan, October 2014).
