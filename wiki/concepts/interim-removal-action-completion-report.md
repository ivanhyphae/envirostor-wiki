The Interim Removal Action Completion Report (Interim RACR) documents that [[caltrans]] and the City of Modesto, through [[geocon-consultants-inc]], consolidated and clean-capped barium-containing soil (BCS) Stockpiles 1 through 3 as embankment fill during Interim Phase 1 construction of the SR 132 West Project, in accordance with the 2019 Remedial Design Implementation Plan (RDIP). The report exists in draft and interim form because remediation is not complete: it covers only Interim Phase 1, which opened to traffic November 3, 2022, and a Final RACR will not be submitted until after Ultimate Phase 2 construction, scheduled for completion in 2028. Its significance is that it is the formal record DTSC used to accept — but not certify — the completed containment work, and it identifies the specific deliverables (as-built plans, survey data, and an Operations and Maintenance Plan) still owed before the remedy can be closed out.

## Origin and submission history

Geocon submitted a Draft Interim RACR to [[dtsc]] on December 23, 2021, presented as a progress summary of BCS grading and clean-fill capping. At that time, remaining work included placement of structural pavement section on the capped stockpiles, erosion control, drainage, and median barrier and signage. DTSC, in consultation with the Central Valley Regional Water Quality Control Board (CVRWQCB), reviewed the draft and issued an acceptance letter dated February 24, 2022, accepting it "as a technical status report with no comments." A site survey performed June 28, 2022 by DTSC, Caltrans, the City, and other project personnel confirmed completed clean capping of the Stockpile 1 and 2 BCS Containment Zones and compliance with the RDIP. The final version of the Interim RACR analyzed here is dated December 1, 2022 (Project No. S1908-01-01), transmitted by John E. Juhrend, PE, CEG of Geocon to Dean Wright, PG, of DTSC, with copies to the CVRWQCB, City of Modesto, and Caltrans.

## What the report documents as completed

The Interim RACR describes removal and consolidation work performed under the RAP and RDIP, including two approved variances (April 17, 2020 and September 21, 2020):

- Excavation of BCS Stockpile 3 (approximately 27,675 cubic yards), transported and placed as embankment fill within the Stockpile 1 containment zone. Of 29 confirmation samples from the excavation bottom, three showed [[barium]] above the 1,000 mg/kg cleanup goal; these areas were deepened roughly one foot and resampled, with final results below cleanup goals for both barium and [[lead]] (80 mg/kg).
- Removal of the western and eastern ends of Stockpile 2 using scrapers, placed within the Stockpile 2 containment zone. Of 8 (west) and 12 (east) verification samples, four locations initially exceeded the 1,000 mg/kg barium goal; after an additional foot of removal, all resampled areas were below both the barium and lead thresholds.
- Removal of soil from the southern slopes of Stockpiles 1 and 2 to permit construction of a mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall. Pre-removal sampling at 200-foot intervals to five feet depth showed lead within background and below cleanup goals, but barium above the site-specific background concentration of 120 mg/kg; this material was placed as embankment fill within the Stockpile 1 and 2 containment zones rather than reused as clean cap.
- Removal of aerially deposited lead-impacted soil from the unpaved shoulder of SR 99 near former Stockpile 3, under the 2016 Soil Management Agreement between DTSC and Caltrans, placed in the eastern portion of the Stockpile 1 containment zone.

The consistent pattern — initial confirmation failures on barium followed by incremental deepening and resampling until both barium and lead cleared cleanup goals — establishes that the containment zones as built do not contain residual BCS above the 1,000 mg/kg (barium) / 80 mg/kg (lead) thresholds at the excavation surfaces, supporting the report's claim that the material placed as embankment fill is properly bounded.

## Conditions attached to DTSC's acceptance

DTSC's February 24, 2022 acceptance letter is conditional, not a closure. It identifies specific deliverables required before a Final Phase 1 RACR can be issued:

- As-Built Plan and cross-section figures (not yet completed at time of acceptance)
- Final survey data
- An approved [[operations-and-maintenance-plan]], to include a soil management plan, annual inspection of pavement and other containment features, and groundwater monitoring

DTSC's letter states the O&M Plan was anticipated for submittal such that the Final Phase 1 RACR could follow in July or August 2022. Following approval of the Final Phase 1 RACR, DTSC stated it will enter into an O&M Agreement with Caltrans incorporating the O&M Plan and will complete a [[land-use-covenant]] for the containment areas. The letter also states explicitly that certification of the RAP will not occur until after Ultimate Phase 2 build-out is completed — meaning the regulatory closure that a land use covenant and O&M Agreement depend on remains open pending a second construction phase roughly 8 to 10 years out from the 2022 letter.

The December 1, 2022 Interim RACR itself (as opposed to the December 2021 draft) already includes the Operations and Maintenance Plan as Appendix H, along with the BCS containment zone legal description and boundary survey (Appendix E) and air monitoring technical memorandum (Appendix G) — indicating these deliverables DTSC flagged as outstanding in February 2022 were assembled into this later submission.

## Outstanding condition: Ultimate Phase 2

The Interim RACR states that a supplemental RDIP will be submitted to DTSC for approval prior to Ultimate Phase 2 construction, covering monitoring and sampling requirements for any BCS disturbance, groundwater monitoring well abandonment or protective measures if applicable, and final grading/improvement design plans. A Final RACR is to follow completed Ultimate Phase 2 construction. This leaves the Interim RACR's containment findings — and the barium and lead concentrations left in place under embankment fill and clean cap — subject to renewed disturbance and re-evaluation whenever Phase 2 proceeds.
