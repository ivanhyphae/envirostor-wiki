A [[remedial-design-implementation-plan]] (RDIP) Variance Request is a formal request submitted to regulatory agencies for approval of deviations from the established RDIP. These variances allow for modifications to the planned remediation or implementation strategies based on site-specific conditions or new information.

## Site Record

Two specific RDIP Variance Requests are documented in the project record:

*   **Variance Request for Stockpile 1 MSE Wall Footing Soil as Clean Cover:** This request, dated April 9, 2020, concerned soil excavated from the upper foot of the mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) wall footing along the southern boundary of [[stockpile-1-mse-wall-footing]]. This material was found to have elevated barium concentrations and was not suitable as clean fill. However, soil extending from 1 to 5 feet from existing grade in this area had barium concentrations with a calculated 95% upper confidence limit (UCL) of 113.1 mg/kg, which was within the range of site-specific naturally occurring background. The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) and the Water Board (WB) agreed that this material could be used as [[clean-cover-embankment-fill]] on the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]]. The approval was contingent on similar analysis for MSE wall footing excavation soil along the southern boundary of Stockpile 2. The DTSC officially accepted this variance request via email on April 17, 2020. The expectation was that this soil would only be used as clean cover or embankment fill within the footprints of the stockpiles and not elsewhere on the project or hauled off-site.

*   **Second Variance Request (RDIP Variance #2):** This request, dated September 2, 2020, proposed the use of [[class-3-aggregate-subbase]] material as an acceptable clean cap alternative over the [[barium-containing-soil-aerially-deposited-lead]] (BCS/ADL) stockpiles. The request indicated that a minimum of 4 inches of Class 3 subbase would provide adequate clean cover until pavement and hardscape completion. However, a visual inspection of the Class 3 stockpiled material on September 15, 2020, revealed it to be primarily a fine sand, likely small enough to pass through a No. 4 sieve. Due to its fine-grained nature, both the DTSC and the WB agreed that a minimum cap thickness of 6 inches was warranted, rather than 4 inches, and that increased monitoring might be necessary, especially since no pavement or hardscaping would be completed before the upcoming rainy season. Additionally, the DTSC and WB requested a single, composite sample for [[barium]] and [[lead]] analysis (EPA Test Method 6010B) from the Class 3 material stockpile adjacent to the Rosemore Overcrossing to ensure it met RDIP standards for use as a clean cap. The DTSC officially accepted this second variance request via email on September 21, 2020.

## Regulatory Status

The RDIP Variance Requests are part of the regulatory process for the [[modesto-soil-stockpiles-project]], requiring approval from the Central Valley RWQCB and the DTSC. Approvals are granted with specific conditions and requirements, such as increased cap thickness and additional sampling.

## Open Items

*   The official approval or rejection of the soil from the MSE wall footing excavation along the southern boundary of Stockpile 2 was pending review at the time of the April 17, 2020 email.
*   Increased monitoring may be necessary for the Class 3 subbase cap due to its fine-grained nature and the impending rainy season, as noted in the September 21, 2020 approval email.
