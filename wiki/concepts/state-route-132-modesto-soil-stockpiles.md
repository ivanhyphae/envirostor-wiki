The State Route 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles represent a remediation case file managed by [[caltrans]] concerning potentially impacted soil stockpiles in Stanislaus County, California. The primary focus of the remediation efforts has been to assess and address potential impacts to groundwater from barium and lead.

### Site Inspections and Groundwater Evaluation

Annual inspections of the soil stockpiles have been conducted. Documentation from these inspections includes images of Stockpile #2, detailing its north slope, drainage structures, and a retaining wall. Inspections of Stockpile #1 also captured images of its north slope and paved surface conditions.

On May 3, 2024, the [[dtsc]], in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], approved the "Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California (Site Code: 900259)". This evaluation, prepared by Geocon Consultants Inc. for Caltrans, concluded that the encapsulated soil stockpiles beneath the State Route 132 Expressway are not impacting groundwater and are not expected to do so in the future. This conclusion was consistent with a 2014 evaluation and was further supported by the recent encapsulation of the soil.

### Regulatory Decision and Next Steps

Following the approval of the groundwater evaluation, the [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] concurred with the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring at the site. The four remaining monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) are to be decommissioned in accordance with the approved [[remedial-action-plan]].
