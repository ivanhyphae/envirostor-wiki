## State Route 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles

### Overview

The State Route 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles are a remediation case file managed by [[caltrans]]. The stockpiles, located in Stanislaus County, California, were identified as potentially impacted by barium and lead. A key aspect of the remediation effort has been the evaluation of potential impacts to groundwater.

### Site Record

Annual inspections of the soil stockpiles have been conducted. Images from an inspection of Stockpile #2 show its north slope, drainage structures, and a retaining wall. Inspections of Stockpile #1 also documented its north slope and paved surface conditions.

In a letter dated May 3, 2024, the [[dtsc]], in consultation with the [[central-valley-rwqcb]], approved the "Updated Comparative Evaluation of Groundwater Data, Caltrans Encapsulated Soil Stockpiles, State Route 132, Stanislaus County, California (Site Code: 900259)". This evaluation, submitted by Geocon Consultants Inc. on behalf of Caltrans, assessed the potential for barium and lead impacted soil stockpiles beneath the newly constructed State Route 132 Expressway to impact groundwater. The data indicated that the encapsulated soil is not impacting groundwater and is not expected to in the future. This conclusion was supported by consistency with an earlier 2014 evaluation and the recent encapsulation of the soil.

As a result of this approval, DTSC and the RWQCB concurred with the recommendation to discontinue groundwater monitoring beneath the soil stockpiles. The four remaining monitoring wells (MW4, MW6, MW9, and MW10) are to be decommissioned in accordance with the approved [[remedial-action-plan]].

### Regulatory Status

The [[dtsc]] and the [[central-valley-rwqcb]] have determined that the encapsulated soil stockpiles are not impacting groundwater. Groundwater monitoring is to be discontinued, and remaining monitoring wells are to be decommissioned as per the approved [[remedial-action-plan]].
